## 1. Ratio and proportion

### 1.1 Core

- **Ratio** a : b = a/b — a pure number; both terms must be in the **same unit**; multiplying/dividing both terms by the same non-zero number leaves it unchanged.
- **Proportion** a : b = c : d ⇔ **ad = bc** (product of extremes = product of means).
- **Mean proportional** between a and b = **√(ab)**. **Third proportional** to a, b = **b²/a**. **Fourth proportional** to a, b, c = **bc/a**.
- **Compounded ratio** of a:b and c:d = ac : bd. **Duplicate** ratio = a² : b²; **sub-duplicate** = √a : √b; **triplicate** = a³ : b³.

### 1.2 Componendo–dividendo ⚡
If a/b = c/d then **(a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d)**. This converts a messy "sum and difference" statement into one line — heavily used when a question gives you a ratio of sums.

### 1.3 The k-method ⚡ (the workhorse)
Whenever a ratio is given, **write the quantities as multiples of k** and let the second condition fix k.

*Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 7; adding 3 to each makes the ratio 3 : 4. Find them.*
→ take them as 5k and 7k → (5k + 3)/(7k + 3) = 3/4 → 20k + 12 = 21k + 9 → k = 3 → the numbers are **15 and 21**.

⚠️ If the k-method throws up a negative or fractional k where the question demands whole numbers, the data is inconsistent — check your reading of the question rather than forcing an option.

### 1.4 Splitting a quantity in a ratio
Divide ₹X in a : b : c → shares are **X·a/(a+b+c)** etc.
⚡ **Chained ratios:** A:B = 2:3, B:C = 4:5 → make B common: A:B = 8:12, B:C = 12:15 → **A:B:C = 8:12:15**. (Multiply the first by B's second value, the second by B's first value.)

### 1.5 Variation

| Type | Relation | Signature phrase |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | y = kx (y/x constant) | "y varies as x", "proportional to" |
| **Inverse** | y = k/x (xy constant) | "varies inversely", "more men, less time" |
| **Joint** | y = kxz | "varies jointly as x and z" |
| **Combined** | y = kx/z | "directly as x and inversely as z" |

**Chain rule for work-type variation ⚡:** (M₁ D₁ H₁)/W₁ = (M₂ D₂ H₂)/W₂ — men, days, hours, work. Everything in the numerator is inversely related to everything else in the numerator; work sits alone in the denominator.

### 1.6 Partnership
- **Simple** (same period): profit divided in the ratio of **capitals**.
- **Compound** (different periods): profit divided in the ratio of **capital × time** ("months' capital").
- **Working vs sleeping partner:** the working partner's salary/commission is deducted first, remainder shared by capital-months.

## 2. Percentage ⭐⭐⭐

### 2.1 The fraction ↔ percentage table (memorise — saves minutes)

| Fraction | % | Fraction | % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 50 | 1/9 | 11⅑ |
| 1/3 | 33⅓ | 1/10 | 10 |
| 1/4 | 25 | 1/11 | 9¹/₁₁ |
| 1/5 | 20 | 1/12 | 8⅓ |
| 1/6 | 16⅔ | 1/15 | 6⅔ |
| 1/7 | 14²/₇ | 1/16 | 6¼ |
| 1/8 | 12½ | 1/20 | 5 |

Multiples follow: 3/8 = 37½%, 5/6 = 83⅓%, 2/7 = 28⁴/₇%.

### 2.2 The formulae that matter

| Situation | Formula |
|---|---|
| x as % of y | (x/y) × 100 |
| **% increase / decrease** | (change ÷ **original**) × 100 ⚠️ always the *original* |
| A is x% more than B ⇒ **B is less than A by** | **[x/(100+x)] × 100 %** |
| A is x% less than B ⇒ **B is more than A by** | **[x/(100−x)] × 100 %** |
| **Successive changes** of a% and b% | net = **a + b + ab/100** (use signs) |
| Value increased by x% then decreased by x% | net **decrease = x²/100 %** (never zero) ⚠️ |
| Population/price after n periods of r% growth | P(1 + r/100)ⁿ |
| Price rises r%, consumption must fall by | **[r/(100+r)] × 100 %** to keep expenditure constant |
| Price falls r%, consumption may rise by | [r/(100−r)] × 100 % |
| Passing marks | (marks obtained + marks short) = pass mark |

⚡ **The "more than / less than" pair is the single most-tested percentage idea in state PSC papers.** If A is 25% more than B, B is 20% less than A (25/125 = 20%). If A is 20% less than B, B is 25% more than A. Learn the pairs: **10↔9¹/₁₁, 20↔16⅔, 25↔20, 33⅓↔25, 50↔33⅓, 100↔50.**

### 2.3 Election, examination and error questions
- **Two-candidate election:** if the winner gets x% and wins by V votes, then (2x − 100)% of total = V.
- **Marks:** if a student scores x% and fails by m marks while the pass mark is p%, total = (m × 100)/(p − x).
- **Error percentage** = (error ÷ true value) × 100. If a length is measured x% in excess, the computed **area** is in excess by (2x + x²/100)%.

## 3. Profit and loss ⭐⭐

### 3.1 Base definitions
CP = cost price, SP = selling price, MP = marked (list) price, D = discount.

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Profit | SP − CP · **Profit% = (SP − CP)/CP × 100** |
| Loss | CP − SP · **Loss% = (CP − SP)/CP × 100** |
| SP from CP | SP = CP × (100 + P%)/100 |
| CP from SP | CP = SP × 100/(100 + P%) |
| Discount | **D = MP − SP; D% = D/MP × 100** ⚠️ discount is on **MP**, profit on **CP** |
| Successive discounts a%, b% | net = a + b − ab/100 |
| MP fixed to give P% profit after D% discount | **MP = CP × (100 + P)/(100 − D)** |

### 3.2 The five recurring APPSC templates ⭐

1. **Two articles sold at the same price, one at x% profit and one at x% loss** → there is **always a net loss** of **x²/100 %**. (Sell two at ₹99 each, +10% and −10% → 1% loss.)
2. **Dishonest shopkeeper using a false weight** — sells at cost price but gives *w* g instead of 1000 g:
 **Gain% = [(True weight − False weight)/False weight] × 100** = (Error/(True value − Error)) × 100.
3. **Marked-up-then-discounted chain:** apply mark-up on CP, then discount on MP; the two never cancel.
4. **Profit % if n articles are sold at the cost of m articles** = **[(m − n)/n] × 100**.
5. **Break-even and "at what price should he have sold"** — always convert back to CP first, then re-apply the desired percentage. Never apply the new percentage to the old SP.

⚠️ **Trap:** "profit of 20% on the selling price" ≠ 20% on cost. On SP, profit 20% means CP = 80% of SP → profit on CP = 25%.

## 4. Simple and compound interest ⭐⭐

### 4.1 Simple interest

**SI = PRT/100** · **A = P + SI = P(1 + RT/100)**

- SI grows **linearly**: the interest for each year is identical.
- If a sum becomes **n times in T years** at SI, then **R = 100(n − 1)/T**.
- **Time to double** at SI: T = 100/R years; to triple: 200/R.

### 4.2 Compound interest

**A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ** · **CI = A − P**

| Compounding | Adjust |
|---|---|
| **Annually** | rate R, periods n |
| **Half-yearly** | rate **R/2**, periods **2n** |
| **Quarterly** | rate **R/4**, periods **4n** |
| **Different rates each year** | A = P(1+R₁/100)(1+R₂/100)… |
| **Fractional year (n + p/q)** | A = P(1+R/100)ⁿ × (1 + (p/q)R/100) |

### 4.3 The differences (asked almost every year) ⭐

- **CI − SI for 2 years = P(R/100)²** ⚡ (equivalently = SI × R/(2×100) for 2 years)
- **CI − SI for 3 years = P(R/100)² × (300 + R)/100** = P·R²(300+R)/10⁶
- SI and CI are **equal for the first year** (single-period compounding).
- **Amount doubles at CI:** roughly by the **Rule of 72** — years ≈ 72/R ⚡ (approximate but excellent for eliminating options).
- If a sum becomes x times in n years at CI, it becomes **x²** times in **2n** years, x³ in 3n years.

### 4.4 Instalments

- **SI instalments:** Present value P = sum of instalments discounted linearly.
- **CI instalments (equal annual instalment X, n years):**
 **P = X/(1+R/100) + X/(1+R/100)² + … + X/(1+R/100)ⁿ**
 For 2 years the standard trick is P = X[100/(100+R) + (100/(100+R))²].

### 4.5 Growth, depreciation, population
- Population after n years: **P(1 + r/100)ⁿ**; n years ago: **P/(1 + r/100)ⁿ**.
- Depreciation: **P(1 − r/100)ⁿ**.
- Growth with different signs (rise then fall) uses the same successive-change formula as percentage: a + b + ab/100.

## 5. Worked PYQ-pattern set

**Q1.** The ratio of two numbers is 3 : 5 and their HCF is 4. Their LCM is:
**Sol.** Numbers are 12 and 20 → LCM = **60**. (Shortcut: LCM = HCF × product of the co-prime ratio terms = 4 × 15 = 60.)

**Q2.** If A : B = 2 : 3, B : C = 4 : 5 and C : D = 6 : 7, then A : D is:
**Sol.** Multiply the chain: A/D = (2/3)(4/5)(6/7) = 48/105 = **16 : 35**.

**Q3.** The salary of a person is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. The net change is:
**Sol.** −(20²/100)% = **4% decrease**.

**Q4.** If A's income is 25% more than B's, by what per cent is B's income less than A's?
**Sol.** 25/125 × 100 = **20%**.

**Q5.** A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above cost and allows a discount of 25%. His profit per cent is:
**Sol.** CP 100 → MP 140 → SP = 140 × 0.75 = 105 → **5% profit**.

**Q6.** A man sells two watches at ₹1,200 each, gaining 20% on one and losing 20% on the other. His overall result is:
**Sol.** Same-price ± same-% rule → loss of 20²/100 = **4%** (loss ₹100 on a total CP of ₹2,500).

**Q7.** A dishonest dealer professes to sell at cost price but uses a weight of 900 g for 1 kg. His gain per cent is:
**Sol.** (100/900) × 100 = **11⅑%**.

**Q8.** The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 10% p.a. is ₹50. The sum is:
**Sol.** P(R/100)² = 50 → P(0.1)² = 50 → **₹5,000**.

**Q9.** ₹8,000 at 10% p.a. compounded half-yearly for 1 year amounts to:
**Sol.** R = 5%, n = 2 → 8000 × 1.05² = 8000 × 1.1025 = **₹8,820**.

**Q10.** A sum doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. In how many years will it become 5 times?
**Sol.** Doubling in 8 years → SI of 100% in 8 years → R = 12.5%. Five times = 400% interest → T = 400/12.5 = **32 years**. ⚡ *Shortcut:* (n₂ − 1)/(n₁ − 1) = T₂/T₁ → (5−1)/(2−1) = 4 → 4 × 8 = 32.

**Q11.** A, B and C invest ₹6,000, ₹8,000 and ₹10,000 for 6, 9 and 12 months respectively. Their profit ratio is:
**Sol.** Capital-months: 36 : 72 : 120 → **3 : 6 : 10**.

**Q12.** The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what per cent must a family reduce consumption so that expenditure is unchanged?
**Sol.** 25/125 × 100 = **20%**.

## 6. Traps checklist ⚠️

1. **% increase is on the original; discount is on MP; profit is on CP.** Identify the base before touching the arithmetic.
2. **x% up then x% down is never zero** — always a loss of x²/100%.
3. "Profit on selling price" must be converted to profit on cost.
4. Successive discounts do **not** add (20% + 10% ≠ 30%; it is 28%).
5. **Same SP, equal profit and loss %** → always a loss.
6. Half-yearly compounding: **halve the rate AND double the periods** — candidates routinely do only one.
7. SI and CI coincide in year 1; the difference formula for 2 years is **P(R/100)²**, not PR²/100.
8. Partnership with unequal periods needs **capital × time**, not capital.
9. In chained ratios, equalise the common term before combining.
10. "n times in T years" — the SI relation uses **(n − 1)**, because the principal itself is not interest.

## 7. Rapid-fire recall

- **a + b + ab/100** — successive percentage change (and successive discount with b negative).
- **x²/100** — net loss for equal ±x% (both the price-change and the two-articles case).
- **x/(100+x) × 100** — the "more than ⇒ less than" conversion.
- **MP = CP(100+P)/(100−D)** — mark-up needed for a target profit after discount.
- **CI − SI (2 yr) = P(R/100)²**; **(3 yr) = PR²(300+R)/10⁶**.
- **Rule of 72** — doubling time at CI ≈ 72/R.
- **Gain% on false weight = Error/(True − Error) × 100.**
- Partnership share ∝ **capital × time**.
