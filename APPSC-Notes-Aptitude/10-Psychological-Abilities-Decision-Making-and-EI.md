# 10 · "Psychological Abilities" — Decision Making, Problem Solving, Emotional Intelligence, Interpersonal & Communication Skills

[← Data Interpretation](09-Data-Interpretation-and-Data-Sufficiency.md) · [Index](README.md) · [Next: Formula Bank & Practice Set →](11-Formula-Shortcut-Bank-and-Full-Practice-Set.md)

**Syllabus phrase covered:** the *"psychological abilities"* half of the section head, together with *"decision making and problem solving"* and *"interpersonal and communication skills"*.

**PYQ signal:** 4–8 questions, and the block candidates most often leave unprepared because "you can't study it". That is wrong. These questions are **not** psychology-theory questions and they are **not** personality tests — they are **rule-governed MCQs** with a consistent underlying value system: the conduct expected of a **public servant**. Once you know the ranking rules in §6, this becomes one of the highest-accuracy blocks in the paper.

Two kinds of question appear:
- **(A) Conceptual** — definitions and models (types of intelligence, Goleman's components, barriers to communication, steps in decision making). Straight recall.
- **(B) Situational** — "You are the Mandal Revenue Officer and X happens. What will you do?" Judgement, scored against an official's expected conduct.

---

# PART I — CONCEPTUAL CORE (the recall half)

## 1. What "mental ability" and "psychological ability" mean

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intelligence** | The general capacity to learn, reason and adapt to new situations |
| **IQ** | **(Mental age / Chronological age) × 100** — Stern's ratio, popularised by Terman. Average = 100 |
| **Aptitude** | **Potential** to acquire a skill *with training* — future-oriented |
| **Achievement** | What has **already** been learned — past-oriented |
| **Attitude** | A learned tendency to respond favourably/unfavourably; components: **cognitive (belief), affective (feeling), behavioural (action)** ⚡ |
| **Personality** | The characteristic pattern of thinking, feeling and behaving |
| **Interest** | Preference/liking that directs attention |

⚠️ **Aptitude vs achievement vs attitude** is a favourite one-line distinction. *Aptitude = can learn; achievement = has learned; attitude = willing to.*

**Theories of intelligence worth one line each:**
- **Spearman — two-factor:** a general factor **"g"** plus specific factors **"s"**.
- **Thurstone — primary mental abilities** (seven: verbal, numerical, spatial, memory, reasoning, word fluency, perceptual speed).
- **Guilford — Structure of Intellect** (operations × contents × products).
- **Gardner — Multiple Intelligences** (linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinaesthetic, **interpersonal**, **intrapersonal**, naturalistic).
- **Sternberg — Triarchic:** **componential (analytical), experiential (creative), contextual (practical)**.
- **Cattell — fluid vs crystallised** intelligence (reasoning power vs accumulated knowledge).

## 2. Emotional intelligence ⭐⭐

**Definition:** the ability to perceive, understand, manage and use emotions — one's own and others' — effectively.
**Coined by Salovey & Mayer (1990); popularised by Daniel Goleman (1995, *Emotional Intelligence*).**

### Goleman's five components — memorise the list and the order ⚡

| # | Component | Meaning | Administrative face |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Self-awareness** | Recognising your own emotions, strengths, limits | Knowing your bias before you decide |
| 2 | **Self-regulation** | Managing impulses; thinking before acting | Not reacting in anger to a hostile delegation |
| 3 | **Motivation** | Inner drive to achieve beyond money/status | Persisting on a difficult scheme rollout |
| 4 | **Empathy** | Sensing what others feel; reading a group | Grasping why displaced families resist a project |
| 5 | **Social skills** | Managing relationships, building networks, persuasion | Negotiating between departments |

⚡ Mnemonic: **S-S-M-E-S** — "**S**elf-aware, **S**elf-regulated, **M**otivated, **E**mpathetic, **S**ocially skilled." The first three are **personal competences** (about oneself), the last two are **social competences** (about others) — a distinction that itself gets asked.

**Related terms:** **EQ** (emotional quotient); **empathy vs sympathy** ⚠️ — *empathy* is understanding another's feelings from their frame of reference; *sympathy* is feeling pity/sorrow **for** them. Administration values empathy; sympathy alone can distort impartiality.

## 3. Decision making ⭐

**Definition:** choosing a course of action from among alternatives.

### 3.1 The standard steps (rational model)
1. **Identify and define the problem**
2. **Gather relevant information / diagnose**
3. **Develop alternatives**
4. **Evaluate alternatives** against criteria (feasibility, cost, legality, equity, time)
5. **Select the best alternative**
6. **Implement**
7. **Monitor, review, take feedback**

### 3.2 Models and terms (one line each) ⭐

| Model / term | Core idea |
|---|---|
| **Rational (economic man)** | Full information, all alternatives evaluated, optimum chosen — an ideal |
| **Herbert Simon — bounded rationality & "satisficing"** ⭐ | Real decision-makers have limited information, time and cognitive capacity; they choose the **first satisfactory** option, not the optimum |
| **Lindblom — incrementalism ("muddling through")** | Decisions proceed by small successive comparisons from the status quo |
| **Etzioni — mixed scanning** | Broad scanning for fundamental decisions + detailed examination for incremental ones |
| **Vroom–Yetton** | How much to involve subordinates: autocratic → consultative → group |
| **Programmed vs non-programmed** | Routine/rule-based vs novel/judgement-based decisions |
| **Groupthink (Janis)** | Cohesive groups suppress dissent and take poor decisions |
| **Brainstorming / Delphi / nominal group** | Techniques for generating alternatives; **Delphi** uses anonymous expert rounds |
| **SWOT, cost–benefit, decision tree, PERT/CPM** | Analytical aids |

⚠️ **Simon's "satisficing" and Lindblom's "incrementalism" are the two names most likely to be asked by name** — they also feature in the Public Administration paper (see [`../APPSC-Notes-PubAd/`](../APPSC-Notes-PubAd/README.md)), so learn them once for both.

## 4. Problem solving

**Steps:** define the problem → analyse causes → generate solutions → evaluate & select → implement → verify.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Algorithm** | Step-by-step procedure guaranteeing a solution (slow but certain) |
| **Heuristic** | A rule of thumb / mental shortcut — fast but fallible |
| **Trial and error** | Unsystematic attempts |
| **Insight learning (Köhler)** | Sudden perception of relationships — the "aha!" moment |
| **Means–end analysis** | Reduce the gap between the present state and the goal, step by step |
| **Root cause analysis / "5 Whys"** | Keep asking why until the underlying cause surfaces |
| **Mental set / functional fixedness** ⚠️ | Barriers: sticking to a familiar approach; failing to see a new use for an object |
| **Convergent vs divergent thinking** | One correct answer vs many creative possibilities (divergent = creativity) |

## 5. Interpersonal and communication skills ⭐

**Communication process:** **Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback** (with **noise** acting at every stage). ⚡ Feedback is what makes communication **two-way**; its absence is the commonest defect in administrative communication.

| Classification | Types |
|---|---|
| By form | **Verbal** (oral, written) and **non-verbal** (kinesics/body language, proxemics/space, paralanguage/tone, haptics/touch, chronemics/time) |
| By direction | **Downward** (orders), **upward** (reports, grievances), **horizontal** (coordination), **diagonal** |
| By channel | **Formal** (official) and **informal ("grapevine")** — fast, but distortion-prone |

**Barriers to communication ⭐ (a favourite list):**
- **Physical** — distance, noise, faulty equipment
- **Semantic/linguistic** — jargon, ambiguity, language difference
- **Psychological** — prejudice, emotion, distrust, perceptual selectivity
- **Organisational** — long hierarchy, information overload, rigid rules
- **Cultural** — differing norms and meanings

**Remedies:** clarity and brevity, **active listening**, feedback loops, appropriate channel, empathy, avoiding jargon, shortening the chain of command.

**Listening ⚡:** *hearing* is physical; **listening** is active and interpretive. **Active listening** = attending, paraphrasing, clarifying, withholding judgement. In situational questions, "**listen to the complainant fully before acting**" is almost always part of the correct answer.

**Interpersonal skills that recur as options:** empathy, assertiveness (**not** aggression, **not** passivity), teamwork, conflict resolution, negotiation, leadership, patience, tolerance of criticism.

**Conflict-handling modes (Thomas–Kilmann)** ⚡ — competing, **collaborating** (usually the "best" answer), compromising, avoiding, accommodating.

**Transactional Analysis (Eric Berne)** — ego states **Parent, Adult, Child**; the mature administrative stance is **Adult-to-Adult**.

**Motivation theories occasionally asked:** Maslow's hierarchy (physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation); Herzberg's **hygiene vs motivators**; McGregor's **Theory X and Theory Y**.

# PART II — SITUATIONAL QUESTIONS: HOW TO ANSWER ⭐⭐

## 6. The ranking rules

Situational MCQs are scored against an implicit hierarchy. Learn it as an ordered filter and apply it in this sequence:

1. **Legality and rules first.** Any option that involves breaking a rule, falsifying a record, accepting a favour or bypassing due process is **out**, however humane it looks.
2. **Do not evade.** Options that amount to "ignore it", "do nothing", "pretend not to notice", "let someone else handle it" are wrong. Passivity is never the answer.
3. **Do not simply escalate either.** "Refer the matter to higher authority" is wrong **when the decision is within your own competence**. It is right only when the matter genuinely exceeds your authority — and even then the better option usually reads "act on what I can *and* report to the superior".
4. **Verify before acting.** Where the situation involves an allegation, rumour or incomplete information, the correct answer almost always includes **finding out the facts first**. Punishing on hearsay is wrong.
5. **Address the immediate harm, then the systemic cause.** The best option usually does **both** — relief now, fix later. This is why "both (I) and (II) follow" is so often correct in course-of-action questions ([File 08](08-Venn-Diagrams-Syllogisms-and-Logical-Reasoning.md)).
6. **Prefer persuasion and dialogue over coercion** — but not to the point of inaction where the law requires enforcement.
7. **Impartiality over personal relationships.** A friend, relative, caste-fellow or influential person gets exactly what any citizen gets.
8. **Empathy without partiality.** Listen to and respect the aggrieved party; do not convert sympathy into a special favour.
9. **Take responsibility.** Options where you accept your own error, or protect a subordinate from unfair blame while correcting them privately, rank high.
10. **Proportionality.** Choose the least drastic measure that solves the problem. Suspension/FIR/force are last resorts, not first responses.

⚡ **The composite-option principle:** where an option combines a **legal, immediate, fact-based** action with a **long-term corrective** one, it is almost certainly the intended answer. Examiners build the "best" option to be the balanced one; the distractors are the extremes — the harsh one, the passive one, the rule-breaking-but-kind one, and the pass-the-buck one.

## 7. Distractor recognition ⚡

| Option smells like… | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Ignore the complaint as it is minor" | ✘ evasion |
| "Immediately suspend the official" (no enquiry) | ✘ disproportionate, no due process |
| "Adjust the record to help the poor applicant" | ✘ illegal, however sympathetic |
| "Ask a colleague to handle it" | ✘ abdication |
| "Consult the rules and existing precedent, then decide" | ✔ typically correct |
| "Meet the affected people, ascertain facts, then take action under the rules and report to the superior" | ✔ typically the best |
| "Do whatever the local MLA/influential person suggests" | ✘ partiality |
| "Wait for the situation to resolve itself" | ✘ passivity |

## 8. Ordering/ranking format
Some papers ask you to **rank four responses** from best to worst rather than pick one. Apply §6 as a scoring rubric: legality > verification > balanced action > escalation > passivity > rule-breaking. Rank the composite balanced option first and the illegal or evasive one last.

## 9. Worked PYQ-pattern set

**Q1.** Who popularised the concept of Emotional Intelligence through his 1995 book?
(a) Howard Gardner (b) **Daniel Goleman** (c) Herbert Simon (d) Alfred Binet → **(b)**. *(Salovey and Mayer coined the term in 1990.)*

**Q2.** "Satisficing" as a description of administrative decision making is associated with:
(a) Charles Lindblom (b) Amitai Etzioni (c) **Herbert Simon** (d) Victor Vroom → **(c)**.

**Q3.** Which of the following is **not** one of Goleman's five components of emotional intelligence?
(a) Self-awareness (b) Empathy (c) **Intelligence quotient** (d) Social skills → **(c)**.

**Q4.** Aptitude differs from achievement in that aptitude refers to:
(a) what has already been learned (b) **the potential to learn with training** (c) a person's attitude to work (d) accumulated knowledge → **(b)**.

**Q5.** The three components of an attitude are:
**Sol.** **Cognitive, affective and behavioural.**

**Q6.** In the communication process, the element that makes communication **two-way** is:
(a) encoding (b) channel (c) **feedback** (d) noise → **(c)**.

**Q7.** "Grapevine" refers to:
(a) downward formal communication (b) **informal communication in an organisation** (c) written official communication (d) non-verbal communication → **(b)**.

**Q8.** Sudden perception of relationships leading to a solution is called:
(a) trial and error (b) **insight learning** (c) conditioning (d) means–end analysis → **(b)**.

**Q9.** *Situational:* You are a Tahsildar. A group of farmers arrives agitatedly claiming their crop-insurance claims have not been credited. You have not yet verified the position. You should:
(a) tell them to come back next week — ✘ evasion
(b) assure them the money will come — ✘ promise without facts
(c) **hear them out, note the details, check the records and the bank status the same day, inform them of the position and escalate the pending cases to the district office** — ✔
(d) ask them to submit a written representation and take no other step — ✘ minimal, procedurally correct but passive
→ **(c)**: listening + verification + action within competence + escalation of what exceeds it.

**Q10.** *Situational:* You find that a subordinate has made a serious error in a land record that has already gone out. He is otherwise sincere and is your junior-most staff member. You should:
(a) ignore it since he is sincere — ✘
(b) **correct the record through the proper procedure immediately, inform your superior of the correction, and counsel the subordinate privately** — ✔
(c) issue a public reprimand and start disciplinary proceedings at once — ✘ disproportionate
(d) ask him to quietly alter the record — ✘ illegal
→ **(b)**: fix the harm, follow procedure, protect dignity while correcting.

**Q11.** *Situational:* An influential person requests you to give priority to his application, which is genuine but out of turn.
**Sol.** Process it **in its turn** under the normal rules; explain the position courteously. Genuineness does not justify jumping the queue — that would be partiality towards the influential.

**Q12.** The conflict-handling mode that seeks a solution fully satisfying the concerns of both parties is:
(a) compromising (b) avoiding (c) **collaborating** (d) accommodating → **(c)**.

## 10. Traps checklist ⚠️

1. **Empathy ≠ sympathy.** Empathy is understanding; sympathy is pity. Options praising pity-driven action are usually wrong.
2. "Refer to higher authority" is wrong when the matter is **within your competence**.
3. "Take no action / wait and watch" is essentially never correct.
4. A kind but **rule-breaking** option is always wrong, no matter how humane the framing.
5. Harsh, immediate punitive action **without enquiry** is always wrong.
6. **Aptitude** = potential, **achievement** = past learning, **attitude** = disposition.
7. Goleman has **five** components — self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills — and EQ is not "IQ for feelings".
8. **Satisficing = Simon; incrementalism = Lindblom; mixed scanning = Etzioni.** Don't swap them.
9. **Hearing ≠ listening**; assertiveness ≠ aggression; feedback is the two-way element.
10. In ranking-format questions, the **balanced composite option** goes first, the illegal one last.

## 11. Rapid-fire recall

- **IQ = (MA/CA) × 100.** Aptitude = potential; achievement = attained.
- Attitude = **cognitive + affective + behavioural**.
- EI: coined **Salovey & Mayer (1990)**, popularised **Goleman (1995)**; five components **S-S-M-E-S** (first three personal, last two social).
- Intelligence theories: Spearman **g + s**; Thurstone **7 PMAs**; Gardner **multiple**; Sternberg **triarchic**; Cattell **fluid/crystallised**.
- **Simon — bounded rationality, satisficing**; **Lindblom — incrementalism**; **Etzioni — mixed scanning**; **Janis — groupthink**; **Delphi — anonymous expert rounds**.
- Problem solving: algorithm (certain) vs heuristic (fast); **functional fixedness** and **mental set** are barriers; **divergent thinking = creativity**.
- Communication: sender→encode→message→channel→decode→receiver→**feedback**, with **noise**; barriers physical/semantic/psychological/organisational/cultural; **grapevine = informal**.
- Conflict modes: competing, **collaborating**, compromising, avoiding, accommodating.
- Situational rule stack: **legality → verify facts → act within competence → relief + systemic fix → impartiality → proportionality**.
