## 0. Frame

Andhra fought two intertwined struggles: the **national** one against the Raj — Vandemataram (1907), Non-cooperation's showpieces (**Chirala-Perala, Palnadu, Pedanandipadu**), **Alluri's Rampa rebellion (1922–24)**, Salt Satyagraha, Quit India — and the **regional** one for a Telugu province, organised through the **ANDHRA MAHASABHAS (from Bapatla, 1913)**. Beside and against both ran the **Justice Party / Non-Brahmin movement** (2020 Q9(a)), while poets from **Garimella to Sri Sri and Jashuva** made Telugu verse a weapon (2023 Q9(b) on Dalit poets).

## 1. Andhra in the national movement ⭐⭐

### 1.1 Early nationalism (1885–1905)
- Telugu men at the founding: **P. Ananda Charlu** (a founder of the **Madras Mahajana Sabha, 1884**; **Congress president, Nagpur 1891**), **Nyapathi Subba Rao Pantulu** (a "founding hand" of Congress in the south, *The Hindu*'s early circle).
- Moderate petitioning + the reform milieu of File 37.

### 1.2 Vandemataram & Swadeshi in Andhra (1906–11) ⭐
- **Bipin Chandra Pal's tour (April 1907)** electrified the coast; at **Rajahmundry**, students wearing Vandemataram badges defied Principal **Mark Hunter** (the "Rajahmundry students' strike"); Kakinada and Machilipatnam boycotts.
- **National education:** **Andhra Jateeya Kalasala, Machilipatnam (1907)** (Kopalle Hanumantha Rao); swadeshi weaving; **Gadicherla jailed** for *Swarajya* articles (File 38).
- **"Vandemataram Ramulu"?** — remember instead the movement's Andhra epithet: the **"Vandemataram movement"** is the exam's label for 1907 Andhra.

### 1.3 Home Rule → Rowlatt (1916–19)
- Besant's League swept the coastal towns (File 37 §3.5); her 1917 internment produced Andhra-wide protest; **first Andhra PCC (a separate Congress "Andhra circle") sanctioned 1917–18** — the Congress's first concession to linguistic identity ⭐ (generalised at **Nagpur, 1920**).

### 1.4 Non-cooperation (1920–22): Andhra's three showpieces ⭐⭐
1. **CHIRALA-PERALA (1921):** rather than pay the new municipality's taxes, **DUGGIRALA GOPALAKRISHNAYYA ("Andhra Ratna")** led virtually the whole town (~13–15,000) out to a bamboo new-town, **"RAMANAGAR"**, for about **11 months** — with his volunteer corps **"Ramadandu"**; the epic collapsed after his arrest, but became the era's legend.
2. **PALNADU FOREST SATYAGRAHA (1921–22):** Palnadu peasants defied forest-grazing fees; **KANNEGANTI HANUMANTHU** martyred in the police firing at **Minchalapadu (1922)**.
3. **PEDANANDIPADU NO-TAX CAMPAIGN (1922, Guntur):** **Parvataneni Veerayya Chowdary**'s parallel-government-style land-revenue refusal — so effective the AICC itself (post-Chauri Chaura) had it called off.
- **Konda Venkatappayya** led the province's Congress machine through all three.

### 1.5 ALLURI SITARAMA RAJU and the Rampa/Manyam Rebellion, 1922–24 ⭐⭐ (2023 Q9(a))
- **Causes:** the **Madras Forest Act (1882)** régime — bans on **podu** cultivation and forest produce, forced labour (vetti) on ghat roads, extortionate muttadar-contractor-police nexus in the **Gudem-Rampa** agency tracts (older fituri tradition: 1879 Rampa rising).
- **The leader:** a young sanyasi-like outsider (b. 1897) who fused **Gandhian idiom** (temperance, panchayats, khadi in early phase) with **armed fituri**; the hill Koyas and Bagatas — commanders **Gam Gantam Dora & Gam Mallu Dora** — became his army.
- **Course:** August 1922 — coordinated raids on **Chintapalli, Krishnadevipeta and Rajavommangi** police stations (arms seized); ambushes (Damanapalli — two officers killed); two years of jungle war tying down the **Malabar Special Police**; special commissioner **Rutherford**'s repression.
- **End:** captured and **shot at Koyyuru (near Mampa), 7 May 1924** — "trial by bullet"; hill memory canonised **"MANYAM VEERUDU"** ⭐.
- **Assessment (PYQ):** not a Congress movement (Gandhi disapproved of violence) yet nationalist in aim ("drive the white man out"); the classic **tribal-subaltern rising with a modern leader** — link §File 38's fituri culture; centenary honours (statues, tableau) show the living memory.

### 1.6 Simon to Quit India (1928–47)
- **Simon boycott (1928):** in Madras, **TANGUTURI PRAKASAM ("ANDHRA KESARI")** bared his chest before police guns — the era's iconic image ⭐.
- **Salt Satyagraha (1930):** Andhra's salt camps — **Chinnapuram (Machilipatnam), Mypadu (Nellore), Chirala coast**; leaders jailed (Konda Venkatappayya, Prakasam; **Durgabai** organised in Madras); forest satyagrahas followed (1932).
- **1937:** Congress ministry in Madras (Rajaji; Prakasam revenue minister) — estate-tenancy inquiries (File 36's zamindari question).
- **QUIT INDIA (1942):** student-led hartals and sabotage across Krishna-Guntur-Godavari (rail/telegraph cuts, e.g. around **Tenali**); heavy firings and collective fines — Andhra among the most active Madras-Presidency regions.
- **Peasant-left stream:** N.G. Ranga's ryot movement (Andhra Ryots' Association, kisan schools), Communist consolidation (a caveat-line: the armed **Telangana rebellion, 1946–51,** belongs to the Nizam's dominion, not British Andhra).

## 2. Justice Party & the Non-Brahmin movement ⭐⭐ (2020 Q9(a))

### 2.1 Circumstances of formation (the PYQ skeleton)
1. **Brahmin preponderance** in the new professions: ~3% of the Presidency's population but the large majority of graduates, judges, deputy collectors and university seats (memorise as "3% ↔ ~70%+ of posts" order-of-magnitude).
2. **Non-Brahmin elite anxiety:** newly wealthy landed and mercantile castes (Reddis, Kammas, Velamas, Chettis, Mudaliars, Nairs…) wanted their share of power.
3. **Ideological seed:** the "Dravidian" thesis (Caldwell's grammar) — non-Brahmins as the land's original people; Theosophy-linked **Besant's Home Rule** read as looming "Brahmin raj".
4. **Organisation:** Nov–Dec **1916, Madras** — **T.M. Nair + P. Theagaraya Chetty + C. Natesa Mudaliar** unite; the **South Indian Liberal Federation** ("Justice Party" after its paper *Justice*); the **"Non-Brahmin Manifesto" (Dec 1916)**.
5. **Constitutional opening:** Montford **dyarchy (1919)** + Congress's boycott of the **1920 elections** handed Justice office.

### 2.2 In office (1920–37) and the Andhra faces
- First ministries led by **A. Subbarayalu** then the **RAJA OF PANAGAL (Panaganti Ramarayaningar — a Telugu, CM 1921–26)** ⭐; later the **RAJA OF BOBBILI (CM 1932–36)** and **Kurma Venkata Reddy Naidu (acting, 1936–37)** — Andhra men headed the party's governments; **A.P. Patro** another pillar.
- **Measures:** the **first Communal G.O.s (1921)** — reservation in public services (ancestor of Madras reservations); **Madras Hindu Religious Endowments Act (1926)**; Staff Selection Board; **women's suffrage — Madras first (1921)**; **Andhra University Act (1925/26)** passed in the Justice era (AU opened 1926 — File 40 §1); Muthulakshmi Reddi's anti-devadasi initiatives (1929–30).
- **Non-Brahmin movement beyond the party:** Periyar's **Self-Respect movement (1925)** echoed in Andhra as **rationalism** — **Tripuraneni Ramaswamy**'s priestless weddings and anti-purana polemics (§3); caste-uplift conferences (Kamma, Reddy, Panchama sabhas).
- **Decline:** pro-Raj image, elitism, 1937 rout by Congress; 1944 → Periyar's Dravidar Kazhagam (Tamil trajectory). **Legacy for Andhra:** reservations, non-Brahmin political self-confidence — the social base of later Andhra politics.

## 3. Nationalist & revolutionary literature ⭐⭐ (2023 Q9(b) Dalit poets)

| Writer | Key works | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| **GARIMELLA SATYANARAYANA (1893–1952)** | **"Makoddee Tella Doratanam"** (1921 — "We don't want this White lordship") | jailed for a song — the anthem of Non-cooperation Andhra |
| **RAYAPROLU SUBBARAO (1892–1984)** | *Trunakankanam*, *Andhravali*; **"Edesamegina Endukalidina…"** | founder of **Bhava kavitvam** (romantic renaissance); "whichever land you go to, praise your motherland" |
| **GURAJADA** (File 37) | "Desamunu preminchumanna" | patriotism = love of people |
| **CHILAKAMARTHI** (File 37) | "Bharatakhandambu…" | milch-cow padyam against exploitation |
| **UNNAVA LAKSHMINARAYANA (1877–1958)** | ***MALAPALLI* (1922)** — the first great Dalit-theme Telugu novel (Gandhian-socialist; banned by the Raj) | jailed non-cooperator; founded **Sarada Niketan** (Guntur) for women; wife Lakshmibayamma his co-worker |
| **TRIPURANENI RAMASWAMY ("KAVIRAJU", 1887–1943)** | *Sutasramam* (Tenali), **Sambhuka Vadha**, *Suta Puranam*, *Khooni* | Dublin-trained barrister; **father of Telugu rationalism** — rewrote the epics from the victims' side; priestless "self-respect" rituals |
| **GURRAM JASHUVA (1895–1971)** | ***GABBILAM* (1941)** — an "untouchable's Meghaduta": a bat carries the outcaste's message to Siva; *Firadausi*, *Kandiseekudu* | "**Navayuga Kavi Chakravarti**"; answered avadhani-sabha humiliations with peerless classical Telugu; Padma Bhushan (1970) |
| **BOYI BHEEMANNA (1911–2005)** | ***Paleru*** (play — the bonded farm-boy who gets educated), ***Gudiselu Kalipotunnayi*** ("the huts are burning"), *Raga Vasishtham* | Dalit self-respect through Gandhian-Ambedkarite lens; Padma Bhushan (2001) |
| **SRI SRI (Srirangam Srinivasa Rao, 1910–83)** | ***MAHAPRASTHANAM*** (poems of the 1930s; published 1950) — "**anadiga saagutunna** … *Mundu ki povu, poru padavoy*"; *Anantam* | **Abhyudaya (progressive) poetry**'s colossus — the future belongs to workers ("**desa charitralu** — what is the substance of all histories? exploitation"); founder-president, **VIRASAM (Revolutionary Writers' Association, 1970)** |

- **2023 Dalit-poets drill:** Jashuva (Gabbilam's inversion of Kalidasa; dignity through mastery) + Bheemanna (Paleru: education as liberation) + the Adi-Andhra print sphere (File 38 §3.3) + later trajectory (Digambara kavulu → 1980s–90s Dalit literary explosion post-Karamchedu 1985) → thesis: "Telugu Dalit writing moved from plea to pride to protest — self-respect and dignity as its constants."
- Also name-droppable: **Basavaraju Apparao** (patriotic lyrics), **Duvvuri Rami Reddy**, **Viswanatha Satyanarayana** (cultural nationalism; first Telugu Jnanpith 1970 — *contrast*, not revolutionary), **Praja Natya Mandali** songs (File 38).

## 4. The Andhra Mahasabhas & the Andhra movement's leaders ⭐⭐

### 4.1 The conferences
- Trigger: Telugu districts' backwardness inside Madras Presidency + the 1911–12 example of Bihar's separation; *Andhra Patrika* 1913 annual number (File 38).
- **FIRST ANDHRA MAHASABHA — BAPATLA, May 1913**, president **B.N. (Bayya Narasimheswara) Sarma**: demanded a **separate Andhra province** ⭐; second conference (Vijayawada, 1914) confirmed the demand; annual sessions thereafter made "Andhra" a political fact.
- Fruits before 1947: **Andhra PCC (1917–18)** → Congress's **linguistic-PCC principle (Nagpur 1920)**; **ANDHRA UNIVERSITY (1926**, first VC **C.R. Reddy)**; **SRIBAGH PACT (1937)** balancing Rayalaseema (File 40); Assembly resolutions for an Andhra province (1937–38).
- **Distinguish** ⭐: the **(Telangana) Andhra Mahasabha — Jogipet 1930, Suravaram Pratapa Reddy** — the parallel movement inside the **Nizam's** dominion (different question; one-line contrast).

### 4.2 Leader gallery (the syllabus's six + the movement's core)
- **ALLURI SITARAMA RAJU** — §1.5 (the movement's martyr-icon).
- **DUGGIRALA GOPALAKRISHNAYYA (1889–1928) — "Andhra Ratna":** Edinburgh MA; Chirala-Perala's Ramanagar (§1.4); minstrel-orator (his "Sri Sakti" bhajan-politics); died young.
- **KONDA VENKATAPPAYYA (1866–1948) — "DESABHAKTA":** founder *Krishna Patrika* (1902); prime organiser of the 1913 Bapatla sabha and the Andhra PCC; Non-cooperation Andhra's general; imprisoned into his seventies.
- **BHOGARAJU PATTABHI SITARAMAYYA (1880–1959):** Machilipatnam doctor-politician; founder, **ANDHRA BANK (1923)** & insurance ventures ⭐; historian — ***The History of the Indian National Congress* (1935)**; lost the 1939 Tripuri presidency to Bose ("**Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat**" — Gandhi); **Congress President (Jaipur, 1948)**; member of the **JVP Committee (1949)** — the "P" ⭐ (File 40); later Governor of Madhya Pradesh.
- **PONAKA KANAKAMMA (1892–1963):** Nellore's Gandhian matriarch — Pinakini ashram, Kasturidevi Vidyalayam, prison-going satyagrahi (File 37 §4).
- **DOKKA SITAMMA (1841–1909):** the annadana saint of the Godavari (File 37 §4) — in this list as the moral exemplar the movement claimed.
- Also: **T. Prakasam ("Andhra Kesari")**, **Bulusu Sambamurti ("Maharshi")**, **N.G. Ranga** (peasants), **Gadicherla** (File 38), **Ayyadevara Kaleswara Rao**, and the grandhalaya trio — the same names run through Files 38–40.

## 5. Prelims nuggets

1. **Rajahmundry students' Vandemataram strike — 1907** (Bipin Chandra Pal's tour; Principal Mark Hunter).
2. **Chirala-Perala 1921 — Duggirala, "Ramanagar", Ramadandu**; **Minchalapadu 1922 — Kanneganti Hanumanthu**; **Pedanandipadu 1922 — Parvataneni Veerayya Chowdary**.
3. **Alluri:** Rampa/Manyam **1922–24**; raids — Chintapalli, Krishnadevipeta, Rajavommangi; Gam Gantam/Mallu Dora; **shot 7 May 1924**; "Manyam Veerudu"; cause-law — **Madras Forest Act 1882**.
4. **Prakasam bares chest — Simon boycott, Madras 1928**; salt camps — **Chinnapuram, Mypadu, Chirala (1930)**.
5. **Justice Party — 1916** (Nair, Theagaraya Chetty, Natesa Mudaliar; Non-Brahmin Manifesto); **first Communal G.O. 1921**; Telugu premiers — **Panagal (1921–26), Bobbili (1932–36), K.V. Reddy Naidu (1936–37)**.
6. **"Makoddee Tella Doratanam" — Garimella (1921)**; **Bhava kavitvam — Rayaprolu**; ***Malapalli* 1922 — Unnava**; **"Kaviraju" — Tripuraneni**; ***Gabbilam* 1941 — Jashuva**; ***Paleru* — Boyi Bheemanna**; ***Mahaprasthanam* — Sri Sri (Virasam 1970)**.
7. **First Andhra Mahasabha — Bapatla 1913 (B.N. Sarma)**; Andhra PCC **1917–18**; **Andhra University 1926 (C.R. Reddy)**.
8. **Andhra Bank 1923 & Congress history 1935 — Pattabhi**; Congress president **Jaipur 1948**.
9. **Telangana's Andhra Mahasabha — Jogipet 1930 (Suravaram)** — different movement.
10. Epithets: **Andhra Ratna = Duggirala; Desabhakta = Konda Venkatappayya; Andhra Kesari = Prakasam; Manyam Veerudu = Alluri; Navayuga Kavi Chakravarti = Jashuva.**

1. **2023 Q9(a) drill (Alluri):** causes (forest law + muttadar oppression) → leadership style (Gandhian idiom + fituri arms) → course (three raids, two-year jungle war) → end & memory → assessment ("a subaltern rising with a nationalist head").
2. **2020 Q9(a) drill (Justice Party):** five circumstances (§2.1) → one-line evaluation ("a movement of elites that democratised the job market — reservation's ancestor").
