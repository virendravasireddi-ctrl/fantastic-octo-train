## 0. Frame

The Andhra movement (File 24) climaxed in **Potti Sreeramulu's fast unto death (Oct–Dec 1952)** → **ANDHRA STATE, 1 October 1953** (India's first linguistic state, capital Kurnool) → merger with Telangana on the **Gentlemen's Agreement** → **ANDHRA PRADESH, 1 November 1956**. Fifty-eight years of uneasy union — **1969 Jai Telangana, 1972 Jai Andhra, Six-Point Formula/Art 371-D, NTR's self-respect wave, TRS from 2001, the 2009 fast, Sri Krishna Committee** — ended with the **AP Reorganisation Act 2014** and the **Appointed Day, 2 JUNE 2014**. PYQs hit this file every cycle (2020 Q9(b) formation of AP; 2025 Q10 Gentlemen's Agreement & 1969; item-10 questions on the Act and its aftermath — see the deep-dive pointers in §5).

## 1. The road to Andhra State (1947–53) ⭐⭐ (2020 Q9(b))

- **Pre-1947 groundwork:** Andhra Mahasabhas from 1913; Andhra PCC 1917–18; **Andhra University 1926**; **SRIBAGH PACT (16 Nov 1937)** ⭐ — coastal leaders conceded to **Rayalaseema**: choice of **capital or High Court**, irrigation priority (Krishna-Tungabhadra-Penna), university facilities — the intra-Andhra federal bargain (venue: Kasinathuni Nageswara Rao's Madras home "Sribagh").
- **Independent India resists:** **DHAR COMMISSION (1948)** — linguistic provinces would harm national unity; **JVP COMMITTEE (1949 — Jawaharlal, Vallabhbhai, PATTABHI)** — deferred the demand, and made Andhra's case conditional on **giving up Madras city** ⭐ (the deal-breaker).
- **Swami Sitaram (Gollapudi Sitarama Sastri)** fasted ~5 weeks for Andhra (Aug–Sept **1951**; ended on Vinoba Bhave's counsel) — the dress rehearsal.
- **POTTI SREERAMULU (1901–52) — "AMARAJEEVI":** Sabarmati-trained Gandhian; famed earlier for **Nellore temple-entry/Harijan fasts (1946)**. Began his **fast for Andhra State (with Madras) on 19 October 1952** at **Bulusu Sambamurti's Madras house**; the Congress governments held aloof; he **died on the 58th day, 15 December 1952**.
- **Aftermath:** three days of spontaneous fury across the Telugu districts (police firings, rail damage) → **Nehru announced Andhra State in Parliament, 19 December 1952** (state to exclude Madras city; **Wanchoo Committee** worked out the modalities; Andhra State Act 1953).
- **ANDHRA STATE, 1 OCTOBER 1953:** 11 Telugu districts of Madras (Circars + Rayalaseema; Bellary district's **Adoni, Alur, Rayadurg** taluks came to Andhra, the rest with Bellary town to Mysore) — **capital KURNOOL, High Court at GUNTUR (the Sribagh logic)**; **first CM T. PRAKASAM**, Dy CM Neelam Sanjiva Reddy, Governor **C.M. Trivedi**; inaugurated by Nehru. *India's first state formed on the language principle — the crowbar that forced the **SRC**.*
- (Prakasam's ministry fell 1954; **Bezawada Gopala Reddy** CM after the 1955 mid-term poll — the famous Congress-vs-Communist election.)

## 2. Emergence of Andhra Pradesh, 1956 ⭐⭐ (2025 Q10(a))

- **Vishalandhra idea:** one state for all Telugus — coastal Andhra + Rayalaseema + the Nizam's **Telangana** (the Communists' "Vishalandhralo Praja Rajyam" was its loudest slogan; Andhra Mahasabha-Telangana stream concurring).
- **SRC (1953–55; Fazl Ali, H.N. Kunzru, K.M. Panikkar):** acknowledged Vishalandhra's logic (Hyderabad city, coal-water complementarity) **but recommended Telangana remain a separate state till 1961**, when its legislature could opt for merger by ⅔ vote — fearing Telangana's fears: its **revenue surplus**, educational backwardness, jobs.
- **Politics overrode caution:** Congress high command favoured immediate merger; Telangana's apprehensions were bought out by the —
- **GENTLEMEN'S AGREEMENT (Delhi, 20 February 1956)** — 8-point safeguard charter signed by four leaders each (Andhra: B. Gopala Reddy, N. Sanjiva Reddy, G. Latchanna, A. Satyanarayana Raju; Telangana: B. Ramakrishna Rao, K.V. Ranga Reddy, M. Chenna Reddy, J.V. Narsing Rao):
  1. **Telangana's revenue surplus** to be spent on Telangana (for five years, extendable);
  2. expansion of **educational facilities** in Telangana (and local preference);
  3. proportionality in **services/recruitment**; **Mulki (domicile) safeguards** (12-year residence rule) for Telangana posts;
  4. a statutory **TELANGANA REGIONAL COUNCIL/Committee** for the region's development;
  5. **cabinet ratio ≈ 60:40** (Andhra:Telangana); **if the CM is from one region, a Dy CM from the other**;
  6. sale of agricultural land in Telangana controlled by the Regional Council; existing prohibition/excise arrangements continued.
- **ANDHRA PRADESH, 1 NOVEMBER 1956** (States Reorganisation Act): Andhra State + nine Telangana districts of Hyderabad State — **first linguistic mega-state**, capital **HYDERABAD**, first CM **NEELAM SANJIVA REDDY** (no Telangana Dy CM appointed — the first breach, say critics ⭐).
- **2025 Q10(a) evaluation:** the Agreement was constitutionally soft law — the Regional Committee (created by Presidential order under Art 371) was advisory; **Mulki rules were evaded** (thousands of non-Mulki appointments), the surplus question festered, the Dy-CM convention lapsed → "the safeguards satisfied the letter for a while, the apprehensions never."

## 3. Andhra Pradesh, 1956–2014 ⭐⭐

### 3.1 Strains and movements
- **1969 — JAI TELANGANA (2025 Q10(b)):** trigger — expiry of safeguards + non-Mulki appointments + the surplus dispute; began with **student agitation (Khammam, Jan 1969)** and swept Osmania — students the movement's spearhead (strikes, "Telangana Praja Samithi" politics); **Marri Chenna Reddy**'s **TPS**; ~**370 killed** in firings; Centre's **Eight-Point & Five-Point plans** failed; **1971**: TPS won 10/14 Telangana Lok Sabha seats, then **merged with Congress** — **P.V. Narasimha Rao** made the first Telangana CM (1971).
- **1972 — JAI ANDHRA:** the Supreme Court's **Mulki-rules judgment (1972)** upholding domicile protection in Hyderabad enraged coastal-Rayalaseema opinion → counter-movement for separation (or full integration); President's rule (Jan 1973).
- **Settlement:** **SIX-POINT FORMULA (Sept 1973)** — Mulki rules & Regional Committee abolished; instead **zonal system** for jobs, backward-area development boards, a **Central University at Hyderabad (1974)** — constitutionalised by the **32nd Amendment → ARTICLE 371-D (1974)** ⭐ (Presidential orders on local cadres; still central to both successor states); **G.O. 610 (1985)** later tried to repatriate zonal-violation appointees — its non-implementation refuelled grievance.
- **1983 — NTR & Telugu self-respect:** **N.T. RAMA RAO** founded **TDP (March 1982)**, swept Jan 1983 on **"Telugu atma-gauravam"** — the first non-Congress government; the Nadendla coup and restoration (Aug–Sept 1984) became a federalism cause célèbre; two-party era began (Congress ↔ TDP).
- **1990s–2000s:** liberalisation-era Hyderabad boom (Cyberabad under N. Chandrababu Naidu, 1995–2004); **Y.S. Rajasekhara Reddy (2004–09)**; welfare-irrigation populism (Jalayagnam).

### 3.2 The road to bifurcation (2001–14)
- **TRS founded 27 April 2001** by **K. CHANDRASHEKAR RAO (KCR)** on the plank of a separate Telangana; 2004 poll pact with Congress (separate-Telangana in the common minimum programme).
- **Sept 2009:** YSR's death in a helicopter crash destabilised the state.
- **29 Nov 2009: KCR's fast-unto-death** → **9 DECEMBER 2009 — Home Minister Chidambaram: "the process of forming Telangana will be initiated"** → Seemandhra's **Samaikyandhra** (united-AP) counter-eruption (mass resignations) → **23 December 2009** — decision put on hold ⭐ (the whiplash that made bifurcation only a matter of time).
- **SRI KRISHNA COMMITTEE (Feb 2010; report 30 Dec 2010):** examined **six options**; called keeping the state united **with constitutional/statutory empowerment of Telangana** the "most workable", with bifurcation (Hyderabad as Telangana's capital) the second-best; satisfied no one.
- **2011–13:** sustained Telangana agitation — million marches, **Sakala Janula Samme** (the 42-day general strike, autumn 2011); **30 July 2013** — Congress Working Committee resolved to create Telangana; Seemandhra's months-long Samaikyandhra strikes (employees, power staff) could not reverse it.
- **AP REORGANISATION ACT 2014:** Lok Sabha **18 Feb**, Rajya Sabha **20 Feb** (amid unprecedented scenes; the PM's floor package for Seemandhra incl. a **Special-Category-Status assurance for five years**), assent **1 March 2014**.
- **APPOINTED DAY — 2 JUNE 2014:** **Telangana** (10 districts) India's 29th state (CM **KCR**); **residuary ANDHRA PRADESH** (13 districts; CM **N. Chandrababu Naidu** from 8 June); **Hyderabad common capital for up to 10 years**; Governor common initially. (**Kiran Kumar Reddy** — last CM of united AP, resigned in protest.)

### 3.3 Causes of bifurcation (analysis grid for Mains ⭐)
1. **Broken safeguards (1956→):** Gentlemen's Agreement → Mulki evasion → G.O. 610 non-implementation — a 58-year credibility deficit.
2. **"Internal colonialism" narrative:** perceived Andhra capture of Hyderabad's land/jobs/contracts, of **Krishna-Godavari waters** (projects and allocations), and of education/employment.
3. **Cultural assertion:** Telangana dialect and festivals (Bathukamma) mocked/marginalised — identity became ideology.
4. **Political entrepreneurship & conjuncture:** TRS's single-issue persistence, coalition-era leverage (2004, 2009), YSR's death, the 9 Dec 2009 announcement-retraction.
5. **Movement mechanics:** students (Osmania), employees' strikes, poets-singers (Telangana Dhoom-Dham) — a society-wide mobilisation the state could not outlast.

## 4. Impact of 2 June 2014 (the residuary state's balance-sheet ⭐⭐)

- **Fiscal shock:** AP retained ~**58%** of the population but lost **Hyderabad**, the united state's revenue engine — a structural **revenue deficit** (≈ ₹16,000 crore in 2014–15) from day one; per-capita income gap vs Telangana.
- **Capital-less state:** interim rule from Hyderabad; **AMARAVATI** chosen (foundation **22 Oct 2015**; ~33,000 acres by land pooling) — against the **Sivaramakrishnan Committee's** advice on decentralisation; the later **three-capitals proposal (2020: Visakhapatnam executive, Amaravati legislative, Kurnool judicial)** and its litigation left capital policy unsettled — Kurnool-capital memories of 1953 echo in the debate.
- **The Act's machinery and disputes:**
  - **Assets & institutions:** Schedule IX (state corporations) & Schedule X (training institutions etc.) division — still partly unresolved (2023 PYQ on "inconsistencies"); cash balances & debt split on population ratio (**58.32 : 41.68**).
  - **Employees:** division under the **Kamalanathan Committee** — protracted "native issues" (options, local cadres, Art 371-D continuing in both states).
  - **Rivers:** **Apex Council + KRISHNA & GODAVARI RIVER MANAGEMENT BOARDS** (Sections 84–89; **Schedule XI** principles; jurisdiction gazette 2021) — Krishna sharing remains the flashpoint; **POLAVARAM declared a NATIONAL PROJECT (Sec 90)** ⭐ (with the merger of seven Godavari-valley mandals from Telangana into AP, 2014).
  - **New institutions (Schedule XIII):** IIT **Tirupati**, IIM **Visakhapatnam**, NIT **Tadepalligudem**, IISER Tirupati, **Central University of AP (Anantapur)**, IIPE Visakhapatnam, **AIIMS Mangalagiri**, agricultural university etc.
  - **Special Category Status:** assured on the floor, not in the Act; 14th-Finance-Commission logic used to decline it → the **2016 "special assistance package"**; SCS remains AP's standing political demand (TDP's 2018 NDA exit).
- **Socio-cultural:** families, businesses, film industry and job markets straddling two states; Telugu now the official language of two states — "one language, two states" as the new normal; for Telangana, statehood delivered symbolic justice (Bathukamma as state festival, Telangana Formation Day 2 June).
