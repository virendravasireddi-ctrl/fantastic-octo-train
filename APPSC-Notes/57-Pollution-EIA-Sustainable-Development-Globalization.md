## 1. Environmental pollution ⭐⭐ (2020 PYQ (a): factors responsible)

### 1.1 Types & key pollutants

| Type | Key pollutants / sources |
|---|---|
| **Air** | **PM2.5 & PM10, SO₂, NOₓ, CO, ground-level O₃, lead**; vehicles, industry, thermal power, stubble/biomass burning, construction dust |
| **Water** | Sewage, industrial effluent, agro-runoff (**eutrophication**), fluoride/arsenic, faecal coliform |
| **Soil** | Agrochemicals, industrial waste, plastics, salinity |
| **Noise** | Traffic, industry, construction, festivals |
| **Others** | **Thermal, radioactive, marine (oil/plastic), e-waste, light** pollution |

### 1.2 Factors responsible (the 2020 answer — organise by driver) ⭐

**Population growth & urbanisation** (waste, sewage, vehicles) → **industrialisation & fossil-fuel energy** (SOₓ/NOₓ, effluent, fly ash) → **transport explosion** (tailpipe PM/NOₓ) → **agricultural intensification** (fertiliser-pesticide runoff, stubble burning — the Green-Revolution cost, File 39) → **deforestation & land degradation** → **mining & construction dust** (Files 19, 41) → **consumerism & plastic/e-waste** → **weak enforcement & externalities** (polluter pays not internalised). Effects: disease (respiratory, waterborne), **acid rain, eutrophication, bioaccumulation, ozone/GHG & climate change, biodiversity loss**.

### 1.3 India's pollution-control framework ⭐

- **Water Act 1974** (created **CPCB & SPCBs**), **Air Act 1981**, **Environment (Protection) Act 1986** (umbrella, post-Bhopal), **National Green Tribunal 2010**, **NAAQS (2009, 12 pollutants) & AQI**, **NCAP 2019** (PM cuts for non-attainment cities), **Namami Gange, Plastic Waste & E-Waste Rules, Ujjwala/BS-VI/FAME** as source-side fixes.
- The **"polluter pays," "precautionary" and "public trust" principles** (Supreme Court-embedded) ⭐.

#### The standards you should be able to quote ⭐⭐

| Instrument | The numbers |
|---|---|
| **National AQI (2014)** | Built from **8 pollutants** (PM10, PM2.5, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃, Pb); six bands — **Good 0–50, Satisfactory 51–100, Moderate 101–200, Poor 201–300, Very Poor 301–400, Severe 401–500** ⭐ |
| **NAAQS (2009)** | **12 pollutants**; the headline annual limits — **PM10 60 µg/m³, PM2.5 40 µg/m³** (against WHO's far stricter guideline — a good comparison line) |
| **CPCB water-use classes** | **A** drinking after disinfection only · **B** outdoor bathing · **C** drinking after conventional treatment · **D** wildlife & fisheries · **E** irrigation/industrial cooling ⭐; key parameters **DO (higher is better), BOD (lower is better), faecal coliform** |
| **Ambient noise (dB(A), day/night)** | **Industrial 75/70 · Commercial 65/55 · Residential 55/45 · Silence zone 50/40**; "day" = 6 a.m.–10 p.m. ⭐ |

### 1.4 Pollution in Andhra Pradesh ⭐

- **Visakhapatnam** — a **CEPI "critically polluted" industrial cluster** (steel-refinery-fertiliser-pharma); **the LG Polymers styrene-gas leak, 7 May 2020 (RR Venkatapuram, ~12 deaths, ~1,000 hospitalised; NGT levied ₹50 cr)** ⭐⭐ — AP's signature industrial-disaster + pollution case.
- **Pharma effluent** — **Jawaharlal Nehru Pharma City (Parawada), Pydibhimavaram (Srikakulam)** clusters; **aquaculture & Kolleru pollution** (feed, antibiotics, encroachment, File 40); thermal-plant ash; coastal & plastic litter.

## 2. Andhra Pradesh Pollution Control Board (APPCB) ⭐⭐ (2025 PYQ (b))

- **Statutory SPCB** under the Water Act 1974 & Air Act 1981; HQ Vijayawada; functions — **Consent to Establish/Operate (CFE/CFO), effluent & emission standards, monitoring (air/water/noise), CEPI industrial-cluster oversight, hazardous-waste authorisation, public complaints, and enforcement (closure directions)**.
- **Post-disaster pollution management (the 2025 answer) ⭐:** after cyclones/floods/industrial accidents the APPCB manages **secondary pollution** — sewage & carcass contamination of flood-water, **debris & hazardous-waste clearance, oil/chemical spills, drinking-water quality testing, air monitoring near damaged plants**, and **industrial-accident response** (the LG Polymers template — containment, health advisories, remediation orders, restart audits). Evaluate: **capacity & manpower gaps, real-time monitoring (CAAQMS) expansion, coordination with APSDMA/NDRF, and prevention (siting, safety audits, on-site emergency plans) over cure.**

## 3. Environmental Impact Assessment ⭐⭐ (2023 PYQ (a): evaluate & suggest)

- **Definition:** a **process to predict and evaluate the environmental consequences of a project before it is cleared**, and to build in mitigation — a *preventive* planning tool.
- **Legal basis:** **EIA Notification 1994 → 2006** under the EPA 1986; **Categories A (appraised centrally by MoEFCC/EAC) and B (by state SEIAA/SEAC; B1 needs EIA, B2 does not)** ⭐. Category is decided by **project type and threshold size**, and any project within a specified distance of a **protected area, critically polluted area or eco-sensitive zone** is pushed up to Category A (the "General Condition") ⭐.
- **Typical exam anchors:** an **EIA report contains a baseline study, impact prediction, alternatives analysis, an Environment Management Plan (EMP), a risk/disaster-management plan and a monitoring programme**; the **public hearing** is conducted by the SPCB (**APPCB** in AP) in the affected district; **environmental clearance carries validity periods and conditions**, and compliance reports are due **half-yearly**; violations attract action under the **EPA 1986** and appeal lies to the **NGT within 30 days** ⭐.
- **The related instruments to name:** **Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) Notification 2011/2019** — the one that actually governs AP's 974 km shoreline (ports, aqua farms, tourism); **Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 clearance** and **FRA 2006 gram-sabha consent** for forest land; **Wildlife clearance/NBWL** near protected areas; **Social Impact Assessment under the LARR Act 2013** for land acquisition ⭐ — a project usually needs several of these, not just "EIA".
- **Process (name the steps):** **screening → scoping (ToR) → baseline data → impact prediction → Environment Management Plan → public hearing/consultation → appraisal → clearance (with conditions) → monitoring & compliance** ⭐.
- **Criticism (the "evaluate" half):** weak or incomplete baseline data & consultant bias, **post-facto/ex-post clearances, dilution & exemptions, tokenistic public hearings, poor post-clearance monitoring, cumulative impacts ignored**; the **Draft EIA 2020** drew strong criticism for legitimising post-facto approval and shrinking public consultation ⭐.
- **Suggestions (the "make it effective" half):** independent professional EIA authority & accredited consultants, **robust & independent baseline data, genuine early public participation (local-language, adequate notice), cumulative & strategic (regional) EIA, carrying-capacity & climate-risk integration, transparent web-disclosure, and strict penal post-clearance monitoring** ⭐. Add **Social Impact Assessment** (LARR Act 2013) as a complement.

## 4. Sustainable Development ⭐⭐ (2020 PYQ (b): concept & importance)

- **Definition (quote it):** *"development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs"* — **Brundtland Commission, "Our Common Future," 1987** ⭐⭐.
- **Three pillars:** **economic viability, social equity, environmental protection** (people-profit-planet); principles — **intergenerational equity, precautionary principle, polluter pays, common but differentiated responsibilities (CBDR)**.
- **The global milestones (timeline):** **Stockholm 1972 (UNEP born)** → **Brundtland 1987** → **Rio Earth Summit 1992 (Agenda 21, UNFCCC, CBD, UNCCD)** → **Rio+20 2012 ("The Future We Want")** → **SDGs 2015 — 17 goals, 169 targets, "2030 Agenda"** ⭐⭐; climate track → Kyoto 1997 → **Paris 2015 (1.5–2 °C)** → **Kunming-Montreal biodiversity 2022**.
- **Why it matters (the "importance"):** reconciles growth with ecological limits; secures **water-food-energy security, climate resilience, public health and equity**; a development model for a resource-constrained, climate-exposed India.
- **India's commitments:** **SDG India Index (NITI Aayog)**, **Panchamrit & net-zero by 2070 (COP26, 2021)**, **Mission LiFE, International Solar Alliance, National Action Plan on Climate Change (8 missions)** ⭐. **AP:** solar leadership (Kurnool park), APCNF natural farming, mangrove restoration, SDG-cell monitoring — a state-level sustainability portfolio (Files 39, 42).

## 5. Globalization & the environment ⭐

- **Concept:** growing integration of economies, cultures and information — India's turn came with **LPG reforms, 1991** ⭐.
- **Environmental double-edge:** **positive** — clean-tech & knowledge transfer, global finance (green bonds, ISA), shared standards, environmental awareness; **negative** — **"pollution-haven" relocation of dirty industry, resource extraction for export, longer carbon-heavy supply chains, biodiversity/biopiracy pressure, and exposure to global shocks** ⭐.
- **AP illustration:** AP is plugged into global value chains — **shrimp & seafood exports, pharma, Sri City MNCs, IT** (Files 40, 42) — which brings jobs and technology but also **export-market vulnerability (US shrimp/pharma tariff shocks, 2025)** and effluent/energy footprints; sustainability is the hedge that keeps globalization's gains net-positive.
- **The two conceptual props to name:** the **Environmental Kuznets Curve** (pollution rises then falls with income — true for local pollutants like SO₂, **false so far for CO₂**, so don't "grow first, clean later") and the **ecological footprint / carbon-leakage** idea — rich countries' consumption is met by poor countries' emissions, which is precisely India's **CBDR** argument at climate talks ⭐. Global responses now reach back through trade: the EU's **carbon border adjustment (CBAM)** puts a climate price on exports, so AP's steel, aluminium and chemical exporters have a *commercial* reason to decarbonise.
