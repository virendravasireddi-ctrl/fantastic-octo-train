# 55 · Environmental Geography II — Natural Hazards I: Earthquakes, Landslides, Floods & Cloudbursts (India and AP)

⭐ The hazards syllabus names six: **earthquakes, landslides, floods, cyclones, cloudbursts** (+ drought). This file takes the four **geophysical/hydrological** ones (cyclones & droughts, the AP-core pair, are in File 56). For each: **mechanism → India distribution → AP position → mitigation**. **Hazard vs disaster:** a hazard becomes a disaster only when it meets **vulnerability** (Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability ÷ Capacity) ⭐.

---

## 1. Earthquakes ⭐⭐

- **Cause:** sudden release of strain along **faults/plate boundaries** (India–Eurasia collision powers Himalayan seismicity); also volcanic & **reservoir-induced** (Koyna 1967).
- **Terms:** **focus/hypocentre** (origin) vs **epicentre** (surface point above); **seismic waves — P (primary, fastest, longitudinal), S (secondary, transverse, no liquids), L (surface, most damaging)** ⭐; **magnitude** (energy — Richter/**Moment Magnitude**) vs **intensity** (felt effects — **Mercalli** scale); seismograph.
- **BIS seismic zoning — Zones II–V** (V highest): **Zone V** — NE India, Kashmir, Kutch, N-Bihar; **Zone IV** — Delhi, Himalayan foothills; **III** — much of the Deccan & Gangetic belt; **II** — stable-craton peninsular interiors ⭐.
- **Landmark Indian quakes:** **Bhuj 2001 (M7.7, Zone V, ~20,000 dead)**, **Latur 1993 (M6.4, Maharashtra — a "stable continental region" shock, surprised science)** ⭐, Uttarkashi 1991, Kangra 1905, Assam 1950 (great), Nepal-Gorkha 2015.
- **AP position:** mostly **Zone II (low) with Zone III patches** — the **Ongole–Bapatla coast and Bhadrachalam belt are Zone III** ⭐; AP sits on the **stable Dharwar craton** (File 19), so damaging quakes are rare, but **reservoir-induced seismicity** near large dams (Srisailam/Nagarjuna Sagar) and occasional Ongole tremors are watch-points. **Low hazard, but low preparedness too** — the honest Mains line.
- **Scale of exposure (quote one figure):** **~59% of India's landmass is prone to moderate-to-severe seismic hazard** (NDMA) ⭐; **Zone V is the smallest by area, Zone II the largest** — so most Indians live in low-to-moderate zones where **building quality, not zone rank, decides the death toll** ("earthquakes don't kill people, buildings do").
- **Prediction & mitigation:** quakes are **not predictable** — the whole strategy is structural. **National Centre for Seismology (NCS, MoES)** monitors and maps; **BIS codes IS 1893 (design), IS 4326/13920 (ductile detailing), IS 13935 (retrofitting)** ⭐; enforce codes through **municipal by-laws and the National Building Code**, retrofit lifeline buildings (hospitals, schools), train masons, and run **Aapda Mitra** community volunteers. Add **microzonation** of major cities as the technical ask.
- **The related coastal hazard:** an undersea quake can generate a **tsunami** — AP's Nellore–Krishna–Godavari coast was hit in **2004**, and warnings now come within minutes from the **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre at INCOIS, Hyderabad (2007)** ⭐ *(detail in the Paper-V Environment series, File 07 — cite it, don't repeat it)*.

## 2. Landslides ⭐

- **Causes:** steep slopes + heavy/prolonged rain + **deforestation, road-cutting, mining, construction, seismicity**. Separate them in an answer into **preparatory factors** (slope, weak/weathered rock, jointing, soil depth) and **triggers** (intense rain, quake, toe-cutting for a road, reservoir drawdown) ⭐.
- **Types (Varnes' classification — name it):** **falls, topples, slides (rotational *slumps* / translational), lateral spreads and flows (debris flow, mudflow, creep)**; a **debris flow after a cloudburst** is the deadliest combination in India ⭐.
- **Scale:** GSI puts roughly **12–13% of India's land area (excluding snow-covered terrain) in the landslide-prone class** — verify the current figure from the **National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping (NLSM)** programme.
- **India hotspots:** **Himalaya (Uttarakhand, HP, J&K, NE) and the Western Ghats (Kerala — Wayanad 2024, Idukki)** ⭐; **GSI** does landslide-hazard zonation; monsoon is the trigger season.
- **AP position:** limited but real in the **Eastern Ghats agency** — **ghat-road slips on the Araku–Paderu–Chintapalli and Tirumala ghat sections**, cut-slope failures during intense rain; **podu cultivation & road-widening raise the risk** in ASR district ⭐. Mitigation: bioengineering, retaining walls, drainage, slope zonation, early warning.

## 3. Floods ⭐⭐

- **Causes:** intense/prolonged rain, **inadequate drainage & siltation, embankment/dam failure, deforestation & catchment degradation, encroachment of floodplains, urbanisation (concretised catchments), storm surge & cyclones, poor river-linking of drains**.
- **India's flood geography:** **~12% of India (≈4 crore hectares) is flood-prone**; the **Ganga–Brahmaputra–Barak belt (Bihar, Assam, UP, WB)** is the worst; **CWC** is the flood-forecasting agency; **Rashtriya Barh Ayog (National Flood Commission, 1980)** is the reference report; the **Flood Management & Border Areas Programme (FMBAP)** funds state works.
- **Types:** riverine, flash, urban, coastal (surge), glacial-lake outburst (GLOF).
- **The paradox to state ⭐:** India's flood *losses* keep rising although its flood *defences* keep growing — because **embankments transfer risk downstream, false security invites floodplain settlement, and drainage congestion + siltation reduce carrying capacity**. This is why NDMA's guidelines shifted the emphasis to **non-structural** measures: **floodplain zoning (a model Bill has existed since 1975 and is still barely adopted by states)** ⭐, flood-risk mapping, insurance and forecast-based early action.
- **Andhra Pradesh ⭐⭐ (recurrent, delta-centric):**
  - **Godavari & Krishna floods** — the deltas flood when upstream catchments and both rivers peak together; **Dowleswaram & Prakasam barrages** regulate but the delta's flat gradient spreads water.
  - **2009 Krishna–Tungabhadra floods** — **Kurnool town submerged, Mantralayam inundated, Srisailam–Nagarjuna Sagar near-overflow** — one of AP's worst ⭐⭐.
  - **Urban floods:** **Vijayawada's Budameru "diversion" overflow (2024)** ⭐, Tirupati–Nellore (Michaung 2023), Hyderabad historically.
  - Compound risk: **cyclone + delta flood** (Diviseema 1977, Michaung 2023 — File 56).
  - Mitigation: barrages & flood banks, **Polavaram flood cushion**, drain remodelling (Budameru), floodplain zoning, real-time forecasting (CWC + APSDMA), interlinking/diversion, mangrove & wetland buffers.

## 4. Cloudbursts ⭐

- **Definition:** **>100 mm of rain in an hour over a small area (~20–30 km²)** — sudden, extreme convective/orographic downpour causing flash floods & landslides ⭐.
- **India:** a **Himalayan speciality** — **Leh 2010, Kedarnath 2013, Amarnath 2022, Himachal 2023**; monsoon moisture forced up steep slopes; hard to forecast (very local, short-lived).
- **AP position:** classic cloudbursts are **rare** (no high mountains), but **intense short-duration convective bursts** cause urban/flash flooding in the Ghats and cities; climate change is raising extreme-rainfall events statewide ⭐. Response: nowcasting (radar), Doppler weather radars (Visakhapatnam, Machilipatnam), urban storm-drainage.

## 5. Common mitigation ladder (reuse for any hazard answer)

**Prevention/mitigation** (zoning, land-use control, engineering — dams, embankments, retrofitting, afforestation) → **preparedness** (early warning, forecasting, drills, shelters, mapping) → **response** (evacuation, NDRF/SDRF, relief) → **recovery** (rehabilitation, build-back-better). Structural (physical works) vs **non-structural** (laws, insurance, awareness, ecosystem-based) measures — a balanced answer names both. Detailed **DM framework (DM Act 2005, NDMA)** → File 56 §3.

**Who warns for what (one-line agency map ⭐⭐ — worth a mark in every hazard answer):** **IMD** — cyclones, heavy rain, heat waves, lightning; **CWC** — river floods; **GSI** — landslide zonation; **NCS (MoES)** — earthquakes; **INCOIS, Hyderabad** — tsunami & storm surge; **NRSC/Bhuvan** — satellite damage mapping; **APSDMA** — state-level dissemination.

## 6. Prelims match-pairs & Mains skeletons

**Match-pairs:** **Focus → origin; epicentre → surface point above** · **P-waves fastest / S-waves cannot pass liquids / L-waves most destructive** · **Richter → magnitude; Mercalli → intensity** · **Zone V → NE India, Kutch, Kashmir, N-Bihar; AP → Zone II with Zone III patches** · **Koyna 1967 → reservoir-induced seismicity** · **Latur 1993 → stable continental region quake** · **Bhuj 2001 → Zone V, Kutch** · **Varnes → landslide classification** · **GSI → landslide zonation; NCS → seismology; CWC → flood forecasting; INCOIS → tsunami** · **Rashtriya Barh Ayog → 1980** · **Cloudburst → >100 mm in an hour over ~20–30 km²** · **Kurnool 2009 → Krishna-Tungabhadra flood** · **Budameru → Vijayawada urban flooding**.

**Mains skeletons**
1. **"A hazard becomes a disaster only where vulnerability meets it." Examine with Indian examples.** *(Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability ÷ Capacity → contrast Latur 1993 (low hazard zone, high vulnerability, huge toll) with AP's cyclone record (high hazard, rising capacity, falling toll) → conclusion: invest in capacity, not only in forecasting.)*
2. **Discuss the causes of recurrent floods in the Krishna–Godavari deltas and suggest a management strategy.** *(Physical: flat gradient, twin-river peaking, siltation, surge coincidence → anthropogenic: drain encroachment, urbanisation, embankment lock-in → strategy: catchment treatment + barrage/reservoir operation rules + Budameru-type drain remodelling + floodplain zoning + mangrove/wetland buffers + CWC-APSDMA forecast-based evacuation → close: manage the floodplain, not just the flood.)*
3. **Andhra Pradesh is seismically quiet but not seismically safe. Comment.** *(Cratonic stability & Zone II/III mapping → but: Zone III coast holds the densest population and critical industry (Vizag, Ongole–Bapatla), reservoir-induced seismicity near Srisailam/Nagarjuna Sagar, near-zero code enforcement in small towns → ask: microzonation of Vizag-Vijayawada, retrofitting lifeline buildings, mason training.)*
