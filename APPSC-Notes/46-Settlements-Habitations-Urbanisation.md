## 1. Rural settlements ⭐⭐ (2023 PYQ (b) — full kit)

### 1.1 The four types (NCERT frame — name, logic, region)

| Type | Form | Why it forms | Where in India | AP example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clustered (agglomerated/nucleated)** | Compact block, clear street plan | Fertile alluvium, assured water, security tradition, caste-ward structure | **Northern plains, NE valleys, Bundelkhand (defence)** | **Delta villages of the Godavari–Krishna canal country** ⭐ |
| **Semi-clustered (fragmented)** | One dominant core + detached wards | Social segregation — dominant groups at the core, service/SC hamlets at the edge ⭐ (be analytical, not casual) | Gujarat plain, parts of Rajasthan | Delta villages with satellite **-palem/-gudem** hamlets |
| **Hamleted** | Village split into several named units (panna, para, palli, **nagla**, dhani) | Large villages fragmenting socially/ethnically | **Middle & lower Ganga plain, Chhattisgarh, lower Himalaya** | Agency villages of several **gudems**; Rayalaseema **palle** clusters |
| **Dispersed (isolated)** | Lone homesteads/farmsteads | Broken terrain, forest plots, small field patches | **Meghalaya, Himachal, Kerala coast** | **Podu-linked hill hamlets of the Eastern Ghats agency** ⭐ |

### 1.2 Factors affecting rural settlements (the "factors" half of the PYQ)

**Physical:** water supply — most villages are **"wet-point" settlements** (tank, well, river) ⭐; relief & drainage (dry points on levees/bhangar above flood line); building material. **Cultural-historical:** defence (fort villages), caste and social structure (ward segregation), land-tenure history, planned colonisation (canal colonies, R&R colonies). **Economic:** land quality, roads/markets.

### 1.3 Patterns (shapes — one line each)

**Linear** (along road, canal, levee, coast — AP's canal-bank and coastal-road villages ⭐), **rectangular/checkerboard** (planned, level plains), **circular** (around a tank/pond — **Rayalaseema tank-foot (cheruvu) villages** ⭐), **radial/star** (routes converging), **T/Y/cross-shaped** (junctions), double village (river crossing).

**AP flourish for Mains:** Telugu village-name suffixes encode settlement history — **-palli/-palle, -palem, -gudem, -peta, -puram, -varam** ⭐ (hamlet, coastal/military hamlet, tribal hamlet, market ward, planned foundation, brahmadeya/agrahara layer).

## 2. Urban settlements — definitions and classes (Prelims bank)

- **Census definitions ⭐⭐:** a **statutory town** = any place with a municipality/corporation/cantonment/notified area; a **census town** = population ≥5,000 + **≥75% male main workers non-agricultural** + density ≥400/km². 2011: **7,935 towns = 4,041 statutory + 3,894 census** — the **census-town boom** was 2001–11's big story ⭐.
- **Urban Agglomeration (UA)** = city + outgrowths/contiguous towns; **size classes I–VI** (Class I ≥ 1 lakh); **million-plus cities: 53 (2011)**; **megacities (≥10 m): Greater Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata**.
- **Functional types with AP examples ⭐:** administrative (**Amaravati**), port-industrial (**Visakhapatnam**), commercial-transport (**Vijayawada**), agro-commercial (**Guntur**), **religious-educational (Tirupati)**, scientific (**Sriharikota**), historic port (Machilipatnam).

## 3. Urbanisation in India

- **Level: 31.2% (2011; ~37.7 cr)** — modest by world standards but **huge in absolute terms**; south & west more urbanised (TN ~48%, Kerala, Maharashtra, Gujarat) vs Bihar ~11% ⭐; growth drivers = natural increase + migration + **reclassification** (census towns) + boundary expansion.
- **Character & problems:** top-heavy (Class-I cities hold ~70% of urban India), **slums (~6.5 cr, 2011)**, informal work, peri-urban sprawl beyond municipal capacity, water-flood-air stress.
- **Policy line-up:** JNNURM → **Smart Cities Mission, AMRUT (water/sewerage), PMAY-Urban, Swachh Bharat-U, NULM**, metro-rail expansion, GatiShakti urban logistics — one line each is enough.

## 4. Andhra Pradesh — rural-urban structure and spatial urbanisation ⭐⭐ (2020a & 2025a PYQs)

### 4.1 The 2020 answer (rural & urban population, Census 2011, residual AP)

- **Rural ≈ 70.5% (≈3.48 cr) : Urban ≈ 29.5% (≈1.46 cr)** — AP is **slightly less urbanised than India (31.2%)** but far more rural in employment terms (Files 39, 44).
- **Two million-plus cities (2011): Visakhapatnam (GVMC ~1.7–2.0 m) and Vijayawada (~1.0–1.5 m UA)** ⭐; next rung — Guntur, Nellore, Kurnool, Rajahmundry, Kakinada, Tirupati, Anantapur, Eluru, Ongole.
- **District contrast (2011):** **Visakhapatnam the most urbanised (~47%)** vs **Srikakulam the least (~16%)** ⭐; Krishna, Guntur, Chittoor above average; Rayalaseema mostly below (Kurnool an urban island).

### 4.2 The 2025 answer (spatial pattern of urbanisation)

1. **A coastal urban corridor, not a primate city:** AP's towns bead along **NH-16/Howrah–Chennai rail** — Srikakulam→Vizag→Rajahmundry→Eluru→Vijayawada→Guntur→Ongole→Nellore ⭐⭐ (the VCIC of File 42 formalises this).
2. **Delta "census-town" urbanisation:** dense, prosperous villages crossing the non-farm threshold — urbanisation by **reclassification** more than migration.
3. **Interior nodes:** temple-education economy (**Tirupati**), district markets (Kurnool, Anantapur, Kadapa), with thin urban fabric between.
4. **The missing metropolis ⭐⭐:** bifurcation left AP **without a megacity** (Hyderabad gone) — low urban primacy is balanced but denies agglomeration economies; responses: Amaravati capital build-out, Vizag's metro ambitions, corridor industrialisation (Part-B overlap — cite 2020/2025 Part-B PYQs).
5. **Stress notes:** **Visakhapatnam's slum share is among the highest of India's big cities (~40%+, 2011)** ⭐; coastal cities face cyclone-surge risk (Hudhud 2014, File 18).

**Close for Mains:** AP's urban geography = "a necklace, not a crown" — policy must thicken the corridor (logistics, housing, resilience) while seeding one true metropolitan anchor.
