## 0. The big picture (memorise this frame first)

| Dynasty | Core period | Capital(s) | Emblem | Heartland |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pallavas** | c. 575–897 (imperial line) | Kanchipuram | Bull (Nandi); lion on coins/seals | Tondaimandalam (N. Tamil country) |
| **Badami (Western) Chalukyas** | 543–753 | Vatapi (Badami) | Varaha (boar) | Upper Deccan (Malaprabha valley) |
| **Eastern Chalukyas** | 624–1075 | Pishtapuram → Vengi → Rajahmundry | Varaha (boar) | Krishna–Godavari deltas (Vengi) |
| **Rashtrakutas** | 753–973 | Manyakheta (Malkhed) | Garuda | Deccan (Maharashtra–Karnataka) |
| **Kalyani (Western) Chalukyas** | 973–c. 1189 | Manyakheta → Kalyani (Basavakalyan) | Varaha (boar) | Upper Deccan |
| **Cholas (imperial)** | 848–1279 | Thanjavur → Gangaikondacholapuram | Tiger | Kaveri delta (Cholamandalam) |

**Three long processes run through all six dynasties** (use as Mains openers):

1. **Temple-and-agrahara order** — land grants (brahmadeya, devadana) turned forest/delta into taxable wet-rice land and made the **temple the nucleus** of economy, education, art and social life.
2. **Rise of the vernaculars** — Sanskrit remained prestige language, but **Tamil (Pallava–Chola), Kannada (Rashtrakuta–Kalyani) and Telugu (Eastern Chalukya)** became languages of inscription and then of high poetry.
3. **Architectural experimentation → classicism** — rock-cut (Mahendra caves, Ellora) → monolithic (Mamallapuram rathas, Kailasa) → structural (Kanchi, Pattadakal) → climax (Thanjavur); the Deccan's mixing of Nagara + Dravida produced the **Vesara** idiom.

## 1. THE PALLAVAS (imperial line c. 575–897)

### 1.1 Quick political frame

- **Simhavishnu** (c. 575–600) founded the imperial line; crushed the Kalabhras; patron of poet **Bharavi**.
- **Mahendravarman I** (600–630): versatile genius — titles **Vichitrachitta** ("curious-minded"), **Mattavilasa**, **Chitrakarapuli**; began the Pallava–Chalukya wars (lost Vengi region to Pulakesin II); converted from Jainism to **Shaivism by Appar (Tirunavukkarasar)**.
- **Narasimhavarman I "Mamalla"** (630–668): defeated and killed **Pulakesin II**, sacked Badami — title **Vatapikonda** (his general **Paranjothi** later became the Nayanar saint **Siruttondar**); naval help to Sri Lankan prince Manavarma; **Hiuen Tsang visited Kanchi c. 640**.
- **Narasimhavarman II "Rajasimha"** (c. 695–722): peaceful reign; **Shore Temple & Kailasanatha temple**; sent **embassies to China**; patron of **Dandin**.
- **Nandivarman II Pallavamalla** (731–796): **Vaikuntha Perumal temple**; Vaishnava saint **Tirumangai Alvar** his contemporary.
- End: **Aparajita** defeated by **Aditya I Chola (c. 897)**.

### 1.2 Society, religion, education

- **Kanchipuram = "Ghatikasthanam"** — the great **ghatika** (Brahmanical college) of the south; Hiuen Tsang counted ~100 Buddhist monasteries and 10,000 monks there and noted Kanchi as the birthplace of Nalanda's **Dharmapala**. Kanchi is one of the seven **mokshapuris**.
- Pallavas institutionalised **brahmadeya/agrahara** grants in Tondaimandalam → Sanskritisation of the far south; caste order consolidated.
- **Bhakti movement's first wave grew under Pallava rule** — the Shaiva **Nayanars** (Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar) and Vaishnava **Alvars** actively contested Jainism/Buddhism (see File 12). Royal conversions (Mahendravarman) show state–bhakti interplay.
- **Cultural export to South-East Asia:** the **Pallava Grantha script** became the parent of Khmer, Mon, Javanese and other SE-Asian scripts; Pallava-style temples influenced early SE-Asian architecture. (Classic Mains "maritime-cultural bridge" point.)

### 1.3 Language & literature

| Author/Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Bharavi — *Kiratarjuniyam*** | Sanskrit mahakavya; visited Simhavishnu's court |
| **Dandin — *Dashakumaracharita*, *Kavyadarsha*, *Avantisundari-katha*** | at Rajasimha's court |
| **Mahendravarman I — *Mattavilasa Prahasana*** | Sanskrit farce satirising Kapalikas & Buddhist monks; *Bhagavadajjuka* also attributed |
| **Perundevanar — *Bharatavenba*** | Tamil rendering of Mahabharata episodes (Pallava era) |
| **Tevaram hymns; Alvar hymns** | Tamil devotional corpus composed largely in Pallava centuries |
| **Kudumiyanmalai & Tirumayam music inscriptions** | notation of early Indian music, attributed to Mahendravarman I's time |

### 1.4 Art & architecture — the **four stages** (favourite Prelims question)

| Stage | Period | Character | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mahendra style** | 610–640 | **Rock-cut cave mandapas** | Mandagapattu (inscription: shrine built *"without brick, timber, metal or mortar"*), Dalavanur, Pallavaram, Mamandur, Tiruchi lower cave |
| **Mamalla style** | 640–674 | **Monolithic rathas + mandapas + open-air relief** at Mamallapuram | **Pancha Rathas** (Draupadi–Arjuna–Bhima–Dharmaraja–Nakula-Sahadeva); Varaha & Mahishasuramardini mandapas; **"Descent of the Ganga / Arjuna's Penance"** bas-relief |
| **Rajasimha style** | 674–800 | First **structural** temples | **Shore Temple** (Mamallapuram), **Kailasanatha (Rajasimheshvara)** at Kanchi, Talagirisvara (Panamalai) |
| **Nandivarman / late style** | 800–897 | Smaller, refined structural temples | **Vaikuntha Perumal** (Kanchi), Mukteshvara, Matangeshvara |

- **Mamallapuram = UNESCO World Heritage (1984).** Pallava murals survive at Kailasanatha (Kanchi) and Panamalai; Pallava sculpture (Somaskanda panels) sets the template Cholas perfect.
- Mains line: *Pallavas carried South Indian architecture across the whole arc rock-cut → monolithic → structural, creating the grammar of the Dravida style that the Cholas monumentalised.*

## 2. THE BADAMI (WESTERN) CHALUKYAS (543–753)

### 2.1 Quick political frame

- **Pulakesin I** (543) fortified **Vatapi**, performed **Ashvamedha**.
- **Pulakesin II** (609–642), the greatest: **Aihole Prashasti (634 CE)** composed by his Jain court poet **Ravikirti** (Meguti temple) — compares himself to Kalidasa & Bharavi; **defeated Harshavardhana on the Narmada** (title **Dakshinapatheshvara / Parameshvara**); installed brother **Kubja Vishnuvardhana as viceroy of Vengi (624 → Eastern Chalukya line)**; exchanged embassies with **Persian king Khusrau II**; **Hiuen Tsang** visited his empire (calls him Pu-lo-ki-she of Mo-ho-la-cha / Maharashtra). Killed when **Narasimhavarman I** sacked Vatapi (642).
- **Vikramaditya II** (733–744) thrice overran Kanchi but **did not destroy it** — donated to Rajasimheshvara temple (recorded there); his queens **Lokamahadevi & Trailokyamahadevi** built the **Virupaksha & Mallikarjuna temples at Pattadakal** to celebrate the Kanchi victory (Pallava craftsmen brought north — architectural cross-pollination fact).
- **Kirtivarman II** overthrown by Rashtrakuta **Dantidurga (753)**.

### 2.2 Society & religion

- Revival of **Vedic sacrifices** and Puranic Hinduism (Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, Karttikeya); **Jainism respected** (Ravikirti); Buddhism visibly declining (Hiuen Tsang's testimony).
- The famous **"Five Hundred Svamis of Ayyavole" (Ainnurruvar)** merchant guild originated at **Aihole** — later dominates peninsular & overseas trade under Rashtrakutas/Cholas (superb cross-dynasty Mains link).
- **⭐ AP link:** the **Navabrahma temples at Alampur** (Tungabhadra–Krishna confluence, historic Telugu country) are **Badami Chalukya, 7th–8th c., in NAGARA style** — proof that northern curvilinear shikharas entered the Deccan under them.

### 2.3 Language & literature

- **Sanskrit:** Aihole Prashasti = high-water mark of prashasti poetry.
- **Kannada:** period of earliest literary Kannada — **Kappe Arabhatta record (c. 700, tripadi metre)**; Badami Chalukyas made Kannada a language of inscription alongside Sanskrit.

### 2.4 Art & architecture — the "cradle" laboratory

| Site | What to remember |
|---|---|
| **Aihole** (~125 temples) | "**Cradle of Indian temple architecture**": **Ladkhan** (assembly-hall type), **Durga temple** (apsidal/gajaprishtha), **Meguti Jain temple** (dated 634), Ravana Phadi cave |
| **Badami** | **Four cave temples** (1 Shaiva, 2 Vaishnava, 1 Jain); Cave 3 (Vishnu, 578 CE, by Mangalesha) carries the **earliest dated Deccan mural fragments**; Bhutanatha group; Malegitti Shivalaya |
| **Pattadakal** (**UNESCO 1987**) | Coronation site; **10 temples: ~4 Nagara + 6 Dravida side by side** — **Virupaksha (Lokeshvara)** & **Mallikarjuna** (Dravida, by the two queens), **Papanatha** (Nagara-Dravida hybrid), Sangameshvara (Vijayaditya), Galaganatha & Kashi Vishwanatha (Nagara), Jain Narayana (added under Rashtrakutas) |

- They began the **Vesara ("Chalukyan") experiment** — deliberate fusion of Nagara and Dravida on one site; Kalyani Chalukyas and Hoysalas complete it.

## 3. THE EASTERN CHALUKYAS OF VENGI (624–1075) — ⭐ APPSC CORE

### 3.1 Quick political frame

- Founded by **Kubja Vishnuvardhana I** (624), younger brother of Pulakesin II; viceroyalty → independent kingdom lasting ~450 years.
- Capitals migrated: **Pishtapuram (Pithapuram) → Vengi (Pedavegi, near Eluru) → Rajamahendravaram (Rajahmundry**, named from Rajaraja Narendra's title *Rajamahendra*).
- Perpetual **buffer state**: fought over by Badami Chalukyas vs Pallavas, then Rashtrakutas, then **Cholas vs Kalyani Chalukyas**.
- **Gunaga Vijayaditya III** (848–892) = militarily greatest; his Telugu general **Pandaranga** subdued the Boya country (Kandukur region).
- **Chalukya Bhima I** (892–922) = the temple-builder (Draksharama, Samalkot).
- **Rajaraja Narendra** (1019–1061): Chola ally & son-in-law line (mother Kundavai's line; wife Ammangadevi, daughter of Rajendra Chola) — his son ascended the **Chola throne in 1070 as Kulottunga I**, merging the two houses (**Chalukya-Chola line**); Vengi became a Chola viceroyalty (line's end conventionally 1075).
- **⭐ Earliest full Telugu inscription is NOT theirs** but of the **Renati Cholas — Kalamalla inscription (Yerragudipadu, Kadapa dist.), c. 575 CE, of Dhanunjaya** — pair this fact with the Eastern Chalukya literary story.

### 3.2 Society & religion

- **Agrahara–temple order planted in the deltas:** hundreds of copper-plate grants (tamra-shasanas) record brahmadeya endowments — the delta became wet-rice, taxable, Brahmanised country; subordinate warrior lineages (samantas: Velanati Chodas, Kotas, Chagis, etc.) grew beneath the dynasty and outlived it (raw material of Kakatiya state).
- **Shaivism dominant** (Pashupata/Kalamukha teachers at big shrines); **Buddhism decayed** in coastal Andhra (Xuanzang, c. 639, already saw ruined monasteries); **Jainism survived in pockets** — king **Vimaladitya** had the Jain teacher Trikalayogi Siddhantadeva.
- **Pancharama cult consolidated:** the five Shiva "Arama" shrines of coastal Andhra — **Amararama (Amaravati), Draksharama, Kumararama (Samalkota), Ksheerarama (Palakollu), Somarama (Bhimavaram)** — the classic APPSC list.

### 3.3 Language & literature — **birth of literary Telugu (their defining contribution)**

| Milestone | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Telugu enters inscriptions** | Under Eastern Chalukyas Telugu steadily displaces Prakrit/Sanskrit in records of the Vengi country |
| **Pandaranga's Addanki inscription (c. 848–892)** | early Telugu **verse** (taruvoja metre) — pre-Nannaya poetry exists in stone |
| **Yuddhamalla II's Bezawada (Vijayawada) inscription (c. 930)** | Telugu verses in madhyakkara metre |
| **NANNAYA BHATTARAKA — *Andhra Mahabharatamu*** (c. 1050s) | At the court of **Rajaraja Narendra**, assisted by **Narayana Bhatta**; translated/recreated **Adi, Sabha and part of Aranya parvas** in **champu** (mixed prose-verse) style. Titles: **"Adikavi"** (first poet of Telugu) and **"Vaganusasanudu"** (law-giver of the language); the Sanskrit grammar of Telugu, ***Andhra Shabda Chintamani***, is traditionally attributed to him. |
| **Kavitraya arc begins** | Nannaya (11th c.) → **Tikkana** (13th c., Nellore court of Manumasiddhi) → **Errana** (14th c., Reddi era) completed the Mahabharata — remember the trio spans three later polities. |

Mains line: *Vengi's lasting conquest was linguistic — under the Eastern Chalukyas Telugu became first a language of record and then, with Nannaya, a language of high poetry; every later Andhra state inherited this.*

### 3.4 Art & architecture

- **Draksharama Bhimeshvara** and **Chalukya Bhimavaram (Kumararama, Samalkota)** temples — built by **Bhima I** (892–922); two-storeyed sandhara vimanas; Draksharama also a major Shakti pitha (Manikyamba).
- **Biccavolu group** (East Godavari, 9th–10th c.) — Golingeshvara and companion temples; the best surviving Eastern Chalukyan structural style (Dravida with local delta idiom).
- Patronage/expansion at the **Pancharamas** and early endowments in the Vengi delta; the **Telugu-Kannada script** matured in their records (ancestor of modern Telugu script).
- Their idiom is **transitional Chalukya→Chola**: after 1070 the Chola style flows north into Vengi and Telugu warriors flow south — a two-way corridor.

## 4. THE RASHTRAKUTAS (753–973)

### 4.1 Quick political frame

- **Dantidurga** (753) overthrew Kirtivarman II; performed **Hiranyagarbha** ritual at Ujjain.
- **Krishna I** (756–774): **Kailasa temple, Ellora**.
- **Dhruva** and **Govinda III**: the only Deccan power to repeatedly win the **tripartite struggle for Kannauj** — Dhruva defeated both **Vatsaraja (Pratihara)** and **Dharmapala (Pala)**; Govinda III repeated it against Nagabhata II. (Mains: "bridge/arbiter between north and south".)
- **Amoghavarsha I** (814–878): 64-year reign; built new capital **Manyakheta**; scholar-king (see literature); Arab merchant **Sulaiman (851 CE)** ranked him among the **four great kings of the world** (with the Caliph, China, Byzantium).
- **Krishna III** (939–967): defeated Cholas at **Takkolam (949)** — Chola crown prince Rajaditya killed on his elephant; marched to Rameshvaram, built temples there.
- Ended by **Tailapa II (973)** → Kalyani Chalukyas.

### 4.2 Society & religion

- **Model of religious tolerance:** Shaivism + Vaishnavism + strong **Jain efflorescence** (Amoghavarsha inclined to Jainism, disciple of **Jinasena**); **Arab Muslim traders welcomed at west-coast ports** — **Al-Masudi (10th c.)** calls the Rashtrakuta emperor ("**Balhara**" = Vallabharaja) *the greatest king of India* and notes mosques functioning in his ports. (Perfect Mains evidence for pre-Sultanate Hindu–Muslim commercial coexistence.)
- Endowed colleges (e.g., the **Salotgi inscription, 945 CE** — Sanskrit college with hostels); agraharas spread in the Deccan.

### 4.3 Language & literature — **Kannada's first golden age + Sanskrit science**

| Author | Work | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Amoghavarsha I (with Srivijaya)** | ***Kavirajamarga*** | **earliest extant Kannada work** (poetics); king also wrote *Prashnottara Ratnamalika* (Sanskrit) |
| **Pampa** ("Adikavi" of Kannada) | *Vikramarjuna Vijaya (Pampa Bharata)*, *Adipurana* | court of **Arikesari II of Vemulavada** (a Rashtrakuta feudatory **in Telangana** — ⭐ AP/Telangana link) |
| **Ponna** | *Shantipurana* | court of Krishna III; title **Ubhaya-kavichakravarti** (master of Kannada & Sanskrit); Pampa–Ponna–Ranna = **"Ratnatraya"** of Kannada |
| **Jinasena** | *Adipurana* (Sanskrit) | Amoghavarsha's guru |
| **Mahaviracharya** | ***Ganitasarasangraha*** | landmark of Indian **mathematics** |
| **Sakatayana** | *Amoghavritti* (grammar) | named for the king |
| **Trivikrama Bhatta / Halayudha** | *Nalachampu* / *Kavirahasya* | Sanskrit belles-lettres |

### 4.4 Art & architecture

- **Kailasa temple (Ellora Cave 16, c. 760s, Krishna I):** world's largest **monolithic rock-cut temple** — carved **top-down** from a single basalt hill; Dravida form (Virupaksha of Pattadakal its model); **Ellora = UNESCO 1983** (Buddhist + Hindu + Jain caves — tolerance in stone).
- **Elephanta caves** (great **Maheshamurti/"Trimurti"**) — 6th-c. origins (Kalachuri) but sustained under Rashtrakuta rule; exam convention often credits Rashtrakutas.
- Jain basadis (e.g., Jain Narayana at Pattadakal); Ellora Jain group (Indra Sabha).

## 5. THE KALYANI (WESTERN) CHALUKYAS (973–c. 1189)

### 5.1 Quick political frame

- **Tailapa II** (973) restored Chalukya power from Manyakheta; **Someshvara I** shifted capital to **Kalyani (Basavakalyan)**; long duel with the Cholas — battles of **Koppam (1054**, Chola Rajadhiraja I killed on his elephant — "**Yanai-mel-thunjina-devar**") and **Kudal Sangamam (c. 1063)**.
- **Vikramaditya VI** (1076–1126), the greatest: started the **Chalukya-Vikrama era (1076)**; seized **Vengi (1118)**; court of **Bilhana** and **Vijnaneshvara** (below).
- Decline: usurpation by **Kalachuri Bijjala** (1157–67) — in whose Kalyana the **Basava/Veerashaiva revolution** erupted; the three great feudatories — **Hoysalas, Seuna Yadavas, KAKATIYAS of Warangal (⭐ AP link)** — partitioned the empire by c. 1189.

### 5.2 Society, law, religion

- **Vijnaneshvara's *MITAKSHARA*** (commentary on Yajnavalkya Smriti, written at Vikramaditya VI's court) — foundational school of **Hindu joint-family/inheritance law** for most of India including Andhra (contrast: Bengal's Dayabhaga of Jimutavahana). ⭐ Very frequent exam point.
- **Someshvara III "Bhulokamalla"** wrote ***Manasollasa / Abhilashitartha-chintamani*** (c. 1129) — Sanskrit **encyclopedia** of statecraft, cuisine, music, games, jewellery — key source for medieval Deccan social life.
- **Veerashaiva / Lingayat movement (12th c.):** **Basaveshwara** (treasurer-minister of Bijjala) with **Allama Prabhu** and **Akka Mahadevi**; **Anubhava Mantapa** ("hall of experience" — proto-parliament of spiritual democracy); rejected caste, temple-centred ritual and Brahmin mediation; produced **vachana literature** (Kannada prose-poems). Spread into Telugu country (see Palkuriki Somanatha, File 12).

### 5.3 Language & literature

- **Kannada:** **Ranna** (*Gadayuddha / Sahasa Bhima Vijaya*; third jewel of the Ratnatraya, court of Tailapa II–Satyashraya); Nagavarma II (*Kavyavalokana*); Durgasimha (Kannada *Panchatantra*); the **vachana corpus** (Basava, Akka Mahadevi, Allama).
- **Sanskrit:** **Bilhana's *Vikramankadevacharita*** (eulogy of Vikramaditya VI); **Mitakshara**; **Manasollasa**.
- ⭐ Contemporary cross-link: while Kannada flowered at Kalyani, **Nannaya was creating literary Telugu at the rival Vengi court** — one exam question often pairs them.

### 5.4 Art & architecture — the **true Vesara ("Gadag") style**

- Character: soapstone/chloritic schist, **lathe-turned polished pillars**, densely carved doorframes, stepped-diamond plans, miniature-tower (kirtimukha) friezes — the **bridge from Badami Chalukya to Hoysala** exuberance.
- Key temples: **Itagi Mahadeva (1112** — inscription calls it *"Devalaya Chakravarti", emperor among temples*), **Kashivishveshvara at Lakkundi** (+ Brahma Jinalaya), **Doddabasappa at Dambal** (24-point stellate plan), Mallikarjuna at Kuruvatti, Trikuteshvara at Gadag; ornate **stepped tanks/wells**.
- ⭐ AP/Telangana link: **early Kakatiya temples (Hanamkonda Thousand-Pillar temple, 1163)** grew directly out of this Kalyani-Chalukyan idiom — Kakatiyas began as their feudatories.

## 6. THE CHOLAS (imperial line 848–1279)

### 6.1 Quick political frame

- **Vijayalaya** (c. 848–850) took Thanjavur from the Muttaraiyars; built the Nishumbhasudini (Durga) temple.
- **Aditya I** ended Pallavas (897). **Parantaka I** (907–955): took Madurai ("**Maduraikonda**"); **defeated at Takkolam (949)**; his **Uttaramerur inscriptions** (919 & 921) codify village elections (below).
- **RAJARAJA I** (985–1014): conquered Kerala (Kandalur Salai), **northern Sri Lanka** (sacked Anuradhapura; province "Mummudi-Chola-mandalam" from Polonnaruwa), Maldives, Gangavadi; **protectorate over Vengi** (married ties — Kundavai line); ordered island-wide **land survey**; built **Brihadishvara** (1010); titles **Mummudi Chola, Sivapada Sekhara, Jayankonda**.
- **RAJENDRA I** (1014–1044): completed Sri Lanka; **Gangetic expedition (1022–23)** — defeated Mahipala I (Pala); titles **Gangaikonda** (built new capital **Gangaikondacholapuram** + the **Cholagangam** tank, the "liquid pillar of victory"); **naval expedition against SRIVIJAYA (1025)** — ruler Sangrama Vijayottungavarman; title **Kadaram-kondan**; embassies to **Song China**.
- **Kulottunga I** (1070–1122): the **Chalukya-Chola** (Vengi mother-line, see §3.1); abolished tolls — "**Sungam Tavirtta Cholan**"; second land survey (1086); the **Kalinga war** immortalised in *Kalingattupparani*.
- Later: Rajaraja II (Darasuram), Kulottunga III (Tribhuvanam); **Rajendra III**, last, overthrown by Pandyas (**1279**).

### 6.2 Administration & local self-government (⭐ guaranteed exam zone)

**Hierarchy:** mandalam (province, viceroys often princes) → **valanadu** → **nadu** → **kurram/kottam** (village cluster) → village.

**Three village types:**
| Type | Composition |
|---|---|
| **Ur** | common (non-Brahmin) village assembly |
| **Sabha / Mahasabha** | assembly of **brahmadeya** villages |
| **Nagaram** | assembly of merchants/towns |

**Uttaramerur inscriptions (919 & 921 CE, Parantaka I — on Vaikunda Perumal temple wall, Kanchipuram dist.):**
- Village divided into **30 wards (kudumbus)**; members chosen by **KUDAVOLAI** (palm-leaf tickets drawn from a pot by a boy).
- **Qualifications:** age 35–70; own **> ¼ veli of tax-paying land**; house on own site; knowledge of Vedic texts (mantra-brahmana).
- **Disqualifications:** on a committee in the past 3 years; failure to submit accounts; the five great sins; incest etc.
- **Committees (variyams):** Samvatsara (annual/general), **Eri-variyam (TANK)**, Totta-variyam (garden), Pon-variyam (gold), Pancha-variyam (standing), plus justice (nyayattar).
- Mains phrase: *"democracy with a difference"* — sophisticated **local** self-rule with property/learning franchise inside an imperial monarchy; committees audited, members recallable.

**Other machinery:** land revenue (kadamai) ≈ **1/3 of produce** after meticulous surveys (985–1013 under Rajaraja; 1086 under Kulottunga I); higher/lower officialdom (**peruntaram/siruntaram**); royal orders drafted by **olai-nayagam**; army with elephant corps, the **velaikkarar** (oath-bound royal guards) and **kaikkolar** (weaver-soldiers); the strongest **navy** in the medieval Indian Ocean.

**Trade & guilds:** **Ainnurruvar (Ayyavole 500)**, **Manigramam**, **Nanadeshi**, and **Anjuvannam** (guild of West-Asian — Jewish, Christian, Muslim — maritime merchants on the coast); nagaram towns knitted the empire to SE Asia and China.

### 6.3 Society, religion, education

- **Temple as the pivot:** employer, landlord (devadana), bank, school, theatre. Rajaraja settled **400 dancing women (talicheri pendir)** around the Thanjavur temple — institutionalised **devadasi** system.
- **Valangai (right-hand) / Idangai (left-hand)** caste bloc division first becomes prominent in Chola records — key fact on caste dynamics.
- **Shaiva bhakti canonised:** **Nambi Andar Nambi** compiled the Shaiva canon (**Tirumurai**) under Rajaraja I ("**Tirumurai Kanda Cholan**"); **Sekkilar's *Periyapuranam*** (lives of the 63 Nayanars, under **Kulottunga II**) became its 12th book. Vaishnava canon (Nalayira Divya Prabandham) compiled by **Nathamuni** (10th c.). Occasional sectarian friction: tradition remembers a Chola persecutor of **Ramanuja** ("Krimikantha"), who fled to Hoysala **Vishnuvardhana** (converted him from Jainism, c. 1116) — see File 12.
- **Education inscriptions (quote in Mains):** **Ennayiram** (Rajendra I: Vedic college, 340 students, 14 teachers, hostel), **Tirumukkudal** (Virarajendra: college + hostel + **hospital "Virasolan"** with 15 beds, physician, surgeon, nurses), **Tribhuvani** (Rajadhiraja).
- **Buddhism's last royal courtesy:** Srivijaya king built the **Chudamani Vihara at Nagapattinam (1006)** with Rajaraja I's patronage — recorded in the **Larger Leiden copper plates** (Smaller Leiden plates = Kulottunga I). ⭐ classic Prelims pair.

### 6.4 Art & architecture — the **Dravida climax**

| Temple | Builder | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| **Brihadishvara (Rajarajeshvara), Thanjavur, 1010** | Rajaraja I | all-granite; **~66 m (216 ft) vimana**; ~80-tonne capstone; monolithic Nandi; Chola **murals** in the ambulatory (Rajaraja with guru **Karuvur Devar**; Tripurantaka); called **Dakshina Meru** |
| **Gangaikondacholapuram, c. 1035** | Rajendra I | slightly shorter, **concave "feminine" curve** of vimana; Cholagangam tank |
| **Airavatesvara, Darasuram, 12th c.** | Rajaraja II | **chariot-shaped mandapa**, "musical steps", nitya-vinoda (perpetual entertainment) theme |
| **Kampahareshvara, Tribhuvanam** | Kulottunga III | fourth of the set |

- The first three = **"Great Living Chola Temples" — UNESCO (1987, extended 2004)**.
- **CHOLA BRONZES:** **lost-wax (cire perdue / madhuchchhishta-vidhana)** casting; the **NATARAJA** (Ananda-tandava) — damaru = creation, fire = destruction, abhaya hand = protection, raised foot = release, dwarf **Apasmara** = ignorance crushed, prabhamandala = cosmos; celebrated in **Ananda Coomaraswamy's "The Dance of Shiva"**. Other classics: Somaskanda, Ardhanarishvara, Kalyanasundara, saint-portraits (Appar, Sambandar, Andal).
- Mural + bronze + temple = *integrated royal cult of Shiva projecting imperial power* — the standard Mains synthesis line.

## 7. Cross-dynasty comparison sheets (revision gold)

### 7.1 Architecture in one line each
- **Pallava:** invented the sequence cave → monolith → structural; gave Dravida its grammar (Mamallapuram, Kanchi).
- **Badami Chalukya:** the laboratory — Nagara & Dravida side by side; Vesara begun (Aihole–Badami–Pattadakal).
- **Eastern Chalukya:** delta Shaiva temples (Draksharama, Samalkota, Biccavolu); Pancharama network.
- **Rashtrakuta:** rock-cut climax and finale — monolithic Kailasa at Ellora.
- **Kalyani Chalukya:** true Vesara refinement (Itagi, Lakkundi) → seedbed of Hoysala & Kakatiya styles.
- **Chola:** structural Dravida climax — royal mega-temples + bronze casting perfection.

### 7.2 Language patronage map
| Language | Made literary under | First landmark |
|---|---|---|
| Tamil (devotional & epic bloom) | Pallavas → Cholas | Tevaram/Divya Prabandham; Kamban |
| Kannada | Rashtrakutas → Kalyani Chalukyas | Kavirajamarga; Pampa–Ponna–Ranna; vachanas |
| **Telugu** | **Eastern Chalukyas** | Addanki/Bezawada verse; **Nannaya's Mahabharatamu** |
| Sanskrit | all six (prashastis, shastra, kavya) | Aihole Prashasti; Mitakshara; Manasollasa |

### 7.3 Traveller/foreign testimony
- **Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang):** Pulakesin II's Deccan; Kanchi under Narasimhavarman I; decaying Buddhism in Vengi/Andhra.
- **Sulaiman (851) & Al-Masudi (10th c.):** Rashtrakuta "Balhara" greatest of Indian kings; mosques at ports.
- **Marco Polo (1290s):** Kakatiya queen Rudramadevi's realm ("Mutfili") — links this unit to the next era.

## 8. Prelims nuggets (one-liners to drill)

1. *Vatapikonda* — Narasimhavarman I; *Maduraikonda* — Parantaka I; *Gangaikonda / Kadaramkonda* — Rajendra I.
2. Aihole Prashasti (634) — Ravikirti on Pulakesin II, at Meguti Jain temple.
3. Mandagapattu inscription — Mahendravarman I's "brick-less, timber-less" cave shrine.
4. Pancha Rathas & Arjuna's Penance — Mamallapuram (Narasimhavarman I).
5. Virupaksha, Pattadakal — queen Lokamahadevi of Vikramaditya II (model for Kailasa, Ellora).
6. Kailasa monolith — Krishna I; Ellora has Buddhist–Hindu–Jain caves together.
7. Kavirajamarga — Amoghavarsha I; Ganitasarasangraha — Mahaviracharya.
8. Kalamalla (c. 575, Kadapa) — earliest full Telugu inscription (Renati Chola Dhanunjaya).
9. Nannaya = Adikavi + Vaganusasanudu; patron Rajaraja Narendra; helper Narayana Bhatta.
10. Draksharama & Kumararama Bhimeshvara — Eastern Chalukya Bhima I; Pancharama five = Amaravati, Draksharama, Samalkota, Palakollu, Bhimavaram.
11. Mitakshara — Vijnaneshvara (Vikramaditya VI); Vikramankadevacharita — Bilhana; Manasollasa — Someshvara III.
12. Uttaramerur (919/921, Parantaka I) — kudavolai, 30 kudumbus, variyams (eri = tank committee).
13. Brihadishvara 1010 (Rajaraja I) → Gangaikondacholapuram (Rajendra I) → Darasuram (Rajaraja II) = Great Living Chola Temples (UNESCO).
14. Chola bronzes — lost-wax; Nataraja symbolism; Coomaraswamy's essay.
15. Larger Leiden plates — Rajaraja I & Chudamani Vihara (Srivijaya); Smaller — Kulottunga I.
16. Basava's Anubhava Mantapa at Kalyana (Bijjala's reign); vachana literature.
17. Ainnurruvar "500 of Ayyavole" guild — born at Aihole; Anjuvannam — foreign maritime merchants.
18. Kakatiyas, Hoysalas, Yadavas — all ex-feudatories of Kalyani Chalukyas.

## 9. Mains angles (frame + evidence, ready to deploy)

1. **"Temple was the axis of early-medieval South Indian life."** Evidence: devadana/brahmadeya grants; Thanjavur's 400 devadasis & payroll inscriptions; temple colleges (Ennayiram) & hospital (Tirumukkudal); guild endowments; Pancharama network in Andhra.
2. **"From Vengi's records to Nannaya's verse — the Eastern Chalukyas made Telugu a literary language."** Evidence: Addanki/Bezawada inscriptions → champu Mahabharatamu; court patronage (Rajaraja Narendra); continuity to Tikkana–Errana.
3. **"Chola village autonomy was real but bounded."** Evidence: Uttaramerur rules; audit & disqualification clauses; royal survey and valanadu restructuring above the village.
4. **"The Deccan as India's architectural crossroads."** Evidence: Nagara+Dravida at Pattadakal; Alampur Navabrahma (Nagara in Telugu land); Vesara at Itagi/Lakkundi; export of Pallava craftsmen after Kanchi wars.
5. **"Royal patronage, not royal faith alone, drove culture."** Evidence: Jain Ravikirti under Shaiva Chalukyas; Amoghavarsha's Jainism with Shaiva grants; Muslim traders under Rashtrakutas; Chola canon-making as statecraft.
