## 0. Frame

Land revenue was **the fiscal engine of British India** — the settlements decided who owned the land, who paid, and who was ruined. Three systems divided the map: **Permanent Settlement (Bengal, 1793)**, **Ryotwari (Madras & Bombay)**, **Mahalwari (NWP-Punjab)**. Their common results — over-assessment, commercialisation, debt, landlordism, famines — plus the political shocks of Dalhousie's decade, exploded in **1857**: the first great challenge to British rule, whose failure remade the Raj (Crown rule, a reorganised army, divide-and-rule). *(2023 Q4a and 2025 Q4a are direct hits on this file.)*

## 1. The three settlements ⭐⭐⭐ (2023 Q4a, 2025 Q4a)

| Feature | **Permanent Settlement (Zamindari)** | **Ryotwari** | **Mahalwari** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Author & year | **Cornwallis, 22 March 1793** (John Shore's plan) | **Thomas Munro & Alexander Read** — Baramahal 1792; Madras Presidency-wide **1820** | **Holt Mackenzie (Regulation VII, 1822)**, revised **Martin Bird / R.M. Bird & James Thomason (1833)** |
| Area (~% of British India) | Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Banaras, **Northern Circars (coastal Andhra ⭐)** — ~19% | Madras (incl. **Rayalaseema/Ceded Districts ⭐**), Bombay, Assam, Coorg — ~51% | NW Provinces, Central Provinces, **Punjab** — ~30% |
| Settled with | **Zamindar** — made *proprietor* of the land | **The ryot (cultivator) directly** — patta to each field-holder | **The village community (mahal)** — jointly, often via **lambardar** |
| Revenue | **Fixed FOREVER**: 10/11 to the state, 1/11 to the zamindar | ~**50% of net produce**; revised every **20–30 years** | **66% initially → 50% (Bird/Thomason)**; periodic revision |
| Sanction | **Sunset Law** ⭐ — pay by sunset of the due date or the estate is auctioned | land forfeited for default; ryot could sell/mortgage his patta | joint village responsibility |
| Logic | secure income, loyal landed class, "improving landlords" à la England | Munro's paternalism — no middlemen, Utilitarian + revenue flexibility | Regulation-VII compromise where village bodies were strong |
| Peculiar evils | zamindar's cultivators reduced to **tenants-at-will**; **absentee landlordism, subinfeudation** (dozens of rent layers); state lost all gains from rising prices | **over-assessment** ⭐, rigid cash collection even in famine; moneylender entered through the mortgageable patta | over-assessment; village elites became de facto landlords |

### 1.1 Permanent Settlement — impact on Indian society ⭐⭐ (2025 Q4a Mains skeleton)
1. **A new social class:** zamindars turned from revenue-farmers into **landowners**; old rajas' estates auctioned under the Sunset Law to **Calcutta banians/merchants** — a loyal, rent-receiving, largely **absentee** class (the Congress-era "landlord interest").
2. **The cultivator dispossessed:** customary occupancy rights extinguished; rack-renting, **illegal cesses (abwabs)**, ejectment; peasant revolts (Rangpur 1783 earlier; indigo & Pabna later).
3. **Fiscal freeze:** state revenue fixed while prices rose — Bengal's surplus flowed to zamindars, not public works; government later squeezed **other provinces** harder.
4. **Subinfeudation & litigation culture**; land became a **marketable commodity** — the deepest structural change ⭐.
5. Balance line: it gave Bengal fiscal certainty and a loyalist class (its very purpose), at the price of **agrarian stagnation and a parasitic rentier order** — Bengal's 19th-century famines and tenancy acts (1859, 1885) are its footnotes.

### 1.2 Ryotwari & Mahalwari in practice
- **Munro's promise vs practice:** direct settlement flattered the ryot but assessments were **pitched too high on estimates**, collected in **cash** regardless of harvest — the ryot met the state's demand **through the moneylender (sahukar)**; land passed to creditors → **Deccan Riots 1875** ⭐.
- Mahalwari's joint responsibility broke village brotherhoods in the NWP — dispossessed proprietors were among the **fiercest rebels of 1857** ⭐ (link the two sections in Mains).

## 2. Economic impact of British revenue-trade policy ⭐⭐ (2023 Q4a second half)

1. **Peasant impoverishment & debt** — high cash assessments → moneylender → land alienation.
2. **Commercialisation of agriculture** — forced shift to indigo, cotton, opium, jute for revenue/cash; food security eroded → **famine chain** (1770 Bengal → 1866 Orissa → 1876–78 Great Famine incl. **Rayalaseema ⭐** → 1896–1900).
3. **DEINDUSTRIALISATION** ⭐ — one-way free trade after 1813 (File 27 §1): mill cloth in, tariff walls in Britain; weavers of **Bengal and Andhra (Masulipatnam kalamkari)** ruined; "the bones of the cotton-weavers bleaching the plains of India" (Bentinck's report line).
4. **DRAIN OF WEALTH** ⭐⭐ — Home Charges, remittances, Investment-from-revenue; theorised by **Dadabhai Naoroji ("Poverty and Un-British Rule in India", drain theory from 1867)** and **R.C. Dutt ("Economic History of India")** — the economic backbone of early nationalism (File 30).
5. **Ruralisation** — de-urbanised artisans fell back on land; pressure on agriculture rose while investment (bar railways-for-export and canals) stayed minimal.

## 3. The Revolt of 1857 ⭐⭐⭐

### 3.1 Causes (layer them)
- **Political:** Doctrine of Lapse (Satara→Jhansi→Nagpur), **annexation of Awadh 1856** (misgovernment) — sepoys' own province; Mughal dignity slighted (Dalhousie/Canning on the succession).
- **Economic-agrarian:** revenue settlements & summary settlements of Awadh (taluqdars dispossessed); resumption of **inam/rent-free lands** (Bentinck's resumptions; **Inam Commission, Bombay 1852**); artisan ruin.
- **Socio-religious:** missionary activity + 1850 converts' inheritance Act + reforms (sati, widow remarriage) read as Christianising design; **"religion in danger"** on both faiths.
- **Military:** sepoy grievances — pay/batta, **General Service Enlistment Act 1856** (overseas service = loss of caste), promotions blocked; disproportion of British troops after Punjab/Awadh annexations.
- **Immediate spark:** the **greased cartridges** of the **Enfield rifle** (cow & pig fat); **Mangal Pandey, Barrackpore, 29 March 1857** ⭐; Meerut sepoys broke out **10 May 1857** → Delhi, **Bahadur Shah II 'Zafar' proclaimed** emperor (11 May).

### 3.2 Centres & leaders (match-pairs ⭐ — prelims certainty)
| Centre | Leader | British opponent / fate |
|---|---|---|
| **Delhi** | Bahadur Shah II; real command **Bakht Khan** ⭐ | John Nicholson (killed), Hudson shot the princes; Zafar exiled to **Rangoon** |
| **Kanpur** | **Nana Saheb** (+ **Tantia Tope**, Azimullah Khan) | Sir Hugh Wheeler; recaptured by Campbell; Nana escaped to Nepal |
| **Lucknow (Awadh)** | **Begum Hazrat Mahal** (son Birjis Qadr) | Henry Lawrence (killed); Campbell retook it |
| **Jhansi → Gwalior** | **Rani Lakshmibai** (+ Tantia Tope) | **Sir Hugh Rose** ("best and bravest"); died 17 June 1858 |
| **Bihar (Jagdishpur)** | **Kunwar Singh** — the 80-year-old zamindar ⭐ | died of wounds, 1858 |
| **Faizabad** | **Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah** | killed 1858 |
| **Bareilly** | Khan Bahadur Khan | executed |

- **Tantia Tope** — the last guerrilla, betrayed and executed **April 1859**. Loyal to the British ⭐: Scindia, Nizam (+ **Salar Jung**), Nepal (Jang Bahadur), most of Punjab & the south — *why the south stayed quiet: no lapse-era annexations there, Madras army's different composition; but note Andhra's own earlier & parallel risings — Palegar revolts, **Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy, Renadu 1846** ⭐ (File 21).*

### 3.3 Why it failed
**No all-India plan or unity** (limited to the north-centre; leaders fought for restorations, not a nation) — **no modern arms, money or sea power** — **no unified command** (Zafar a figurehead) — **educated middle class, merchants & most princes aloof** — British telegraph, railways and Crimean-freed troops.

### 3.4 Nature debate (one Mains line each)
Sepoy mutiny only (**Sir John Lawrence, Seeley**) · "First National War of Independence" (**V.D. Savarkar, 1909**) · elitist-restorationist (**R.C. Majumdar** — "neither first, nor national, nor war of independence") · civil-agrarian rebellion emphasised (**S.B. Chaudhuri, Eric Stokes**) — best formula: **a sepoy mutiny that widened into a popular agrarian-political revolt of old India, the greatest anti-colonial upheaval of the century, but not yet nationalism**.

### 3.5 Impact ⭐⭐⭐ (the standard 6)
1. **Government of India Act 1858** — Company abolished; **Crown rule**; **Secretary of State for India** (+ Council of 15); Governor-General became **VICEROY (Canning first)**.
2. **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 Nov 1858, Allahabad)** ⭐ — no more annexations, adoption recognised, religious non-interference, equal employment promise (honoured in the breach).
3. **Army reorganisation** — British:Indian ratio raised (~1:2 in Bengal), artillery to Europeans only, **"martial races"** recruitment (Punjab/Nepal), mixed regiments — **divide-and-rule institutionalised**.
4. **Princes preserved** as "breakwaters in the storm" — 560+ states frozen under paramountcy till 1947 (Patel's task, File 30).
5. **Racial gulf & conservatism** — end of the reforming zeal; alliance with landlords & orthodoxy; deepened divide-et-impera between communities.
6. **Fiscal squeeze** — the Revolt's £36-million cost charged to India; income tax (1860), paper currency, financial decentralisation begin.

## 4. Andhra tie-ins ⭐

1. **Northern Circars under Permanent Settlement (1802–03)** vs **Rayalaseema under Munro's ryotwari** — Andhra contains both systems (File 21) — perfect illustrative pair for 2023 Q4a.
2. **Renadu revolt of Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy, 1846** — anti-revenue rising a decade before 1857.
3. Weaver ruin at **Masulipatnam**; famine of **1876–78 in Rayalaseema** ("Dhatu Karuvu").
4. **The Nizam & Salar Jung stayed loyal in 1857** — why the Deccan stayed quiet.
