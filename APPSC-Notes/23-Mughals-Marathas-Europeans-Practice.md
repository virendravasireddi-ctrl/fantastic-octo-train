## 3. "Firsts & onlys"

- **First Mughal grand garden-tomb (charbagh):** Humayun's Tomb (prototype of the Taj). **First full-marble tomb + first pietra dura:** Itmad-ud-Daula's tomb ("Baby Taj").
- **First standardised silver rupiya:** Sher Shah. **First Dahsala/Zabti settlement:** Todar Mal, 1580.
- **Only woman-authored Mughal memoir:** *Humayun-nama* (Gulbadan Begum). **Emperor who abolished jizya:** Akbar (1564); **who re-imposed it:** Aurangzeb (1679).
- **First printing press in India:** Portuguese, Goa 1556. **First English factory in India:** Surat (1613); **first on the east coast:** Masulipatnam (1611).
- **First Maratha Chhatrapati:** Shivaji (Raigad, 1674). **"Second founder" of Maratha power:** Balaji Vishwanath (Peshwa).
- **Battle that ended French power in India:** Wandiwash (1760). **Treaty that abolished the Peshwaship:** end of the Third Anglo-Maratha War (1818).
- **First Presidency fort of the English:** Fort St. George, Madras (1639).

## 7. Mains practice questions (10/15-mark pattern)

1. **"Sher Shah Suri was the forerunner of Akbar's administration."** Discuss. *(skeleton below)*
2. Examine the structure and significance of the **mansabdari** system; why did it decay?
3. **"Sulh-i-kul, not the Din-i-Ilahi, was Akbar's enduring religious legacy."** Comment.
4. Trace the evolution of **Mughal architecture** from Humayun's Tomb to the Taj Mahal.
5. **"Aurangzeb's Deccan policy achieved the empire's greatest extent and sowed its ruin."** Assess, with reference to Bijapur, Golconda and the Marathas. *(skeleton below)*
6. **"Shivaji built a state, not a plundering band."** Evaluate his administration and ideology. *(skeleton below)*
7. Account for the transformation of the Marathas from a regional kingdom into a pan-Indian confederacy under the Peshwas, and explain its limits.
8. **"The Carnatic Wars decided the fate of India."** Discuss with special reference to the Andhra coast. *(skeleton below)*
9. Why did the English, rather than the Portuguese, Dutch or French, establish territorial empire in India?
10. **"European commercial penetration both exploited and accelerated Mughal decline."** Examine.

### Answer skeletons

**Q1 (Sher Shah → Akbar):**
- *Opening:* the five years of Sur rule (1540–45) supplied the template the Mughals perfected.
- *Body:* revenue (measurement, patta-qabuliyat, ~1/3 demand → Todar Mal's Dahsala); currency (rupiya-dam → Akbar's standard); communications (GT Road, sarai-dak); local structure (sarkar-pargana, paired shiqdar-munsif → Mughal faujdar-amalguzar); army muster (dagh-chehra continuity).
- *Close:* Akbar added scale, the mansab frame and sulh-i-kul — but the fiscal-administrative DNA is Sher Shah's; "forerunner," not mere predecessor.

**Q5 (Aurangzeb's Deccan):**
- *Opening:* the Deccan gave Aurangzeb his greatest conquests and his fatal war.
- *Body:* the annexations — **Bijapur 1686, Golconda 1687** (ending the Qutb Shahis, cross-ref File 17) — extended the empire to the Kaveri; but the **27-year Maratha guerrilla war** ("the Deccan ulcer," Sarkar) drained treasury and army; layered onto jizya/temple policy alienating Rajputs-Sikhs-Jats and the **jagirdari crisis** (Athar Ali, Satish Chandra) and **agrarian crisis** (Habib).
- *Close:* the machine seized the moment Aurangzeb died (1707); the Deccan that he "won" birthed the **autonomous Nizam (1724)** and the Maratha ascendancy — verdict: maximum extent, minimum durability.

**Q6 (Shivaji the state-builder):**
- *Opening:* against Khafi Khan's "freebooter," the institutions tell another story.
- *Body:* **Ashtapradhan** council; a near-**ryotwari** revenue system (Annaji Datto's survey, ~2/5 demand, curbing watan-middlemen); **chauth-sardeshmukhi** as *systematic* fiscal policy, not loot; **forts + navy + cash-paid Mavali army**; Sanskritised administration (*Rajya-vyavahara-kosha*) and the **Swaraj/Hindavi-Swarajya** ideology; the **1674 coronation** as legitimation.
- *Close:* a compact, mobilising Deccan state — enough to outlast Aurangzeb's whole army; the "band" charge is Mughal-chronicle bias.

**Q8 (Carnatic Wars & Andhra):**
- *Opening:* the Anglo-French decider was fought largely on the Coromandel/Andhra coast.
- *Body:* First War (St. Thome 1746 — the discipline lesson; Aix-la-Chapelle); Second War (Dupleix's succession-intervention model; **Bussy at Hyderabad → Northern Circars**; Arcot 1751); Third War (**Forde takes Masulipatnam 1759; Wandiwash 1760**, Eyre Coote); **Bobbili 1757**; the **Circars ceded to the EIC (1765–66)** reducing the Nizam to a subsidiary ally.
- *Close:* the five reasons the English prevailed (navy, private capital, three presidencies + Bengal, leadership, sepoy army) — commerce on the Andhra coast became empire.
