# 13 · Shivaji and the Rise of the Maratha Empire (1630–1818)

[← Mughal Socio-Religious Life & Culture](12-Mughal-Socio-Religious-Life-and-Culture.md) · [Index](README.md) · [Next: Advent of Europeans →](14-Advent-of-Europeans-in-India.md)

## 0. Frame

Out of the Deccan sultanates' service-gentry rose a **Hindu warrior state** built on hill-forts, light cavalry, a mobile fiscal system (**chauth & sardeshmukhi**) and an ideology of **Swaraj / Hindavi Swarajya**. **Shivaji (r. 1674–80)** forged it; after a Mughal near-eclipse the **Peshwas** turned it into a **pan-Indian confederacy** that, at its 1750s zenith, collected tribute from the Punjab to Bengal to the Karnataka — before **Panipat (1761)** and three **Anglo-Maratha wars** ended it. For Andhra: Shivaji's **1677 Karnataka expedition** was launched from an alliance with **Qutb Shahi Golconda** (ministers **Madanna–Akkanna**), and the **Bhonsles of Nagpur** later levied chauth across the eastern Deccan up to the edges of the Circars.

| Phase | Dates | Core |
|---|---|---|
| **Shivaji & sons** | 1630–1707 | foundation; Ashtapradhan; Mughal war; Sambhaji, Rajaram, Tarabai |
| **Peshwa ascendancy** | 1713–1761 | Balaji Vishwanath → Baji Rao I → Nana Saheb; confederacy; **Panipat 1761** |
| **Confederacy & decline** | 1761–1818 | Madhav Rao's recovery; Anglo-Maratha Wars I–III |

## 1. Shivaji Bhonsle (1630–1680) — the founder ⭐⭐

- Born **Shivneri fort (1630)**; father **Shahji Bhonsle** (a jagirdar-general of Bijapur, holding **Pune** & **Bangalore** jagirs); mother **Jijabai**; administrator-guru **Dadaji Kondadev**; spiritual influences **Samarth Ramdas** (*Dasbodh*) and **Tukaram** (the Varkari saint — Bhakti undertow to the movement).
- **Early forts (the base):** seized **Torna (1646)**, built **Raigad**, took **Purandar, Kondana (Sinhagad), Rajgad** from Bijapur's control by the 1650s.

### 1.1 The making of a power — the great episodes
| Year | Episode |
|---|---|
| **1659** | **Afzal Khan** (Bijapur) killed by Shivaji at **Pratapgarh** with the **bagh-nakh (tiger-claws)**; Bijapuri army routed |
| **1660** | Siddi Johar's siege of Panhala; escape (Baji Prabhu's stand at **Pavan Khind**) |
| **1663** | night-raid on **Shaista Khan** (Mughal viceroy, Aurangzeb's uncle) in **Pune** — Shivaji cut off his fingers |
| **1664** | first **sack of SURAT** (the Mughal port) — repeated **1670** |
| **1665** | **Treaty of PURANDAR** with **Raja Jai Singh I** (Mughal) — surrendered **23 of 35 forts**, agreed to serve; son Sambhaji made a mansabdar |
| **1666** | visited **Agra**; slighted at Aurangzeb's court, imprisoned — **escaped in sweetmeat baskets** ⭐ |
| **1670** | resumed war; retook forts; second Surat raid; levied chauth widely |
| **1674** | **CORONATION at RAIGAD** — assumed **"Chhatrapati"**, **Haindava Dharmoddharak**; **Gaga Bhatt** (Benares pandit) officiated; struck the **Hon/Shivrai** coin and began the **Rajyabhisheka Shaka** era ⭐ |
| **1677–78** | **KARNATAKA (Karnatik) EXPEDITION** — see §1.3 |
| **1680** | died at **Raigad**; succeeded by **Sambhaji** |

### 1.2 Shivaji's administration ⭐⭐ (asked every cycle)
- **ASHTAPRADHAN** — a council of eight ministers (advisory, not hereditary cabinet):
  | Minister | Portfolio |
  |---|---|
  | **Peshwa (Mukhya Pradhan)** | prime minister, general administration |
  | **Amatya / Majumdar** | finance & accounts |
  | **Sachiv / Surnis** | royal correspondence & secretariat |
  | **Mantri / Waqia-navis** | intelligence, records, personal safety |
  | **Senapati / Sar-i-Naubat** | commander-in-chief |
  | **Sumant / Dabir** | foreign affairs |
  | **Nyayadhish** | justice (Hindu law, *Vyavahara Mayukha*) |
  | **Panditrao / Sadr** | religious & charitable affairs |
  (All except Nyayadhish & Panditrao also held **military commands**; Persian titles deliberately replaced by **Sanskrit** ones — *Rajya-vyavahara-kosha* lexicon commissioned.)
- **Revenue:** land assessed by **Annaji Datto's survey (kathi/measuring-rod)**, state demand ~**40% (2/5)**; curbed hereditary **jagir/watan/mirasdar** middlemen where possible → a near-**ryotwari** direct link with the peasant; **taqavi**-style relief.
- **CHAUTH & SARDESHMUKHI** ⭐ — the mobile fiscal engine: **chauth = 1/4 of the revenue** levied on territories **outside** direct Maratha rule (protection-money / claim to abstention from raids); **sardeshmukhi = an additional 1/10**, claimed as the hereditary "chief-headman (sardeshmukh)" of Maharashtra. Together they financed expansion without annexation — and later became the Peshwas' pan-Indian tribute system.
- **Military:** disciplined, **cash-paid** infantry (**Mavali** hillmen) & light cavalry (**bargirs** state-horsed, **silahdars** self-equipped); **~forts (Sindhudurg, Vijaydurg, Raigad, Pratapgarh)** as the strategic skeleton; a genuine **navy** (the root of later **Kanhoji Angre's** fleet) — *Shivaji = "father of the Indian navy"* in nationalist historiography.
- **Sources:** **Sabhasad Bakhar** (Krishnaji Anant), Chitnis Bakhar, and Mughal accounts (Khafi Khan — grudging respect).

### 1.3 The Karnataka expedition & the Andhra link (⭐⭐ AP-core)
- **1677:** Shivaji marched into the Deccan-Karnataka via **Golconda** — feted by **Qutb Shahi Abul Hasan Tana Shah**, whose Brahmin ministers **Madanna & Akkanna** brokered an **alliance and subsidy** (cross-ref File 09).
- Captured **Gingee (Jinji), Vellore** and much of the Bijapuri Carnatic; settled matters with his half-brother **Ekoji (Venkoji)** of **Thanjavur**. Gingee later became **Rajaram's** great refuge against Aurangzeb — anchoring Maratha resistance in the Tamil-Andhra south.

## 2. Successors & the Mughal war of survival (1680–1707)

- **Sambhaji (1680–89):** fought Aurangzeb's full Deccan onslaught; **captured and executed (1689)** at Sangameshwar — a rallying martyrdom.
- **Rajaram (1689–1700):** shifted the war-capital to **GINGEE (Jinji)** in the Tamil-Andhra country (held out 1690–98); institutionalised guerrilla resistance; ministers granted revenue-farming rights that later fragmented authority.
- **Tarabai (regent, 1700–07+):** Rajaram's widow led the confederacy through Aurangzeb's last years — the war that made the Deccan "Aurangzeb's ulcer" (File 11). Aurangzeb died **1707** having taken every fort and won nothing lasting.

## 3. The Peshwas — from ministers to empire-makers ⭐

| Peshwa | Years | Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| **Balaji Vishwanath** | 1713–20 | "**second founder**"; reunited the confederacy for **Shahu**; **1719** deal with the **Sayyid Brothers** — Mughal grant of **chauth + sardeshmukhi of the six Deccan subas** and swaraj → legalised Maratha fiscal claims |
| **BAJI RAO I** | 1720–40 | the greatest Peshwa; "**strike at the trunk, not the branches**"; doctrine of **Hindu-pad-padshahi**; **Palkhed (1728)** vs the Nizam, **Delhi raid (1737)**, **Bhopal (1737** — Nizam again), **captured Bassein & Salsette from the Portuguese (1739)**; raised the great **confederate houses** |
| **Balaji Baji Rao ("Nana Saheb")** | 1740–61 | territorial zenith (tribute from Punjab to Bengal) — then the catastrophe of **PANIPAT (1761)** |
| **Madhav Rao I** | 1761–72 | remarkable **recovery**; restored authority over north India; **Raghunath Rao & Nana Phadnavis** the coming powers |

### 3.1 The Maratha Confederacy ("the five houses") ⭐
| House | Seat | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Peshwa** | **Poona** | nominal head of the confederacy |
| **Gaekwad** | **Baroda** | Gujarat |
| **Holkar** | **Indore** | Malhar Rao & the redoubtable **Ahilyabai Holkar** |
| **Scindia (Shinde)** | **Gwalior** | Mahadji Scindia — controlled Delhi/the emperor in the 1780s |
| **Bhonsle** | **NAGPUR** | **Raghuji Bhonsle** raided **Bengal (the "Bargi" terror), the Carnatic and the eastern Deccan** — the house whose chauth-claims brushed the **Andhra/Circars** frontier ⭐ |
- The confederacy's strength (elastic reach) was also its weakness — **centrifugal chiefs**, no unified command, which told at Panipat and against the British.

### 3.2 Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761) ⭐⭐
- **Marathas (Sadashiv Rao Bhau, Vishwas Rao)** vs **Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)** + Rohillas + Awadh.
- A **catastrophic Maratha defeat** — Bhau and Vishwas Rao killed, a generation of leadership lost ("**not a home in Maharashtra untouched**"). It did **not** hand India to Abdali (he withdrew) but it **broke Maratha momentum in the north** for a decade and left a vacuum the **English** would fill (File 14). *Causes (Mains): alienation of Rajput/Jat/Sikh allies, over-extended supply lines, a set-piece battle against a superior cavalry-artillery foe, confederate half-heartedness.*

## 4. Decline & the Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–1818)

- Post-Madhav Rao succession strife (**Narayan Rao's murder; Raghunath Rao's bid**) opened the door to the English.
| War | Years | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **First Anglo-Maratha** | 1775–82 | Maratha success; **Treaty of Salbai (1782)** — status quo, ~20 years' peace |
| **Second Anglo-Maratha** | 1803–05 | after the **Treaty of Bassein (1802**, subsidiary alliance with Peshwa Baji Rao II); British beat Scindia & Bhonsle (Assaye, Laswari) |
| **Third Anglo-Maratha** | 1817–18 | final crushing; **Peshwaship abolished (1818)**; Baji Rao II pensioned to **Bithur**; Satara restored as a puppet |
- **Why the Marathas fell (Mains):** a confederacy, not a unitary state; fiscal dependence on **chauth** (extraction without integration bred resentment); no naval/artillery modernisation to match the British; internal jealousies (Scindia vs Holkar). *Yet* they had been the **strongest indigenous power** and the real inheritors of Mughal India until the British.

## 5. Andhra / Deccan sheet (⭐ reuse in AP answers)

1. **1677 Karnataka expedition** launched from the **Golconda alliance** (Madanna-Akkanna) — Gingee, Vellore; Rajaram's Gingee resistance kept the Maratha state alive in the Tamil-Andhra south.
2. **Bhonsle of Nagpur** levied **chauth** across the eastern Deccan/Berar and raided toward the Coromandel — a standing pressure on the **Nizam's** Deccan and the fringes of the **Northern Circars**.
3. The **Maratha–Nizam contest** (Palkhed 1728, Bhopal 1737, later Kharda 1795) shaped the balance of power on the Deccan plateau into which the **French and English** intervened (Carnatic Wars — File 14).
4. **Institutional afterlife:** chauth-sardeshmukhi and the fort-and-cavalry model influenced Deccan revenue practice; Maratha-Peshwa administrative Marathi replaced some Persian usages in the western Deccan.

## 7. Mains angles

1. **"Shivaji was a nation-builder, not a mere freebooter."** Institutions (Ashtapradhan, forts, navy, Sanskritised administration, chauth as system not loot), the Swaraj ideology, and social base (Mavali peasant-soldiery) vs Khafi Khan's "plunderer" charge.
2. **"Chauth and sardeshmukhi explain both the reach and the limits of Maratha power."** Extraction-without-integration financed expansion but bred resentment and prevented a unitary state — the fiscal root of the confederacy's fragility.
3. **"The Peshwas turned a Deccan kingdom into a pan-Indian confederacy — and its dispersal was its doom."** Baji Rao I's expansion + the five houses; Panipat and the Anglo-Maratha wars as the price of centrifugalism.
4. **"Panipat 1761 was a Maratha defeat but not a British victory — yet it enabled one."** Analyse causes and the vacuum it left in the north for the Company.
5. **"Assess the Maratha impact on the Deccan and Andhra."** 1677 Golconda alliance & Gingee; Nagpur Bhonsle chauth on the eastern Deccan; the Maratha-Nizam balance into which the Carnatic Wars broke (bridge to File 14).
