# 20 · The Mughals — Socio-Religious Life & Cultural Developments

---

## 0. Frame

Two centuries in which a Persianate Muslim dynasty and Indian society **interpenetrated** to produce the classic **Indo-Persian (Ganga-Jamuni) high culture**: Akbar's ideology of **sulh-i-kul** turned tolerance into statecraft; the parallel rise of **Sikhism** and the maturing **Bhakti** streams reshaped religious life; and imperial patronage lifted **architecture, painting, literature and music** to a peak whose vocabulary — charbagh, pietra dura, the Mughal miniature, khayal, Rekhta/Urdu — still defines "classical" North India. The counter-swing under **Aurangzeb** (jizya, temple policy, withdrawal of music patronage) is the other half of the story — and dispersed artists seeded the **Rajput, Pahari and Deccani** schools.

# PART A — SOCIO-RELIGIOUS LIFE

## 1. Religious policy — an evolution, not a constant ⭐⭐

| Reign | Direction | Landmarks |
|---|---|---|
| **Akbar** | tolerationist → universalist | pilgrim tax abolished **1563**, **jizya abolished 1564**; **Ibadat Khana 1575**; **Mahzar 1579**; **Din-i-Ilahi 1582**; **sulh-i-kul** |
| **Jahangir** | pragmatic, mixed | broadly tolerant, Jain & Vaishnava contacts; yet **executed Guru Arjan (1606)** for blessing rebel Khusrau |
| **Shah Jahan** | mildly orthodox | some temple-demolition orders early; but **Dara Shukoh's** liberal universalism at court |
| **Aurangzeb** | orthodox reaction | **jizya re-imposed 1679**; temple demolitions; court music & festivals curbed; **Fatawa-i-Alamgiri**; executed **Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675)** |

### 1.1 Akbar's religious journey (the set-piece)
- **Ibadat Khana (House of Worship, Fatehpur Sikri, 1575):** first Muslim-only debates, **opened 1578 to Hindus, Jains, Zoroastrians, and Jesuits** (Fathers **Aquaviva & Monserrate** from Goa). Disillusion with sectarian wrangling pushed Akbar beyond orthodoxy.
- **Mahzar (1579):** drafted by **Shaikh Mubarak** (father of Abul Fazl & Faizi) — let Akbar, as a just ruler (**Imam-i-Adil / Mujtahid**), choose between conflicting ulema opinions on doubtful points. *Not* a claim to prophethood (correct a common myth) — it made the emperor the **arbiter, not the author**, of law.
- **Din-i-Ilahi / Tauhid-i-Ilahi (1582):** an ethical **order of discipleship**, not a mass religion — virtues over ritual (celibacy encouraged, vegetarianism, sun-reverence, *chalisa* etc.); disciples greeted with **"Allah-u-Akbar"/"Jalla Jalaluhu."** Barely **~18–19 followers**; **Birbal the only Hindu**. It died with Akbar — its real significance is as the emblem of **sulh-i-kul**.
- **Sulh-i-kul ("absolute/universal peace"):** the operative doctrine — the state stands **above sect**, guaranteeing all faiths; theorised by **Abul Fazl** as the duty of the "perfect man" sovereign. This, not Din-i-Ilahi, is the exam-answer core. ⭐

### 1.2 Aurangzeb's orthodoxy (balance it, don't caricature)
- **Jizya (1679), pilgrim taxes on Hindus, temple demolitions** (Kashi Vishwanath 1669, Keshava Rai Mathura), ban on court music and on Nauroz; personally austere (stitched caps, copied the Quran).
- **But:** issued **more temple-protection farmans than is popularly remembered** (e.g., grants to Balaji/Someshwar, Vrindavan, Guwahati temples — cited by B.N. Pande, R. Nath); employed the **highest-ever proportion of Hindu (esp. Maratha & Rajput) mansabdars**; the **Fatawa-i-Alamgiri** was a legal digest for the empire. *Mains line: his was a shift of state ideology from sulh-i-kul back toward Sharia-legitimacy — with political costs (Rajput, Sikh, Jat, Maratha revolts) more than a simple bigotry.*

## 2. Rise of Sikhism under Mughal rule ⭐

- **Guru Nanak (1469–1539)** — founder; *Ik Onkar*, nirguna devotion, langar, rejection of caste & ritual (Bhakti-Sant milieu — cross-ref File 12).
- The **institution-builders:** **Guru Angad** (Gurmukhi script), **Guru Amar Das** (langar, anti-sati/purdah), **Guru Ram Das** (founded **Amritsar**, dug the tank), **Guru Arjan** (built the **Harmandir Sahib/Golden Temple**; compiled the **Adi Granth, 1604** — including verses of **Kabir, Farid, Namdev, Ravidas**; **martyred by Jahangir, 1606**).
- **Militarisation:** **Guru Hargobind** — doctrine of **miri-piri** (temporal + spiritual swords), built the **Akal Takht**, kept an army. **Guru Tegh Bahadur executed by Aurangzeb (1675)** for defending Kashmiri Pandits' freedom of conscience.
- **Guru Gobind Singh** — founded the **Khalsa (Baisakhi 1699)**, the **five Ks**, *Panj Pyare*; ended the personal guruship, vesting authority in the **Guru Granth Sahib** (eternal Guru) and the **Khalsa Panth**. The Sikh–Mughal conflict feeds directly into 18th-century Punjab politics.

## 3. Society — orders, groups, everyday life

- **The nobility (umara):** a service aristocracy graded by mansab, ethnically plural (Turani, Irani, Afghan, Rajput, Indian-Muslim, later Marathas) — conspicuous consumption, no primogeniture-secured estates (escheat), hence a culture of spending, patronage and display.
- **Zamindars & peasants:** hereditary revenue-right holders above a peasantry split into privileged **khudkasht** (resident, land-owning cultivators) and **pahi-kasht** (migrant tenants); artisans (often caste-guilds) fed the karkhanas and export trade.
- **Women:** **purdah** and the **harem** among the elite (managed by eunuchs, run by senior women); yet formidable individual power — **Nur Jahan** (coins, farmans), **Jahanara** (Shah Jahan's chief lady, builder of Chandni Chowk & the Agra sarai), **Roshanara**, **Zebunnisa** (Aurangzeb's poet-daughter, "Makhfi"). **Sati** existed among Rajputs/upper castes; **Akbar discouraged it** (required consent, tried to prevent coercion) — reform by persuasion, not abolition. Widow remarriage rare among upper castes, commoner among lower.
- **Slavery** (military & domestic) continued but Akbar curbed the enslavement of war-captives.

## 4. The composite ("Ganga-Jamuni") ethos — Mains closer
> Persian became the language of power and **Urdu/Rekhta** the language of the bazaar; Rajput blood sat on the Mughal throne; **Dara Shukoh** translated the **Upanishads** as **Sirr-i-Akbar ("the Greatest Secret")** and wrote **Majma-ul-Bahrain ("Mingling of the Two Oceans")** to show Vedanta and Sufism as one; qawwali, the miniature, the charbagh and biryani all belong to no single community. The Mughal century is the classical age of Indian composite culture — a fact Aurangzeb's reaction complicates but does not erase.

# PART B — CULTURAL DEVELOPMENTS

## 5. Architecture — the Mughal style, reign by reign ⭐⭐ (high-yield)

**New grammar:** the **charbagh** (four-quartered paradise garden), **bulbous dome** on a constricted neck, slender **four-minaret** framing, **pietra dura (parchin kari)** inlay, the great **iwan** portal, and the signature progression **red sandstone → white marble**.

| Reign | Monuments | Firsts / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Babur** | mosques at **Panipat (Kabuli Bagh)** & **Sambhal**; laid out *Aram Bagh* (Ram Bagh) garden, Agra | imported the **charbagh** idea |
| **Humayun** | **DIN PANAH** (his Delhi city); **Humayun's Tomb (1560s–70s**, built by widow **Bega Begum**, architect **Mirak Mirza Ghiyas**) | **first grand Mughal garden-tomb** — the **"prototype of the Taj"**; UNESCO |
| **Akbar** | **FATEHPUR SIKRI** (Buland Darwaza — India's tallest gateway, on the Gujarat win; Panch Mahal; Diwan-i-Khas; Jodha Bai palace; **Salim Chishti's marble tomb**), **Agra Fort**, **Lahore Fort**, **Allahabad Fort**; his own **tomb at Sikandra** (begun by him, finished by Jahangir) | red-sandstone synthesis of Hindu–Islamic forms (**trabeate brackets + arcuate**) |
| **Jahangir** | **Itmad-ud-Daula's tomb (Agra, 1622–28**, by **Nur Jahan** for her father) — **first all-marble & first true pietra dura** ⭐, the "**Baby Taj**"; **Shalimar Bagh (Kashmir & Lahore)**; Akbar's Sikandra tomb completed | marble & inlay era begins; painting > building under him |
| **Shah Jahan** | **TAJ MAHAL (1632–53**, for **Mumtaz Mahal**; architect tradition **Ustad Ahmad Lahauri**; calligrapher **Amanat Khan**); **Red Fort, Jama Masjid & Shahjahanabad (Delhi)**; **Moti Masjid (Agra)**; the **Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus)** | the marble zenith; charbagh + pietra dura + perfect symmetry |
| **Aurangzeb** | **Badshahi Mosque (Lahore)**; **Bibi ka Maqbara (Aurangabad** — a lesser echo of the Taj, the "**poor man's Taj**"); **Moti Masjid** inside the Red Fort | patronage thins; decorative decline |

**Regional & successor styles to name:** **Bijapur (Gol Gumbaz), Golconda–Hyderabad (Charminar, Qutb Shahi tombs — File 17)**, Bengal do-chala roofs feeding into later temple forms, and the **Sikh (Golden Temple)** idiom.

## 6. Painting — the Mughal miniature ⭐

- **Origins:** Humayun brought the Safavid masters **Mir Sayyid Ali & Abd-us-Samad** from Persia → the imperial **tasvir-khana (atelier)**.
- **Akbar** — fusion & narrative: a large karkhana of mostly **Hindu artists** (**Daswanth, Basawan, Kesu Das**); the huge illustrated **Hamzanama, Razmnama** (Persian Mahabharata), **Akbarnama**; European perspective absorbed via Jesuit prints.
- **Jahangir — the golden age:** naturalism, portraiture, album (**muraqqa**) art; **Ustad Mansur ("Nadir-ul-Asr")** — matchless flora-and-fauna (the Siberian crane, dodo, turkey, Kashmir flowers); **Abul Hasan ("Nadir-uz-Zaman")**; **Bishandas** (portraits); **Manohar, Govardhan, Bichitr** (the famous "Jahangir preferring a Sufi to kings" allegory).
- **Shah Jahan** — formal, jewel-like, gold-heavy; the **Padshahnama** album; **decline of vitality** into court stiffness.
- **Aurangzeb** — withdrawal of patronage **scatters the artists → seeds the Rajput (Mewar, Kishangarh, Bundi), Pahari (Basohli, Kangra) and Deccani schools** ⭐ (an important continuity point).

## 7. Literature & language ⭐

### 7.1 Persian — the language of power
| Author | Work | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Abul Fazl** | ***Akbarnama*** + ***Ain-i-Akbari*** | the official history + the great administrative gazetteer |
| **Badauni** | *Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh* | the **hostile** counter-view of Akbar — read against Abul Fazl |
| **Faizi** | Persian poetry; *Nal-Daman* | Akbar's **poet-laureate (malik-ush-shu'ara)** |
| Babur (tr.) / **Gulbadan Begum** | Baburnama / ***Humayun-nama*** | Gulbadan = the rare **woman-authored** Mughal memoir ⭐ |
| **Jahangir** | *Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri* | royal memoir, naturalist's eye |
| **Abdul Hamid Lahori** | *Padshahnama* | Shah Jahan's reign |
| **Dara Shukoh** | *Majma-ul-Bahrain*; *Sirr-i-Akbar* (Upanishads) | Hindu-Muslim philosophical synthesis |
| **Khafi Khan** | *Muntakhab-ul-Lubab* | Aurangzeb's reign (written in secret) |
| **Inayat Khan / Bhimsen** | *Shahjahannama* / *Tarikh-i-Dilkasha* | Bhimsen = a Hindu munshi's view of the Deccan wars |

- **The translation bureau (Maktab Khana):** Persian renderings of the **Mahabharata (Razmnama), Ramayana, Rajatarangini, Atharvaveda, Lilavata, Panchatantra** — Akbar's two-way textual bridge (Badauni, reluctantly, worked on the Mahabharata).

### 7.2 Hindavi / Braj / the birth of Urdu
- **Braj-bhasha & Awadhi devotional poetry flowered alongside the court:** **Tulsidas** (*Ramcharitmanas*, Akbar's age), **Surdas** (*Sursagar*), **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** (the Muslim general's Hindi **dohas** — Bhakti in a courtier's voice ⭐), **Keshavdas**, **Bihari** (*Satsai*, Shah Jahan's era).
- **Rekhta / Urdu** matured out of the Delhi camp-bazaar; recall from §2 that **Dakhni** had flowered earlier at **Golconda-Bijapur** (Wajhi's *Sab Ras*) — the southern nursery of Urdu (cross-ref File 17).

## 8. Music ⭐

- **Akbar's court:** **Tansen** (of **Gwalior**; titled **Miyan**; credited with **Miyan ki Malhar, Miyan ki Todi, Darbari Kanada**), **Baiju Bawra**, **Swami Haridas** (Tansen's guru, Vrindavan) — the fountainhead of **Hindustani khayal & dhrupad**.
- **Amir Khusrau's legacy** (qawwali, the sitar/tabla tradition — Sultanate inheritance, File 10) fused further with Persian modes.
- **Aurangzeb banned court music** — yet **music treatises multiplied** in his reign (the paradox); his own tomb-town aside, the ban **pushed musicians to regional courts** (and, tradition says, prompted a mock "funeral of music" procession by Delhi's musicians). **Faqirullah's *Rag Darpan*** and other theory works date to this era.

## 9. Andhra / Deccan cultural sheet (⭐ reuse in AP answers)

1. **Composite Deccan feeds the Mughal mainstream:** when Aurangzeb annexed **Golconda (1687)**, **Deccani painting, Kalamkari-chintz, bidriware and Dakhni-Urdu** (Files 17–18) entered the imperial orbit and dispersed onward.
2. **Persian record-craft deepened in Telugu administration** (the *jilla-taluka-kacheri-dastavezu* vocabulary of File 11 hardened under Mughal-then-Nizam rule).
3. **Artist dispersal after Aurangzeb** enriched **Hyderabad's Asaf Jahi atelier** and Deccani miniature — a direct AP-relevant afterlife of Mughal painting.
4. **Sikh-style militant devotion & Mughal-Maratha-Sikh conflicts** frame the 18th-century order into which the **Nizam (1724)** and the **Europeans** (File 22) stepped on the Andhra coast.

## 10. Prelims nuggets

1. Jizya abolished **1564** (Akbar) → re-imposed **1679** (Aurangzeb).
2. **Ibadat Khana 1575**; **Mahzar 1579** (Shaikh Mubarak) — emperor as arbiter, *not* prophet.
3. **Din-i-Ilahi/Tauhid-i-Ilahi 1582**; ~18–19 followers; **Birbal** the only Hindu; real doctrine = **sulh-i-kul**.
4. Guru **Arjan** compiled Adi Granth **1604**, martyred **1606** (Jahangir); Guru **Tegh Bahadur** executed **1675** (Aurangzeb); **Khalsa 1699** (Gobind Singh).
5. **Humayun's Tomb** = first grand charbagh tomb (prototype of Taj); **Itmad-ud-Daula's tomb** = first full-marble + first pietra dura ("Baby Taj," by Nur Jahan).
6. **Taj Mahal 1632–53**; Ustad Ahmad Lahauri; calligrapher Amanat Khan; **Buland Darwaza** = Gujarat victory.
7. **Bibi ka Maqbara** (Aurangabad) = "poor man's Taj"; **Badshahi Mosque** (Lahore) — Aurangzeb.
8. **Mansur** ("Nadir-ul-Asr") flora-fauna, **Abul Hasan** ("Nadir-uz-Zaman") — Jahangir's painters.
9. **Ain-i-Akbari & Akbarnama** — Abul Fazl; **Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh** — Badauni; **Humayun-nama** — **Gulbadan Begum**.
10. **Dara Shukoh:** *Majma-ul-Bahrain*, *Sirr-i-Akbar* (Upanishads in Persian).
11. **Tansen** — Gwalior; Darbari Kanada, Miyan ki Malhar; **Aurangzeb banned court music** (but treatises flourished).
12. Rahim's Hindi dohas; Tulsidas' *Ramcharitmanas*; Surdas' *Sursagar* — Braj/Awadhi Bhakti in the Mughal age.

## 11. Mains angles

1. **"Sulh-i-kul, not Din-i-Ilahi, was Akbar's real religious achievement."** Show Din-i-Ilahi as a tiny order; sulh-i-kul as the durable state ideology co-opting Rajputs/Hindus — the ideological cement of the empire.
2. **"Aurangzeb reversed the ideology but not the composite reality of Mughal India."** Jizya/temple policy & the political costs vs the surviving Hindu nobility, temple farmans, and unbroken composite culture; weigh the caricature against the evidence.
3. **"Trace the evolution of Mughal architecture from Humayun's Tomb to the Taj and beyond."** Red-sandstone→marble, charbagh, pietra dura, the four-minaret plan; end with Aurangzeb's thinning patronage and the regional afterlife.
4. **"Jahangir's reign was the golden age of the brush as Shah Jahan's was of the trowel."** Contrast painting's naturalism (Mansur, Abul Hasan) with architecture's marble climax; note artist-dispersal under Aurangzeb into Rajput/Pahari/Deccani schools.
5. **"The Mughal court was a workshop of composite culture."** Persian histories + Braj Bhakti + Dara's Upanishads + Tansen's khayal + Urdu's birth; close with the Deccan/Andhra afterlife (File 17–18) to earn the AP tie-in.
