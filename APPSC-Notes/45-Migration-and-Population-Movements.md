## 1. Concepts and theory (open answers with one of these)

- **Push factors** (drought, landlessness, unemployment, conflict) vs **pull factors** (jobs, wages, education, city lights); **Ravenstein's laws** and **Lee's model** (origin/destination factors + **intervening obstacles** + personal factors).
- **Types:** internal (intra-district / inter-district / inter-state) vs international; by streams — **rural→rural, rural→urban, urban→urban, urban→rural**; by duration — permanent, **seasonal/circular** (the Indian speciality), commuting.
- Data sources: Census D-tables (place of birth / last residence), NSS, and the Economic Survey 2016-17's railway-flow estimate (~90 lakh inter-state moves a year — far above census counts) ⭐.

### 1.1 The theory bank ⭐ (one line each is enough — but *name* the theorist)

| Theorist / model | The claim worth quoting |
|---|---|
| **E.G. Ravenstein**, *Laws of Migration* (1885) ⭐⭐ | Most migrants move **short distances**; movement is **step-wise** (village → town → city); **every stream generates a counter-stream**; long-distance migrants head for the **great commercial centres**; **town-dwellers migrate less than rural people**; **females dominate short-distance** moves, males long-distance; migration grows with **transport, industry and commerce**; the **dominant motive is economic** |
| **Everett Lee** (1966) | Push–pull re-cast as **origin (+/−) → intervening obstacles → destination (+/−)**, filtered by **personal factors** — the safest frame to draw as a diagram |
| **W. Arthur Lewis** (dual-sector) | Transfer of **surplus labour** from a low-productivity farm sector to a high-productivity modern sector = the engine of structural change |
| **Michael Todaro / Harris–Todaro** | People migrate on **expected** (not actual) urban income — which is why migration continues **despite urban unemployment** ⭐ (the best single explanation of Indian slum growth) |
| **Wilbur Zelinsky** (mobility transition, 1971) | Mobility passes through stages alongside the demographic transition — rural→urban peaks in the transitional phase, then urban→urban and circulation dominate (India is here now) |
| **Stouffer** (intervening opportunities) | Migration is proportional to opportunities at the destination and **inversely to opportunities in between** |
| **New Economics of Labour Migration** (Stark) | The migrating unit is the **household**, not the individual — migration is a **risk-diversification and remittance strategy**, which is exactly what AP's Rayalaseema circular migration looks like ⭐ |

### 1.2 How migration is measured (the caveat paragraph that earns marks)

- Census counts by **place of birth (POB)** and **place of last residence (POLR)** — POLR is the wider, preferred measure; **duration of residence** separates recent (0–9 yrs) from lifetime migrants.
- Derived measures: **in-migration, out-migration, net migration (I − O)**, **migration turnover (I + O)** and **migration effectiveness**.
- **Why every number understates reality ⭐:** the census misses **seasonal/circular and commuting** moves (a 3–6 month brick-kiln or harvest move leaves no "usual residence" trace); it is decadal and now **14 years stale**; NSS-PLFS's *Migration in India* rounds and the **e-Shram registry (30 cr+ unorganised workers, verify latest)** are the interim substitutes. Always write "census-counted migration" rather than "migration".

## 2. Migration in India — the facts (Census 2011; verify 2027)

- **45.6 cr internal migrants ≈ 37–38% of Indians** ⭐; but most moves are **short-distance and female**: **marriage is the single largest stated reason** (~70% of female migration); work dominates male migration.
- **Streams:** rural→rural is the largest stream overall (marriage + farm labour); **rural→urban ≈ one-fifth but carries the development story**; the classic **corridors: UP–Bihar → Delhi NCR, Maharashtra (Mumbai), Gujarat, Punjab** ⭐; south-bound streams (Odisha/Bihar → Kerala-TN-Karnataka) grew fastest recently.
- **Urban migration (the syllabus term):** fuels city growth alongside natural increase and reclassification; consequences — remittance economies at origin, **slums & informal work at destination (6.5 cr slum residents, 2011)**, feminisation of agriculture at origin ⭐, skill drain vs "brain gain."
- **COVID-19 reverse migration (2020)** exposed the invisibility of circular migrants → policy responses: **e-Shram registry, One Nation One Ration Card (portable PDS)** ⭐, affordable rental housing (ARHC).
- **International:** **the world's largest diaspora (~3.5 cr)** and **the world's top remittance recipient (~$125–130 bn)** ⭐; Gulf corridor for labour (Kerala model), North America for skills; historical layer — **19th-century indentured "Girmitiya" migration** (Mauritius, Fiji, Caribbean).

### 2.1 The composition table ⭐ (Census 2011, by last residence — quote proportions, not decimals)

| Cut | Split | The line to write |
|---|---|---|
| **By distance** | **~62% intra-district**, ~26% inter-district within the state, **~12% inter-state**; international <1% | "Indian migration is overwhelmingly **local** — Ravenstein's first law holds" ⭐ |
| **By stream** | **rural→rural largest** (~half), rural→urban ~1/5, urban→urban ~1/8, urban→rural smallest | The development story sits in the *smaller* stream |
| **By sex** | Women are **~68–70% of all migrants** | Because **marriage** is counted as migration — a statistical artefact with real social content |
| **By reason (all migrants)** | **Marriage ~49%**, moved with household ~15%, **work/employment ~10%**, education ~1%, business ~1% | Split it by sex: **work dominates male** migration; **marriage dominates female** migration ⭐ |
| **Net receivers vs senders** | Receivers: **Maharashtra, Delhi, Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, TN**; senders: **UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, MP, Odisha, Jharkhand** | The "**BIMARU→west/south**" axis of India's labour market |

## 3. Population movements in and out of Andhra Pradesh ⭐⭐ (make answers AP-specific with these)

| Stream | Pattern | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Rayalaseema → Bengaluru/Chennai** | Drought-push circular & permanent labour (construction, services) | Anantapur–Chittoor's rainfall lottery (File 18) exports people, not groundnuts, in bad years |
| **Coastal Andhra → Hyderabad (pre-2014)** | Educated/professional + business migration to the joint capital | Post-bifurcation AP lost its own biggest magnet — **the "loss of Hyderabad" migration reversal is a Part-B PYQ theme** ⭐⭐ |
| **Andhra → USA / abroad** | Skill migration (IT, medicine); **Telugu among the fastest-growing languages in the US** ⭐ | Remittance-and-real-estate effects in the deltas & Amaravati region |
| **Gulf pockets** | Kadapa, East Godavari and north-coastal labourers to the Gulf | Smaller than Kerala's but locally significant; welfare of Gulf returnees is a live policy item |
| **Srikakulam fishermen → Gujarat/Veraval boats** | Documented seasonal marine-crew migration ⭐ | Ties fisheries (File 40) to migration; cyclone/livelihood push |
| **Into the agency** | Non-tribal in-migration into Scheduled Areas | Land alienation → **LTR 1 of 70 (1970)** protection, Samata backdrop (File 19) ⭐ |
| **Into coastal AP** | Odisha/other labour into aqua, mills, construction | Counter-stream proof (Ravenstein) |
| **Displacement (forced movement)** | **Polavaram submergence R&R** (~lakh-scale, largely tribal) ⭐; capital-region land pooling | "Development-induced displacement" is the balancing paragraph of any migration answer |

- **Intra-AP drift:** delta and Rayalaseema villagers → Vijayawada–Guntur–Visakhapatnam–Tirupati; AP's urbanisation (File 46) is migration-led in the corridor cities but reclassification-led in the deltas (census towns).
- **Post-2014 dynamic for Mains:** bifurcation converted an *intra-state* stream (to Hyderabad) into an *inter-state* one — AP's challenge is building pull centres (Amaravati, Vizag) faster than its youth leave; the demographic-dividend clock (File 44) makes this urgent.

## 4. Consequences & policy checklist (works for any migration answer)

- **Economic:** remittances (household floor), labour-market matching across the staggered dividend (File 44), but origin-area skill drain.
- **Social:** women left behind / feminised farms; children's schooling breaks (seasonal hostels); urban integration & nativism frictions.
- **Demographic:** youthful destinations, ageing origins (AP!); urban sex ratios skew male.
- **Policy:** portability (ONORC, e-Shram), inclusive city housing, seasonal-migrant schooling, destination-state language bridges, diaspora engagement (PBD, OCI), R&R justice for displacement.

## 5. The legal & policy architecture ⭐ (the "way forward" half of any migration answer)

| Instrument | Year | What it does / why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Inter-State Migrant Workmen (RE&CS) Act** | **1979** | Registration of contractors, displacement allowance, journey fare, equal wages — **almost wholly unimplemented**; now subsumed into the **Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions Code, 2020** ⭐ |
| **Unorganised Workers' Social Security Act** | 2008 | The statutory hook on which **e-Shram** hangs |
| **Working Group on Migration (MoHUA)** | **2017** | The reference report — recommended portable entitlements, rental housing, migrant-inclusive city planning ⭐ |
| **One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC)** | 2019→ | **Portable PDS** across states — the single biggest post-COVID gain for circular migrants |
| **e-Shram** | **2021** | National database of unorganised/migrant workers — a prerequisite for portable welfare |
| **Affordable Rental Housing Complexes (ARHC)** | 2020 | Converts vacant public housing into rental stock for urban migrants (PMAY-U sub-scheme) |
| **Draft National Policy on Migrant Workers (NITI Aayog)** | **2021** | Proposes a **rights-based** (not handout-based) framework, migration resource centres, inter-state coordination ⭐ |
| **Emigration Act / e-Migrate; Pravasi Bharatiya Bima Yojana** | 1983 / 2015 | Regulates recruitment for overseas employment — the Gulf-corridor protection layer (relevant to AP's Gulf pockets) |

**AP-specific hooks:** APNRT Society (NRI/Gulf-returnee assistance), **AP Building & Other Construction Workers Welfare Board** cess funds, seasonal hostels/residential schools in migration-prone Rayalaseema mandals, and **Polavaram R&R under the LARR Act 2013** — the last is where AP's migration answer meets its rehabilitation obligation.
