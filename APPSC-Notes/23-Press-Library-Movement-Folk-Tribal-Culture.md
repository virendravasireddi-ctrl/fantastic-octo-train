## 0. Frame

Three connected engines carried the awakening (File 22) to the villages: the **Telugu newspaper** (reform → nation → linguistic province), the **GRANDHALAYA (library) movement** — India's most organised, with its own association (1914) and journal (1916) — and the deep reservoir of **folk, tribal and subaltern culture** through which ordinary Telugus remembered Bobbili and Palnadu, sang Burrakatha, and eventually heard nationalism in their own idiom. **2020 Q8(a) asked the library movement straight.**

---

## 1. Role of the newspaper ⭐⭐

### 1.1 The landmark papers (match-pairs are exam gold)

| Paper | Year & place | Founder/editor | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Satyadoota*** | 1835, Bellary (mission) | missionaries | often counted the **first Telugu periodical** |
| ***Vartamana Tarangini*** | 1842, Madras | Puvvada Venkata Rao | early regular Telugu weekly |
| ***Vivekavardhini*** | **1874, Rajahmundry** | **Veeresalingam** | reform journalism born (File 22) |
| ***Krishna Patrika*** | **1902, Machilipatnam** | **Konda Venkatappayya** (founder); **Mutnuri Krishna Rao** (editor 1907–49) | nationalist conscience of the Krishna delta; Mutnuri's editorials = political education |
| ***Andhra Patrika*** | **1908 Bombay → 1914 Madras (daily)** | **KASINATHUNI NAGESWARA RAO ("Viswadata"/"Desoddharaka")** | THE paper of the Andhra movement; its **1913 annual number** argued the case for a separate Andhra; Amrutanjan profits funded it |
| ***Swarajya*** | 1922, Madras (Telugu/English/Tamil daily) | **T. PRAKASAM** | fire-breathing national daily; earlier a weekly *Swarajya* (c. 1908) cost **GADICHERLA HARISARVOTTAMA RAO** a sedition sentence — the first Telugu editor jailed for nationalism |
| ***Bharati*** | 1924 (monthly) | Nageswara Rao's house | literary-cultural monthly of record |
| ***Zamin Ryot*** | 1930, Nellore | N. Venkatarama Naidu | peasant/anti-zamindari advocacy |
| ***Golconda Patrika*** | 1926, Hyderabad | Suravaram Pratapa Reddy | the Telangana counterpart (context only) |
| ***Andhra Prabha*** | 1938 | Indian Express group | mass-circulation daily era |

### 1.2 What the press did (answer plan)
1. **Made Telugu public opinion** — reform (1870s–90s), Vandemataram swadeshi (1907), Home Rule, Gandhian campaigns (each paper a recruiting office).
2. **Made the Andhra identity:** *Andhra Patrika*'s special numbers, *Krishna Patrika*'s polemics — the demand for a **separate Andhra province** was argued in print before it was voted in conferences (File 24).
3. **Made modern Telugu prose:** vyavaharika bhasha won in the newspaper column first (Gidugu's allies were editors).
4. **Paid the price:** Vernacular Press Act 1878, sedition prosecutions (Gadicherla), securities and bans (Quit India-era censorship).

## 2. The Library (Grandhalaya) Movement ⭐⭐ (2020 Q8(a))

### 2.1 Growth
- **Roots (1870s–1900s):** town reading rooms; **Gautami Grandhalayam, Rajahmundry (1898)**; **Sri Krishnadevaraya Andhra Bhasha Nilayam, Hyderabad (1901)** — a Telugu library inside the Nizam's dominion; **Saraswata Niketanam, Vetapalem (1918)** — "the Vetapalem library" famous for rare books; **Ram Mohan Library, Vijayawada** — the movement's engine-room.
- **Organisation (the movement proper):** first **Andhra Desa Library Conference, Vijayawada, 1914** → **Andhra Desa Grandhabhandagara Sangham (Andhra Library Association), 1914 — the first provincial library association in India**; journal ***GRANDHALAYA SARVASVAMU* (1916)** — India's oldest library-science journal; **All India Public Libraries Association (1919)** grew from the same circle.
- **Method:** travelling/village libraries, night schools and **adult literacy (vayojana vidya)**, ladies' sections, Harijan access, reading festivals (Grandhalaya varotsavalu); libraries doubled as **national-movement cells** — Congress literature, khadi, discussion.
- **Climax:** the campaign for legislation matured (with S.R. Ranganathan's librarianship) in the **Madras Public Libraries Act, 1948 — India's first library law**; united AP later passed its own **Public Libraries Act (1960)**.

### 2.2 The three grandhalaya men (with Potti Sreeramulu's generation they reappear in Files 24–25)
- **AYYANKI VENKATA RAMANAYYA (1890–1979) — "Grandhalayodyama PITAMAHA":** secretary of the Ram Mohan Library; convened the 1914 conference; founded the Association and edited *Grandhalaya Sarvasvamu*; drove the all-India association and library legislation for six decades.
- **GADICHERLA HARISARVOTTAMA RAO (1883–1960):** the jailed editor (§1.1) turned **librarian-general of Andhra** — led the Andhra (later AP) Library Association, adult-education apostle, and a front-rank Andhra-movement leader (File 24).
- **KASINATHUNI NAGESWARA RAO (1867–1938):** financier-philanthropist of the whole ecosystem — *Andhra Patrika*, *Bharati*, **Andhra Grandhamala** cheap-classics series; his Madras home "**Sribagh**" hosted the **1937 Sribagh Pact** (File 25) ⭐.
- Supporting cast: **Vignana Chandrika Grandha Mandali (1906, Komarraju Venkata Lakshmana Rao)** — published Telugu histories and science, began the **Andhra Vignana Sarvasvam** encyclopaedia; **Vavilla Ramaswamy Sastrulu & Sons** — classic Telugu publishing.
- **Mains line (2020):** growth (reading room → association → legislation) + functions (literacy, women, Harijan access, nationalism) + names (Ayyanki, Gadicherla, Nageswara Rao) + verdict: "the library was the village's Congress office, night school and public sphere in one."

## 3. Folk culture & oral traditions ⭐

### 3.1 Performing arts (form → community/region pairs)
- **HARIKATHA:** devotional story-song-dance; **Ajjada Adibhatla Narayana Dasu (1864–1945)** of Vizianagaram — "**Harikatha Pitamaha**", polyglot composer ("Sangeeta Sahitya Sarvabhauma").
- **BURRAKATHA:** two-drone (tambura + burra) narrative trio; modernised by the **Praja Natya Mandali (1940s)** — **Sheik Nazar ("Burrakatha Pitamaha")**; carried nationalist and left messages village to village.
- **TOLU BOMMALATA:** leather shadow-puppetry (Ramayana/Bharata cycles) — hereditary troupes.
- **VEEDHI NATAKAM / VEEDHI BHAGAVATAM:** street theatre; **Chindu Bhagavatam** — performed by the **Madiga-associated Chindu** community (subaltern art, §4).
- **PAGATI VESHALU** (daytime guises), **Kolatam** (stick dance), **Butta Bommalu** (basket effigies, W. Godavari), **Garagalu** (pot dance, E. Godavari), **Tappeta Gullu** (drum dance, Srikakulam), **Veeranatyam** (Veerabhadra cult), **Dappu** percussion.
- **Oral epics/ballads:** **Palnati Veerula Katha** (Palnadu war — sung by Mala/picchuka-kuntla bards), **Katamaraju Katha** (cattle-herder epic, Nellore), **Bobbili Yuddha Katha** (1757, File 21), Desingu Raja Katha — history as the people remembered it.
- **Songs of work and home:** field, boat (Godavari **"O laila"** rowing songs), grinding and lullaby songs; wedding **suvvi** songs; **samethalu** (proverbs) and **podupu kathalu** (riddles) — the syllabus's "oral traditions".

### 3.2 Tribal culture (AP's scheduled tribes ≈ 5% of population; Eastern Ghats belt)
- **Major tribes:** **Chenchus** (Nallamala food-gatherers; priests-kin of Srisailam — Chenchu Lakshmi legend), **Koyas & Konda Reddis** (Godavari valley; podu cultivation, Kommu horn dance), **Savaras/Soras, Jatapus, Gadabas** (Srikakulam-Vizianagaram; Gidugu's Savara work — File 22), **Bagatas, Valmikis, Kondadoras, Kotias** (Araku — **DHIMSA** dance ⭐), **Yanadis & Yerukalas** (plains; Yerukala women famed as fortune-tellers), **Sugalis/Lambadas**.
- **Institutions of culture:** village-goddess **jataras**, seasonal festivals (e.g., Chenchu and Koya hunting-harvest rites), megalith-memory, tribal dormitory traditions among some groups; **ITDA** (Paderu, Rampachodavaram, Seethampeta…) as the modern administrative frame (Samata 1997 — File 19 tie-in).
- **Tribal protest as culture:** the fituri tradition of the Rampa-Gudem hills (1879 Rampa rising → Alluri 1922–24, File 24) lives in Koya songs — resistance remembered orally.

### 3.3 Subaltern culture ⭐ (definition + Andhra examples)
- **Concept:** *subaltern* (Gramsci) = groups outside the power bloc; **Subaltern Studies (Ranajit Guha, 1982)** read Indian history "from below" — peasants, tribals, Dalits, women as makers of their own politics.
- **Andhra materials for such a reading:**
  1. **Caste-bard genres:** every major caste had dependent bardic castes (Chindu, Dakkali, Baindla, Pichchukuntla…) singing caste puranas — e.g., the **Jamba Puranam** for Madigas — parallel sacred histories that dignify "untouchable" origins.
  2. **Folk epics** (§3.1) where the heroes are herders (Katamaraju), women (Sanyasamma), or defeated little kings (Bobbili) — not Sanskritic gods.
  3. **Dalit assertion:** mission-era literacy (File 22) → the **ADI-ANDHRA movement** — first **Adi-Andhra Mahajana Sabha, Bezwada 1917** (with Bhagya Reddy Varma of Hyderabad's Adi-Hindu movement in the wider stream); "Adi-Andhra" became a census identity (1921); temple-entry and Harijan campaigns of the 1930s (Gandhi's Andhra tours; Potti Sreeramulu's Nellore temple-entry fasts, 1946).
  4. **Subaltern nationalism:** Burrakatha troupes, Palnadu forest satyagraha's peasant volunteers, Alluri's Koya army — nationalism translated into subaltern idiom.
- **Mains line:** "In Andhra the archive of the subaltern is sung, not written — the ballad, the caste purana and the jatara carry the histories the colonial record ignored."
