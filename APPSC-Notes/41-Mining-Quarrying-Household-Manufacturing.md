## 1. Mining in the economy — India frame

- Mining & quarrying ≈ **2–2.5% of GVA**; India produces ~95 minerals; **fuel minerals ≈ two-thirds of mineral value**; **Odisha is the top mineral-value state** (chromite-iron-bauxite-coal), with Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan.
- **Legal frame:** MMDR Act 1957 — **major minerals** (Centre) vs **minor minerals** (state concession rules: granite, sand, gravel, building stone → this is "quarrying"); **2015 amendment — compulsory auctions + District Mineral Foundation (DMF/PMKKKY)** for mining-affected people ⭐; 2021 (captive-merchant liberalisation); **2023 — exploration licence + 24 "critical minerals"** (lithium, cobalt, REEs…); Offshore Areas Mineral Act amended 2023; **National Critical Mineral Mission (2025)** ⭐ current affairs.
- National Mineral Policy 2019; Star-rating of mines; issues — illegal mining, forest/tribal overlap (**Samata judgment 1997**, PESA — see File 19), DMF under-utilisation.

## 3. Quarrying — the "minor minerals" economy

- **What counts:** building stone, granite, marble, sand, gravel, morrum, slate, ordinary clay — regulated by **state** minor-mineral rules; a huge informal employer.
- **AP's signature quarries ⭐ (match-pairs):**
  - **Black Galaxy granite — Chimakurthy (Prakasam)**: world-famous export variety (East Asia); Srikakulam blue/red granites.
  - **Betamcherla–Bethamcherla (Nandyal)**: Kurnool limestone **flooring slabs & polishing industry**; "Cuddapah slabs"/**Napa slabs** from the Kadapa basin.
  - **MARKAPUR (Prakasam) — roofing slates & slate pencils** ⭐ (one of the few slate-pencil centres anywhere).
  - **Allagadda (Nandyal)** stone-carving cluster (links to §5 crafts); **Durgi (Palnadu)** soft-limestone sculpture.
  - River-sand reaches of the Godavari–Krishna — recurring **sand-policy** controversies (free-sand vs auction models).
- **Issues:** dust/silicosis, riverbed degradation, child/bonded labour risks in informal quarries, royalty leakage — pair any "quarrying" answer with **DMF** and mine-rehabilitation.

## 4. Household manufacturing — concept and national frame

- **Definition:** production within the household with family labour and traditional tools (own-account enterprises) — the classic **cottage/village industry**; the bridge between the primary sector and factory industry; overwhelmingly **unorganised, rural, female**.
- **Scale:** handlooms + handicrafts are India's biggest rural employer after agriculture; **4th All-India Handloom Census (2019-20): ~35 lakh handloom workers, ~72% women, ~88% rural** ⭐; India weaves ~**95% of the world's hand-woven fabric**.
- **Institutions & schemes:** KVIC (khadi), Handloom & Handicraft boards, **National Handloom Day — 7 August (Swadeshi movement, 1905)** ⭐, GI tags as market armour, SFURTI clusters, ODOP, **PM Vishwakarma (2023, ₹13,000 cr, 18 traditional trades)** ⭐.
- **Why it matters analytically (Mains):** low capital per job, women's incomes, dispersed (counters regional inequality), culture-carrying — but squeezed by powerlooms, credit, design & marketing gaps; GI + e-commerce + cluster infrastructure is the standard "way forward".

### 5.3 Allied household industries

- **Sericulture:** India = world #2 silk producer; **AP #2 state after Karnataka** ⭐ — mulberry belt at **Hindupur–Madakasira–Dharmavaram** (Rayalaseema), feeding Dharmavaram looms.
- **Coir:** **Konaseema–East Godavari cluster (Ambajipeta)** — among the largest outside Kerala.
- **Jaggery-making** (Anakapalli), **palm products**, agency **bamboo craft & hill brooms**, **APCO** as the state handloom marketing cooperative; weaver welfare schemes (pension/thrift — names change with governments; verify).

**Mains close:** AP's craft map is really a *geography of specialised villages* — one village, one product, now formalised by GI tags; the policy task is converting heritage into livelihoods (design, credit, e-marketing, cluster common facilities) before powerloom and plastic substitutes hollow them out.
