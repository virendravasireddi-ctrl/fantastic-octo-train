# 10 · The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)

[← South Indian Dynasties](09-South-Indian-Dynasties.md) · [Index](README.md) · [Next: Advent of Islam & its Impact →](11-Advent-of-Islam-and-Impact.md)

---

## 0. Frame

Five successive dynasties ruled from Delhi for 320 years, creating India's first durable **Turko-Afghan state**: a new ruling class, the **iqta** revenue system, a standing army, Persian court culture, **Indo-Islamic architecture**, and — decisive for Andhra — the hammer-blows that ended the **Kakatiyas (1323)** and triggered the **Vijayanagara–Bahmani** era.

| Dynasty | Years | Founder | Key rulers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mamluk ("Slave")** | 1206–1290 | Qutbuddin Aibak | Iltutmish, Razia, Balban |
| **Khalji** | 1290–1320 | Jalaluddin Khalji | **Alauddin Khalji** |
| **Tughlaq** | 1320–1414 | Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq | **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**, Firoz Shah |
| **Sayyid** | 1414–1451 | Khizr Khan | Mubarak Shah |
| **Lodi** (first Afghan) | 1451–1526 | Bahlul Lodi | Sikandar, Ibrahim (Panipat 1526) |

## 1. Mamluk dynasty (1206–1290)

### Qutbuddin Aibak (1206–1210)
- Ghori's slave-general; capital **Lahore**; never got formal manumission-investiture in time — hence "Slave" tag.
- Title **"Lakh Bakhsh"** (giver of lakhs); began **Qutub Minar** (after Sufi **Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki**) and built **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque (Delhi)** and **Adhai Din ka Jhonpra (Ajmer)**.
- Died in a **chaugan (polo)** fall at Lahore (1210).

### Iltutmish (1211–1236) — *real founder of the Sultanate*
- Shifted capital **Lahore → Delhi**; got **investiture from the Abbasid Caliph (1229)** — legitimacy.
- Created the **Turkan-i-Chihalgani ("The Forty")** nobility and the **IQTA system** (transferable revenue assignments to officers/muqtis).
- **Coinage:** pure **silver TANKA + copper JITAL** — the Sultanate's monetary backbone.
- Saved India from Chingiz Khan (1221) by **refusing asylum** to Khwarizm prince Jalaluddin Mangbarni.
- Completed Qutub Minar; built **Sultan Ghari** (for son Nasiruddin — **first Islamic tomb in India**); nominated daughter **Razia**.

### Razia Sultan (1236–1240)
- **Only woman sultan of Delhi**; discarded purdah, held open court; promoted the Abyssinian **Malik Yaqut** (Amir-i-Akhur) → Turkish nobles' revolt (Altunia); killed 1240. Shows the Forty's stranglehold on the crown.

### Ghiyasuddin Balban (1266–1287)
- Served as Naib to Nasiruddin Mahmud (1246–66) before ruling.
- **Theory of kingship:** sultan as **Zil-i-Ilahi (shadow of God)**; claimed descent from Afrasiyab; imposed **sijda** (prostration) & **paibos** (kissing feet); Persian ceremonial (Nauroz).
- **"Blood and iron"** against Mewatis and rebels; **destroyed the Chihalgani**; strong frontier defence vs Mongols (son Muhammad, the "Martyr Prince", died fighting them in 1285 — his court sheltered the young **Amir Khusrau**).
- Created a strong central army (diwan-i-arz reorganised under his watch); justice as royal duty.

## 2. Khalji dynasty (1290–1320) — the **"Khalji Revolution"**

(= end of the Turkish racial monopoly of high office; careers opened to Indian Muslims and non-Turks.)

### Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316) — the Sultanate's centraliser
**Conquests**
- North: **Gujarat (1299–1300)**, **Ranthambhor (1301** — Hammiradeva), **Chittor (1303** — the Padmini story comes from **Jayasi's *Padmavat*, 1540**, not contemporary record), **Malwa (1305)**.
- **Deccan (via MALIK KAFUR, the "Hazar-Dinari" slave-general):** Devagiri (1307–08, Yadava Ramachandra), **WARANGAL (1309–10) — Kakatiya Prataparudra II submitted, paying enormous tribute (tradition says including the Koh-i-noor)** ⭐ AP-relevant; Dwarasamudra (1310–11, Hoysala Ballala III); Madurai (1311, Pandya succession dispute). Policy in the south: **tribute, not annexation**.
- **Mongols** repulsed repeatedly (Kili 1299 — general Zafar Khan martyred; Amroha 1305); frontier forts + standing army.

**Reforms (⭐ most-asked block)**
| Sphere | Measures |
|---|---|
| **Army** | First **permanent standing army** paid in **cash**; **dagh** (horse branding) & **chehra/huliya** (descriptive muster roll) |
| **Market control** | Fixed prices for grain, cloth, horses, slaves; four regulated markets; officers — **Shahna-i-Mandi** (market superintendent), **Diwan-i-Riyasat**; **Sarai-i-Adl** (cloth market); state **granaries**; rationing in famine; spies (**barid, munhi**) reported daily. Objective: cheap, large army + curb on hoarders (Barani's account). |
| **Land revenue** | **Kharaj at ½ of produce** in the Doab, assessed by **measurement (masahat)**; cut the perquisites of **khuts, muqaddams, chaudharis** (rural intermediaries); new arrears department **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj** |
| **Nobility control** | Ban on private assemblies & inter-marriage of nobles without leave; prohibition of wine; confiscation of religious land grants; espionage network |
- Coins styled him **"Sikandar-i-Sani"** (Second Alexander); patron of **Amir Khusrau**; built **Alai Darwaza, Siri fort, Hauz-i-Alai (Hauz Khas tank)**; began the (never-finished) Alai Minar.

### End of the line
- Mubarak Khalji; then the usurper **Khusrau Khan** (1320), overthrown by Ghazi Malik = **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq**.

## 3. Tughlaq dynasty (1320–1414)

### Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (1320–1325)
- Built **Tughlaqabad**; restored order; sent son Ulugh Khan south: **1323 — FALL OF WARANGAL; Kakatiya kingdom annexed as "Sultanpur"; Prataparudra II died (tradition: by suicide on the Narmada bank while being taken to Delhi)** — ⭐ the pivotal date of medieval Andhra history.

### Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325–1351) — "a mixture of opposites" (Barani's portrait)
The most learned sultan (mathematics, medicine, philosophy, Persian poetry) whose five projects mostly failed:
1. **Doab tax enhancement** — raised kharaj in a famine cycle → flight of peasants, rebellion.
2. **Capital transfer Delhi → Daulatabad (Devagiri), 1327** — strategically rational (centring the Deccan) but forced mass migration; abandoned. **Unintended legacy: a Persianate-Sufi cultural corridor to the Deccan** (Chishtis at Khuldabad/Daulatabad; roots of Dakhni Urdu). ⭐
3. **Token currency (1329–30)** — bronze/copper tokens valued at the silver tanka; mass forgery ("every Hindu household a mint" — Barani); withdrawn with exchange honoured. (Edward Thomas still called him "**prince of moneyers**".)
4. **Khurasan expedition** — army raised & paid, never marched.
5. **Qarachil (Kumaon-Himalaya) expedition** — destroyed by terrain/disease.
- Innovations: **Diwan-i-Amir-Kohi** (agriculture department; **sondhar** advance loans ≈ ancestor of taqavi), rotating crops experiment; employment of Indian converts & even Hindus in service.
- **Rebellions everywhere:** **1336 — VIJAYANAGARA founded (Harihara & Bukka, guided by Vidyaranya); 1336 — MUSUNURI Kapaya Nayaka retook Warangal from the Sultanate; 1347 — BAHMANI kingdom founded (Alauddin Bahman Shah / Hasan Gangu) at Gulbarga.** ⭐ The whole later history of the Telugu country flows from these three dates.
- **Ibn Battuta** (Moroccan traveller, in India 1333–45) served him as **Qazi of Delhi**; his **Rehla** describes MBT's court, the efficient **postal system (uluq — horse relay; dawa — foot-runners)**, and the token-currency fiasco.

### Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351–1388) — the welfare-and-orthodoxy sultan
- **Public works:** five **canals** (Yamuna–Hisar the most famous), ~300 new towns claimed — **Hisar, Firozabad (Firoz Shah Kotla), Jaunpur, Fatehabad**; 200 sarais; repaired Qutub Minar's top storeys; **transported two Ashokan pillars** (Topra → Delhi Kotla; Meerut → Delhi Ridge).
- **Welfare:** **Diwan-i-Khairat** (marriage aid for poor girls), **Dar-ul-Shifa** (free hospital), **employment bureau**, pensions.
- **Orthodoxy & structural rot:** ruled by Sharia taxes only — **kharaj, zakat, jizya, khams** (+ **haqq-i-sharb** irrigation cess); **first to levy JIZYA on Brahmins**; persecuted "heresies"; made **iqtas and offices hereditary**; kept **180,000 slaves (Diwan-i-Bandagan)** — both accelerated decline. Patron of **Barani** (*Tarikh-i-Firozshahi*, *Fatawa-i-Jahandari*) and **Afif**; his own memoir **Futuhat-i-Firozshahi**; had Sanskrit works from Nagarkot translated (*Dalail-i-Firozshahi*).

### Collapse
- Succession wars; **TIMUR sacked Delhi (1398)** under Nasiruddin Mahmud — massacre, artisans deported to Samarkand; the Sultanate shrank to a city-state ("the rule of the Lord of the World extends from Delhi to Palam").

## 4. Sayyids (1414–1451) & Lodis (1451–1526)

- **Khizr Khan** (Timur's deputy) ruled as **"Rayat-i-Ala"**, never as full sultan; **Mubarak Shah** (patron of **Yahya Sirhindi**, *Tarikh-i-Mubarakshahi*); last Sayyid **Alam Shah** retired to Badaun.
- **Bahlul Lodi**: first **Afghan** dynasty; absorbed Jaunpur (Sharqis).
- **Sikandar Lodi (1489–1517):** ablest — **founded AGRA (1504)** as second capital; **Gaz-i-Sikandari** (standard yard) for land measurement; abolished grain duties; poetry as "Gulrukhi"; religiously rigid (temple destruction at Mathura, jizya zeal).
- **Ibrahim Lodi (1517–1526):** alienated Afghan nobles (Daulat Khan invited Babur); **First Battle of Panipat, 21 April 1526** — Babur's artillery (Ustad Ali & Mustafa) + **tulughma** flanking ended the Sultanate.

## 5. Administration (⭐ Prelims table zone)

**Sovereignty:** no fixed succession law (sword + nobles' choice); symbols = **khutba** (Friday sermon in ruler's name) & **sikka** (coinage); nominal deference to the **Caliph** (Iltutmish 1229, MBT and Firoz sought investitures).

### Central departments
| Department | Head | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Wizarat** | **Wazir** | revenue & finance (with Mushrif-i-Mumalik = accountant-general; Mustaufi = auditor) |
| **Diwan-i-Arz** | **Ariz-i-Mumalik** | military: recruitment, muster, dagh-chehra |
| **Diwan-i-Insha** | Dabir-i-Khas | royal correspondence/records |
| **Diwan-i-Risalat** | (with Sadr) | religious affairs, grants, appeals |
| **Sadr-us-Sudur / Qazi-ul-Quzat** | often one person | ecclesiastical endowments / chief justice |
| **Barid-i-Mumalik** | — | intelligence & posts |
| Household | **Wakil-i-Dar**, **Amir-i-Hajib**, Amir-i-Akhur, Amir-i-Shikar | palace, ceremonies, stables, hunt |
| Ad-hoc creations | **Diwan-i-Mustakhraj** (Alauddin, arrears); **Diwan-i-Amir-Kohi** (MBT, agriculture); **Diwan-i-Khairat / Bandagan** (Firoz) | — |

### Provincial → local
**Iqta (province)** under **muqti/wali** — collects revenue, maintains troops, remits surplus (**fawazil**) → **shiqq** (shiqqdar) → **pargana** (amil; chaudhari; patwari) → village (**muqaddam, khut, patwari** — Hindu rural intermediaries survived throughout).

### Taxation
| Tax | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kharaj** | land tax on non-Muslims (peaked at ½ under Alauddin) |
| **Ushr** | tithe on Muslim-held land |
| **Zakat** | 2.5% wealth tax on Muslims |
| **JIZYA** | poll tax on protected non-Muslims (zimmis); women, children, ascetics — and Brahmins until **Firoz** — exempt |
| **Khams** | 1/5 of war booty (sultans in practice took 4/5) |
| **Haqq-i-sharb** | irrigation cess (Firoz) |

**Nobility ranks:** Khan > Malik > Amir > Sipahsalar. Economy of the elite ran on **iqtas + slaves + karkhanas** (royal workshops).

## 6. Economy, technology, society

- **Urban revival:** Delhi ("Hazrat-i-Dehli", refuge of Islamic learning after the Mongol sack of Baghdad 1258), Multan, Lahore, Jaunpur, Gulbarga; artisan migration; monetisation (tanka-jital); **sarrafs** (bankers) and **hundi** (bills of exchange).
- **New technologies diffused in Sultanate centuries (Irfan Habib's list):** **paper**, the **spinning wheel (charkha)**, the **Persian wheel (rahat/saqiya)** for lift-irrigation, an improved (liquid) **distillation**, lime-mortar & the **true arch/dome**, sericulture expansion — quiet productivity revolution. ⭐ favourite Mains point.
- **Society:** ruling class stratified (Turk → Khalji broadening → Afghans & Indian Muslims); **ulema** as clerical elite; artisan & slave labour in karkhanas (Firoz's 180,000 slaves); **purdah** spread among urban upper classes; **sati** noted (Ibn Battuta says royal permission was needed); caste order among Hindus continued — conversions were gradual, regional, and driven more by **Sufi settlement, patronage and social mobility than by force** (historiographic consensus — see File 11).

## 7. Art & architecture — the Indo-Islamic synthesis (⭐ high-yield)

**New grammar:** true **arch** & **dome** (arcuate) replacing trabeate beams; **minars**; lime **mortar**; **arabesque**, geometry & **calligraphy** replacing figural sculpture; **jaali**; later **charbagh**. Indian mason + Saracenic form = **Indo-Islamic style**.

| Phase | Monuments | Firsts to memorise |
|---|---|---|
| **Mamluk** | **Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque** (materials of 27 temples), **QUTUB MINAR** (Aibak began 1199 → Iltutmish completed; Firoz rebuilt top storeys — 5 storeys, 72.5 m, red sandstone + marble; **UNESCO 1993**), Adhai-Din-ka-Jhonpra (Ajmer), **Sultan Ghari** (first Islamic tomb), **Iltutmish's tomb** (squinches), **Balban's tomb — FIRST TRUE ARCH** | true arch (Balban) |
| **Khalji** | **ALAI DARWAZA (1311)** — **first true dome** + horseshoe arch, red sandstone with white marble; Siri (2nd city of Delhi); Jamaat Khana mosque (Nizamuddin) | true dome; "Seljuk" ornament |
| **Tughlaq** | **Tughlaqabad** (3rd city), **Ghiyasuddin's tomb** (sloping **batter** walls, marble dome, fortress-tomb), Jahanpanah (4th, MBT), **Firozabad/Kotla** (5th) with **Ashokan pillar**, Hauz Khas madrasa-tomb; austere rubble style | batter; austerity |
| **Sayyid–Lodi** | **Octagonal tombs** (Mubarak Sayyid, Muhammad Sayyid, **Sikandar Lodi's tomb — first DOUBLE DOME**, c. 1518), square tombs (**Bara Gumbad, Shish Gumbad**), Moth-ki-Masjid — the **Lodi Gardens** ensemble | double dome; garden-tomb idea → Mughals |

**Regional sultanate styles** (one-liners): Jaunpur Sharqi (Atala Masjid — arched pylons), Bengal (Adina mosque, brick), Gujarat (Jami Masjid Ahmedabad), Malwa (Mandu — Jahaz Mahal), Kashmir (wooden), **Deccan-Bahmani: Gulbarga Jami Masjid (fully covered courtyard), Bidar — Mahmud Gawan's madrasa** ⭐ (Deccan tie-in for AP papers).

## 8. Language & literature

- **Persian = court language**; historiography blossomed:
| Historian | Work | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Minhaj-us-Siraj** | *Tabaqat-i-Nasiri* | up to 1260 |
| **Ziauddin Barani** | ***Tarikh-i-Firozshahi*** + ***Fatawa-i-Jahandari*** (political theory) | Balban → Firoz; our chief (biased) source |
| **Shams Siraj Afif** | *Tarikh-i-Firozshahi* | Firoz's reign |
| **Isami** | *Futuh-us-Salatin* | written at the **Bahmani** court ⭐ |
| **Yahya Sirhindi** | *Tarikh-i-Mubarakshahi* | Sayyids |
| Firoz Shah | *Futuhat-i-Firozshahi* | royal memoir |

- **AMIR KHUSRAU (1253–1325)** — "**Tuti-i-Hind** (Parrot of India)"; disciple of **Nizamuddin Auliya**; served under some **seven sultans** from Balban's sons to Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq. Works: *Qiran-us-Sadain*, *Miftah-ul-Futuh*, ***Khazain-ul-Futuh*** (Alauddin's Deccan campaigns — Warangal!), *Ashiqa*, ***Nuh Sipihr*** (celebrates India's climate, languages, learning), *Tughlaq Nama*. Pioneer of **Hindavi** verse; father of **qawwali**; credited by tradition (contested) with the **sitar and tabla**; created early **khayal** seeds. ⭐ inexhaustible exam source.
- **Amir Hasan Sijzi** — *Fawaid-ul-Fuad* (recorded conversations of Nizamuddin; the model **malfuzat**) — "Saadi of Hind".
- **Sanskrit & vernaculars continued**: regional courts (Vijayanagara, Reddi kingdom — Srinatha, Potana in Telugu ⭐) flourished precisely as Delhi weakened; **Hindavi/early Urdu** crystallised in the camp-city, and its **Dakhni** cousin travelled south with Sufis & MBT's Daulatabad transfer.

## 9. The Sultanate and the South / Andhra (⭐ APPSC lens — one paragraph to reuse everywhere)

Alauddin's raids (Devagiri 1307, **Warangal 1310**, Dwarasamudra 1311, Madurai 1311) extracted tribute; Ghiyasuddin's son annexed **Warangal in 1323**, ending the Kakatiyas. Within a generation the Sultanate's over-extension produced its own successors: **Musunuri Nayakas recovered Warangal (1336)**, **Vijayanagara rose (1336)** as the great Hindu successor-state south of the Krishna, and the **Bahmani Sultanate (1347)** as the Deccan's Muslim power — their **Raichur-doab rivalry** framed Andhra politics for two centuries, while **Reddi (Kondavidu) and Velama (Rachakonda/Devarakonda)** kingdoms filled the Telugu lowlands. Culturally the invasion corridor brought **Sufi khanqahs (Khuldabad, Gulbarga — Gesudaraz), Dakhni language, paper, and Indo-Islamic building craft** into the peninsula — the matrix of later Deccani-Telugu composite culture.

## 10. Decline of the Sultanate — cause bundle (Mains)

1. Despotism resting on army + nobles' loyalty; **no succession law** → war at every accession.
2. **Over-centralisation vs over-extension** (MBT's Deccan) — distance defeated administration.
3. **Hereditary iqtas & slave bureaucracy** under Firoz → fiscal-military decay.
4. Orthodoxy alienating the Hindu majority (jizya on Brahmins) and even sects of Muslims.
5. **Mongol pressure**, then **Timur (1398)** — the coup de grâce.
6. Rise of confident provincial states (Jaunpur, Bengal, Malwa, Gujarat, Bahmani, **Vijayanagara**).

## 11. Prelims nuggets

1. Lakh Bakhsh = Aibak; died playing chaugan.
2. Tanka & jital, iqta, Chihalgani, Caliph's investiture — all **Iltutmish**.
3. Sijda-paibos, Zil-i-Ilahi, destruction of the Forty — **Balban**.
4. Dagh & chehra, market control (Shahna-i-Mandi), kharaj = ½, Diwan-i-Mustakhraj — **Alauddin**.
5. Malik Kafur = Hazar-Dinari; Warangal tribute 1310.
6. Token currency, Daulatabad, Diwan-i-Amir-Kohi, Ibn Battuta as qazi — **MBT**.
7. Canals, Diwan-i-Khairat, jizya on Brahmins, Ashokan pillars shifted, hereditary iqta — **Firoz**.
8. Timur 1398 (Nasiruddin Mahmud Tughlaq on throne).
9. Agra founded 1504 — Sikandar Lodi; Panipat-I 21 Apr 1526.
10. Firsts: true arch — Balban's tomb; true dome — Alai Darwaza; double dome — Sikandar Lodi's tomb; first Islamic tomb — Sultan Ghari.
11. Uluq/dawa post — Ibn Battuta's Rehla.
12. *Fatawa-i-Jahandari* & *Tarikh-i-Firozshahi* — Barani; *Futuh-us-Salatin* — Isami (Bahmani court).
13. Warangal → "Sultanpur" (1323); recovered by Kapaya Nayaka (1336); Vijayanagara 1336; Bahmani 1347.

## 12. Mains angles

1. **"Alauddin's reforms were military measures with economic instruments."** Link army-in-cash ↔ price control ↔ ½-kharaj ↔ spy system; note Barani's Delhi-centric evidence and post-Alauddin collapse of controls.
2. **"MBT failed not for want of ideas but of means."** Assess each project against 14th-c. logistics; end with the ironic legacy — Daulatabad transfer seeded Deccan's Indo-Persian culture and, by reaction, Vijayanagara & Musunuri Andhra.
3. **"The Sultanate was an Indian state run on Turko-Persian lines."** Village-level Hindu officialdom (khut-muqaddam-patwari), Hindu-majority taxation base, composite technologies & architecture.
4. **"Trace the evolution of Indo-Islamic architecture, 1206–1526."** Use the firsts table; end at Lodi double-dome → Mughal garden-tomb.
5. **"1323 is the hinge of Andhra history."** Kakatiya fall → Musunuri interlude → Vijayanagara/Bahmani duality → Reddi-Velama order; cultural corridor (Dakhni, Sufis) — cross-reference File 12 and the "making of Andhra" synthesis (File 35).
