## 0. Frame

English education and the printing press were meant to produce clerks and consent; instead they produced **critics and Congressmen**. The same generation that read Mill and Milton also rediscovered India's past and attacked its social evils — the **19th-century renaissance**: reformist (**Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana, Aligarh**) and revivalist (**Arya Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Theosophy**) wings of one awakening, whose common ground was **reason, scripture-based reform, women's uplift and anti-caste critique** — and whose by-product was **national consciousness**. *(2023 Q4b — Muslim reform & Sir Syed — is a direct hit here.)*

## 1. Education under the Raj ⭐⭐

| Milestone | Substance |
|---|---|
| **Charter Act 1813** | first state grant — **₹1 lakh/yr** (unspent for years; Orientalist–Anglicist quarrel over its use) |
| **General Committee of Public Instruction 1823**; **Raja Ram Mohan Roy's 1823 letter** ⭐ | Roy protested the Sanskrit College grant — demanded **Western scientific education** |
| **MACAULAY'S MINUTE — 2 Feb 1835** (Bentinck's resolution) ⭐⭐ | English medium, Western learning; **"filtration theory"** — educate an elite, culture trickles down; "a class Indian in blood and colour, English in taste" |
| **Persian replaced by English/vernaculars (1837)**; English for govt jobs (1844) | education tied to employment — enrolment surged |
| **WOOD'S DESPATCH 1854** — "Magna Carta of English education in India" ⭐⭐ | graded system village school → university; **universities at Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1857)** ⭐; grants-in-aid; vernacular primary education; women's & teacher education; Education Departments |
| **Hunter Commission 1882** | primary education to local bodies; secondary split (literary/vocational); noted female education's neglect |
| Later: **Raleigh Commission → Universities Act 1904; Gokhale's free-primary bill 1911 (failed); Sadler Commission 1917–19** | expansion with control; Gokhale's failure = nationalist grievance |

- **Assessment for Mains:** literacy stayed abysmal (~16% in 1941; female ~7%); science & mass education starved; but the by-product was decisive — **a pan-Indian English-educated class** that ran the press, the professions and the Congress ⭐.

## 2. The press ⭐⭐

- **Firsts:** **James Augustus Hicky — "Bengal Gazette" 1780** (first newspaper in India) ⭐; first vernaculars — **Samachar Darpan (Bengali, Serampore 1818)**, Roy's **Sambad Kaumudi (1821)** & Persian **Mirat-ul-Akhbar (1822)**.
- **Control & freedom pendulum:** Censorship 1799 (Wellesley) → **Licensing 1823 (Adams)** → **Metcalfe, "Liberator of the Indian Press", 1835** ⭐ → **"Gagging Act" 1857** → **VERNACULAR PRESS ACT 1878 (Lytton — "the vilest of Acts", against Indian-language papers only; *Amrita Bazar Patrika* turned English overnight ⭐)** → repealed by **Ripon 1882** → Newspaper (Incitement) Act 1908 & **Indian Press Act 1910** (Tilak's trials; *Kesari*).
- **Nationalist press gallery ⭐ (match-pairs):** *The Hindu* (1878, G. Subramania Iyer) · *Kesari* & *Mahratta* (Tilak) · *Amrita Bazar Patrika* (Ghosh brothers) · *The Bengalee* (Surendranath Banerjea) · *Voice of India* (Naoroji) · *Sudharak* (Gokhale) — and in Andhra **Krishna Patrika & Andhra Patrika** (File 23).
- **Role:** the press manufactured public opinion, linked provinces, taught political vocabulary — **the daily school of nationalism**.

## 3. Cultural changes & rise of national consciousness ⭐⭐ (feeds 2025 Q4b context)

Causes to enumerate: **political-administrative unification** (one state, one law, railways-post-telegraph) · **English as lingua franca** of the educated · **press & modern literature** (Bankim's *Anandamath* — **"Vande Mataram" 1882** ⭐) · **rediscovery of the past** (Jones, Max Müller, ASI — Cunningham) restoring self-respect · **economic critique** (drain theory — Naoroji, R.C. Dutt; File 28) · **racial arrogance** (the **ILBERT BILL controversy 1883** ⭐ — European revolt against Indian judges taught Indians the value of organised agitation) · **Lytton's provocations** (Vernacular Press Act, Arms Act 1878, Delhi Durbar during famine 1877, civil-service age cut). → Result: provincial associations (Poona Sarvajanik Sabha 1870, **Indian Association 1876 — Surendranath Banerjea**, Madras Mahajana Sabha 1884) → **Indian National Congress 1885** (File 30).

## 4. Socio-religious reform movements ⭐⭐⭐

### 4.1 RAJA RAM MOHAN ROY (1772–1833) — "father of the Indian Renaissance / first modern man of India" ⭐⭐
- **Atmiya Sabha 1814** → **BRAHMO SABHA/SAMAJ, 20 Aug 1828**: monotheism, no idolatry, no priesthood; scripture tested by **reason** (*Precepts of Jesus* 1820; *Tuhfat-ul-Muwahhidin* — Persian, against idolatry).
- **Anti-sati crusade → Regulation XVII, 1829 (Bentinck)** ⭐; against polygamy, kulinism, caste; for women's property rights.
- Educationist (English education advocate — 1823 letter; Hindu College 1817 with David Hare; Vedanta College 1825); press pioneer (§2); internationalist (title **"Raja" from Akbar II**; died **Bristol 1833**).
- **After Roy:** **Debendranath Tagore** (Tattvabodhini Sabha 1839, joined Brahmo 1843) → **Keshab Chandra Sen** (radical; schisms — Brahmo Samaj of India 1866; Sadharan Brahmo Samaj 1878 after the Cooch-Behar marriage ⭐). Brahmo fruit: **Civil Marriage Act 1872** (inter-caste, min. marriage age).
- Allied crusader: **ISHWAR CHANDRA VIDYASAGAR** — **Widow Remarriage Act 1856** ⭐, girls' schools, against child marriage & polygamy.

### 4.2 SWAMI DAYANANDA SARASWATI (1824–83) & the ARYA SAMAJ ⭐⭐
- Founded **Arya Samaj, Bombay, 1875** (HQ Lahore); *Satyarth Prakash*; slogan **"Back to the Vedas"** ⭐ — Vedas infallible, later Puranic Hinduism corrupt ("revivalist in form, reformist in content" — write this line).
- Against idolatry, polytheism, child marriage, untouchability, hereditary caste (varna by merit); for widow remarriage & women's education; **niyoga**; the **SHUDDHI movement** (reconversion) ⭐ — energised Hinduism but sharpened communal edges.
- Wings after 1893: College party (**D.A.V. College, Lahore 1886 — Hansraj**) vs Gurukul party (**Gurukul Kangri 1902 — Swami Shraddhananda**); political nursery of Lajpat Rai & Punjab nationalism ("Dayananda — a father of Indian nationalism" claim; also called **"Luther of Hinduism"**).

### 4.3 SRI RAMAKRISHNA (1836–86) & SWAMI VIVEKANANDA (1863–1902) ⭐⭐
- Ramakrishna Paramahamsa (Dakshineswar): **"as many faiths, so many paths" (yato mat, tato path)**; **service to man is service to God (Shiva Jnane Jiva Seva)** ⭐.
- **Narendranath Datta → Vivekananda: Chicago Parliament of Religions, 11 Sept 1893** ⭐⭐ — Vedanta on the world stage; **Ramakrishna Mission, 1 May 1897** (Belur Math 1898) — humanitarian service + practical Vedanta; journals *Prabuddha Bharata*, *Udbodhan*.
- Message: strength ("religion of man-making"), fearlessness, dignity of the masses ("Daridra Narayana"), no social reform by denunciation — **cultural self-confidence that fed nationalism** (Subhas Bose: "spiritual father of the modern nationalist movement"; called the **"cyclonic monk"**).

### 4.4 ANNIE BESANT (1847–1933) & THEOSOPHY ⭐
- **Theosophical Society — Blavatsky & Olcott, New York 1875 → Adyar, Madras 1882** ⭐; Hindu-Buddhist revival from a Western source (occult-Vedantic universal brotherhood).
- **Besant arrived 1893**: **Central Hindu College, Banaras 1898** (nucleus of **BHU, 1916 — Malaviya**) ⭐; translated the Gita; then politics — **New India & Commonweal**, **HOME RULE LEAGUE, Sept 1916 (Adyar)**, interned 1917, **first woman President of the INC (Calcutta 1917)** ⭐⭐ (File 30 & her Andhra links, File 22).

### 4.5 SIR SYED AHMAD KHAN (1817–98) & the ALIGARH MOVEMENT ⭐⭐ (2023 Q4b)
- Diagnosis after 1857 (*Causes of the Indian Revolt*, *Loyal Mohammedans of India*): Muslim salvation = **loyalty + modern Western education**, not agitation.
- Works: Scientific Society (Ghazipur/Aligarh **1864**, translations); *Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq* (1870, social reform journal); **MUHAMMADAN ANGLO-ORIENTAL COLLEGE, ALIGARH, 1875** ⭐⭐ (→ **Aligarh Muslim University 1920**); rationalist tafsir — Quran and modern science compatible; against purdah excesses, for reformed education of women (cautiously).
- Politics: kept Muslims **out of the Congress** (fear of majoritarian competition; "two eyes of the bride" earlier, later the divergence) — groundwork, via successors (Mohsin-ul-Mulk), of the **Muslim League 1906** — write both his **educational uplift (positive)** and **separatist drift (critique)** for 2023 Q4b balance.
- Other Muslim streams ⭐: **Deoband (Dar-ul-Ulum 1866 — Nanautavi & Gangohi; anti-British, later Jamiat-ul-Ulema supported Congress)** — the Aligarh contrast pair; **Ahmadiyya (Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, 1889)**; earlier **Wahhabi/Tariqah-i-Muhammadiya (Syed Ahmed of Rae Bareli)** and **Faraizi (Haji Shariatullah, Dudu Miyan — Bengal agrarian)** movements.

### 4.6 The wider gallery (one-liners that fetch marks)
- **Prarthana Samaj (Bombay 1867; Atmaram Pandurang; M.G. RANADE & R.G. Bhandarkar)** ⭐ — Maharashtra's Brahmo cousin: widow remarriage, inter-caste dining, **Ranade's Widow Marriage Association 1861, Deccan Education Society**.
- **Young Bengal — Henry Louis Vivian DEROZIO** (d. 1831) — radical rationalists of Hindu College; "the first nationalist poet of modern India".
- **Balshastri Jambhekar** (*Darpan* 1832 — first Marathi paper); **Gopal Hari Deshmukh 'LOKAHITAWADI'**; **Jyotiba Phule's Satyashodhak Samaj 1873** ⭐ (full treatment with Self-Respect stream, File 30 §4).
- **Veda Samaj (Madras 1864)** — southern Brahmoism (K. Sridharalu Naidu); in Andhra → **Veeresalingam's Rajahmundry reform** (File 22 — cross-use!).
- **Sikh reform:** Nirankari, Namdhari (Kuka), **Singh Sabhas (1873) → Akali/Gurdwara Reform 1920s (SGPC 1925)**; **Parsi reform:** Rahnumai Mazdayasnan Sabha 1851 (Naoroji, S.S. Bengalee).
- Women-focused landmarks: **Pandita Ramabai (Sharada Sadan 1889)**, D.K. Karve (Widows' Home; **SNDT Women's University 1916**), B.M. Malabari's **Age of Consent Act 1891** ⭐; child-marriage capstone — **Sarda Act 1929**.

### 4.7 Assessment (Mains close)
Common methods — press, schools, petitions, scripture-plus-reason; achievements — legal reforms (1829, 1856, 1872, 1891, 1929), women's education, caste questioned, cultural self-respect; limits — urban upper-caste base, revivalism's communal shadow (shuddhi vs tabligh), the masses barely touched until Gandhi and Phule-Periyar's plebeian stream (File 30). Net: the reform movements were **the seedbed of national consciousness** — the same names staff the early Congress.
