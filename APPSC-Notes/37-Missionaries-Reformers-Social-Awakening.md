## 0. Frame

Between roughly **1805 (LMS at Visakhapatnam)** and **1919 (death of Veeresalingam)**, coastal Andhra experienced its **renaissance**: missionaries brought **schools, printing and prose**; English education and the press created a reforming middle class; and a chain of reformers — **Veeresalingam → Gurajada → Venkataratnam Naidu → Gidugu** — attacked child marriage, enforced widowhood, kanyasulkam (bride-price), the nautch, caste exclusion and a fossilised literary language. **2025 Q9 asked both halves of this file:** (a) missionaries and education, (b) factors of social awakening in the later 19th century.

## 1. Christian missionaries in Andhra ⭐⭐ (2025 Q9(a))

### 1.1 The missions and their stations

| Mission | Station & year | Founders / famous names |
|---|---|---|
| **London Missionary Society (LMS)** | **Visakhapatnam, 1805** — first Protestant mission in Andhra | **George Cran & Augustus Des Granges**; pioneer Telugu Scripture translation (aided by the Serampore press network) |
| **Church Missionary Society (CMS)** | **Masulipatnam, 1841** | **Robert Turlington NOBLE** — **Noble School/College (1843)**; Henry W. Fox; Noble died after the **1864 cyclone** relief |
| **American Baptist Telugu Mission** | **Nellore, 1840** (Samuel S. Day); **Ongole, 1866** | the **"LONE STAR" mission**; **John E. CLOUGH** — famine relief works on the **Buckingham Canal (1876–78)**; **mass movement of Madigas — 2,222 baptised in a day at Velumpilly on the Gundlakamma (3 July 1878)**; Ongole church briefly the world's largest |
| **Lutherans** | **Guntur, 1842** — "**Father**" **C.F. HEYER** (first American Lutheran missionary); Rajahmundry (1840s, North German mission) | → Andhra Evangelical Lutheran Church; **Andhra Christian College, Guntur (1885)** |
| **Canadian Baptists** | **Cocanada (Kakinada), 1874** — John McLaurin | Godavari-delta school & hospital network |
| **Roman Catholics** | older Golconda-coast presence; 19th-c. dioceses | schools, orphanages |

### 1.2 What the missionaries changed (2025 answer plan)
1. **Education:** pioneer English and vernacular schools decades before the state — **Noble College (1843), A.C. College Guntur (1885)**, hundreds of village schools; after **Wood's Despatch (1854)** their grant-in-aid network became the backbone of Andhra schooling.
2. **Women's education:** first **girls' schools**, boarding schools and **zenana missions**; women missionaries and Bible-women normalised the literate girl (the soil Veeresalingam built on).
3. **Dalit access:** the Ongole/Guntur **mass movements** among **Madigas and Malas** — schooling, famine relief (Clough's canal contract fed thousands in 1876–78) and a new self-respect; the roots of Dalit assertion and of **Adi-Andhra** consciousness (File 38 §4).
4. **Language & print:** Telugu **Bible translation**, grammars, dictionaries and mission presses made **Telugu prose and print** a mass medium (early experiments go back to **Benjamin Schultze**, 18th c.; the missionary journal **"Satyadoota" (1835)** is often counted the first Telugu periodical).
5. **Medicine & philanthropy:** mission hospitals, leprosy and famine work.
- **Critique (balance for Mains):** conversion agenda and cultural denigration provoked resentment; but the **competitive shock** it gave Hindu society (Brahmo, Arya Samaj, reform associations) was itself a motor of the awakening.

## 2. Factors of the socio-cultural awakening (2025 Q9(b) skeleton) ⭐⭐

1. **English education:** Wood's Despatch 1854 → **Madras University 1857**; district colleges (Rajahmundry Government College, Noble, A.C. College); a new lawyer-teacher-clerk middle class in Godavari-Krishna towns.
2. **Print & press:** mission presses → Telugu journals (*Vivekavardhini* 1874, *Krishna Patrika* 1902, *Andhra Patrika* 1908 — File 38) as reform's megaphone.
3. **Missionary challenge** (§1) and the **Brahmo Samaj**'s arrival via Madras (Veeresalingam's prayer societies, Brahmo Mandir at Rajahmundry) — also **Theosophy** (Adyar 1882) among the coastal elite.
4. **Colonial infrastructure:** railways, canals, the prosperous deltas after Cotton (File 36) — surplus that funded schools, presses and endowments.
5. **Individual agency:** Veeresalingam's crusades made Rajahmundry the "**Navasamaja**" laboratory; princely patrons (Vizianagaram, Pithapuram) financed colleges and reformers.
6. **Law as ally:** Widow Remarriage Act 1856, Age of Consent Act 1891, anti-nautch campaigns — reformers used colonial legality against orthodoxy.

## 3. The reformers ⭐⭐

### 3.1 KANDUKURI VEERESALINGAM (1848–1919) — "Gadya Tikkana", the Yugakarta (2020 Q8(b))
- Rajahmundry teacher who became the **father of the Telugu renaissance**; influenced by Keshab Chandra Sen's Brahmoism.
- **Journalism:** ***Vivekavardhini* (1874)** — Telugu's first crusading journal (exposed corrupt officials, priestcraft); ***Satihitabodhini*** for women; satirical *prahasanas*.
- **Widow remarriage:** organised the **first Telugu widow remarriage, 11 December 1881, Rajahmundry** (police protection against orthodoxy; ~40 marriages followed); founded widows' homes.
- **Campaigns:** against **kanyasulkam** (bride-price sale of girl children), child marriage, **nautch (anti-devadasi)**, and for **women's education** — girls' schools (from 1874) and co-education.
- **Literature:** first Telugu **novel** *Rajasekhara Charitramu* (1878, after the *Vicar of Wakefield*); first Telugu **autobiography** (*Sweeya Charitramu*); plays, science primers, a history of Telugu poets — modern Telugu **prose** built almost single-handedly ("Gadya Tikkana").
- **Institution-builder:** Rajahmundry town hall, prayer samaj, schools; deeded everything to the **Hitakarini Samajam (1908)**.
- **Assessment (PYQ "critically evaluate"):** peerless in courage and range; limits — reform stayed largely within the coastal upper-caste milieu, caste itself was not frontally attacked, and later he stood aloof from mass politics. Still the reference point every later reformer measured against.

### 3.2 GURAJADA APPARAO (1862–1915) — the modernist (2023 Q8(a)(i))
- Court scholar of **Vizianagaram** (patron: Ananda Gajapati).
- ***KANYASULKAM* (staged 1892; published 1897; recast 1909)** — Telugu's greatest play: a savage comedy on **bride-price, child marriage to old men, enforced widowhood and fake piety**; heroine Madhuravani (a courtesan with conscience) upends moral hierarchy; written in **spoken Telugu** — a manifesto in itself.
- **"Desamunu preminchumanna…" (1910):** the *Desabhakti* song — "**a country is not soil; a country is its people**" ("Desamante matti kaadoi, desamante manushuloi") — nationalist anthem beyond caste and creed.
- **Firsts:** *Diddubatu* (1910) — usually counted the **first modern Telugu short story**; **mutyala saralu** (pearl-string) metre; realist poetry (*Lavana raju kala*, *Kanyaka*).
- With Gidugu, fought for **vyavaharika bhasha** on the university composition committee — literature's language = the people's language. *Mains line: Veeresalingam preached reform; Gurajada made the audience laugh at its own sins — art as social reform.*

### 3.3 RAGHUPATHI VENKATARATNAM NAIDU (1862–1939) — "Kulapathi"
- **Brahmo missionary-educator**; Principal, **Pithapuram Raja's (P.R.) College, Kakinada** — hostel-father to a generation ("Kulapathi"), insisted on ethics over ritual.
- Led the **SOCIAL PURITY movement**: **anti-nautch** (no dancing-girls at weddings/festivals), temperance, against the devadasi system; his students carried purity pledges across the deltas.
- Fellow and **Vice-Chancellor, Madras University (1925)**; knighted; bridged Brahmo theism, Dalit uplift (supported depressed-classes schooling) and constructive social work.

### 3.4 GIDUGU VENKATA RAMAMURTHY (1863–1940) — the language liberator (2023 Q8(a)(ii))
- Teacher at **Parlakimidi**; father of the **VYAVAHARIKA BHASHA UDYAMAM** — the war to replace fossilised **grandhika** (pseudo-classical) Telugu with living **spoken Telugu** in prose, textbooks and examinations.
- Fought pandit orthodoxy with pamphlet, platform and his journal ***Telugu* (1919)**; his *Andhra Pandita Bhishakkula Bhasha Bheshajam* demolished the "standard grandhika" claim; allies — Gurajada, J.A. Yates; the cause triumphed fully only after his death (modern textbooks, newspapers, All India Radio Telugu).
- **Savara apostle:** created script materials, primers and a **Savara–English dictionary** for the tribal **Savara (Sora)** language — pioneering tribal linguistics (honoured with the **Kaisar-i-Hind** medal).
- His birthday, **29 August, is Telugu Language Day (Telugu Bhasha Dinotsavam)** ⭐.

### 3.5 ANNIE BESANT (1847–1933) and the Theosophical stream
- President, **Theosophical Society (Adyar HQ)** from 1907 — lodges across coastal Andhra towns drew the English-educated into religious-nationalist self-respect.
- **New India** & **Commonweal**; **HOME RULE LEAGUE (Sept 1916)** — Andhra was among its strongest fields (town branches, lawyer-leaders; her **internment at Ooty, June–Sept 1917** set off Andhra-wide protest); first woman **Congress President (Calcutta, 1917)**.
- Education: Central Hindu College tradition → national education movement that Andhra answered with the **Andhra Jateeya Kalasala, Machilipatnam (1907)** (national college of the Vandemataram era — Kopalle Hanumantha Rao, Mutnuri Krishna Rao).

### 3.6 "And others" (name-drop bench ⭐)
- **Chilakamarthi Lakshmi Narasimham (1867–1946):** blind playwright-poet (*Gayopakhyanam*); patriotic padyam "Bharatakhandambu…"; ran night schools and journals, championed Harijan uplift.
- **Bandaru Acchamamba (1874–1905):** first Telugu woman essayist-historian — *Abala Saccharitra Ratnamala* (lives of great women); among the earliest Telugu short-story writers ("Dhana Trayodasi", 1902 — some count it before *Diddubatu*).
- **Unnava Lakshminarayana & Tripuraneni Ramaswamy** — reform through fiction and rationalism (bios with the literature set, File 39 §3).
- **Komarraju Venkata Lakshmana Rao** — Vignana Chandrika Mandali, first Telugu encyclopaedia (File 38 §2).

## 4. Role of women ⭐ (syllabus tail of item 8)

- **Objects → agents:** the first generation of reform was *about* women (widow remarriage, kanyasulkam, education); by the 1920s women were the reformers and satyagrahis.
- **DOKKA SITAMMA (1841–1909)** — "**Apara Annapurna**" of **Lankala Gannavaram (E. Godavari)**: fed every traveller regardless of caste, crossing the Godavari mid-flood to serve; tradition holds the King-Emperor honoured her with a seat/portrait at a coronation durbar she declined to attend — the exam's favourite icon of indigenous womanly philanthropy.
- **PONAKA KANAKAMMA (1892–1963)** — Nellore Gandhian: moving spirit of the **Pinakini Satyagrahasramam (1921)**, founded the **Sri Kasturidevi Vidyalayam** girls' school, went to prison in civil disobedience; patron of **Potti Sreeramulu**'s causes (Files 39–40).
- **Bandaru Acchamamba** (women's history), **Kanuparti Varalakshmamma** (*Sarada lekhalu* — columns for women), **Duvvuri Subbamma** (khadi & prohibition satyagrahi), **DURGABAI (Deshmukh) (1909–81)** — Rajahmundry child-satyagrahi → salt satyagraha organiser in Madras → founder, **Andhra Mahila Sabha (1937)** → Constituent Assembly & Planning Commission member ("mother of social work").
- Institutions: girls' schools (missionary, Veeresalingam's, Kanakamma's), widows' homes, **Andhra Mahila Sabha**, women's journals (*Satihitabodhini*, *Hindusundari*, *Grihalakshmi*).
- **Mains line:** in Andhra the "women's question" evolved from *rescue* (1870s–1900s) to *participation* (1920s–40s) — Sitamma's ladle, Acchamamba's pen, Durgabai's jail term mark the three stages.
