## 0. Frame

From **624 CE (Kubja Vishnuvardhana at Vengi)** to c. 1076, coastal Andhra lived under the **Eastern Chalukyas** — four centuries in which **Telugu became a language of inscriptions, then of literature (Nannaya, c. 1051)**, while inland Andhra was parcelled among **Renati Cholas (Kadapa), Vaidumbas, Banas, and the Rashtrakuta-orbit Vemulavada Chalukyas**. The full Eastern-Chalukya political-cultural treatment is in **File 01 §4** and the essay [`Telugu-Country-Vengi-to-Reddi.md`](../Telugu-Country-Vengi-to-Reddi.md); this file adds the Part-B specifics — the **2023 Q6b social-restructuring angle, the minor dynasties, and the Telugu script-language story** the syllabus names.

## 1. Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi (624–1076) — Part-B angles ⭐⭐⭐

- **Spine to hold (File 01):** Kubja Vishnuvardhana (624) → **Vijayaditya III "Gunaga"** (the warrior peak ⭐) → **Bhima I** (founder of **DRAKSHARAMA & Bhimavaram temples** ⭐) → the Chola-Chalukya marriage century → **RAJARAJA NARENDRA** (Rajahmundry, **Nannaya's patron** ⭐⭐) → merger into the Chola line (**Kulottunga I, 1070** — the "Chalukya-Chola" synthesis).
- **Socio-religious restructuring (2023 Q6b — asked verbatim):**
  1. **Immigrations** ⭐ — waves of Brahmana settlers from the north & Karnataka via **agrahara grants** (the Chalukyan instrument of colonisation); artisan-merchant inflows to the delta towns; the **"Komati" (Vaishya) and Sudra peasant castes crystallise** in this era.
  2. **Caste mobility** ⭐ — a frontier society: peasant clans rising through military service (the ancestors of the **Kamma-Velama-Reddy** cultivator-warrior castes trace to this fluid period); dynasties themselves of mixed Kshatriya claims; guild castes negotiating rank via temple donations.
  3. **Social awakening / religious shift** — from residual Buddhism to **temple-centred Shaivism** (the **PANCHARAMAS — Amararama, Draksharama, Somarama, Ksheerarama, Kumararama** ⭐⭐ — the devotional geography of the delta); Sun & Vishnu shrines alongside; temples became the **bank, school and employer** of the delta economy (devadana lands, dancers, artisans).
- **Art & architecture:** the Pancharama temples, **Biccavolu group** ⭐ (the "Chalukyan Ellora of Andhra" — 9th–10th c. shrines), Chalukyan vesara idiom flowing between Vengi and Kalyani.

## 2. Renati Cholas & the first Telugu inscriptions ⭐⭐⭐

- **Who:** a Telugu Choda line ruling **RENADU (the "Renadu 7000" country — Kadapa-Kurnool-Chittoor margins)**, c. 6th–8th c., claiming Karikala Chola descent; capitals in the Cuddapah basin (Erikal, later Pottapi tradition).
- **Why they matter — THE FIRST TELUGU RECORDS ⭐⭐⭐:**
  - **KALAMALLA inscription (Yerragudipadu, Kadapa dist.), c. 575 CE, of DHANUNJAYA** — the **earliest full Telugu inscription** (the fact File 01 flags; pair it against Nannaya's later *literary* first).
  - Followed by the **Erragudipadu, Tippaluru, MALEPADU records — PUNYAKUMARA's Malepadu plates** ⭐ (early Telugu Choda titles: *Chola-maharaja*).
  - Significance line: **Telugu enters public record 475 years before it enters literature** — administration and donative piety, not poetry, made Telugu a written language ⭐ (a favourite 5-marker).
- Neighbours of the same layer: **VAIDUMBAS** (Kalyani/Chittoor tract, 9th–10th c. Telugu inscriptions), **BANAS** (western marches), **Telugu Chodas of Pottapi/Konidena/Nellore** (the Nellore line later hosts **Tikkana** — File 37 ⭐).

## 3. Rashtrakutas in Andhra & the Vemulavada Chalukyas ⭐⭐

- **Rashtrakutas** (File 01 §5 for the empire): a century of **Vengi wars** (Govinda III, Amoghavarsha, Krishna II vs Gunaga Vijayaditya III & Bhima I) kept Andhra a battlefield; Rashtrakuta cultural imprint came through their **feudatories**:
- **CHALUKYAS OF VEMULAVADA (Karimnagar, c. 755–968)** ⭐⭐ — the Telangana court that mattered:
  - **Arikesari II — patron of PAMPA, the "Adikavi" of KANNADA** (*Vikramarjuna Vijaya/Pampa Bharata*) ⭐⭐ — the great trick fact: Kannada's first classic was written in Telangana.
  - **Somadeva Suri** (*Yasastilaka Champu*) at the same court; **III Baddega's Jain temples** — Vemulavada as a **JAIN cultural capital** (ties to File 35 §5.2; Danavulapadu).
  - The **Kurkyala inscription of Jinavallabha (Pampa's brother)** — trilingual (Sanskrit-Kannada-**Telugu verse**) ⭐ — early Telugu poetry outside Vengi.
- Rashtrakuta-era Telangana Shaivism & tank-building foreshadow the Kakatiyas (who began as Rashtrakuta-then-Kalyani-Chalukya feudatories — File 06).

## 4. Telugu script & language — the growth story ⭐⭐⭐ (syllabus: "Telugu Script and Language")

| Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Seed script** | **Ashokan Brahmi** in Andhra (Yerragudi ⭐) + the aberrant **BHATTIPROLU script** (relic-casket letters, c. 3rd c. BCE) — the southern Brahmi variant stream |
| **Telugu words in Prakrit records** | "**Nagabu**" (Amaravati, c. 1st c. BCE); place-names & titles in Satavahana-Ikshvaku charters |
| **Telugu-Kannada script** ⭐⭐ | the common southern script that Chalukyan (Badami-Vengi-Rashtrakuta) charters spread, 6th–10th c. — **Telugu and Kannada shared one script till c. 13th–14th c.**, then diverged into the modern rounded Telugu lipi (palm-leaf curves ⭐) |
| **First full Telugu inscription** | **Kalamalla, c. 575 (Renati Chola Dhanunjaya)** §2 |
| **First Telugu verse-records** | Addanki & Kandukur pillar records of **Pandaranga (Gunaga's general, c. 848)** ⭐ — *taruvoja* metre; Kurkyala verse (§3) |
| **First literature** | **NANNAYA, c. 1051–61 — *Andhra Mahabharatamu* begun at Rajaraja Narendra's Rajahmundry court** ⭐⭐; styled **"ADIKAVI"**; with it the grammar tradition (*Andhra Sabda Chintamani*, attributed ⭐ — "write *attributed*"); his diction set the **tatsama (Sanskrit-heavy) marga** path |
| **The counter-current** | **desi/dwipada** stream (Palkuriki Somanatha) & the Kavitraya's completion — File 37 |

- **One-line thesis for Mains:** Telugu's rise was **state-led** — donative records (575) → court verse (848) → courtly epic (1051) — each step sponsored by a dynasty (Renati Cholas → Eastern Chalukyas), which is why the language carries so heavy a Sanskrit overlay until the desi revolt (File 37).
