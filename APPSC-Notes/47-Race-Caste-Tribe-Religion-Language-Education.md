## 1. Race — classifications (treat as historiography, not current science)

- **Risley (1915):** seven "types" (Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Mongoloid, Aryo-Dravidian, Mongolo-Dravidian, Scytho-Dravidian, Turko-Iranian) — colonial anthropometry, now criticised.
- **B.S. GUHA (1935-ish, the exam-standard list) ⭐⭐ — six racial elements:** **Negrito** (Andamanese; traces claimed in some southern hill tribes), **Proto-Australoid** (large tribal belt — Munda, Gond; older ethnography placed **Chenchus and Yanadis** near this stock), **Mongoloid** (Himalaya & NE), **Mediterranean** (the dominant element of peninsular/Dravidian-speaking India — most of AP), **Western Brachycephals** (Alpo-Dinaric/Armenoid, west coast pockets), **Nordic** (late NW entrants).
- **Modern caveat (one line earns marks):** genetics reads Indians as **ANI–ASI mixtures**; "race" was last enumerated in 1931 and is scientifically obsolete — the census counts **caste (SC/ST), religion and language**, not race ⭐.

## 2. Caste — SC composition and distribution

- **India:** SCs = **16.6% (20.14 cr, 2011)**; largest numbers in UP; **highest share Punjab (~32%)** ⭐; SCs are historically **landless-labour concentrated**, more rural, with literacy below average — geography follows the agrarian belts.
- **Andhra Pradesh:** SCs ≈ **17.1% (~84.4 lakh)** — above the national share ⭐; **distribution tilts to the south-coastal belt — Prakasam and Nellore have the highest shares (~22–23%)**, deltas high in absolute numbers, agency lowest; major communities: **Mala and Madiga (with sub-groups such as Adi Andhra, Relli)**.
- **Current-affairs layer (cite carefully):** the Supreme Court's **2024 sub-classification judgment** permitted states to sub-categorise SC quotas; **AP moved first to implement sub-categorisation (2025 — verify the final grouping details)** ⭐; the **2027 caste-enumerating census** will give the first full caste data since 1931.
- Analytical line: caste in AP is also *spatial* — village ward geography (File 46's semi-clustered type), land-ownership patterns and the labourer-heavy workforce (File 39) are the material base of the state's welfare politics.

### 2.1 Backward Classes — the layer the census does not count ⭐

- **No caste census since 1931**, so BC numbers rest on **commission estimates and state surveys**: **Mandal Commission (1980) put OBCs at ~52%**; NSS-based estimates run lower (~41–45%); **the 2027 census will enumerate caste** — say this before quoting any figure ⭐.
- **AP's distinctive design:** BCs are **sub-grouped into lettered groups (A, B, C, D, E)** — aboriginal/nomadic (A), vocational/artisan (B), converted-Christian SCs (C), other backward castes (D), and **socially & educationally backward Muslim groups (E)** — a sub-categorisation model older than the national debate, and the reason AP is cited whenever **sub-classification of quotas** is discussed ⭐⭐.
- **Reservation arithmetic (verify the current position — it is litigated):** roughly **SC 15% + ST 6% + BC ~29%** in AP, plus **EWS 10%** under the **103rd Amendment (2019)**; the **Kapu quota (a BC-F group, 2017)** and the **Muslim BC-E quota** have both been contested in court — describe them as *contested*, never as settled law.
- **The connecting argument for Mains:** AP's social composition — a large BC middle, two numerically strong dominant peasant castes (Kamma, Reddy), a big SC population and a bifurcated tribal geography — makes **group-differentiated welfare** the state's default policy instrument; the SC sub-classification judgment (2024) simply nationalised a debate AP has run since the 1970s.

## 3. Tribe — ST composition ⭐⭐ (2020 PYQ (b) — full kit)

- **India:** STs = **8.6% (10.43 cr, 2011)**; ~705 communities, **75 PVTGs**; largest numbers MP; highest shares Mizoram/Nagaland/Meghalaya (states), Lakshadweep (UT); constitutional scaffolding — Fifth/Sixth Schedules, Art. 342, FRA 2006, PESA 1996; **PM-JANMAN (2023) for PVTGs** ⭐.
- **AP: STs ≈ 5.5% (~27.4 lakh, 2011)** with two very different worlds:
  - **The agency (Scheduled Areas) belt** of the north Eastern Ghats — old Srikakulam–Vizianagaram–Visakhapatnam–Godavari agencies; since 2022 the tribal-majority districts **Alluri Sitharama Raju & Parvathipuram Manyam** ⭐. Communities: **Koya, Konda Dora, Savara (Sora), Jatapu, Gadaba, Bagata, Valmiki, Kondh (Khond), Porja, Konda Reddi**.
  - **Plains & southern tribes:** **Chenchu (Nallamala forests — NSTR co-residents)** ⭐, **Yanadi (Nellore–Pulicat)**, **Yerukala**, **Sugali/Lambada (Banjara)** — the plains tribes are numerically large.
  - **PVTGs of AP:** Chenchu, Konda Reddi, Konda Savara, Gadaba groups, Khond groups (Dongria/Kutia fringe) — hedge the exact list, it follows notified nomenclature.
- **Old-district anchor (2011):** **Visakhapatnam had the highest ST share (~14%)** among the 13; agency mandals are 70–90% tribal.
- **Protective-development grid ⭐ (use as answer spine):** Fifth Schedule + **LTR 1 of 70 (1970, land-transfer ban)** + Samata (1997) → **ITDAs (Seethampeta, Parvathipuram, Paderu, Rampachodavaram, Chintur/KR Puram; Srisailam ITDA for Chenchus)** ⭐ → GCC (1956) NTFP economy (File 40) → FRA titles for podu → education (ashram schools, Eklavya/EMRS) → health (sickle-cell, malaria) → the friction list: land alienation, podu disputes, displacement (Polavaram — largely tribal submergence ⭐), mining pressure (File 19).

## 4. Religion — composition and distribution

- **India (2011):** Hindu **79.8%**, Muslim **14.2%**, Christian **2.3%**, Sikh **1.7%**, Buddhist **0.7%**, Jain **0.4%** ⭐; geography — Muslims: J&K, Assam-WB, Kerala, UP-Bihar towns; Christians: NE + Kerala + coastal south; Sikhs: Punjab.
- **AP (residual, 2011, recorded):** **Hindu ≈ 90.9%, Muslim ≈ 7.3%, Christian ≈ 1.4%** ⭐.
  - **Muslim geography:** strongest in **Rayalaseema urban centres (Kadapa, Kurnool, Guntur–Nellore towns)** — the Deccani/Qutb Shahi legacy (Files 09, 14); **Urdu is an additional official language in specified districts**.
  - **Christian geography:** the 19th–20th-century **missionary belt of the south coast (Guntur, Krishna, W. Godavari, Ongole)** — Lutheran/Baptist heritage (cross-link File 14 & Part-B modern-Andhra PYQs); scholarship notes the **recorded share likely understates practice** (dual-identity among Dalit Christians) — phrase academically ⭐.
- For Mains: religious composition in AP is historically layered (Buddhist Andhra → temple Hinduism → Deccani Islam → mission Christianity) — a heritage frame scores better than bare percentages.

## 5. Language — linguistic composition

- **India (2011):** **Indo-Aryan ~78%, Dravidian ~19.6%**, Austroasiatic & Tibeto-Burman small but historic; **22 Eighth-Schedule languages**, 121 languages/1,369 mother tongues; **Hindi 43.6%**, Bengali 8.0%, Marathi 6.9%, **Telugu 6.7–6.9% (~8.1 cr) — the 4th largest language and the largest Dravidian language** ⭐⭐.
- **Telugu:** granted **Classical Language status in 2008** ⭐ (with its 2,000+-year record — Units 1–2 files); the linguistic-state legacy (1953 Andhra State — Part-B).
  - **The classical list to memorise (in order of grant):** **Tamil 2004 → Sanskrit 2005 → Kannada & Telugu 2008 → Malayalam 2013 → Odia 2014**, with **Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese and Bengali added in 2024** ⭐ — Telugu shares its year with Kannada, a favourite pairing trap.
  - **Constitutional scaffolding:** **Art. 343** (official language of the Union), **Art. 345** (state official language — the basis of AP's Telugu Official Language Act, 1966), **Art. 347** (special provision for a language spoken by a section — the Urdu hook), **Art. 350A** (mother-tongue primary instruction for linguistic minorities) and **Art. 350B** (Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities) ⭐; the **Eighth Schedule** began with **14 languages and now has 22** (Sindhi 1967; Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali 1992; **Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali 2003**).
  - **Classification for Prelims:** Telugu belongs to the **South-Central (South-Dravidian II) branch** with Gondi, Konda and Kui — *not* the same sub-branch as Tamil-Malayalam-Kannada; the **Telugu script descends from Brahmi via the Telugu-Kannada script of the Eastern Chalukya era** (File 36) ⭐.
- **AP composition:** Telugu ≈ **88–89%**, **Urdu ≈ 7%** (second language of the towns above), plus border tongues (Odia, Tamil, Kannada) and **tribal languages — a living museum ⭐: Koya & Kui-Kuvi (Dravidian), but SAVARA/SORA and GUTOB GADABA are AUSTROASIATIC (Munda) islands in the AP agency** (Ollari Gadaba is Dravidian — the Gadaba label covers both!); "Adivasi Odia" as an agency lingua franca; **Lambadi/Banjara** across the plains.
- Policy hooks: Official Language Act (Telugu), mother-tongue foundational teaching (NEP 2020) vs AP's **English-medium government-school push** — present both sides ⭐.

## 6. Education characteristics ⭐⭐ (AP's paradox — a predicted Mains theme)

- **India (2011):** literacy **72.99%** (M 80.9 / F 64.6 — a **16-point gender gap**); **Kerala top (~94%), Bihar bottom (~62%)**; current surveys ≈ 77–80%; schooling: NEP 2020 (FLN "NIPUN", 6% GDP goal), ASER learning-gap caveat; higher-ed GER ≈ 28% (AISHE).
- **AP: literacy ≈ 67.4% (2011) — the LOWEST among major states** ⭐⭐ (and recent PLFS rounds still place AP at/near the bottom — quote as "surveys continue to show…").
  - **Gender & social gaps:** female ≈ 60%; **ST literacy ≈ 49%** — the agency is the deepest deficit; SC literacy also below average.
  - **District spread (2011): West Godavari highest (~74.6%) vs Vizianagaram lowest (~58.9%)** ⭐ — literacy maps onto the delta-prosperity gradient *except* that even rich deltas underperform their income.
  - **Why (the analytical core):** early farm/child-labour absorption (chillies, aqua, mills), historic first-generation-learner base, dropout at transition grades, migration churn.
  - **The paradox ⭐:** AP pairs bottom-rung literacy with **above-national higher-education GER (~36%)** and a famous engineering-college density — a thin educated layer atop a broad low-literacy base; also pairs **high health indicators with low literacy** (File 44) — "AP's social transition is health-first, school-later."
  - **Interventions (name neutrally; schemes renamed post-2024 — verify):** school-infrastructure upgrading (Nadu-Nedu lineage), enrolment cash transfers (Amma Vodi lineage → successor scheme), mid-day meals, English-medium & CBSE alignment, adult-literacy missions.
  - **Institutional layer worth naming:** residential-school systems for SC/ST/BC/minority students (**APSWREIS / APTWREIS "Gurukulam" societies**) ⭐, **Eklavya Model Residential Schools** in the agency, **Jagananna Vidya Deevena-type fee-reimbursement** (the instrument that built AP's engineering-college density), central institutions of the post-bifurcation package — **IIT Tirupati, IIM Visakhapatnam, IISER Tirupati, NIT Tadepalligudem, IIIT Sri City, Central University of AP (Anantapur), IIPE Visakhapatnam** ⭐⭐ (a ready-made list for both education and AP Reorganisation Act questions), plus the older **Andhra, Sri Venkateswara, Nagarjuna, Andhra Loyola** university-college network and **RGUKT (IIIT) Nuzvid–Idupulapaya–Srikakulam** for rural talent.
  - **Health–education contrast to close with:** AP delivers babies in hospitals better than it keeps teenagers in school — the policy conclusion is **retention and learning outcomes**, not enrolment, and the target group is the first-generation learner in the agency and the aqua-mill belt.
