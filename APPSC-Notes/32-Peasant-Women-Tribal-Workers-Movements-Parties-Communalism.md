## 0. Frame

Nationalism was a coalition of struggles: **peasants** against zamindar-planter-state, **tribals** against forest laws and outsiders (*dikus*), **workers** against mill and plantation capital, **women** breaking into public life — flowing into and beyond the Congress, alongside a spectrum of **parties** (League, Mahasabha, CPI, CSP, Justice, Akali, Unionist) and **local movements** (temple-entry, Praja Mandals). Against this stood the counter-current of **communalism** — British-fostered, elite-led, culminating in Partition. *(2023 Q5a — peasant movements & their impact — and 2023 Q5b — communalism — are direct hits here.)*

## 1. Peasant movements ⭐⭐⭐ (2023 Q5a)

### 1.1 Nineteenth century (against planter & zamindar)
| Movement | Facts to hold |
|---|---|
| **INDIGO REVOLT, Bengal 1859–60** ⭐⭐ | ryots refused to sow indigo; leaders **Digambar & Bishnu Biswas**; backed by Bengali intelligentsia — **Harish Chandra Mukherjee's *Hindoo Patriot***, **Dinabandhu Mitra's play *NIL DARPAN* (1860)** ⭐; Indigo Commission 1860 → planting virtually ended in Bengal |
| **PABNA agrarian leagues 1873–76** | East Bengal ryots vs zamindars' enhancements & ejectments; legal resistance largely; → **Bengal Tenancy Act 1885** |
| **DECCAN RIOTS 1875** ⭐ | Poona-Ahmednagar ryots vs Marwari-Gujar moneylenders (debt bonds burnt); → **Deccan Agriculturists' Relief Act 1879** (File 28 §1.2) |
| Earlier: **Faraizi** (Dudu Miyan) & Moplah outbreaks (19th c.) | religion-tinged agrarian protest |

### 1.2 Gandhian & Kisan-Sabha era
- **Champaran 1917, Kheda 1918, BARDOLI 1928** — the Gandhian trio (Files 30 §5, 30 §7.2).
- **Awadh Kisan Sabha 1920 — BABA RAMCHANDRA** ⭐ (with JN/Nehru's involvement); **EKA movement 1921–22 (Madari Pasi)**; **MOPLAH rebellion, Malabar 1921** ⭐ — began agrarian-Khilafat, degenerated communally (use carefully in the communalism answer too).
- **ALL INDIA KISAN SABHA — Lucknow, 11 April 1936** ⭐⭐: founding president **SWAMI SAHAJANAND SARASWATI**, general secretary **N.G. RANGA (the Andhra kisan leader ⭐⭐** — Kisan Congress background, peasant institutes); Faizpur Congress 1936 (first village session) adopted an agrarian programme; red-flag drift towards the Left.
- **TEBHAGA, Bengal 1946–47** ⭐ (share-croppers demanded 2/3 — "adhi to tebhaga"; Floud Commission backdrop; CPI-Kisan Sabha led) and **TELANGANA ARMED STRUGGLE 1946–51** ⭐⭐ — Andhra Mahasabha + CPI vs the Nizam's jagirdars & *vetti* (Doddi Komarayya's martyrdom, July 1946; ~3,000 villages' gram raj; continued against the Indian state till 1951; women's mass role) — **the biggest peasant guerrilla war of modern India and a mandatory Andhra tie-in**.
- **Impact on the national movement (2023 Q5a second limb):** made Congress agrarian in programme (Karachi 1931; 1937 ministries' tenancy-debt laws), fed post-1947 **zamindari abolition** ⭐; built peasant class-consciousness (Kisan Sabha, CPI base); limits — kulak-rich-peasant leadership in many areas, no land-to-the-tiller until after independence.

## 2. Tribal movements ⭐⭐

| Movement | Facts |
|---|---|
| **SANTHAL HUL 1855–56** ⭐ | **Sidhu & Kanhu Murmu**, Rajmahal hills, vs dikus & mahajans; → Santhal Parganas Tenancy Act |
| **MUNDA ULGULAN 1899–1900** ⭐⭐ | **BIRSA MUNDA ("Bhagwan/Dharti Aba")** — vs beth begari & landlordism, for *Munda raj*; died Ranchi jail 1900; → **Chotanagpur Tenancy Act 1908** (khuntkatti protected) |
| **RAMPA/MANYAM rebellion 1922–24** ⭐⭐ | **ALLURI SITARAMA RAJU** — Andhra's fituri tradition vs the 1882 Madras Forest Act (podu banned, vetti); guerrilla war, caught & shot 7 May 1924 — "Manyam Veerudu" (full file 24 — the exam's favourite tribal question) |
| **TANA BHAGAT (Oraon, 1914–)** | Jatra Oraon; monotheist-Gandhian, no-rent |
| **RANI GAIDINLIU (Naga, 1930s)** ⭐ | Heraka movement vs the British; jailed 14 years; "Rani" — Nehru's title |
| Others | Kol 1831–32 (Buddho Bhagat); Bhil, Koya (1879–80 Tamman Dora ⭐), Kuki, Chenchu-Gond (Komaram Bheem — "Jal, Jangal, Zameen" ⭐, Telangana 1940) |

Common causes: **forest laws & reserved forests, moneylender-landlord (diku) penetration, missionary-state intrusion, begar** — millenarian leadership, restorative aims; the tribal stream joined Gandhi's name to local grievance (Alluri invoked and outgrew NCM).

## 3. Workers' movements ⭐⭐

- Early: Sasipada Banerjee, Lokhande's **Bombay Mill-Hands' Association 1890** (first labour organisation ⭐); wartime price-squeeze → strike wave 1919–20.
- **AITUC — 31 Oct 1920, Bombay** ⭐⭐: first president **LALA LAJPAT RAI**; Congress-Left condominium; splits (NTUF 1929 moderates; red TUC).
- Legislation: Factories Acts; **Trade Union Act 1926** ⭐ (legal status, immunities); Trade Disputes Act 1929 (curbs).
- Repression & radicalisation: **KANPUR (1924) & MEERUT CONSPIRACY CASES (1929–33)** ⭐ (31 communists incl. Dange, Muzaffar Ahmed; galvanised sympathy); **Girni Kamgar Union & the Bombay textile general strike 1928**.
- Political weight: workers in Simon boycott, CDM (Sholapur 1930 "commune" ⭐), RIN-strike wave 1946 — labour as nationalism's urban muscle; post-1947 INTUC (1947) split from AITUC.

## 4. Women's participation ⭐⭐

- **Phase-wise:** Swadeshi-era symbolic roles → **Home Rule (Besant the exemplar)** → Gandhian breakthrough: charkha-picketing-prabhat pheris made politics respectable for women (**salt & liquor picketing 1930 was substantially a women's movement** ⭐) → 1942's street leadership.
- **Gallery ⭐ (match-pairs):** **Sarojini Naidu** (Dharasana command; first Indian woman INC President 1925; first woman Governor); **Kasturba** (died Aga Khan Palace 1944); **Aruna Asaf Ali** (Gowalia Tank flag 1942); **Usha Mehta** (Congress Radio); **Matangini Hazra** (Tamluk, 73); **Kanaklata Barua** (Assam, 17); **Kalpana Datta & Pritilata Waddedar** (Chittagong); **Bina Das** (shot at Governor Jackson, 1932); **Captain Lakshmi Sahgal** (Rani Jhansi Regiment, INA); **Rani Gaidinliu**; Andhra — **Ponaka Kanakamma, Duvvuri Subbamma, Durgabai Deshmukh** ⭐ (File 22/24).
- **Organisations:** **Women's Indian Association 1917 (Besant-Margaret Cousins, Madras)**; **All India Women's Conference 1927 (Margaret Cousins)** ⭐ — Sarda Act lobbying; NCWI 1925. Franchise: limited from 1919-provinces; universal in 1950.

| Party | Role in one line |
|---|---|

| **Muslim League (1906)** | loyalist origin → Lucknow Pact partner 1916 → communal veto player (1928–39) → **Pakistan demand (Lahore Resolution, 23 March 1940** ⭐) → Direct Action & Partition |
| **Hindu Mahasabha (1915; Savarkar era 1937–)** | communal counterpart; "Hindutva" (Savarkar 1923); kept aloof from QIM |
| **CPI (1925 Kanpur; M.N. Roy's Tashkent group 1920)** ⭐ | led Kisan-worker radicalism (Meerut, Tebhaga, Telangana); zig-zags — anti-war 1939–41 → **"People's War" line 1942** (aloof from QIM ⭐) — costly to its nationalist credit |
| **Congress Socialist Party (1934 — JP, Lohia, Narendra Dev, Patwardhan)** ⭐ | Left ginger-group inside Congress; ran the 1942 underground |
| **Forward Bloc (1939 — Bose)** | left-consolidation attempt; banned 1942 |
| **Swaraj Party (1923)** | council-entry interlude (File 31 §3) |
| **JUSTICE PARTY (1916, Madras)** ⭐⭐ | Non-Brahmin manifesto; dyarchy ministries 1920–37; social justice (communal G.O.s, temple-entry support) but pro-British politics; → Periyar's DK 1944 (Files 24 & 30 §4.3 — the Andhra/Madras question) |
| **Unionist Party (Punjab — Fazl-i-Husain, Sikandar Hayat)** & **Krishak Praja (Bengal — Fazlul Huq)** | agrarian cross-communal provincial parties the League swallowed by 1946 |

## 6. Local & regional movements ⭐

- **Temple-entry & anti-caste:** **VAIKOM SATYAGRAHA 1924–25** (KP Kesava Menon, TK Madhavan; Periyar "Vaikom Virar"; Gandhi-Narayana Guru dialogue) ⭐⭐; **GURUVAYUR SATYAGRAHA 1931–32 (K. Kelappan; AKG's fast)**; **Temple Entry Proclamation, TRAVANCORE, 12 Nov 1936** ⭐ (Chithira Thirunal, C.P. Ramaswami Aiyar's advice; Gandhi's "miracle of modern times").
- **PRAJA MANDAL / States' People's movements** ⭐: **All India States' Peoples' Conference 1927**; Congress's Haripura line (1938 — moral support) → **Nehru AISPC president 1939**; struggles in Rajkot (Gandhi's fast 1939), Mysore, Travancore, Kashmir (Sheikh Abdullah's National Conference), **HYDERABAD — State Congress ban 1938, Vande Mataram movement in colleges, Andhra Mahasabha's radicalisation → Telangana struggle** ⭐ (Files 24–25).
- Regional samples: Assam's Kanaklata & Phulaguri dhawa (earlier), Odisha's **Paika legacy**, Tamil Nadu's **Vedaranyam salt march (Rajaji 1930)** ⭐, Kerala's Malabar satyagrahas, **Andhra's Mypadu-Dandi salt camps & Pedanandipadu** (File 24).

## 7. Inter-religious unity & communalism ⭐⭐⭐ (2023 Q5b)

- **Definition:** communalism = the ideology that religious community is the basis of political identity and interest — **"one religion, one political community"**; Bipan Chandra's three stages ⭐: (1) communal nationalism (my community's interests differ), (2) liberal communalism (separate but negotiable — Lucknow-era), (3) extreme/fascist communalism (irreconcilable nations — 1937 onwards).
- **British contribution (the 2023 Q5b core — yes, examine "indirectly helped"):** divide-and-rule after 1857 (Wood: "one part against the other"); **separate electorates 1909 → extended 1919 → Communal Award 1932** ⭐⭐ — the constitutional incubator; official patronage swinging between communities; communal history-writing (James Mill's periodisation ⭐); acceptance of the League as sole Muslim voice (1940s).
- **Indigenous roots (balance the answer):** socio-economic backwardness & job competition among middle classes; revivalist tinge in nationalism (Ganapati-Shivaji festivals, cow-protection riots 1893, shuddhi vs tabligh-tanzim 1920s); Urdu-Hindi controversy (1867 onwards); elite manipulation (League's landed origins, Mahasabha).
- **Unity assets to cite:** 1857's joint fight under Zafar; **Lucknow Pact 1916**; **KHILAFAT-NCM 1919–22 — the high tide of Hindu-Muslim unity** ⭐ (Gandhi: "an opportunity of a hundred years"); nationalist Muslims — **Azad ("Islam and Indian nationalism are not incompatible"), Ansari, Ajmal Khan, the Ali brothers (early), Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan's Khudai Khidmatgars ⭐, Deoband's Jamiat**; joint shrines & composite culture arguments.
- **The descent, 1922–47:** post-Khilafat riots (Kohat 1924; Gandhi's 21-day fast); Nehru Report vs 14 Points; **1937 — League's ministry rebuff & mass-contact failure → Pirpur/Shareef "atrocity" reports**; **Lahore Resolution 23 March 1940 (two-nation theory — Jinnah: "Islam and Hinduism are not religions but nations")** ⭐; wartime League-official symbiosis; **DIRECT ACTION DAY 16 Aug 1946 — the Great Calcutta Killings → Noakhali (Gandhi's pilgrimage ⭐) → Bihar** — the riot chain that made Partition "acceptable" (File 33).
- **Verdict line:** communalism was **not the growth of religiosity but the political use of religion** — a joint product of colonial policy, elite competition and nationalist neglect; its cost was Partition; its answer, the secular Constitution (File 33).
