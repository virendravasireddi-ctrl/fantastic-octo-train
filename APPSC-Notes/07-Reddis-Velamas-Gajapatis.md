# 07 · Andhra Between Empires: Reddi Kingdom, Recherla Velamas, Gajapatis (c. 1325–1518)

[← Kakatiyas](06-Kakatiyas-and-Contemporaries.md) · [Index](README.md) · [Next: Vijayanagara →](08-Vijayanagara-Empire.md)

*The century-and-a-half between Warangal's fall and Golconda's rise, when middle-sized Telugu and Odia states carried the region's language, temples and arts. APPSC treats the Reddis as a favourite; the "making of Andhra" synthesis in [File 38](38-Ancient-Medieval-Andhra-Synthesis-Practice.md) is the Mains enrichment for this file.*

---

## 1. THE REDDI KINGDOM (Kondavidu & Rajahmundry, 1325–1448)

### 1.1 Political frame
- Founder **PROLAYA VEMA REDDY** (1325–53): rose amid the post-1323 resistance; capital **Addanki**, later **KONDAVIDU** (hill-fort, Palnadu edge); coastal power from Nellore to the Godavari.
- Line: Anavota → **Anavema** → **Kumaragiri** (delegated the Godavari country to **Kataya Vema** → separate **RAJAHMUNDRY branch**) → **Pedakomati Vema** (Kondavidu) → Racha Vema; squeezed by Velamas + Bahmanis + Gajapatis + Vijayanagara; **Kondavidu fell to the Gajapatis by 1448**, Rajahmundry earlier to Gajapati-Vijayanagara contest.

### 1.2 Socio-cultural conditions
- **Self-image = restorers of dharma after the Sultanate storm:** Prolaya Vema's grants boast the **re-establishment of agraharas** and Vedic rites ("a Parashurama in gift-giving"); the Reddi state is the clearest example of **legitimacy through cultural repair**.
- Caste context: Reddis = cultivator-warrior (kapu/reddi) lineage; their rise (with Velamas) marks the **arrival of non-Kshatriya peasant-warrior castes as king-makers** — the medieval Andhra social headline.
- **Temple patronage as statecraft:** **Srisailam** — Anavema built the **Veerasiromandapa**; Prolaya Vema cut **ghat steps to Srisailam and at Ahobilam/Patalaganga**; endowments at Simhachalam, Draksharama, Tirupati.
- Ports & trade: **Motupalli continued** (a later Reddi-era charter renews merchant protection); exports of textiles, rice, diamonds.

### 1.4 Fine arts
- Court dance & music codified by royalty themselves (*Vasantarajiyam*, *Sangita Chintamani*); Vasantotsava court culture (Kumaragiri) — literature and festival as one institution; Kondavidu-fort architecture, Srisailam mandapas.

## 2. RECHERLA VELAMAS ("Padma Nayakas") of Rachakonda & Devarakonda (1368–c. 1475)

- **Anapota Nayaka** killed Musunuri Kapaya (1368) → Telangana under twin fort-capitals **Rachakonda & Devarakonda**; long see-saw alliances, usually **with the Bahmanis against Reddis + Vijayanagara**; absorbed by Bahmani expansion (and Gajapati pressure) by c. 1475.
- **Culture despite war:** **Singama Nayaka II "Sarvajna Singabhupala"** — author/patron of ***RASARNAVA SUDHAKARA*** (major Sanskrit treatise on rasa & dramaturgy) ⭐; court hosted scholars like Visvesvara (*Chamatkara Chandrika*); the **Potana** dedication legend attaches to this house; Telangana temple-tank patronage continued the Kakatiya template.
- Social note: Velamas as the second great peasant-warrior lineage-state — the Reddi-Velama rivalry is the political form of a shared social revolution.

## 3. THE GAJAPATIS of Odisha in Andhra (1435–c. 1541)

- **Kapilendra Gajapati** (founded the Suryavamsi line, 1435): stormed down the coast — Rajahmundry, Kondavidu, even to the Tamil country; son **Hamvira** ruled the southern conquests.
- **Purushottama** (Kanchi-Kaveri legend); **PRATAPARUDRA GAJAPATI (1497–1540)** — the devotee-king: **Chaitanya lived at Puri (1510–33)** and transformed him — Gajapati court becomes Vaishnava-bhakti court ⭐ (cross-link File 04).
- **Krishnadevaraya's Kalinga war (1512–19)** stripped the Gajapatis of coastal Andhra: **Udayagiri (1514) → Kondavidu (1515, prince Virabhadra captured) → Kondapalli → victory pillar at POTNURU (near Simhachalam, 1516)**; peace sealed by KDR's **marriage to a Gajapati princess (Tukka/Annapurna in the sources)** and return of lands **north of the Krishna** ⭐ — the Krishna as the agreed border.
- **Odia vernacular growth (syllabus point):** **Sarala Das's Odia *Mahabharata*** (Kapilendra's era) and the **Panchasakha** poets (**Jagannatha Das's *Bhagabata***, Balarama Das, Achyutananda, Yasovanta, Ananta) under Prataparudra — the Odia parallel to Telugu's Potana moment; **Jagannath cult** radiating into north-coastal Andhra (Simhachalam's world).

## 4. Socio-cultural synthesis of the interlude (Mains paragraph)

Between 1323 and 1518 no empire held the Telugu country, yet its culture accelerated: peasant-warrior dynasties (Reddi, Velama) legitimised themselves by **rebuilding the agrahara-temple order** and by **patronising Telugu and Sanskrit letters in person** (kings writing on dance, music, poetics); the Kavitraya's Mahabharata was **completed** (Errana), courtly kavya scaled its peak (**Srinatha**), bhakti found its Telugu classic (**Potana**) outside court patronage altogether, and folk memory entered literature (Palnati Veera Charitra). Meanwhile Odisha's Gajapatis stitched coastal Andhra into a Jagannath-Vaishnava world just as Chaitanya's movement crested. **The "gap" between empires was in fact Telugu literature's second golden age** — that inversion is the essay.

## 5. Prelims nuggets

1. Prolaya Vema Reddy — capital Addanki → Kondavidu; agrahara restoration; Srisailam/Ahobilam steps.
2. **Errana** — court of Prolaya Vema; completed Aranya Parva; "Prabandha Parameshvara".
3. **Srinatha** — Kavi Sarvabhauma; Vidyadhikari of Pedakomati Vema; Kashikhandam → Rajahmundry court; **kanakabhishekam under Devaraya II**; Palnati Veera Charitra (dwipada).
4. Kumaragiri — *Vasantarajiyam* (dance); Pedakomati Vema — *Sangita Chintamani*; Kataya Vema — Kalidasa commentaries. (Kings as shastra-authors — unique cluster.)
5. **Rasarnava Sudhakara — Sarvajna Singabhupala (Velama, Rachakonda)**.
6. Potana — Bhagavatamu dedicated to Rama, not a king (legend ties refusal to Singama demand).
7. Gajapatis: Kapilendra 1435; Prataparudra + Chaitanya; **Sarala Das & Panchasakha = Odia vernacular bloom**.
8. KDR vs Gajapati: Udayagiri 1514 → Kondavidu 1515 → **Potnuru pillar 1516** → marriage + Krishna-river border.
9. Rachakonda & Devarakonda = Velama capitals; Anapota killed Kapaya 1368.

## 6. Mains angles

1. **"Evaluate the Reddi kingdom's contribution to Telugu literature and religious culture."** Use §1.2–1.4; thesis: cultural repair as state policy.
2. **"Srinatha is the representative man of 15th-century Andhra."** Court poet, education minister, pilgrim of shrines (Bhimeshvara/Kashi themes), folk-epic collector, celebrated at Vijayanagara — one career maps the whole inter-state cultural network.
3. **"Kings who wrote shastras: what does Reddi-Velama royal authorship tell us about medieval kingship?"** Legitimacy via learning; compare Someshvara III's Manasollasa (Unit 1) and KDR's Amuktamalyada (next file).
4. **"The Gajapati episode integrated coastal Andhra into two worlds."** Militarily into Odisha's empire; devotionally into the Jagannath-Chaitanya orbit; then the KDR settlement fixed the Krishna frontier — Andhra's north-coast identity argument.
