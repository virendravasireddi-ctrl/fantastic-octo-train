## 0. Quick facts

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Period | Feudatories of Kalyani Chalukyas from c. 1000; **sovereign from Rudradeva's Anumakonda inscription, 1163**; fell **1323** |
| Capitals | **Anumakonda (Hanamkonda) → Orugallu (Warangal, "Ekashila Nagaram")** |
| Great rulers | Rudradeva → Mahadeva → **GANAPATIDEVA (1199–1262)** → **RUDRAMADEVI (1262–89)** → **PRATAPARUDRA II (1289–1323)** |
| Origin | Records indicate **fourth-varna (Shudra) origin, proudly stated** — textbook case of warrior status won by service, not birth |
| Sources | Inscriptions (mostly **Telugu** — the shift is itself the exam point), Motupalli charter, **Marco Polo**, Vilasa grant (retrospective), *Prataparudra Yashobhushanam*, *Kridabhiramam* |

**Political spine in five lines:** Rudradeva declared independence (1163) as Kalyani collapsed; Ganapatideva unified almost the whole Telugu-speaking land for the first time (deltas taken from Velanati Chodas; Nellore Chodas as allies); **Rudramadevi** — one of India's few sovereign queens — ruled as "Rudradeva Maharaja", died fighting the rebel Kayastha **Ambadeva** (**Chandupatla inscription, 1289**); Prataparudra II withstood repeated Sultanate attacks (1303 repulse, **1310 Kafur — tribute**, 1318, 1321 six-month siege survived) before **Ulugh Khan took Warangal in 1323**; tradition (Vilasa grant) says he died on the Narmada's banks en route to Delhi.

## 1. Socio-cultural conditions

### 1.1 Society
- **Open warrior society:** Kakatiyas and their nayakas advertised Shudra origins as honourable — inscriptions show **status followed service** (Cynthia Talbot's studies); the great right-hand/left-hand divide of the Tamil country mattered less here.
- **Nayankara in embryo:** land assignments to warrior-chiefs (**nayakas**) in return for troops — the system **Vijayanagara later perfected**; records mention the "**Bahattara Niyogas**" (72 departments/offices) under a chief superintendent.
- **Women's agency at the top:** Rudramadevi's reign (Marco Polo praises the justice of the "queen of Mutfili"); women appear as donors and tank-builders; **sati-stones and hero-stones (veeragallu)** show the warrior ethos's cost.
- **Peasantisation of the plateau:** the **TANK REVOLUTION** — chains of tanks (*golusukattu cheruvulu*): **Pakhal, Ramappa, Lakhnavaram, Ghanpur** lakes; **dasabandha** (tax-free tenure for tank upkeep) institutionalised — dry Telangana made rice-growing. ⭐ This is THE Kakatiya socio-economic answer.
- **Trade:** **Motupalli port — Ganapatideva's ABHAYA SHASANAM (1244/45)**, a security charter fixing duties and guaranteeing foreign merchants' safety; **Marco Polo landed at Motupalli (c. 1290s)** and praised the kingdom's **diamonds** (Krishna mines) and fine **muslins ("buckrams")**.

### 1.2 Religion
- **Shaivism dominant** in layered forms: Kalamukha/Pashupata legacies → **Saiva-Siddhanta GOLAKI MATHA** network (Ganapatideva's diksha-guru **Visvesvara Shivacharya** from the Golaki matha of central India) → popular **Veerashaiva/Aradhya** currents (Palkuriki Somanatha).
- Early Jain phase (Padmakshi temple, Hanamkonda) faded; Vaishnavism present (Ranganatha Ramayanamu!); village goddesses and vira-cults thrived — a genuinely plural countryside.

## 2. Language & literature — **Telugu becomes the language of state**

⭐ Core thesis: under the Kakatiyas the **majority of public inscriptions are in Telugu** for the first time — the vernacular moved from poetry (Vengi age) to **administration and everyday record**.

| Author | Work | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **TIKKANA Somayaji** (13th c., at Nellore Choda **Manumasiddhi's** court — Ganapatideva's ally) | **Andhra Mahabharatamu, 15 parvas** (Virata onward); *Nirvachanottara Ramayanamu* | Second of the **Kavitraya**; titles "**Kavi Brahma**", "**Ubhaya-kavi-mitra**" (friend of both Sanskrit & Telugu poets); dedicated his epic to **Hariharanatha** (Shiva-Vishnu united) — a deliberate gesture of sectarian peace |
| **KETANA** ("Abhinava Dandi") | *Dasakumara Charitramu* (dedicated to Tikkana!); ***Andhra Bhashabhushanamu*** — **first Telugu grammar written in Telugu**; *Vijnaneshvaramu* (Telugu rendering of the Mitakshara!) | grammar + law in the vernacular = language of governance |
| **Marana** (Tikkana's disciple) | *Markandeya Puranamu* | Purana translation stream continues |
| **Baddena** | *Nitisastra Muktavali*; ***Sumati Satakam*** (traditional attribution) | the **sataka** (century-of-verses) genre's police-station of morals — Telugu's most memorised verses |
| **PALKURIKI SOMANATHA** | ***Basava Puranamu*, *Panditaradhya Charitramu*** in **dwipada** | Veerashaiva people's poetry — "plain Telugu of the Jangamas", parallel canon to the courtly champu |
| **GONA BUDDA REDDY** | ***Ranganatha Ramayanamu*** (dwipada) | **first complete Telugu Ramayana** — for singing, not just reciting |
| **Vidyanatha** (Sanskrit) | ***Prataparudra Yashobhushanam*** | poetics treatise with Prataparudra II as the running example — king as alankara-shastra's hero |
| **JAYAPA SENANI (Jayana)**, Ganapatideva's elephant-commander (Gaja-sahini) | ***NRITTA RATNAVALI*** (Sanskrit, c. 1250s; with Gita/Vadya Ratnavali attributed) | India-class **dance treatise** — codifies desi alongside marga, including **PERINI** ⭐ |
| (Post-Kakatiya source) Vinukonda Vallabharaya | *Kridabhiramam* (c. 1400) | street-level portrait of Orugallu — social-history goldmine |

## 3. Art, architecture, fine arts

### 3.1 Kakatiya temple style (out of the Kalyani-Chalukyan idiom)
| Monument | Facts to quote |
|---|---|
| **Thousand Pillar Temple (Rudreshvara), Hanamkonda, 1163** | built by **Rudradeva**; **trikuta** (Shiva–Vishnu–Surya on one platform — theology of harmony in plan); black-basalt polish, monolithic Nandi |
| **RAMAPPA TEMPLE (Rudreshvara), Palampet, 1213** | built by general **Recherla Rudra** under Ganapatideva; **UNESCO World Heritage 2021** ("Kakatiya Rudreshwara Temple"); engineering — **sandbox foundations** and **floating (lightweight porous) bricks** in the shikhara; **bracket figures of dancers (madanikas/Naginis)** in polished basalt; sculpted **Perini poses** (the temple is the dance-manual in stone); possibly India's only major temple popularly named after its **sculptor (Ramappa)** |
| **Warangal Fort** | Ekashila hill + triple ring of walls; the four free-standing **KIRTI-TORANAS** (gateways) — the icon of Kakatiya art (adopted in Telangana's state emblem); Svayambhu temple remains |
| **Ghanpur (Kota Gullu), Pillalamarri temples** | the style's spread with nayaka patronage |

### 3.2 Performing & applied arts
- **PERINI SHIVATANDAVAM** — the warriors' ecstatic Shaiva dance performed before battle; codified in Jayapa's Nritta Ratnavali; **revived in the 20th century by Nataraja Ramakrishna** ⭐ (favourite one-liner).
- Metal & weaving crafts of the realm: **Pembarthi sheet-brass work** (temple ritual craft), fine cottons Marco Polo admired; diamond mining on the Krishna (the later "Golconda" trade's roots).

## 5. Bridge: Musunuri Nayakas (1325–1368) — remember as §2 connective tissue

- **Prolaya Nayaka** (base at Rekapalli; the **VILASA GRANT** — the classic description of post-1323 devastation and of **restoring agraharas and Vedic order**).
- **Kapaya Nayaka**: with ~75 confederate nayakas **retook Warangal (1336)**; styled "**Andhradesadhishvara**" and even "**Andhra Suratrana** (Sultan of Andhra)" ⭐ — vernacular kingship borrowing Sultanate vocabulary; ceded **Golconda fort (with the turquoise throne, *Takht-i-Firoza*) to the Bahmanis (c. 1364)**; killed by Recherla **Anapota Nayaka** at **Bhimavaram (1368)** → Velama age in Telangana.

## 6. Prelims nuggets

1. Anumakonda inscription 1163 = Rudradeva's independence + Thousand Pillar Temple same reign.
2. Motupalli **Abhaya Shasanam** — Ganapatideva, 1244/45; Marco Polo landed there; "Mutfili" = Motupalli.
3. Rudramadevi = "Rudradeva Maharaja"; died vs **Ambadeva** — **Chandupatla inscription (1289)**.
4. Ramappa temple 1213 — Recherla Rudra; **UNESCO 2021**; sandbox foundation, floating bricks.
5. **Nritta Ratnavali — Jayapa Senani**; Perini revived by **Nataraja Ramakrishna**.
6. Tikkana = Kavi Brahma, 15 parvas, Hariharanatha dedication, Manumasiddhi's court.
7. Ketana — first Telugu grammar (*Andhra Bhashabhushanamu*) + Telugu Mitakshara (*Vijnaneshvaramu*).
8. Ranganatha Ramayanamu — Gona Budda Reddy (dwipada); Basava Puranamu — Palkuriki (dwipada).
9. Prataparudra Yashobhushanam — Vidyanatha.
10. Golaki matha — Saiva-Siddhanta network; guru Visvesvara Shivacharya.
11. Tanks: Pakhal, Ramappa, Lakhnavaram; **dasabandha** tenure.
12. **Sangita Ratnakara — Sharngadeva — Yadava Devagiri court** (not Kakatiya — classic trap).
13. Kapaya Nayaka: Warangal 1336; title Andhra Suratrana; Golconda ceded 1364; killed 1368 (Bhimavaram).

## 7. Mains angles

1. **"Kakatiya rule made Telangana."** Tank irrigation + dasabandha + nayaka settlement = agrarian integration; Telugu as record-language = administrative integration; Motupalli charter = commercial integration; one dynasty, one region-formation argument.
2. **"Assess Kakatiya contributions to Telugu literature and the fine arts."** Court Sanskrit (Vidyanatha) + Telugu champu (Tikkana line) + popular dwipada (Palkuriki, Gona Budda) + grammar/law in Telugu (Ketana) + dance codification (Jayapa/Perini) + temple-as-art (Ramappa).
3. **"Rudramadevi's reign tests medieval attitudes to female sovereignty."** Male regnal persona, nobles' revolts (Ambadeva), foreign admiration (Polo), succession via daughter's son (Prataparudra) — evidence-rich mini-essay.
4. **"The fall of Warangal (1323) was an end and a beginning."** Link to Musunuri revival, Vijayanagara-Bahmani world, and the "making of Andhra" synthesis (File 35).
