## 0. Frame

Modern Andhra begins on the **Coromandel coast**: European factories at **Masulipatnam, Nizampatnam, Pulicat and Yanam** (17th c.) → the **Northern Circars ceded to the Company (1765–66)** → the **Ceded Districts (Rayalaseema) acquired from the Nizam (1800)** → the whole Telugu country (minus the Nizam's Telangana) absorbed into the **Madras Presidency**. The Company then re-engineered rural Andhra with **two rival land systems** — the **Permanent (Zamindari) Settlement of 1802** in the Circars and **Munro's Ryotwari** in the Ceded Districts — while crushing the old military elite of **poligars (palegallu)** and reducing rajas to **"little kings"** of hollow crowns. Four Company officials — **Thomas Munro, Colin Mackenzie, C.P. Brown, Arthur Cotton** — are APPSC's favourite quartet (**2023 Q8(b) asked exactly Munro–Cotton–Brown**).

## 1. European trade establishments in Andhra (17th–18th c.) ⭐⭐

*(Detail on the five Companies and the Carnatic Wars is in [File 22](22-Advent-of-Europeans-in-India.md); this section is the Andhra-specific sheet.)*

| Power | Andhra stations | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dutch (VOC)** | **Masulipatnam (1605)**, **Nizampatnam/"Petapoli" (1606)**, **Pulicat/Fort Geldria (1610)** — Coromandel HQ & pagoda mint; Bhimunipatnam, Palakollu, Draksharama, Jagannathapuram (Kakinada) | came for **textiles to barter for Indonesian spices**; Andhra was their Indian core |
| **English (EIC)** | **MASULIPATNAM (1611)** — first English factory on the east coast (ship *Globe*, Capt. Hippon); Armagaon (1626); later **Madapollam, Injaram (Godavari delta), Visakhapatnam (c. 1682)** | **"Golden Firman" (1632)** of Abdullah Qutb Shah — free trade for 500 pagodas/yr; **Madras (1639)** grew out of this Andhra trade |
| **French** | **Masulipatnam (1669)**, **YANAM (Godavari delta, 1720s)** — French till **1954** | Bussy's decade at Hyderabad (1751–58) won France the **Northern Circars** |
| **Portuguese/Danes** | San Thome nearby; Danes marginal | — |

- **Export engine:** Coromandel **cotton textiles — kalamkari/chintz of Masulipatnam**, muslins of the Godavari delta ("madapollam" entered English as a cloth name), plus **Golconda diamonds (Kollur)** — paid for in **New-World silver** (the bullion-for-cloth circuit).
- **Trade → territory:** Carnatic Wars fought largely on the Andhra coast — **Bobbili (1757)**, **Forde takes Masulipatnam (1759)** → **Northern Circars to the EIC by Shah Alam II's firman (1765) + treaty with Nizam Ali Khan (12 Nov 1766)**; **Guntur Circar added 1788**.

## 2. Andhra under Company rule — building "British Andhra" ⭐⭐

### 2.1 Acquisition in three bites
1. **Northern Circars (1765–66, Guntur 1788):** Chicacole (Srikakulam), Rajahmundry, Ellore, Kondapalli, Guntur — the coastal belt; run first by a **Chief and Council at Masulipatnam**, from **1794 by Collectors** under the Board of Revenue (regular districts organised 1802–03).
2. **Ceded Districts (1800):** after the 4th Mysore War, the **Nizam ceded** the districts he had gained in 1792/1799 (**Bellary, Cuddapah, Anantapur tracts**) to pay for the **subsidiary force** (subsidiary alliance, 1798/1800) — hence **"Rayalaseema = the Ceded Districts"**; **Munro their first Principal Collector (1800–07)**. **Kurnool** remained under its **Nawab until 1839**, when Ghulam Rasul Khan was deposed (arms-conspiracy charge) and the state annexed.
3. **The rest by treaty:** the **Nizam of Hyderabad** (first prince to sign the **subsidiary alliance, 1798**) kept Telangana as a **native state**; petty states like **Banganapalle** (Nawabi enclave in Kurnool) survived inside British Andhra.

### 2.2 Early armed resistance (before 1857 ⭐)
- **Battle of Padmanabham (10 July 1794):** **Vijaya Rama Raju of VIZIANAGARAM** died fighting the Company's demand to cut his troops and pay tribute — the great northern zamindari then reinstated as a loyal estate.
- **Poligar risings (1799–1805):** Rayalaseema and Nellore palegallu resisted disarmament; suppressed by Munro's administration (see §3.2).
- **Hill-chief/tribal risings:** Palakonda & Gumsur disturbances; **Rampa country's chronic revolts (major rising 1879)** — muttadar-Koya grievances that resurface with **Alluri (1922–24, File 39)**.
- **UYYALAWADA NARASIMHA REDDY — the Renadu (Koilkuntla) revolt, 1846–47** ⭐: a Kurnool poligar-family leader whose kattubadi (pension) grievance became a popular anti-Company rising of some thousands; defeated, **hanged 22 Feb 1847** — Andhra's most celebrated pre-1857 martyr.
- **1857 in Andhra:** muted — coastal Andhra stayed quiet; sympathetic stirrings (e.g., Kadapa/Kurnool conspiracies) were contained. *Mains line: Andhra's "1857" had already happened piecemeal — 1794 Padmanabham, the poligar wars, 1846 Renadu.*

### 2.3 Economic change under the Company
- **Deindustrialisation:** Masulipatnam's chintz/kalamkari exports collapsed (British mill cloth, loss of court markets, the 1818–30s weaver distress); the great port itself decayed (silting + the catastrophic **1864 cyclone**).
- **Famines:** **Guntur famine 1832–33 ("Dokkala karuvu" — famine of skeletons, the Nandana year)** killed lakhs; the **Great Madras famine 1876–78 ("Dhatu karuvu")** devastated Rayalaseema — backdrop to missionary mass conversions (File 37).
- **Canal-and-anicut turnaround:** **Cotton's Godavari anicut (1847–52)** and the **Krishna anicut (1852–55)** turned the deltas into rice bowls and knit them with navigation canals (§4.4) — the deltas' prosperity later financed the Andhra movement's schools, presses and libraries.
- **Railways & new towns:** Madras Railway from the 1850s; **Bezwada (Vijayawada)** rose as rail-canal hub; Madras city became the Telugu elite's capital (till 1953).

## 3. The two land systems ⭐⭐ (compare-and-contrast is a standard question)

### 3.1 Zamindari / Permanent Settlement in Andhra
- **Madras Regulation XXV of 1802** extended Cornwallis's Bengal model to the **Northern Circars**: revenue fixed **in perpetuity** with **zamindars** as proprietors; ancient military-chief families became pure rent-collecting landlords.
- **Great Andhra zamindaris:** **Vizianagaram** (the largest), **Bobbili, Pithapuram, Venkatagiri, Nuzvid, Challapalli, Munagala, Kalahasti** — plus new "proprietary estates" auctioned out of Company **haveli** (crown) lands.
- **Effects:** rack-renting and absentee landlordism; many estates failed and were sold/resumed → ryotwari crept into the Circars; the zamindar-rajas survived as **courtly patrons** (Vizianagaram's patronage of Gurajada, Pithapuram's of reformers — Files 37–39) until **estate abolition (Madras Estates Abolition Act, 1948)**.

### 3.2 Poligari system and its destruction
- **Origin:** Vijayanagara's **palayam/nayankara** order (File 16) — **palegallu (poligars)** held forts and armed retainers for military service and local policing (kavali fees).
- The Company saw them as **rival armed powers**: campaigns of **1799–1805** (parallel to the Tamil poligar wars of Kattabomman) disarmed them; forts razed; policing taken over; chiefs pensioned on **kattubadi** quit-rents or converted into ordinary zamindars.
- **Munro's Ceded-Districts settlement** was precisely this — some **80 poligars** reduced in his first years; the disaffected simmered on (→ **1846 Renadu revolt**).
- **"Native states and little kings":** beyond the one great native state (**Hyderabad**) and mini-state (**Banganapalle**), Andhra's samsthanams — Vizianagaram, Venkatagiri, Bobbili, Pithapuram, Gadwal (Telangana side) — were **"little kings"**: ritual sovereignty, durbars and titles retained, real power gone (the "**hollow crown**" — Nicholas Dirks's phrase for colonial little kingdoms).

### 3.3 Ryotwari (Munro system)
- Pioneered by **Alexander Read & Thomas Munro** (Baramahal, 1792) → applied by **Munro in the Ceded Districts (1800–07)** → made Madras policy when **Munro became Governor (1820)**.
- **Features:** settlement **directly with each ryot** (no zamindar); land surveyed and assessed field-by-field; **annual/periodic revision** (not permanent); the ryot a registered occupant — could sell/mortgage, but **revenue demand was high** (nominally ⅓–½ of gross produce) and collected rigidly in cash.
- **Evaluation:** removed parasitic intermediaries and fit Rayalaseema's poligar-broken society; but over-assessment + cash demand + famine cycles = rural indebtedness (the moneylender replaced the zamindar). *A brief "village-lease" experiment (1808–20s) intervened before ryotwari won.*

| Point | Zamindari (Circars, 1802) | Ryotwari (Ceded Dists → most of Andhra) |
|---|---|---|
| State's contract with | Zamindar (fixed forever) | Each ryot (revisable) |
| Proprietor | Zamindar | Ryot (occupancy right) |
| Risk | Ryot rack-rented | Ryot over-assessed |
| Legacy | Estates till 1948; courtly patronage | Dominant Madras system; Munro cult |

## 4. The Company's great officials in Andhra ⭐⭐ (2023 PYQ set)

### 4.1 Sir THOMAS MUNRO (1761–1827)
- Glasgow-born soldier-administrator: **Baramahal (1792, with Read — ryotwari born)** → Kanara (1799) → **Principal Collector, Ceded Districts (1800–07)** — pacified the poligars, settled ryotwari, learnt Telugu/Kannada, toured village-by-village.
- **Judicial Commission (1814):** restored **native agency** — village **munsiffs and panchayats**, collector-magistrates — against wholesale Anglicisation ("**British rule should prepare Indians to govern themselves**" — his minute of 1824 is quotable).
- **Governor of Madras (1820–27):** ryotwari made general; **education minutes (1822, 1826)** — the famous **survey of indigenous schools** and a plan for state-aided vernacular education.
- Died of **cholera at Pattikonda (Kurnool district), 6 July 1827**, touring the Ceded Districts; Rayalaseema folk memory deified the just "**Munrolappa**" (legends attach him to Mantralayam). Equestrian statue, Madras.

### 4.2 Colonel COLIN MACKENZIE (1754–1821)
- Madras Engineers; surveyed the **Nizam's dominions and Mysore** (after 1799); **first Surveyor-General of India (1815)**.
- **The Mackenzie Collection** — the largest early archive of South Indian history: thousands of **manuscripts, kaifiyats (village histories), inscriptions, coins, plans and drawings**, gathered through trained **Telugu Brahmin assistants — the KAVALI brothers (Kavali Venkata Boriah, then Lakshmayya)** — the first Indian "field historians".
- **Reported the AMARAVATI stupa (1797; detailed survey 1816)** ⭐ — modern rediscovery of Andhra's Buddhist past (File 06 tie-in).
- *Mains line:* Munro taxed the country, **Mackenzie catalogued its memory** — colonial knowledge-gathering that accidentally founded Andhra historiography.

### 4.3 CHARLES PHILIP BROWN (C.P. Brown, 1798–1884) — "Andhra Bhashoddharaka"
- Madras Civil Servant (from 1817), long posted at **CUDDAPAH (Kadapa)**; spent his private fortune employing **pundits to collect, copy and edit Telugu manuscripts** when court patronage had died — *"Telugu literature was dying out; the flame was flickering… I lit it"* (his own claim, near-true).
- **Rescued VEMANA** ⭐: collected and published the *Verses of Vemana* (1829; enlarged 1839) with English translation — made the people's poet canonical (Files 15/10 tie-in).
- **Telugu–English (1852) and English–Telugu dictionaries**, grammar, editions of the *Mahabharatam*, *Vasucharitra*, "Wars of the Rajas", cyclic tables of dates; his manuscripts seeded the **Madras Oriental Manuscripts Library**.
- Memorialised in the **C.P. Brown Memorial Library, Kadapa**; the bridge figure between old pandit learning and the print-age Telugu revival (File 37).

### 4.4 Sir ARTHUR COTTON (1803–99) — "the Delta Architect"
- Madras Engineers; after the Coleroon anicuts (1830s), built the **GODAVARI ANICUT at Dowleswaram (1847–52)** — barrage + delta canal grid conceived also as **navigation**; the **Krishna anicut at Bezwada (1852–55**, executed by Maj. Charles Orr on Cotton's plan) followed.
- Turned famine-struck deltas into the **"rice bowl of the south"**; advocated a national scheme of **irrigation-cum-navigation canals** (Godavari–Krishna–Buckingham Canal chain) and criticised railway-first policy before the 1878 Famine Commission.
- Konaseema still venerates "**Cotton dora**": statues on the barrage, a **Cotton Museum (Rajahmundry)**; the modern barrage (1970–82) is named **Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage** ⭐.

## 5. Prelims nuggets

1. **Masulipatnam:** Dutch **1605**, English **1611** (first east-coast factory), French 1669; **Golden Firman 1632**.
2. **Pulicat/Fort Geldria (1610)** — Dutch Coromandel HQ; **Yanam** — French, till **1954**.
3. **Northern Circars:** firman of **Shah Alam II, 1765** + **Nizam treaty 12 Nov 1766**; **Guntur 1788**.
4. **Ceded Districts = Rayalaseema, 1800** (Nizam → Company, subsidiary-force payment); **Kurnool Nawabi annexed 1839**.
5. **Padmanabham 1794** — Vijaya Rama Raju (Vizianagaram) killed.
6. **Uyyalawada Narasimha Reddy** — Renadu revolt **1846–47**, hanged **22 Feb 1847**.
7. **Permanent Settlement in Madras: Regulation XXV of 1802** (Circars zamindaris — Vizianagaram largest).
8. **Ryotwari:** Read–**Munro**, Baramahal 1792 → Ceded Districts 1800–07 → Madras-wide from 1820.
9. **Munro** — Governor of Madras **1820–27**; died **Pattikonda 1827**; judicial (panchayat/munsiff) and education minutes.
10. **Mackenzie** — **first Surveyor-General of India (1815)**; **Kavali brothers**; **Amaravati 1797/1816**; kaifiyats.
11. **C.P. Brown** — Kadapa; **Vemana verses 1829**; **Telugu–English dictionary 1852**; "Andhra Bhashoddharaka".
12. **Arthur Cotton** — **Dowleswaram anicut 1847–52**; Krishna anicut 1852–55; Cotton Barrage/Museum.
13. Famines: **1832–33 Guntur "Dokkala karuvu"**; **1876–78 "Dhatu karuvu"**.
14. **Battle of Bobbili 1757**; Forde takes Masulipatnam **1759** (File 22).

## 6. Mains angles

1. **"From factory to province."** Trace Masulipatnam 1611 → Circars 1765–66 → Ceded Districts 1800 → Madras Presidency Andhra — commerce becoming sovereignty on the Coromandel.
2. **"Two settlements, two Andhras."** Compare zamindari Circars and ryotwari Rayalaseema — design, working, social consequences (rack-rent vs over-assessment; patron-rajas vs moneylenders).
3. **"The poligar to the pensioner: the making of little kings."** Disarmament 1799–1805, kattubadi, hollow-crown samsthanams — with Padmanabham 1794 and Renadu 1846 as the resistance bookends.
4. **2023 Q8(b) drill:** Munro (ryotwari + native agency + education), Cotton (deltas + navigation), Brown (Vemana + dictionaries + manuscript rescue) — 3×3 sentences each, close with their Andhra memorials (Pattikonda/Munrolappa, Cotton Barrage, Brown Library Kadapa).
5. **"Colonial rule destroyed and documented."** Deindustrialised Masulipatnam and famine cycles vs Mackenzie's archive, Brown's revival, Cotton's deltas — write a balanced "impact of Company rule on Andhra".
