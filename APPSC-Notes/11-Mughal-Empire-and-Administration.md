# 11 · The Mughal Empire (1526–1707/1857) — Political Narrative & Administration

[← Vernaculars & Fine Arts (Unit 2)](10-Vernaculars-Scripts-FineArts-Practice.md) · [Index](README.md) · [Next: Mughal Socio-Religious Life & Culture →](12-Mughal-Socio-Religious-Life-and-Culture.md)

## 0. Frame

The Timurid-Chaghtai house founded India's second durable pan-Indian empire after the Guptas — a **centralised, Persianate, cash-nexus state** resting on three pillars: the **mansabdari–jagirdari** service nobility, a **measured land-revenue (zabti) system** built on Sher Shah's foundations, and a deliberate ideology of **sulh-i-kul** (universal peace) that co-opted Rajputs and Hindus into the ruling class. Decisive for Andhra: **Shah Jahan's 1636 submission treaties** with Bijapur & Golconda and **Aurangzeb's annexation of Bijapur (1686) and Golconda (1687)** — after which the 27-year Deccan war became the empire's grave, and Mughal Deccan passed (1724) to the **Asaf Jahi Nizams of Hyderabad**.

| Emperor | Reign | One line |
|---|---|---|
| **Babur** | 1526–30 | Founder; Panipat-I, Khanwa; *Baburnama* |
| **Humayun** | 1530–40, 1555–56 | Lost to Sher Shah; regained throne; died on library stairs |
| *(Sur interregnum)* | 1540–55 | **Sher Shah Suri** — the administrative blueprint |
| **Akbar** | 1556–1605 | Empire-builder; mansabdari; sulh-i-kul; Dahsala |
| **Jahangir** | 1605–27 | Nur Jahan junta; Roe's embassy; painting's peak |
| **Shah Jahan** | 1628–58 | Architecture's golden age; Deccan submission treaties |
| **Aurangzeb "Alamgir"** | 1658–1707 | Greatest extent + orthodox reaction; Deccan ulcer |
| **Later Mughals** | 1707–1857 | Decline; Nadir Shah 1739; Buxar 1764; puppet-emperors |

---

## 1. Babur (1526–1530) — the founder

- **Zahiruddin Muhammad Babur**: Timurid (paternal) + Chingizid (maternal via mother) prince of **Farghana**; lost Samarkand thrice, took **Kabul (1504)**, then India.
- **Four battles that made the empire:**
  | Battle | Year | Beaten | Note |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Panipat-I** | 1526 | Ibrahim Lodi | ended the Sultanate; **tulughma** flanking + **artillery** (Ustad Ali Quli, Mustafa) behind carts (**araba/"Ottoman device"**) |
  | **Khanwa** | 1527 | Rana Sanga (Mewar) | broke Rajput confederacy; took title **Ghazi**; jihad-declaration to rally troops |
  | **Chanderi** | 1528 | Medini Rai | Malwa Rajputs |
  | **Ghaghra** | 1529 | Afghan–Bengal coalition | secured the east |
- **Legacy:** introduced **gunpowder field-artillery** and the tulughma to India; wrote the ***Tuzuk-i-Baburi / Baburnama*** in **Chaghtai Turki** (candid memoir — ranks **Krishnadevaraya** the most powerful Indian king of the day ⭐); ranked India rich but unlovely. Died at Agra 1530; buried finally at **Kabul** by his wish.

---

## 2. Humayun (1530–40, 1555–56) & the Sur interregnum

- **Humayun** ("fortunate" — ironically): divided his inheritance among brothers (Kamran, Askari, Hindal); lost to the Afghan **Sher Khan** at **Chausa (1539)** and **Kannauj/Bilgram (1540)** → **15-year exile** (Sind, then Safavid Persia — Shah Tahmasp).
- Recovered Delhi **1555** with Persian help (general **Bairam Khan**); died **1556** falling down the stairs of his library **Sher Mandal** ("stumbled out of life as he had stumbled through it" — Lane-Poole). His widow **Bega/Haji Begum** built **Humayun's Tomb** — the first grand **charbagh** garden-tomb, template of the Taj (see File 12).

### Sher Shah Suri (1540–45) — the interregnum that taught the Mughals to govern ⭐
- Afghan of Bihar (born Farid Khan; "Sher Khan" for killing a tiger); built the **Sur empire** from Bengal to the Punjab.
- **Revenue reform (the model Akbar completed):** land **measurement (with a standard *sikandari gaz* and hemp *jarib*)**, soil-graded rates, demand fixed at **~1/3 of produce**, cultivator issued a **patta** (title deed) and giving a **qabuliyat** (deed of agreement); *nasaq* also used.
- **The RUPIYA** — standardised **silver rupiya (178 grains)** + copper **dam** — direct ancestor of the modern rupee ⭐; trimetallic system.
- **Communications:** rebuilt the **Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-i-Azam**, Sonargaon→Attock); **1,700 sarais** (also served as **dak-chowki** post relays); revived trade.
- **Buildings:** **Rohtasgarh**; **Purana Qila** & the **Qila-i-Kuhna mosque** (Delhi); the octagonal **Sasaram tomb** in a tank — masterpiece of pre-Mughal Indo-Islamic architecture.
- **Administration:** strong central control, spies, *iqta*-like *sarkar–pargana* structure with paired **shiqdar-i-shiqdaran & munsif-i-munsifan**. *Standard verdict: "the forerunner of Akbar."*

---

## 3. Akbar (1556–1605) — the real empire-builder

- Accession at 13 after **Second Battle of Panipat (1556)** — regent **Bairam Khan** defeated **Hemu** (the Hindu general-claimant of Adil Shah Sur). **Petticoat government** phase (Maham Anga) till 1562.
- **Conquests (a sub-continental empire):** Malwa; **Gondwana** (1564, **Rani Durgavati**'s heroic defence); **Chittor (1568)** & Ranthambhor (Rajput submission, except Mewar); **Gujarat (1573)** — victory commemorated by the **Buland Darwaza** at Fatehpur Sikri; **Bengal (1576, Haldighati year)**; Kabul, Kashmir (1586), Sind, Orissa, Baluchistan, Kandahar; **Deccan — Ahmadnagar (Chand Bibi's defence, 1596–1600), Asirgarh (1601)** — the Deccan door that Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb would push through to Andhra.
- **Rajput policy** — the master-stroke: **matrimonial alliance** (married Amber's **Raja Bharmal's daughter, 1562**), **abolition of the pilgrim tax (1563) and jizya (1564)**, and high mansabs for Rajputs (**Man Singh, Bhagwant Das, Todar Mal, Birbal**). Only **Mewar (Rana Pratap — Haldighati, 1576)** held out.
- **Navaratnas (Nine Gems):** **Abul Fazl** (chronicler), **Faizi** (poet-laureate), **Raja Todar Mal** (revenue), **Raja Man Singh** (general), **Birbal** (wit; died 1586), **Tansen** (music), **Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan** (poet-general), **Mulla Do Piyaza**, **Fakir Aziao-din**.
- Religious experiments (Ibadat Khana 1575, Mahzar 1579, **Din-i-Ilahi 1582**, sulh-i-kul) → **File 12**.
- Sources: **Abul Fazl's *Akbarnama* + *Ain-i-Akbari*** (the great administrative gazetteer ⭐) and the hostile **Badauni** (*Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh*) — read together.

---

## 4. Jahangir (1605–1627)

- **Nuruddin Salim**; his reign = consolidation, not expansion. Crushed son **Khusrau's revolt** (and executed **Guru Arjan**, 1606 — File 12). Annexed **Mewar (1615, peace with Amar Singh)** and **Kangra**; **lost Kandahar to Persia (1622)**.
- **The Nur Jahan "junta":** married **Mihr-un-Nisa (Nur Jahan)** 1611; she + her father **Itmad-ud-Daula (Mirza Ghiyas Beg, wazir)** + brother **Asaf Khan** + prince Khurram formed the power-bloc; coins struck in her name; she a builder and markswoman.
- **English contact:** **Captain William Hawkins (1608–11)** and **Sir Thomas Roe (1615–19, ambassador of James I)** won trading rights at **Surat** — the hinge of File 14.
- **"Chain of Justice" (Zanjir-i-Adl)** at Agra fort; memoir **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri** (Persian; naturalist's eye); **golden age of Mughal painting** (Ustad Mansur, Abul Hasan — File 12).

---

## 5. Shah Jahan (1628–1658) — the magnificent

- **Khurram**; reign = architectural zenith and imperial over-reach.
- **Deccan (⭐ AP hinge):** **1636 treaties** forced **Bijapur** and **Golconda (Qutb Shahi Deed of Submission** — tribute, khutba, Mughal suzerainty) — the first firm Mughal grip on the Deccan; his son **Aurangzeb** served two long viceroyalties there.
- **Frontiers:** lost **Kandahar (1649)** finally to Persia; the costly **Balkh-Badakhshan (Central Asia) campaign** failed; **expelled the Portuguese from Hooghly (1632)**.
- **Buildings:** **Taj Mahal (1632–53)**, **Red Fort & Jama Masjid** of **Shahjahanabad**, **Moti Masjid (Agra)**, the **Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus)** — File 12.
- **War of succession (1657–58):** four sons — liberal **Dara Shukoh** (heir; *Majma-ul-Bahrain*, Upanishad-translator), Shuja, Murad, and **Aurangzeb**, who won at **Dharmat** and **Samugarh (1658)**, imprisoned Shah Jahan in **Agra Fort** (d. 1666, gazing at the Taj — tradition) and executed Dara (1659).

---

## 6. Aurangzeb "Alamgir" (1658–1707) — greatest extent, deepest strain

- Territorial climax (Kabul to the Kaveri) but ideological narrowing:
  - **Re-imposed jizya (1679)**; ended court music & festivals; temple demolitions (Kashi Vishwanath, Keshava Rai at Mathura); codified **Fatawa-i-Alamgiri** — File 12.
  - Wars on **Jats (Gokla, Rajaram), Satnamis, Sikhs (executed Guru Tegh Bahadur, 1675)**, and **Rathor Rajputs (Marwar war; Durgadas Rathore)** — alienating former allies.
- **The Deccan campaigns (⭐⭐ AP-core):** annexed **Bijapur (1686)** and **GOLCONDA (1687** — 8-month siege, fall by treachery; end of the Qutb Shahis — cross-ref File 09); then the endless **27-year Maratha war** ("the **Deccan ulcer** that devoured the empire" — Jadunath Sarkar) — executed **Sambhaji (1689)** but never crushed the Maratha guerrilla state. Died at **Ahmadnagar (1707)**, buried simply at **Khuldabad** near Daulatabad.
- **Assessment (Mains):** greatest area yet gravest overstretch — Athar Ali's *jagirdari crisis* (too many nobles, too few jagirs) + Satish Chandra's *bewatani/be-jagiri* + Irfan Habib's *agrarian crisis* (peasant flight under revenue pressure) together explain why the machine seized up the moment the strong hand was gone.

---

## 7. Later Mughals (1707–1857) — the long twilight ⭐

| Ruler | Years | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| **Bahadur Shah I** (Shah Alam I) | 1707–12 | conciliation; "Shahi-i-Bekhabar" (Khafi Khan) |
| Jahandar Shah | 1712–13 | puppet of Zulfiqar Khan |
| **Farrukhsiyar** | 1713–19 | made & unmade by the **Sayyid Brothers** ("king-makers"); **1717 farman to the English EIC — the Company's "Magna Carta"** ⭐ (duty-free Bengal trade) |
| **Muhammad Shah "Rangila"** | 1719–48 | provinces break away — **Hyderabad (Nizam-ul-Mulk, 1724), Awadh, Bengal**; **NADIR SHAH sacked Delhi (1739)** — took the **Peacock Throne & Koh-i-noor** ⭐ |
| Ahmad Shah / Alamgir II | 1748–59 | **Ahmad Shah Abdali's** repeated invasions |
| **Shah Alam II** | 1759–1806 | defeated at **Buxar (1764)**; granted the **Diwani of Bengal-Bihar-Orissa to the EIC (1765)** — "a pensioner of the Company"; blinded by Ghulam Qadir (1788) |
| Akbar II | 1806–37 | titular; sent **Ram Mohan Roy** to London |
| **Bahadur Shah II "Zafar"** | 1837–57 | poet; figurehead of **1857**; exiled to Rangoon — the empire formally ends |

**Andhra footnote:** the same 1724–65 collapse that birthed the **Nizam's Hyderabad** also let the **French (Bussy) and then the English** carve out the **Northern Circars** on the Andhra coast — File 14.

## 8. Central administration (⭐ Prelims table zone)

**Theory of kingship:** Akbar's **Padshah** as **farr-i-izadi** (divine effulgence) and **insan-i-kamil** (perfect man), fount of **sulh-i-kul** — sovereignty grounded in the ruler's person, not the Caliph (unlike the Sultanate). No fixed succession law → wars of succession.

### The four "pillars" (central departments)
| Department | Head | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Diwan-i-Ala** (finance) | **Wazir / Diwan** | revenue, expenditure, the whole fiscal machine |
| **Mir Bakhshi** | Head of military | mansab records, muster (**dagh & chehra**), pay, **intelligence (waqia-navis, harkara)** |
| **Mir Saman (Khan-i-Saman)** | Household | the imperial **karkhanas**, stores, workshops, supplies |
| **Sadr-us-Sudur** | Chief Sadr | religious endowments, charity (**madad-i-maash / suyurghal** grants), often also **Qazi-ul-Quzat** (chief justice) |

Other key officers: **Muhtasib** (censor of morals/market), **Daroga-i-Dak-Chowki** (posts), **Mir Arz** (petitions), **Mustaufi** (auditor).

### 8.1 MANSABDARI — the steel frame ⭐⭐
- Codified by Akbar (**c. 1595**); every officer holds a **numerical rank (mansab)** fixing status, pay and troop-quota.
- **Dual rank:** **ZAT** (personal status & salary) + **SAWAR** (number of cavalry to maintain). Ranks ran **10 → 5,000** (higher — 7,000+ — reserved for princes; e.g., Man Singh, Aziz Koka at 7,000).
- **Non-hereditary and transferable** (on death, estate escheated — *the law of escheat*); a bureaucratic, not feudal, nobility.
- **Checks:** **dagh (horse-branding)** and **chehra (descriptive roll)** at muster (inherited from Alauddin/Sher Shah); **month-scales (dahsala/nisbat)** later adjusted the gap between paper *sawar* and real troops.
- **Composition of the nobility:** **Turani** (Central-Asian), **Irani** (Persian, Shia), **Afghan**, **Rajput**, **Indian Muslim (Shaikhzada)** — Akbar's balancing of blocs was itself statecraft; the bloc-arithmetic broke down under Aurangzeb (too many Deccani/Maratha entrants for the jagir pool).

### 8.2 JAGIRDARI — how mansabdars were paid
- Most nobles paid not in cash (**naqdi**) but by assignment of the **land revenue (jama) of a jagir** — a **claim on revenue, not on land or people** (Habib's crucial distinction; the peasant dealt with the state's schedule, not a "feudal lord").
- **Types:** **jagir-i-tan** (in lieu of pay), **mashrut** (conditional), **watan jagir** (hereditary homeland of a zamindar-chief, e.g., Rajput rajas), **al-tamgha** (Jahangir — permanent, for Muslim nobles in their native tracts).
- Frequent **transfer** prevented entrenchment but discouraged long-term investment → the **be-jagiri / jagirdari crisis** of the later empire (paper *jama* far exceeded collectable *hasil*).

## 9. Land revenue & the agrarian order (⭐ most-asked)

### 9.1 The Dahsala / Zabti (Bandobast) system — Todar Mal, 1580
- Built on Sher Shah; perfected as the **Ain-i-Dahsala**:
  1. **Measurement** of every field with the **jarib** (a bamboo pole joined by **iron rings**, replacing the stretch-prone hemp rope).
  2. **Soil-and-continuity classes:** **polaj** (annually cropped), **parauti** (rested a year), **chachar** (fallow 2–3 yrs), **banjar** (uncultivated 5+ yrs) — each assessed differently.
  3. **Ten-year average** of produce **and** prices → a fixed cash **dastur** (schedule of rates) per crop per locality.
  4. **State demand = 1/3 of the average produce**, taken **in cash** wherever possible.
- Collected by the **amil / amalguzar**; concessions (**taqavi** loans) in bad years; the peasant preferred zabti's predictability.

### 9.2 Other assessment methods (used where zabti didn't fit)
| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Ghalla-bakhshi / batai** | crop-sharing at harvest (division of the actual heap) |
| **Kankut** | estimate (kan = grain, kut = estimate) of standing crop |
| **Nasaq** | rough lump assessment from past records — common in Bengal |

### 9.3 The zamindars
- Intermediary right-holders who collected revenue and kept a share (**nankar / malikana**); **not owners of the land** but of a saleable, hereditary **revenue right**. Ranged from petty village *zamindars* to great autonomous **rajas/chieftains** — a class the empire needed and feared (Nurul Hasan's typology: primary/intermediary/autonomous zamindars).

## 10. Provincial → local administration

**Suba (province)** — 12 under Akbar (*Ain* lists them), rising to 15+ (Deccan subas added):
| Level | Officer | Charge |
|---|---|---|
| **Suba** | **Subadar / Nazim (Sipah-Salar)** | executive head; the **Diwan** (revenue) reported **separately to the centre** — a deliberate check |
| Suba (other) | **Bakhshi, Sadr, Qazi, Waqia-Navis, Kotwal** | army-pay, religion, justice, news, city police |
| **Sarkar** (district) | **Faujdar** (law & order/military) + **Amalguzar/Amil** (revenue) | |
| **Pargana** | **Shiqdar, Amil, Qanungo** (record-keeper), **Bitikchi** (accountant) | |
| **Village** | **Muqaddam** (headman), **Patwari** (accountant), **Chaukidar** | the durable base — largely Hindu |

The **Kotwal** (city) — police, prices, sanitation, morals, registration of population — is a favourite one-liner.

## 11. Economy — the cash-nexus empire

- **Trimetallic coinage:** gold **mohur**, silver **rupiya** (the workhorse), copper **dam** (40 dam = 1 rupiya) — inherited from Sher Shah, standardised by Akbar.
- **World-class manufacturing & trade surplus:** cotton textiles (Bengal muslin, Gujarat & Coromandel calicoes/chintz), **indigo, saltpetre, spices, silk**; India ran a chronic **bullion-absorbing surplus** — Europe paid in silver (the reason the Companies came — File 14).
- **Karkhanas** (royal workshops) for arms, textiles, jewellery; **sarrafs** (bankers), **hundi** (bills of exchange) and the great **Jagat Seths** financed the fisc.
- **Ports:** **Surat** ("Bab-ul-Mecca", the western gateway) and, on the east, **Masulipatnam/Machilipatnam** ⭐ (the Golconda world-port the Companies fought over).
- **Historiography of the economy:** Irfan Habib, ***The Agrarian System of Mughal India*** — surplus-extraction and the agrarian crisis; Tapan Raychaudhuri & Shireen Moosvi on national income; the debate on whether the 17th-c. economy was "potentially capitalist" or structurally blocked.

## 12. Decline of the Mughal Empire — the cause-bundle (Mains) ⭐

1. **Aurangzeb's over-extension & Deccan war** — 27 years, treasury and army bled white (Sarkar's classic thesis).
2. **Religious reaction** — jizya, temple policy, Rajput & Sikh & Jat & Maratha alienation (the "wrong ideology" thesis, now nuanced).
3. **Jagirdari & agrarian crisis (structural):** Athar Ali (too many nobles for too few jagirs), Satish Chandra (the *jagir* system's collapse — *Parties and Politics at the Mughal Court*), Irfan Habib (peasant revolts against surplus extraction) — the "no single villain, the system seized" school.
4. **Weak later Mughals & noble factionalism** (Irani vs Turani; the **Sayyid Brothers**' king-making).
5. **Foreign invasions:** **Nadir Shah (1739)** and **Ahmad Shah Abdali** shattered prestige and finance.
6. **Rise of regional successor states** — Hyderabad, Bengal, Awadh, the **Marathas** (File 13) — and then the **European Companies** (File 14) filled the vacuum.
7. Newer view (Alam, Bayly): the "decline" was also a **decentralisation** — vibrant regional states, not mere chaos.

## 13. Prelims nuggets

1. Panipat-I 1526 (Ibrahim Lodi); Khanwa 1527 (Rana Sanga); Chanderi 1528; Ghaghra 1529 — **Babur**; memoir *Baburnama* in **Turki**.
2. Chausa 1539 & Kannauj 1540 — Sher Shah beat **Humayun**; died 1556 on **Sher Mandal** stairs.
3. **Sher Shah:** rupiya + dam, GT Road, sarai-dak, patta-qabuliyat, Sasaram tomb.
4. Second Panipat 1556 — Bairam Khan vs **Hemu**.
5. Akbar abolished **pilgrim tax 1563, jizya 1564**; **Buland Darwaza** = Gujarat 1573 victory.
6. **Mansab = zat + sawar**; codified c. 1595; dagh & chehra; non-hereditary; law of escheat.
7. **Dahsala/Zabti — Todar Mal 1580**; state demand 1/3, in cash; polaj-parauti-chachar-banjar.
8. Jagir types: tan, mashrut, **watan**, **al-tamgha** (Jahangir).
9. Four pillars: Diwan, **Mir Bakhshi**, Mir Saman, Sadr-us-Sudur; **Kotwal** = city police.
10. **1636** — Bijapur & Golconda submit to Shah Jahan; **1686 Bijapur, 1687 Golconda** annexed by Aurangzeb.
11. Aurangzeb died **1707**, buried **Khuldabad**; "Deccan ulcer" — Jadunath Sarkar.
12. Farrukhsiyar's **1717 farman** = EIC's "Magna Carta"; **Nadir Shah 1739** (Peacock Throne, Koh-i-noor); **Buxar 1764 → Diwani 1765** (Shah Alam II).
13. Nizam-ul-Mulk founded autonomous **Hyderabad, 1724**.

## 14. Mains angles

1. **"Sher Shah was the forerunner of Akbar."** Match reform-for-reform (measurement, patta-qabuliyat, sarai-dak, currency) → Todar Mal's Dahsala; assess how much Akbar merely perfected.
2. **"The mansabdari system was the Mughal empire."** Explain zat-sawar, non-heredity, the Turani-Irani-Rajput balance, dagh-chehra checks — then show its breakdown (jagirdari crisis) as the empire's structural fault-line.
3. **"Akbar built an Indian empire, not merely a Muslim one."** Rajput alliances, jizya abolition, sulh-i-kul, Hindu mansabdars (Todar Mal, Man Singh) — the composite ruling class.
4. **"Aurangzeb achieved the empire's greatest extent and its deepest crisis simultaneously."** Deccan annexations (Bijapur-Golconda) vs the Maratha ulcer + jagirdari/agrarian strain; weigh Sarkar's "ideology" thesis against Habib/Chandra/Ali's "structural" theses.
5. **"The 18th-century 'decline' was as much decentralisation as collapse."** Successor states (Hyderabad, Bengal, Awadh, Marathas) and their fiscal-military competence; set up File 13 (Marathas) and File 14 (Europeans).
