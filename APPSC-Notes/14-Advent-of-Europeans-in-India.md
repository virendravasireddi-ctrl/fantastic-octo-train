# 14 · The Advent of the Europeans in India (1498–1765)

[← Shivaji & the Marathas](13-Shivaji-and-Rise-of-Maratha-Empire.md) · [Index](README.md) · [Next: Unit-3 Practice →](15-Mughals-Marathas-Europeans-Practice.md)

---

## 0. Frame

Five trading nations came for **spices, textiles and Indian bullion-absorbing surpluses**, planted **fortified factories** on the coasts, and — as Mughal authority decayed after 1707 — turned commerce into **territorial power**. On the **Coromandel/Andhra coast** this is not background but the main stage: **Machilipatnam (Masulipatnam)** was the first English factory in the east (1611); **Fort St. George (Madras, 1639)** was granted by a **Vijayanagara-successor Nayaka from Chandragiri**; and the **Northern Circars** — the Andhra littoral — passed from **French (Bussy)** to **English** hands in the Carnatic Wars, sealing British paramountcy in the south. First in, last out: **Portuguese (1498) → Dutch (1602) → English (1600) → French (1664) → Danish (1616)**.

| Power | Company & year | Indian HQ | Fate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Portuguese** | *Estado da India* (crown), 1498/1505 | **Goa** | first in; shrank to Goa-Daman-Diu (till 1961) |
| **Dutch** | **VOC**, 1602 | **Pulicat → Nagapattinam** | spice-focused; beaten by English (Bedara 1759) |
| **English** | **EIC**, 31 Dec **1600** | Surat → **Calcutta** | last man standing — empire |
| **French** | **Cie des Indes**, **1664** | **Pondicherry** | lost the Carnatic Wars; trading posts only |
| **Danish** | Dansk Ostindisk, 1616 | **Tranquebar** | minor; sold out to English 1845 |

## 1. The Portuguese (1498–1961) — first in, longest resident ⭐

- **Vasco da Gama** reached **Calicut, 20 May 1498** — guided across the Arabian Sea (tradition names the pilot **Ibn Majid**); received by the **Zamorin**; a cargo that paid **~60× the voyage's cost** lit the fuse. **Cabral** followed (1500).
- **Francisco de Almeida (1st Viceroy, 1505–09):** the **"Blue Water Policy" (Cartaz)** — control the sea, not land; won the **Battle of Diu (1509)** against a Gujarat–Egypt–Zamorin fleet → Portuguese naval mastery of the Indian Ocean.
- **Afonso de Albuquerque (1509–15) — the real founder:** captured **GOA (1510)** from **Bijapur (Adil Shah)** — the eastern capital of the **Estado da India**; took **Malacca (1511)**, **Hormuz**, Socotra; the **cartaz** naval-licence/pass system taxed all Asian shipping.
- **Nino da Cunha** shifted the capital formally to **Goa (1530)**; acquired **Diu, Daman, Salsette, Bassein, Bombay, San Thome (Mylapore), Hooghly**.
- **Decline:** union with Spain (1580–1640) diverted resources; **Dutch & English** broke the monopoly; **Shah Jahan expelled them from Hooghly (1632)**; the **Marathas took Bassein & Salsette (1739)** (File 13). Left only **Goa, Daman, Diu** (till **1961**).
- **Legacy (⭐ often asked):** the **cartaz/armed-trade** template every later Company copied; **New-World crops** — tobacco, **potato, chilli**, cashew, pineapple, tomato; the **printing press (Goa, 1556)** — first in India; **Indo-Portuguese/Gothic-Baroque architecture** (**Bom Jesus Basilica**, tomb of **St. Francis Xavier**); the **Goa Inquisition** and Jesuit missions (the darker side).

## 2. The Dutch (VOC, 1602–1825) ⭐

- The **Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie (VOC, 1602)** — the world's first joint-stock chartered mega-company; strategy = **spice monopoly** centred on the **Indonesian archipelago**, with India as a supplier of **cotton textiles to barter for spices**.
- **Coromandel/Andhra was their Indian core** ⭐: **Masulipatnam factory (1605)** — one of their earliest; **Pulicat (1610), Fort Geldria** — their Coromandel HQ and **mint of gold "pagodas"**; also **Nizampatnam, Bhimilipatnam, Draksharama**; later **Nagapattinam** (new HQ), **Chinsura (Bengal)**, **Cochin (from the Portuguese, 1663)**, Surat.
- The **Amboyna Massacre (1623)** — the Dutch expelled the English from the Spice Islands, **redirecting English energy to the Indian mainland** (a pivotal accident ⭐).
- **Eclipse:** ruined by wars with the English — the **Battle of Bedara/Biderra (1759, Bengal)** ended the Dutch as a power; VOC dissolved 1799. *Their lasting Indian mark is commercial, not territorial.*

## 3. The English East India Company (EIC, 1600–1858) ⭐⭐

- Chartered by **Elizabeth I, 31 December 1600** ("Governor and Company of Merchants of London trading into the East Indies") — a **regulated → joint-stock** monopoly of English eastern trade.
- **Getting a footing in the Mughal west:** **Captain William Hawkins** at Jahangir's court (1609); **Sir Thomas Roe (1615–19)**, James I's ambassador, won a firman for a **factory at SURAT (1613/1618)** — the first English factory in India (west coast).
- **The Coromandel/Andhra beginnings (⭐⭐ AP-core):**
  - **MASULIPATNAM (Machilipatnam) factory, 1611** — the **first English factory on the east coast**, in **Qutb Shahi Golconda** territory (the **"Golden Firman," 1632**, gave free trade for 500 pagodas a year — File 09); then **Armagaon (Durgarazapatnam, 1626)**.
  - **FORT ST. GEORGE / MADRAS, 1639–40** — **Francis Day** obtained the village of **Madraspatnam** from **Damarla Venkatadri (Ayyappa) Nayaka**, a chief of the **Vijayanagara-Aravidu successor at Chandragiri** (cross-ref File 08 ⭐) — the first English **fortified** settlement and the eastern presidency capital.
- **Bombay:** came to **Charles II** as **Catherine of Braganza's dowry (1661)** and was leased to the **EIC (1668)** — the western presidency, safer than Surat.
- **Calcutta / Fort William:** **Job Charnock** founded the Sutanuti-Kalikata settlement (**1690**); fortified as **Fort William**; the Bengal presidency — soon the richest.
- **The legal keystones:** **Farrukhsiyar's farman (1717)** — the Company's "**Magna Carta**" (duty-free Bengal trade for ₹3,000/year) — the fiscal grievance that led to Plassey; the three **Presidencies (Madras, Bombay, Bengal/Calcutta)** became the springboard of empire.

## 4. The French (Compagnie des Indes, 1664–1954) ⭐

- Founded **1664** by **Colbert** under **Louis XIV** — a **state-controlled** company (its great weakness vs the private-capital English).
- Settlements: **Surat (1668)**, **Masulipatnam**, **PONDICHERRY (1674, Francois Martin — the HQ)**, **Chandernagore (Bengal, 1690)**, **Mahe (Malabar)**, **Karaikal**, and **YANAM/Yanaon (Godavari delta, Andhra) ⭐** — the AP French foothold.
- The Anglo-French duel for India (§5) is fought largely on the **Andhra-Tamil Coromandel**.

## 5. The Carnatic Wars — the Anglo-French decider (1746–1763) ⭐⭐

Three wars, extensions of European conflicts, fought over the **succession disputes of the Nizam (Hyderabad) and the Nawab of the Carnatic** — with **Andhra at the centre**.

| War | Years | European context | Key events | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First Carnatic** | 1746–48 | Austrian Succession | **La Bourdonnais took Madras (1746)**; **Battle of St. Thome (1746)** — a small French-sepoy force routed **Nawab Anwaruddin's** army (the epoch's lesson: **European discipline & artillery > large Indian levies**) ⭐ | **Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)** — Madras returned |
| **Second Carnatic** | 1749–54 | no European war (local) | **Dupleix's** peak — installed **Muzaffar Jung** (Hyderabad) & **Chanda Sahib** (Carnatic); **BUSSY stationed at Hyderabad** → France granted the **NORTHERN CIRCARS** ⭐⭐; **Clive's defence of Arcot (1751)** turned the tide; **Dupleix recalled (1754)** | **Treaty of Pondicherry (1754)** |
| **Third Carnatic** | 1756–63 | Seven Years' War | **Count de Lally** besieged Madras (failed); **Forde captured Masulipatnam from the French (1759)** ⭐; **BATTLE OF WANDIWASH (22 Jan 1760)** — **Sir Eyre Coote** crushed Lally → French power in India ended | **Treaty of Paris (1763)** — Pondicherry etc. returned but **unfortified**; France reduced to trading posts |

### 5.1 The Northern Circars & the Andhra dimension ⭐⭐ (AP-core)
- **Bussy's decade at Hyderabad (1751–58)** made the French the power behind the **Nizam** and won them the **Northern Circars** — the coastal Andhra tracts (**Chicacole/Srikakulam, Rajahmundry, Ellore, Kondapalli, Guntur/Murtizanagar**).
- When Bussy was pulled south for the Third War, the English struck: **Colonel Forde**, allied with **Ananda Raju/Vizianagaram**, won at **Chandurti** and **took Masulipatnam (1759)**.
- **Battle/Massacre of BOBBILI (1757)** ⭐ — Bussy with the **Raja of Vizianagaram** stormed **Bobbili**; the defenders' self-immolation ("**Bobbili Yuddham/Katha**") became enduring Telugu folk-epic and Burrakatha.
- **The Circars pass to the British:** by the **firman of Shah Alam II (1765)** and the **Treaty of 1766 with Nizam Ali Khan**, the **Northern Circars were ceded to the EIC** (Guntur added 1788) — the whole **Andhra coast now British**, the Nizam retained as a **subsidiary ally**.

### 5.2 Why the English beat the French (Mains skeleton) ⭐
1. **Naval supremacy** — the Royal Navy could reinforce and blockade; France couldn't relieve Pondicherry.
2. **Private-enterprise vs state company** — the EIC's independent credit and merchant drive vs the cash-starved, Versailles-controlled French company (Dupleix unsupported, then recalled).
3. **Three secure, resource-rich presidencies** (esp. **Bengal after Plassey 1757**) financed the war; France had only Pondicherry.
4. **Leadership & continuity** — Clive, Coote, Forde vs the recalled Dupleix and the abrasive Lally.
5. **A disciplined sepoy army** on European lines (a technique both sides pioneered, the English scaled).

## 6. The trade goods & the "why they came" (economic engine)
- **Exports from India:** **cotton textiles** (Bengal muslin, Coromandel **chintz/kalamkari/"palampore"**, Gujarat calico), **indigo, saltpetre** (gunpowder), **spices, silk, diamonds (Golconda/Kollur — File 09)**.
- **The bullion drain into India:** India wanted little from Europe except **silver** — so the Companies shipped **precious metal** to pay for goods (the mercantilist anxiety that drove them to seek **territorial revenue** to fund the "investment"). The **Coromandel/Andhra ports — Masulipatnam, Pulicat, Vizag, Nizampatnam** — were textile-and-diamond emporia at the heart of this trade.

## 7. Andhra / Coromandel sheet (⭐ reuse in AP answers)

1. **Masulipatnam (Machilipatnam)** — the pivotal Coromandel port: **Dutch factory 1605, English 1611 (first in the east), French 1669**; captured by **Forde from the French, 1759**.
2. **Pulicat (Fort Geldria)** — Dutch Coromandel HQ & pagoda-mint; **Bhimilipatnam, Nizampatnam, Draksharama** — Dutch/textile stations.
3. **Madras/Fort St. George (1639)** granted by a **Chandragiri (Aravidu-Vijayanagara) Nayaka, Damarla Venkatadri** — the seed of the Madras Presidency that governed Andhra till 1953.
4. **Yanam (Yanaon)** — French enclave in the Godavari delta (French till 1954, with Pondicherry).
5. **Northern Circars** — French (Bussy) → English (**1765–66**); **Bobbili 1757**; the making of British Andhra and the **subsidiary Nizam**.

## 9. Mains angles

1. **"The Portuguese wrote the script every later Company followed."** Cartaz/armed trade, fortified factories, monopoly-by-force — trace the template from Almeida-Albuquerque to the EIC.
2. **"The Carnatic Wars decided who would rule India."** The three wars, the succession-intervention model (Dupleix's innovation), Bussy and the Circars, Wandiwash — and the five reasons the English won.
3. **"On the Coromandel, commerce became conquest."** Masulipatnam-Pulicat-Madras-Circars: trace how Andhra's ports and the Circars cession (1765–66) turned trade into territory and reduced the Nizam to a subsidiary ally.
4. **"Why did the English, not the Dutch or French, build the empire?"** Naval power, private-capital dynamism, three presidencies + Bengal's revenue, leadership, sepoy armies — a comparative company analysis.
5. **"European entry both exploited and reflected Mughal decline."** Firmans (1632, 1717), succession-wars intervention, and the vacuum after 1707 (Files 11 & 13) — commerce filling a political void.
