## 1. Cyclones ⭐⭐ (2025 Q14b — AP cyclone/flood vulnerability)

### 1.1 Mechanism & structure

- A **tropical cyclone** needs: **sea-surface temperature >26.5 °C, deep warm layer, high humidity, low vertical wind shear, pre-existing low, and Coriolis force** (so none form on the equator) ⭐; energy source = **latent heat of condensation**.
- **Structure:** calm **eye** → violent **eyewall** (max winds) → spiralling **rain bands**; hazards = **wind, torrential rain, and the deadly STORM SURGE** ⭐ (the sea wall that drowns deltas).
- **Naming:** the **IMD is one of six RSMCs worldwide** and names North-Indian-Ocean cyclones for a **13-country panel** ⭐; IMD issues colour-coded warnings (green→yellow→orange→red).

### 1.2 Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea; India & AP

- The **Bay of Bengal spawns ~4× more cyclones than the Arabian Sea** (warmer, moisture, funnelling coast) ⭐; **primary season Oct–Dec (post-monsoon)**, secondary Apr–May.
- **AP's ~974 km coast is a "cyclone alley"** (File 18). Landmark storms: **Diviseema (19 Nov 1977, ~10,000 deaths, storm surge)** ⭐⭐, 1990 Machilipatnam, **HUDHUD (12 Oct 2014, Cat-4, devastated Visakhapatnam, ~₹22,000 cr)** ⭐, **Titli (2018, Srikakulam)**, **Michaung (Dec 2023, landfall Bapatla — Tirupati-Nellore-Chennai floods)** ⭐.
- **Vulnerability drivers:** flat delta terrain + dense coastal population + surge-prone estuaries + aquaculture/paddy exposure.
- **The success story ⭐:** post-1977 and post-Hudhud, AP built **cyclone shelters, embankments, early-warning dissemination and mangrove buffers (Coringa)** — the **Phailin (2013) & Hudhud model of near-zero-casualty evacuation** is a globally cited disaster-risk-reduction win; contrast the 1977 toll with recent near-zero deaths to show capacity growth.

## 2. Droughts ⭐⭐⭐ (2023b definition-types-areas; 2025a Rayalaseema)

### 2.1 Definition & types (2023 PYQ (b) core)

**Drought = a prolonged period of abnormally deficient rainfall / water scarcity** relative to normal. **Four types (name all four):**
1. **Meteorological** — rainfall deficit (IMD: **"deficient" = 26–50% below normal; "scanty" = >50% below**) ⭐.
2. **Hydrological** — depleted rivers, reservoirs, groundwater.
3. **Agricultural** — soil-moisture/water shortfall harming crops (the farmer's drought).
4. **Socio-economic / ecological** — food, fodder, water and livelihood crisis; ecosystem stress.

### 2.2 Drought-prone areas of India (2023 PYQ (b))

The **semi-arid rain-shadow & interior belts**: **Rayalaseema (AP), Marathwada & interior Maharashtra, Bundelkhand (UP-MP), Kutch–Saurashtra, western Rajasthan, interior Karnataka, Telangana, Kalahandi (Odisha), Purulia** ⭐. Programmes: **DPAP (1973), Desert Development Programme, Drought-Prone Area watershed schemes** → now under **PMKSY watershed / MGNREGA / Jal Shakti**.

### 2.3 Rayalaseema drought vulnerability & consequences ⭐⭐⭐ (2025 PYQ (a) — full kit)

- **Why Rayalaseema is chronically drought-hit:** **double rain-shadow** (behind both the Western Ghats for the SW monsoon *and* dependent on the fickle **NE monsoon**), interior location, **hard-rock (granite-gneiss) aquifers that store little groundwater**, thin red soils, high evapotranspiration; **Anantapur ≈ 550 mm — India's 2nd-driest district after Jaisalmer** ⭐⭐ (Files 18–19).
- **Socio-economic consequences (the second half of the PYQ):** groundnut mono-crop failures & **agrarian distress/farmer suicides**; **collapse of groundwater → deeper borewells → debt**; **fluorosis** from over-drawn groundwater (Anantapur–Prakasam belt) ⭐; **fodder & drinking-water scarcity**; **distress out-migration to Bengaluru/Chennai** (File 27); loss of livestock; school dropout; feminisation of labour.
- **Responses:** **lift-irrigation schemes (HNSS/Handri-Neeva, Galeru-Nagari, Telugu Ganga, Veligonda), watershed & tank restoration, horticulture + micro-irrigation (APMIP — Files 18, 21), millets & natural farming (APCNF), crop insurance, MGNREGA water works, and the solar push (Kurnool park)**; evaluate their **energy cost, tail-end reliability and groundwater-recharge limits** ⭐.
- **Close:** drought here is **structural, not episodic** — the durable fix is water-budgeting + dryland-appropriate cropping + diversification, not only inter-basin transfers.

## 3. Disaster Management framework ⭐⭐ (attach to any hazard answer)

### 3.1 The disaster-management cycle

**Mitigation → Preparedness → Response → Recovery/Rehabilitation** (pre-disaster risk reduction now emphasised over post-disaster relief — the "**paradigm shift from response-centric to prevention/mitigation-centric**") ⭐.

### 3.2 India's institutional architecture (Disaster Management Act, 2005)

| Level | Body | Head |
|---|---|---|
| National | **NDMA** (2005, statutory) | **Prime Minister** |
| — | **NEC**, **NIDM** (training), **NDRF** (2006, response force) | — |
| State | **SDMA** | **Chief Minister** |
| District | **DDMA** | **District Collector/Magistrate** |

- **International:** **Sendai Framework for DRR 2015–2030** (4 priorities, 7 targets; succeeded the Hyogo Framework) ⭐; India champions the **Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI, 2019)**.

### 3.3 Andhra Pradesh ⭐

- **APSDMA** with a real-time **State Emergency Operations Centre**; AP is nationally regarded for **cyclone preparedness** (shelters, evacuation SOPs, mangrove buffers) — the Hudhud/Phailin near-zero-casualty model ⭐.
- Instruments: cyclone & flood early warning (IMD + Doppler radars at Visakhapatnam & Machilipatnam), **coastal cyclone shelters (World Bank/NCRMP)**, drought monitoring cells, MGNREGA drought-proofing works, crop insurance, and community-based disaster preparedness.
- **Gaps to flag:** urban-flood drainage (Vijayawada-Budameru), heat-wave & lightning action plans, seismic-preparedness complacency (Zone III coast), climate-proofing of the deltas (sea-level rise, salinity).
