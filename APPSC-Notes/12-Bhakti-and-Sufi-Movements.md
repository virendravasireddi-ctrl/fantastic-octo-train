# PART A — THE BHAKTI MOVEMENT

## A1. What it was
**Bhakti** = salvation through loving devotion to a personal God, open to **all castes and to women**, expressed in the **mother tongue**, guided by a **guru**, and largely indifferent (or hostile) to ritualism and priestly mediation. Roots: Upanishads → **Bhagavad Gita** (bhakti-yoga) → Puranas → **Tamil saints**.

> ⭐ Sequencing nuance every examiner rewards: **Bhakti began in the Tamil South (6th–9th c.) — centuries before Islam mattered in India.** The **Sultanate-era North** then gave it new accents (nirguna radicalism, Sufi contact). "Bhakti as a mere reaction to Islam" is wrong; "Islam-era conditions shaped its northern phase" is right.

## A2. Phase I — the Tamil dawn (6th–9th c., under Pallavas/Pandyas)

| Stream | Saints | Corpus |
|---|---|---|
| **Alvars** (12, Vaishnava) | Periyalvar, **ANDAL (the only woman; *Tiruppavai*)**, **Nammalvar (*Tiruvaymoli*)**, Tirumangai | ***Nalayira Divya Prabandham*** ("Tamil Veda"), compiled by **Nathamuni** (10th c.) |
| **Nayanars** (63, Shaiva) | **Appar** (converted Pallava Mahendravarman I), **Sambandar**, **Sundarar** (their hymns = ***Tevaram***); **Manikkavachakar (*Tiruvachakam*)** | canonised as ***Tirumurai*** (12 books) by **Nambi Andar Nambi** under Rajaraja I; 12th book = **Sekkilar's *Periyapuranam*** |

- Saints came from **every caste** (Nandanar — a Dalit Nayanar; Karaikkal Ammaiyar — a woman ascetic) ⭐.
- Function: rolled back Jainism/Buddhism, welded temple, language and community — the template for all later bhakti.

## A3. Phase II — the philosopher-acharyas (giving bhakti a metaphysics)

| Acharya | Century | School | Key works / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shankara** (Kaladi, Kerala) | 8th | **Advaita** (non-dualism; *nirguna* Brahman; world = maya) | Brahmasutra-bhashya; four mathas (Sringeri, Dwaraka, Puri, Badari) — bhakti's intellectual sparring partner |
| **Ramanuja** (Sriperumbudur → Srirangam) | 11th–12th | **Vishishtadvaita** (qualified monism); **prapatti** (surrender) open to all | *Sribhashya*; persecuted by a Chola ("Krimikantha" in tradition) → **Melkote** (converted Hoysala Vishnuvardhana); admitted **Tirukulattar (Dalits) into the Melkote temple** on fixed days ⭐; fountainhead of **Sri Vaishnavism** (→ Tirumala's rise) |
| **Madhva** (Udupi) | 13th | **Dvaita** (dualism) | Udupi Krishna matha-circle (ashta mathas) |
| **Nimbarka** | 12th–13th | **Dvaitadvaita** | Radha-Krishna cult; ⭐ **tradition makes him Andhra-born (Nimbapura)** |
| **Vallabha** | 15th–16th | **Shuddhadvaita**; **Pushti-marga** (grace) | ⭐ **Telugu Brahmin descent** (family from Andhra; born Champaran); Srinathji (Nathdwara); Ashtachhap poets incl. Surdas |
| **Basaveshwara** (Kalyana) | 12th | **Veerashaivism / Lingayat** | Minister of Kalachuri Bijjala; **Anubhava Mantapa**; **vachanas** (with **Allama Prabhu, AKKA MAHADEVI**); rejected caste, rebirth-ritualism, temple cult; ishtalinga worn on body |

## A4. Phase III — the Sultanate-and-after North (13th–17th c.)

**Two streams:**
- **SAGUNA** (God with form/attributes — Rama, Krishna): Tulsidas, Surdas, Mira, Chaitanya, Shankaradeva.
- **NIRGUNA** (formless God; "sant" tradition; strongest social radicalism; artisan-caste leadership): Kabir, Ravidas, Nanak, Dadu.

| Saint | Region/century | Essentials |
|---|---|---|
| **Ramananda** | Varanasi, c. 14th–15th | Bridge from Ramanuja's line to the north; taught in **Hindi**; **opened discipleship to all castes and women** — his circle (traditionally): **Kabir (weaver), Ravidas (cobbler), Sena (barber), Sadhana (butcher), Dhanna (Jat peasant), Pipa (Rajput)** ⭐ artisan base = social history point |
| **Kabir** (c. 1440–1518) | Varanasi julaha | **Nirguna**; God as Ram-Rahim beyond both mosque & temple; dohas; ***Bijak*** (Sakhi-Sabad-Ramaini); verses in **Adi Granth**; Kabirpanth |
| **Ravidas** | Varanasi | Dalit sant; hymns in Adi Granth; "Begumpura" (city without sorrow) — egalitarian utopia |
| **Guru Nanak** (1469–1539) | Talwandi, Punjab | Nirguna one God; **sangat & langar** (caste-free commensality); *Japji*; udasi travels; → Sikhism (Adi Granth compiled 1604 by Guru Arjan) |
| **Chaitanya** (1486–1533) | Nabadwip, Bengal → Puri | Krishna **sankirtan** (congregational song-dance); **achintya-bhedabheda**; Gaudiya Vaishnavism; embraced all castes & Muslims (Haridas) |
| **Jnaneshwar** (1275–96) | Maharashtra | ***Jnaneshwari*** (Marathi Gita commentary at ~21); **Varkari** panth of Vithoba (Pandharpur) |
| **Namdev** (1270–1350) | Maharashtra tailor | Abhangas; also revered in Punjab — hymns in **Adi Granth** ⭐ |
| **Eknath / Tukaram / Ramdas** | 16th–17th Maharashtra | Eknathi Bhagavata & bharuds; Tukaram's **abhangas** (Shivaji's contemporary); Ramdas's ***Dasbodh*** |
| **Surdas** (16th c.) | Braj | ***Sursagar***; Krishna's childhood; Vallabha's **Ashtachhap** |
| **Tulsidas** (1532–1623) | Awadh | ***Ramcharitmanas*** (Awadhi), *Vinaya Patrika*, *Hanuman Chalisa* — Rama bhakti as social glue |
| **Mirabai** (1498–1546) | Merta, Rajasthan | Rathore princess; Girdhar Gopal padas; defied patriarchy/court — women's voice ⭐ |
| **Shankaradeva** (1449–1568) | Assam | **Eka-sarana-nama-dharma**; *Kirtan-ghosha*, **Borgeet**, Ankia-naat/bhaona; **sattra & namghar** institutions |
| **Narsinh Mehta** (15th c.) | Gujarat | "**Vaishnava jana to**" (Gandhi's favourite) |
| **Lal Ded** (14th c.) | Kashmir | Shaiva mystic **vakhs**; influenced the Muslim **Rishi order** (Nund Rishi) — Kashmiriyat ⭐ |
| Others one-line | — | Vidyapati (Maithili), Chandidas (Bengali), Dadu Dayal (Rajasthan, nirguna), Haridasa movement of Karnataka (**Purandaradasa — "father of Carnatic music", Kanakadasa — shepherd-saint**) ⭐ |

## A5. ⭐ THE ANDHRA BHAKTI STREAM (APPSC's favourite corner)

| Figure | Period | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Mallikarjuna Panditaradhya** | 12th c. | **Aradhya Shaivism** — Veerashaiva bhakti accommodated with Brahmanical practice in Telugu country; Srisailam orbit |
| **PALKURIKI SOMANATHA** | c. 13th c. | Veerashaiva poet; ***Basava Puranamu*** & *Panditaradhya Charitramu* in **dwipada** (native couplet metre) — deliberately "Telugu of the people, not of the pandits" ⭐ |
| **Tikkana** (context) | 13th c. | Dedicated his Mahabharata to **Hariharanatha** (Shiva-Vishnu combined deity) — literary statement of sectarian harmony after bitter Shaiva-Vaishnava strife |
| **TALLAPAKA ANNAMACHARYA (Annamayya)** | **1408–1503** | **32,000 sankirtanas** to **Lord Venkateshwara of Tirumala** (~13,000 survive — found engraved on **copper plates** in the temple's Sankirtana Bhandagaram, rediscovered 1922); **"Padakavita Pitamaha"** (grandsire of the padam genre); radical equality verse ***"Brahmam Okate"*** — all beings equal before God; wife **Timmakka (*Subhadra Kalyanam*) = first known Telugu poetess** ⭐⭐ |
| **BAMMERA POTANA** | 15th c. | ***Andhra Maha Bhagavatamu*** — Telugu Bhagavata; farmer-poet who **refused to dedicate it to any king**, dedicating it to Lord Rama ("Palikedidi Bhagavatamata…") — bhakti's independence from patronage ⭐ |
| Continuum (later, one-liners) | 16th–17th c. | **Kshetrayya** (Movva; padams to Muvva Gopala), **Kancherla Gopanna "Ramadasu"** (Bhadrachalam temple; kirtanas; imprisoned by Golconda's Tana Shah), **Vemana's** sataka social critique (dates debated) → feeds into **Tyagaraja** (18th c.) |
| Institutions | — | **Srisailam** (Mallikarjuna jyotirlinga; Veerashaiva mathas), **Tirumala** (Sri Vaishnava ascendancy after Ramanuja's reorganisation-tradition), **Ahobilam** (Narasimha; **Ahobila Matha**, c. 1400), **Simhachalam** (Varaha-Narasimha) — the temple network that carried Andhra bhakti |

## A6. Common features → causes → effects (Mains block)

**Features:** one God under many names; devotion over ritual & knowledge-pride; guru's centrality; **vernacular medium**; congregational **song (kirtan/sankirtana/abhang/vachana)**; caste & gender inclusiveness (in worship, if not always in society); pilgrimage-shrine foci; nirguna wing's social radicalism.

**Why it surged (causes):** internal devotional continuity (Gita→Puranas→Tamil saints); revulsion at ritualism, caste arrogance and sectarian strife; **insecurity and church-lessness of Hindu society under Turkish rule** (temple destruction, elite collapse) → religion re-centred on the heart and the home; **Sufi example & dialogue**; growth of **artisan-urban classes** under the Sultanate needing a dignified religious voice (weaver Kabir, cobbler Ravidas).

**Influence/effects:**
1. **Vernacular literatures founded or transformed:** Hindi/Awadhi/Braj (Tulsi, Sur, Kabir), Punjabi (Adi Granth), Marathi (Jnaneshwari→Tukaram), Bengali-Maithili (Chaitanya-era padavali), Assamese (Shankaradeva), Kannada (vachanas; **Haridasa devaranamas**), **Telugu (dwipada purana, sankirtana, sataka)**.
2. **Music & performing arts:** kirtan, qaul-adjacent bhajan culture, **Carnatic music's devotional spine (Purandaradasa → Tyagaraja)**, Annamayya's padam genre, Sattriya dance (Shankaradeva) — living heritage.
3. **Social loosening, not revolution:** caste stigma challenged in the sacred sphere (temple entry at Melkote; langar; panths of low-caste gurus) though the secular caste order survived — honest evaluation scores.
4. **New communities & institutions:** Sikhism; Kabirpanth; Varkari; Gaudiya; Veerashaiva mathas; sattras — durable civil-society structures.
5. **Hindu–Muslim bridge:** Kabir-Nanak's "neither Hindu nor Turk", shared shrines, Sufi-sant idiom exchange → composite culture.
6. **Women's religious voice:** Andal→Akka Mahadevi→Lal Ded→Mira→Timmakka — a canon of female authorship rare in the medieval world.
7. **Long fuse:** 19th-century reformers (Brahmo, Arya, Prarthana Samaj; Gandhi's Ram-dhun & "Vaishnava jana to") consciously drew on bhakti's egalitarian monotheism.

# PART B — THE SUFI MOVEMENT

## B1. What Sufism is
Islamic **mysticism (tasawwuf)** — the inner path (**tariqa**) to direct experience of God through love, remembrance (**zikr**), asceticism and annihilation of ego (**fana**), under a master (**pir/shaikh/murshid**) guiding disciples (**murids**) in a lodge (**KHANQAH**), organised in orders (**SILSILAS**). Doctrinal keystone in India: **Ibn Arabi's *WAHDAT-UL-WUJUD* ("unity of being" — all existence one in God)** — the natural bridge to Vedantic monism ⭐ (contrast the later orthodox correction, *wahdat-ush-shuhud* of Sirhindi, Mughal era).

**Vocabulary drill:** sama (musical audition) · qawwali · urs (death-anniversary "wedding with God") · ziyarat (shrine visit) · futuh (unasked charity that ran khanqahs) · langar (free kitchen) · **ba-shara** orders (within Sharia) vs **be-shara** (antinomian qalandars).

## B2. The silsilas in India (⭐ table = guaranteed marks)

| Silsila | Founder in India & centres | Traits |
|---|---|---|
| **CHISHTI** (most Indian) | **Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti — Ajmer** (came c. 1192, d. 1236; "**Gharib Nawaz**"); chain: **Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki** (Delhi; Qutub Minar's namesake) → **Baba Farid Ganj-i-Shakar** (Ajodhan/Pakpattan; **his verses are in the Adi Granth** ⭐) → **NIZAMUDDIN AULIYA** (Delhi, 1238–1325; "Mehboob-i-Ilahi"; saw seven sultans, entered no court — "my house has two doors"; the *Hunuz Dilli dur ast* — "Delhi is yet far" — story vs Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq; disciples **Amir Khusrau & Amir Hasan**) → **Nasiruddin Chiragh-i-Dehli** (d. 1356) | Poverty, no state service or jagirs, acceptance of **sama/qawwali**, langar for all castes/creeds, Hindavi speech — hence deepest Indian roots |
| Chishti → Deccan ⭐ | **Burhanuddin Gharib** (Khuldabad, with MBT's Daulatabad shift); **SAYYID MUHAMMAD GESUDARAZ "KHWAJA BANDE NAWAZ"** (disciple of Chiragh; migrated **1398 → GULBARGA**; d. 1422; patronised by **Bahmani** sultans; wrote also in early **DAKHNI** — *Miraj-ul-Ashiqin* traditionally attributed) | Carried the order — and Dakhni Urdu — into the Telugu-Kannada Deccan; his **urs at Gulbarga** still draws all communities |
| **SUHRAWARDI** | **Bahauddin Zakariya — Multan** (13th c.); grandson-line's **Rukn-i-Alam** (tomb = Tughlaq architecture gem) | Opposite policy: **accepted state office (Shaikh-ul-Islam), wealth and court links**; Punjab-Sind-Multan belt |
| **FIRDAUSI** | **Sharafuddin Yahya Maneri** (Bihar Sharif, d. 1381) | *Maktubat-i-Sadi* (100 letters) — the model letter-corpus |
| **KUBRAWIYA** | **Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani → Kashmir (1372+)** | Brought **700 sayyids & Persian crafts (shawl/carpet weaving)** — economy-changing migration ⭐ |
| **QADIRI** | From 15th c. (Uch, Sindh-Punjab) | Flourished under Mughals (Miyan Mir, Dara Shukoh) — note as later |
| **NAQSHBANDI** | Baqi Billah → **Ahmad Sirhindi** | Orthodox, anti-sama, anti-wujudi — the Mughal-era reaction; only flag it |
| **SHATTARI** | Abdullah Shattari (d. 1485, Mandu); later Muhammad Ghaus of Gwalior | Yogic-practice absorption (translated *Amritakunda* tradition) |
| **RISHI order (indigenous!)** | **Sheikh Nooruddin Wali "Nund Rishi"** (1377–1440), Charar-i-Sharief, Kashmir | Vegetarian, Shaiva-tinged; heir to **Lal Ded** — the perfect syncretism example ⭐ |

## B3. Sufi literature (source-types examiners love)
- **Malfuzat** (recorded table-talk): ***Fawaid-ul-Fuad*** — Nizamuddin's sayings by **Amir Hasan Sijzi**; *Khair-ul-Majalis* (Chiragh's, by Hamid Qalandar).
- **Maktubat** (letters): Maneri's *Maktubat-i-Sadi*.
- **Tazkira** (hagiographic biographies): *Siyar-ul-Auliya* (Mir Khwurd).
- **Premakhyans — Sufi romances in Hindavi/Awadhi using Indian tales as mystical allegory:** **Mulla Daud's *Chandayan* (1379)**, Qutban's *Mrigavati* (1503), **Jayasi's *PADMAVAT* (1540)**, Manjhan's *Madhumalati* (1545) ⭐ — the clearest literary proof of cultural fusion.
- Qawwali corpus of **Amir Khusrau** (Chhaap tilak, etc.) — living tradition.

## B4. Influence of the Sufis
1. **Mass-level cultural bridge:** khanqah & dargah open to all; langar; saint-veneration shared by Hindus — **Ajmer, Nizamuddin, Gulbarga** remain inter-community shrines.
2. **Conversion by attraction & settlement**, especially on agrarian frontiers — gradual acculturation around khanqahs (Eaton), not campaigns.
3. **Languages:** Sufis preached in the people's tongues — Farid's Punjabi, Hindavi in Delhi, **Dakhni in the Deccan** (Gesudaraz) — midwifing Urdu's two branches.
4. **Music:** sama → **qawwali**; defended music against orthodox objection; fed back into bhakti's kirtan culture.
5. **Moral critique of power:** Chishti refusal of court service modelled an ethical counter-authority (contrast Suhrawardi collaboration — a ready compare-contrast).
6. **Dialogue with yoga & Vedanta:** Nath-yogi conversations, *Amritakunda* translations, wahdat-ul-wujud ↔ Advaita resonance.
7. Softening of rulers' sectarianism at times (intercession, sanctuary), and legitimacy rituals (sultans courting shaikhs' blessing — Aibak-Kaki, Tughlaqs-Nizamuddin tension included).

## B5. Bhakti ↔ Sufi: comparison table (Mains finisher)

| Dimension | Bhakti | Sufi |
|---|---|---|
| Matrix | Hindu devotional tradition (Gita → Tamil saints) | Islamic mysticism (Quranic piety + Persian tasawwuf) |
| God | Saguna (Rama/Krishna/Shiva/Venkateshwara) or Nirguna | Strictly one God; wujudi monism as mystic frame |
| Guide | **Guru** | **Pir/Murshid** |
| Practice | Kirtan/bhajan, nama-japa, prapatti | Zikr, sama/qawwali, muraqaba |
| Institution | Temple, matha, panth, sattra | Khanqah, dargah, silsila |
| Language | Vernaculars (Telugu, Tamil, Hindi…) | Persian + vernaculars (Punjabi, Hindavi, Dakhni) |
| Social edge | Anti-caste in worship; women saints | Egalitarian langar; open lodge |
| Shared core | **Love of God, guide's grace, music, poverty of spirit, indifference to ritual-legalism, mass vernacular reach** | (same) |

**Joint influence (one sentence to close any answer):** Together they created medieval India's **composite devotional culture** — the emotional infrastructure of Hindu–Muslim coexistence — and its richest legacy of song, poetry and shrine that APPSC's own region embodies from **Annamayya's Tirumala to Bande Nawaz's Gulbarga**.

## Prelims nuggets (both movements)

1. 12 Alvars (Andal the woman; Nathamuni compiled Prabandham); 63 Nayanars (Tevaram trio; Periyapuranam = Sekkilar).
2. Advaita-Vishishtadvaita-Dvaita-Dvaitadvaita-Shuddhadvaita = Shankara-Ramanuja-Madhva-Nimbarka-Vallabha.
3. Ramanuja: Sribhashya; Melkote Dalit temple entry; converted Hoysala Vishnuvardhana.
4. Basava: Bijjala's minister; Anubhava Mantapa; vachanas; Akka Mahadevi.
5. Ramananda's disciples: Kabir, Ravidas, Sena, Sadhana, Pipa, Dhanna.
6. Adi Granth contains: Nanak+Gurus, **Kabir, Ravidas, Namdev, Baba Farid**.
7. Annamayya: 1408–1503, Tallapaka (Kadapa), Venkateshwara sankirtanas on copper plates, "Padakavita Pitamaha", "Brahmam Okate"; wife Timmakka first Telugu poetess.
8. Potana: Andhra Maha Bhagavatamu, dedicated to Rama, refused royal patronage.
9. Palkuriki Somanatha: Basava Puranamu in dwipada (Telugu Veerashaiva).
10. Purandaradasa — father of Carnatic music (Haridasa movement, Vijayanagara era).
11. Chishti chain: Moinuddin (Ajmer) → Kaki (Delhi) → Farid (Pakpattan) → Nizamuddin (Delhi) → Chiragh (Delhi) → (Deccan) Gesudaraz (Gulbarga, Bahmani).
12. Suhrawardi = state-linked (Multan, Bahauddin Zakariya); Firdausi = Maneri (Bihar); Kubrawi = Hamadani (Kashmir crafts); Rishi = Nund Rishi (vegetarian, Lal Ded's heir).
13. Malfuzat model = Fawaid-ul-Fuad (Amir Hasan Sijzi).
14. Premakhyans: Chandayan (1379, first), Padmavat (1540, Jayasi).
15. Wahdat-ul-wujud = Ibn Arabi; Indian bridge to Advaita; countered later by Sirhindi's wahdat-ush-shuhud.

## Mains angles

1. **"Bhakti was born in the South and re-born in the North."** Tamil dawn (Pallava file cross-ref) → acharyas → Sultanate-era sants; explain what changed (nirguna radicalism, artisan base, Sufi contact) and what persisted (vernacular song, guru, anti-ritualism).
2. **"Assess the Andhra contribution to the Bhakti movement."** Aradhya Shaivism & Palkuriki's dwipada; Tikkana's Hariharanatha; Annamayya's sankirtana revolution & social verses; Potana's patronage-free Bhagavatamu; temple network (Srisailam-Tirumala-Ahobilam); continuity to Ramadasu-Tyagaraja.
3. **"Chishti aloofness vs Suhrawardi engagement — two Sufi theories of power."** Use Nizamuddin ("two doors") vs Zakariya (Shaikh-ul-Islam); evaluate which strategy embedded Islam deeper in Indian society.
4. **"Bhakti and Sufism as parallel and converging streams."** Deploy B5 table + shared music/language evidence + shrines; close with composite-culture verdict and modern constitutional echo (equality, fraternity).
5. **"Evaluate the social limits of both movements."** Sacred-sphere equality vs untouched agrarian caste order; women's voice vs continuing patriarchy; sects ossifying into new orthodoxies (Lingayat caste-hood, panth institutionalisation) — nuanced criticism earns the top band.
