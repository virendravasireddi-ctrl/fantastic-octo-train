## 1. Major landforms

### 1.1 The Himalayas — young fold mountains

Formed by the **collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates** (Tethys geosyncline squeezed up); still rising — hence earthquakes. Length **~2,400 km**, arcuate (convex to south); width 400 km (Kashmir) → 150 km (Arunachal).

**Three parallel longitudinal ranges:**

| Range | Alt. | Facts to quote |
|---|---|---|
| **Himadri (Greater/Inner Himalaya)** | avg **6,000 m+** | Loftiest, continuous, granite core; all 8,000-m peaks — **Everest 8,849 m** (Nepal; *Sagarmatha/Chomolungma*), **Kanchenjunga 8,586 m** — **highest peak within India** (Sikkim); perpetual snow, glaciers (Gangotri, Siachen — Karakoram) |
| **Himachal (Lesser/Middle)** | 3,700–4,500 m | Ranges: **Pir Panjal** (longest lesser-Himalayan range), **Dhauladhar**, **Mahabharat**; valleys of **Kashmir, Kangra, Kullu**; hill stations — Shimla, Mussoorie, Darjeeling; famous **margs** (Gulmarg, Sonamarg) |
| **Shiwaliks (Outer)** | 900–1,100 m | Youngest, unconsolidated sediments; longitudinal valleys = **DUNS** (**Dehradun**, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun) ⭐ favourite MCQ |

**West–east (regional) divisions** (between rivers): **Punjab/Kashmir Himalaya** (Indus–Satluj) · **Kumaon** (Satluj–Kali) · **Nepal** (Kali–Teesta) · **Assam** (Teesta–Dihang).

- **Trans-Himalaya** (north of Great Himalaya): **Karakoram** (K2/Godwin Austen **8,611 m** — highest in the Indian subcontinent's India-administered claim, in the Karakoram not the Himalaya), **Ladakh, Zaskar, Kailash** ranges; cold desert of Ladakh.
- **Purvanchal** (eastern hills, beyond Dihang gorge): **Patkai Bum, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills, Mizo (Lushai) Hills** — bend southwards along Myanmar border.
- Passes to memorise: **Zoji La** (Ladakh), **Shipki La** (Satluj/HP), **Nathu La & Jelep La** (Sikkim), **Bomdi La** (Arunachal), **Banihal** (Pir Panjal), **Rohtang** (HP).

### 1.2 The Northern Plains

- Built by **alluvium of Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra** systems in the foredeep between Himalaya and Peninsula; ~**7 lakh sq km**, ~2,400 km long, 240–320 km wide; agriculturally India's most productive belt.
- **North–south micro-belts** ⭐ (classic Prelims set):
  - **Bhabar** — pebble/porous belt at the Shiwalik foot; **streams disappear** underground.
  - **Terai** — streams **re-emerge**; wet, marshy, once forested (now farmland).
  - **Bhangar** — **older alluvium**, terrace-like uplands, contains calcareous **KANKAR** nodules.
  - **Khadar** — **newer alluvium** of floodplains, renewed yearly, most fertile.
- Regional parts: **Punjab plains** (doabs between the five rivers), **Ganga plains** (Ghaggar→Teesta), **Brahmaputra plains** (Assam). **Majuli** (Brahmaputra) — **world's largest inhabited river island**.

### 1.3 The Peninsular Plateau

- **Oldest landmass** — part of **Gondwanaland**; stable shield of gneisses and granites; mineral storehouse.
- Two broad units:
  - **Central Highlands** (north of Narmada): **Malwa plateau** (Aravali–Vindhya), **Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Chotanagpur plateau** (Damodar drains it — India's mineral heartland).
  - **Deccan Plateau** (south of Narmada, triangular): flanked by **Satpura**, **Mahadev–Kaimur–Maikal** in north; **black lava (Deccan Trap)** in NW; **Dandakaranya**, Telangana and Karnataka plateaus.
- **Western Ghats (Sahyadri):** continuous wall, avg 900–1,600 m; passes — **Thal, Bhor, Pal ghats**; peaks — **Anamudi 2,695 m** (**highest of Peninsular India**, Anaimalai hills), **Doddabetta 2,637 m** (Nilgiris). Rainfall-maker (orographic).
- **Eastern Ghats:** **discontinuous, lower**, cut by Mahanadi–Godavari–Krishna–Kaveri; peaks — **Arma Konda (Sitamma Konda) ~1,680 m in the Visakhapatnam hills of AP — the highest of the Eastern Ghats** (older books quote **Mahendragiri 1,501 m**, Odisha); **Jindhagada (~1,690 m, AP)** also claimed highest in recent surveys — flag both in Mains. ⭐ AP tie-in.
- Ghats meet at the **Nilgiris**; south of Palghat Gap — Anaimalai, Palani, **Cardamom hills**.
- **Aravalis** — **oldest fold mountains in India** (Precambrian relict), Delhi→Gujarat; **Guru Shikhar 1,722 m** (Mt Abu).

### 1.4 The Indian (Thar) Desert

- NW of Aravalis; arid (<150 mm rain), sand dunes — crescent **barchans**, longitudinal dunes; **LUNI** — the only significant river (ends in the Rann); salt lakes — **Sambhar** (India's largest inland salt lake), Didwana.

### 1.5 The Coastal Plains

| Western coast (narrow, submergent) | Eastern coast (broad, emergent) |
|---|---|
| **Konkan** (Mumbai–Goa) → **Kannad/Karnataka coast** → **Malabar** (Kerala — **kayals/backwaters**, e.g. Vembanad; lagoon tourism) | **Northern Circars** (Ganga delta→Krishna delta stretch incl. AP coast) → **Coromandel** (south of Krishna→Kanniyakumari) |
| Rivers form **estuaries** (Narmada, Tapti) | Rivers form **DELTAS** — Mahanadi, **Godavari, Krishna**, Kaveri ⭐ |
| Ports: Kandla, Mumbai, Mormugao, Kochi | Ports: Chennai, **Visakhapatnam**, Paradip |

- Lakes/lagoons of the east coast: **Chilika** (largest brackish lagoon, Odisha), **Kolleru** (freshwater, AP), **Pulicat** (brackish; AP–TN; **Sriharikota** barrier island — SHAR/ISRO launches). ⭐ AP tie-ins.

### 1.6 The Islands

- **Andaman & Nicobar** (Bay of Bengal): submerged extension of Arakan Yoma; **Barren Island — India's only active volcano**; **Saddle Peak 732 m** (highest); **Ten Degree Channel** separates Andamans from Nicobars; **Indira Point** southernmost.
- **Lakshadweep** (Arabian Sea): **coral atolls**; capital Kavaratti; **Pitti island** — bird sanctuary; **Eight/Nine Degree Channels** (Minicoy separated by 9° Channel from the rest, 8° Channel from Maldives).

## 2. Climate and climatic changes

### 2.1 The monsoon mechanism (why India's climate is "tropical monsoon")

- Controls: **latitude** (Tropic of Cancer splits India into tropical/sub-tropical halves), **Himalayas** (climatic barrier — block Central-Asian cold winds, force monsoon to shed rain), **land–sea differential heating**, northward shift of **ITCZ** into the Ganga plain in summer, **subtropical westerly jet** (its withdrawal north of the Himalaya triggers onset) and **tropical easterly jet**, **El Niño/La Niña (ENSO)** & Indian Ocean Dipole modulating rainfall.
- **South-West monsoon (Jun–Sep):** ~**75% of annual rainfall**; "burst" at Kerala ~1 June; two branches — **Arabian Sea branch** (Western Ghats: Konkan–Malabar rain; rain-shadow east of Ghats) and **Bay of Bengal branch** (NE India & Ganga plain; **Mawsynram/Cherrapunji — world's highest rainfall**). Monsoon **breaks** = dry spells.
- **Retreating monsoon (Oct–Nov):** clear skies, "**October heat**"; **cyclones strike the east coast (AP, Odisha, TN)** ⭐; **North-East monsoon** rains Tamil Nadu and **south-coastal AP/Rayalaseema**.
- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** NE trade winds land→sea (dry); **western disturbances** (Mediterranean cyclones) give Punjab–Himachal rain/snow — vital for rabi wheat.
- **Summer (Mar–May):** **loo** (hot winds, north India), **Kalbaisakhi/Norwesters** (Bengal–Assam; good for tea/jute), **mango showers** (Kerala–Karnataka pre-monsoon).
- Rainfall distribution: >200 cm (west coast, NE); <50 cm (W Rajasthan, Ladakh, **rain-shadow interior incl. Rayalaseema-adjacent Deccan**).

### 2.2 Climatic change — exam angles

- Trends reported for India: **~0.7°C warming (1901–2018)**, more **extreme rainfall events** amid stagnant/declining monsoon totals, **sea-level rise** threatening deltas (Godavari–Krishna included), more intense **Arabian Sea cyclones**, Himalayan **glacier retreat** (Gangotri), heatwaves.
- Impacts: crop-yield stress (wheat sensitivity), water stress, coastal erosion & salinity ingress in deltas, coral bleaching (Lakshadweep, Gulf of Mannar).
- Responses to name-drop: **NAPCC (2008, 8 missions incl. National Water Mission & Green India Mission)**, State Action Plans, **Panchamrit/net-zero by 2070** (COP26), International Solar Alliance, CDRI.

## 3. Soil types of India (ICAR 8-fold) ⭐ table is the whole game

| Soil | Where | Character | Crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alluvial** (~45% area — largest) | Northern plains, coastal deltas (incl. **Godavari–Krishna deltas**) | Khadar/Bhangar; **rich in potash & lime, poor in N & humus**; very fertile | Rice, wheat, sugarcane |
| **Black (Regur)** | Deccan Trap — Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat, **W. AP (Kurnool–Anantapur–Kadapa), Krishna–Guntur** | From basalt; **moisture-retentive, self-ploughing** (cracks in summer); rich in **lime, iron, magnesia, alumina**; poor in **N, P, humus** | **Cotton** ("black cotton soil"), chillies, tobacco |
| **Red & yellow** | Peninsular granite-gneiss country — TN, Karnataka, **most of AP (~⅔)**, Odisha, Jharkhand | Colour from **iron-oxide diffusion**; porous, **poor in N, P, humus**; responds to fertiliser + irrigation | Groundnut, millets, pulses |
| **Laterite** | High-rain alternating wet-dry: W. Ghats crests, NE, patches of Odisha/AP uplands | Intense **leaching**; rich iron-aluminium, acidic, infertile; **hardens into brick** (building stone!) | Cashew, tea, coffee, tapioca |
| **Arid/desert** | Rajasthan, N. Gujarat | Sandy, saline, **kankar layers restrict infiltration**; fertile if irrigated (Indira Gandhi canal) | Bajra, guar |
| **Saline/alkaline (usara)** | Canal-irrigated tracts, dry deltas | Sodium/salt efflorescence; reclaimed by **gypsum** | — |
| **Peaty/marshy** | Kerala (**kari** of Kottayam), Sunderbans, deltaic swamps | High organic matter, acidic | — |
| **Forest/mountain** | Himalayan slopes | Thin, immature, acidic in coniferous zones | Orchards, tea |

Soil problems: erosion (gullies of **Chambal ravines**), waterlogging, salinisation, fertility loss → conservation: contour bunding, terracing, shelter belts, watershed development.

## 4. Rivers, water and streams

### 4.1 Himalayan vs Peninsular rivers ⭐ (compare-question staple)

| Himalayan | Peninsular |
|---|---|
| **Perennial** (rain + snow/glacier) | Mostly **seasonal** (monsoon-fed) |
| Long courses, huge basins, meanders, antecedent gorges | Shorter, fixed courses in hard rock, **graded profiles** (older) |
| Youthful, heavy erosion & silt | Senile topography, low silt |
| Good for navigation/irrigation in plains | Good for **hydro-power in gorges** + delta irrigation |

### 4.2 The Himalayan systems

- **Indus (Sindhu):** rises near **Mansarovar**; enters India in Ladakh; Panjnad tributaries — **Jhelum, Chenab (largest), Ravi, Beas, Satluj**. **Indus Waters Treaty 1960**: India — eastern rivers (Ravi–Beas–Satluj); Pakistan — western (Indus–Jhelum–Chenab).
- **Ganga:** **Bhagirathi + Alaknanda join at Devprayag**; **longest river of India (~2,525 km)**; left-bank — Ramganga, **Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi ("sorrow of Bihar")**; right-bank — **Yamuna** (largest tributary), **Son, Damodar ("sorrow of Bengal")**; distributary **Hooghly**; delta — **Sundarbans (world's largest)**. National River (2008); **Namami Gange**.
- **Brahmaputra:** **Tsangpo** in Tibet; enters Arunachal as **Dihang/Siang**; joined by **Dibang & Lohit**; tributaries Subansiri, Manas, Teesta; braided channel, **Majuli**; floods of Assam.

### 4.3 The Peninsular rivers

**East-flowing (delta-formers, into Bay of Bengal):**

| River | Source | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Godavari** | **Trimbakeshwar (Nashik)** | **1,465 km — largest Peninsular river ("Dakshina Ganga/Vriddha Ganga")** | Tributaries: Pranhita, Indravati, **Manjira, Sabari**; delta in AP ⭐ (detail in File 13) |
| **Krishna** | **Mahabaleshwar** | ~1,400 km | Tributaries: **Koyna, Bhima, TUNGABHADRA, Musi, Munneru**; AP–TS–KA–MH sharing disputes (Bachawat / Brijesh Kumar tribunals) |
| **Kaveri** | Talakaveri (Brahmagiri, Coorg) | 800 km | "Ganga of the South"; Sivasamudram falls (first hydel station in India, 1902); KA–TN dispute |
| **Mahanadi** | Sihawa (Chhattisgarh) | 851 km | **Hirakud** dam |
| **Penna (Pinakini)** | **Nandi Hills (Karnataka)** | 597 km | Flows through **Rayalaseema–Nellore** ⭐ (File 13) |
| Others | — | — | Subarnarekha, Brahmani, **Vamsadhara, Nagavali** (AP–Odisha ⭐), Palar, Vaigai |

**West-flowing (estuary-formers):**
- **Narmada** — **rift valley** between Vindhya & Satpura; Amarkantak source; **Dhuandhar falls (marble rocks, Bhedaghat)**; Sardar Sarovar.
- **Tapti (Tapi)** — parallel rift; Multai source.
- **Sabarmati, Mahi** (Gujarat), **Periyar** (Kerala), **Sharavati — Jog/Gersoppa falls** (Karnataka, highest plunge among major falls).
- Why no deltas? Short courses, hard-rock beds, low silt, submerged coast — classic 2-marker.

### 4.4 Water resources & streams

- India: ~**4% of world's freshwater for ~18% of world population**; average annual precipitation ~4,000 BCM; utilisable ~**1,123 BCM (690 surface + 433 ground)**; per-capita availability falling (**water-stressed** <1,700 m³/person; India ~1,486 m³ in 2021 est.).
- **Groundwater**: largest user is **irrigation (~89%)**; over-exploited blocks in Punjab-Haryana-Rajasthan-TN-**Rayalaseema**; regulation — CGWB, **Atal Bhujal Yojana**.
- Streams/drainage patterns to know: **dendritic** (Ganga plain), **radial** (Amarkantak), **trellis**, **centripetal** (Sambhar); **antecedent** rivers (Indus, Satluj, Brahmaputra cut Himalaya).
- Missions: **Jal Jeevan Mission** (tap water to rural homes), **Jal Shakti Abhiyan**, **National Water Mission** (20% efficiency), river-linking (**Ken–Betwa** first approved link; **Godavari–Krishna links already operational via Polavaram right canal/Pattiseema** ⭐ AP tie-in), **PMKSY** (irrigation).
- Rainwater harvesting: tanks of the Deccan (historic **golusukattu** chains — Kakatiya legacy, File 06 link!), johads, kudimaramath.
