## 0. The ferment

Buddhist texts count **62/63 heterodox schools** in the 6th century BCE — including the **Ajivikas (Makkhali Gosala — fatalism/niyati)** and **Charvaka/Lokayata (materialism)**. Jainism and Buddhism are the two that survived — best understood as **responses to a specific crisis**, not free-floating spiritual events.

## 1. Why THEN? — the causes ⭐ (2020 PYQ verbatim: "causes of the emergence of Buddhism")

1. **Religious:** revolt against **costly, priest-monopolised Vedic ritualism** and unintelligible Sanskrit; the Upanishadic turn had already primed an inward, ethical mood.
2. **Social:** rejection of the **rigid varna order**; both founders were **Kshatriyas** — a Kshatriya assertion against Brahmin ritual dominance.
3. **Economic (mercantile):** the rising **merchant/gahapati class** wanted dignity the varna order denied and a faith that sanctioned trade — both faiths won **mercantile patronage**.
4. **Economic (agrarian):** **ahimsa protected the plough-ox** — ending wasteful cattle sacrifice suited the new plough-and-cart economy. *(The "ahimsa had an economic logic" line is the analytical clincher.)*

## 2. Jainism

- **24 Tirthankaras:** first **Rishabhanatha/Adinatha**; 23rd **Parshvanatha** (historical, c. 8th c. BCE, **four vows**); 24th **Vardhamana Mahavira** — the **reformer, NOT the founder** (classic trap).
- **Mahavira (c. 599–527 BCE):** born **Kundagrama near Vaishali** (Jnatrika Kshatriya clan); renounced at 30; **kaivalya** after 12 years; titles **Jina** ("conqueror"), Mahavira; **nirvana at Pavapuri**.
- **Doctrine:** liberation of the **jiva (soul)** from **karma — literally subtle MATTER** clinging to it; path = **Triratna (right faith, knowledge, conduct)**; **five vows** — ahimsa, satya, asteya, aparigraha, brahmacharya (**the fifth added by Mahavira** to Parshva's four); **extreme asceticism**, incl. **sallekhana** (ritual fasting to death); **no creator God**; six dravyas (jiva, pudgala, dharma, adharma, akasha, kala); bondage → samvara → nirjara → moksha.
- **Logic — the Jain signature:** **anekantavada** (many-sidedness) via **syadvada/saptabhangi** (seven-fold conditional predication) and nayavada — a distinctive contribution to Indian epistemology.
- **Canon & schism:** Agamas (**12 Angas**; the 12th with the Purvas lost) compiled finally at the **Council of Vallabhi (5th c. CE) in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit**; split into **Digambara** (sky-clad, southern; holds the original canon lost; denies women's liberation in that body) vs **Shvetambara** (white-clad, northern).
- **Spread & patrons:** **Chandragupta Maurya** (tradition — migrated with **Bhadrabahu** to **Shravanabelagola**, died by sallekhana); **Kharavela of Kalinga** (Hathigumpha); western-Indian merchants → survival in **Gujarat, Rajasthan, Karnataka**. Contributions: **Prakrit/Apabhramsha and regional-language literature (Kannada, Tamil, Gujarati)**, temple art (**Dilwara; Gomateshvara at Shravanabelagola**), the ahimsa-vegetarian ethic.

## 3. Buddhism

- **The Buddha (c. 563–483 BCE; some date c. 480–400):** Siddhartha Gautama, born **Lumbini** to Shuddhodana, chief of the **Shakya gana-sangha**; the **Four Sights** → **Great Renunciation**; **enlightenment at Bodh Gaya**; **first sermon (Dharmachakra-pravartana) at Sarnath**; **mahaparinirvana at Kushinagara**. (Four great sites: Lumbini–Bodh Gaya–Sarnath–Kushinagara.)
- **Teaching — the Middle Path** (implicit critique of Jain mortification): **Four Noble Truths** (suffering; craving its cause; it can cease — nirvana; the way = **Eightfold Path**); **anatman (no permanent soul — the sharpest contrast with Jainism), anitya, pratityasamutpada** (dependent origination); silent on God; the **Sangha open across caste** — its social dynamite; preached in **Pali/Prakrit**, not Sanskrit.
- **The four councils ⭐ (guaranteed match question):**

| Council | Date | Place | Patron | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | c. 483 BCE | **Rajagriha** | Ajatashatru | **Sutta (Ananda) & Vinaya (Upali)** compiled |
| Second | c. 383 BCE | **Vaishali** | Kalashoka | First schism (Sthaviravada vs Mahasanghika) |
| Third | c. 250 BCE | **Pataliputra** | **Ashoka** | **Abhidhamma added → Tripitaka**; Moggaliputta Tissa; missions (Mahinda & Sanghamitra → Sri Lanka) |
| Fourth | c. 1st c. CE | **Kashmir (Kundalavana)** | **Kanishka** | Vasumitra/Ashvaghosha; Sanskrit texts; **Hinayana–Mahayana split crystallises** |

- **Hinayana vs Mahayana:** self-liberation, Pali, aniconic, historical Buddha (survives Sri Lanka/SE Asia) VS **Bodhisattva ideal**, image worship, Sanskrit (spreads China–Japan–Tibet); **Vajrayana** (tantric) later.
- **Canon & schools:** **Tripitaka** + Jatakas (birth-stories — social history and the seedbed of early Buddhist art) + **Milindapanha** (Menander–Nagasena dialogue); four philosophical schools — **Vaibhashika & Sautrantika** (Hinayana realists), **Yogachara/Vijnanavada (Asanga, Vasubandhu — mind-only)**, **Madhyamika (NAGARJUNA — shunyata)** ⭐.
- **Spread:** royal patronage (Ashoka, Kanishka) + Sangha + vernacular preaching + trade routes. **Decline in India:** loss of patronage; revived-reformed Hinduism (bhakti, Shankara); Sangha's ritualisation (tantra); **destruction of the monastic universities (Nalanda, Vikramashila) in the Turkish invasions** — Buddhism was institutionally concentrated, so destroying the monasteries destroyed it; absorbed finally as **Buddha = an avatara of Vishnu**.

## 4. Jainism vs Buddhism ⭐ (the comparison examiners love)

| Axis | Jainism | Buddhism |
|---|---|---|
| Soul | **Eternal jiva** in everything | **Anatman** — no permanent soul |
| Ahimsa | **Absolute** (even microbes) | Moderate — Middle Path |
| Asceticism | Severe (sallekhana) | Extremes rejected |
| God | No creator; eternal universe | Silent; ethical-psychological focus |
| Language | **Prakrit (Ardhamagadhi)** | **Pali** (later Sanskrit) |
| Reach | Stayed Indian — Gujarat, Rajasthan, Karnataka | **World religion** across Asia |
| Fate | **Survives in India** | Declined in India, thrives abroad |

## 5. Andhra and the Buddhist world ⭐⭐ (APPSC's home turf — add to every answer)

| Site/fact | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Bhattiprolu (Guntur)** | Relic-casket inscriptions (c. 3rd–2nd c. BCE) in the distinctive **"Bhattiprolu script"** — among the earliest Brahmi-family writing in the south |
| **Amaravati (Dhanyakataka)** | Stupa traditionally founded in the Mauryan age → the **Mahastupa** and the Amaravati school (File 00F) |
| **Nagarjunakonda (Sriparvata-Vijayapuri)** | Great monastic complex; tradition links the Madhyamika master **NAGARJUNA** to Sriparvata ⭐ |
| **Thotlakonda & Bavikonda (Visakhapatnam), Salihundam (Srikakulam)** | The coastal chain of monasteries on the sea-lanes to SE Asia — Andhra as Buddhism's maritime gateway |
| The through-line | Mauryan-age arrival → Satavahana-Ikshvaku patronage → Andhra's centuries as a Buddhist heartland (Files 00D, 00F) |

## 6. Prelims nuggets

1. **Mahavira = 24th tirthankara (reformer, not founder)**; Parshvanatha 23rd — four vows; fifth vow (brahmacharya) added by Mahavira.
2. Mahavira: **Kundagrama birth, Pavapuri nirvana**; kaivalya after 12 years.
3. **Anekantavada/syadvada = Jain**; karma = subtle matter; **sallekhana** = fast unto death (Chandragupta Maurya at **Shravanabelagola**, with **Bhadrabahu**).
4. Jain canon: **Council of Vallabhi, 5th c. CE, Ardhamagadhi**; Digambara vs Shvetambara.
5. Buddha: **Lumbini–Bodh Gaya–Sarnath (first sermon)–Kushinagara**; Shakya clan = a gana-sangha.
6. **Anatman, anitya, pratityasamutpada**; Middle Path; Sangha caste-blind; language **Pali**.
7. Councils: **Rajagriha-Ajatashatru; Vaishali-Kalashoka; Pataliputra-Ashoka (Tripitaka complete); Kashmir-Kanishka (Mahayana)** ⭐.
8. **Milindapanha** = Menander–Nagasena; **Nagarjuna = Madhyamika/shunyata**; Asanga-Vasubandhu = Yogachara.
9. Ajivikas — **Makkhali Gosala** (fatalism); Charvaka — materialism.
10. **Bhattiprolu script (Guntur)**; Thotlakonda-Bavikonda-Salihundam = AP coastal monasteries ⭐.
11. Buddhism's decline: patronage loss + Hindu revival + tantricisation + **Turkish destruction of Nalanda-Vikramashila**.
12. Kharavela (Jain patron) — **Hathigumpha inscription**.
