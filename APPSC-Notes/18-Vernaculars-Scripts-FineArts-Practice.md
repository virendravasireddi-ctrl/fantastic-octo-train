# 18 · Growth of Vernacular Languages, Scripts, Literature & Fine Arts (c. 1000–1687) — Synthesis + §2 Practice

[← Bahmanis & Qutb Shahis](17-Bahmani-and-Qutb-Shahi.md) · [Index](README.md) · [Next: The Mughal Empire & Administration →](19-Mughal-Empire-and-Administration.md)

*This file answers the syllabus phrase head-on — "Growth of Vernacular Languages, Scripts, Literature, Fine Arts" — as a cross-dynasty theme, then gives §2 revision tables, MCQs and Mains questions.*

---

## 1. WHY the vernaculars grew (the mechanism — open any Mains answer with this)

1. **New kings needed new audiences:** peasant-warrior dynasties (Kakatiya, Reddi, Velama, Nayaka) and even Persianate courts (Qutb Shahi) legitimised themselves **in the language of their soldiers and taxpayers**, not only in Sanskrit/Persian.
2. **Bhakti & Veerashaiva publics:** devotion demanded songs people could sing — dwipada, vachana, sankirtana, abhang, padam.
3. **Administration vernacularised:** Telugu became the **language of inscriptions and revenue records** under the Kakatiyas (grammar + Mitakshara rendered into Telugu by Ketana); ayagar-karanam record-keeping fixed it permanently.
4. **Temple & festival economy:** puranas recited, sthala-mahatmyas composed, dance-dramas staged — culture had paying institutions.
5. **Paper (a Sultanate-era import)** cheapened writing; courts kept multilingual chancelleries (Persian ↔ Telugu ↔ Marathi).

## 2. SCRIPTS — one paragraph to memorise

Southern **Brahmi** (Bhattiprolu variant in Andhra) evolved through the **Pallava-Grantha** and **Telugu-Kannada (Chalukyan)** scripts of the 7th–12th centuries; by the **Kakatiya 13th century** the common script had split into **distinct Telugu and Kannada scripts**; **Grantha** continued for Sanskrit in the Tamil country, **Nandinagari** was used for Sanskrit in Vijayanagara records, and the **Perso-Arabic script** arrived with Dakhni — so by 1500 the Telugu country wrote **Telugu script for Telugu, Nandinagari/Grantha for Sanskrit, and Persian script for the Sultanate's records**. (Pallava-Grantha's export to South-East Asia — §2 — completes the script story.)

## 3. LITERATURE — the growth table by language

| Language | Take-off | Medieval landmarks (this unit) |
|---|---|---|
| **TELUGU** | Nannaya (11th c.) | **Kakatiya age:** Tikkana, Ketana's grammar, dwipada canon (Palkuriki, Gona Budda) → **Reddi age:** Errana completes Kavitraya; **SRINATHA**; Potana's Bhagavatamu → **Vijayanagara:** KDR's *Amuktamalyada* + **Ashtadiggaja prabandhas** → **Qutb Shahi:** first **accha-Telugu** kavya (Telaganarya), Kshetrayya padams, Ramadasu kirtanas → **Nayaka Thanjavur:** southern school (Chemakura, women poets) |
| **KANNADA** | Kavirajamarga (9th c.) | vachanas (12th) → **Kumara Vyasa's Bharata** (Devaraya II) → **Haridasa dasa-sahitya** (Purandara, Kanaka) |
| **TAMIL** | Sangam/bhakti antiquity | Arunagirinathar's *Tiruppugazh* (Devaraya II); Nayaka-era temple literature; Kamban earlier (§2) |
| **MARATHI** | Yadava era | *Jnaneshwari* (1290) → Eknath-Tukaram (Bhakti file) |
| **ODIA** | Gajapati era ⭐ | **Sarala Das's Mahabharata** (Kapilendra); **Panchasakha** & Jagannatha Das's *Bhagabata* (Prataparudra-Chaitanya era) |
| **DAKHNI (early Urdu)** | Gulbarga Sufis (Gesudaraz) | **Muhammad Quli's diwan (first in Urdu)**, Wajhi's *Qutb-Mushtari* & *Sab Ras*, Ghawwasi — **Deccan before Delhi** ⭐ |
| **PERSIAN** | court language | Ferishta's history (Bijapur), Gawan's insha manuals, Qutb Shahi chancellery |
| **SANSKRIT** (the constant) | — | Vidyanatha (Kakatiya), royal treatises of Reddi-Velama courts, **Sayana's Veda-bhashyas, Madhuravijayam (Gangadevi)**, Vyasatirtha's dialectics (VJN) |

**One-line thesis:** *between 1000 and 1700 every South Indian language acquired its classic epic, its grammar, and its singing saint — and the state, whether Hindu or Muslim, paid for it.*

## 4. FINE ARTS — the unit's checklist

### 4.1 Music (treatise chain ⭐ — learn as a sequence)
**Sangita Ratnakara** (Sharngadeva, Yadava Devagiri, c. 1240 — the fork of Hindustani/Carnatic) → **Sangita Chintamani** (Pedakomati Vema, Reddi) → **Svaramelakalanidhi** (Ramamatya, 1550, Vijayanagara — mela system) → **Sangita Sudha** (Raghunatha Nayaka/Govinda Dikshita, Thanjavur) → **Chaturdandi Prakashika** (Venkatamakhin, c. 1660 — **72 melakartas**). Performance stream: **Annamayya's sankirtanas (Tirumala copper plates) → Purandaradasa's pedagogy → Kshetrayya's padams → Ramadasu's kirtanas** → (18th c.) the Trinity.

### 4.2 Dance & theatre
- **PERINI** (Kakatiya; Jayapa's *Nritta Ratnavali*; Ramappa sculptures; Nataraja Ramakrishna's revival).
- **KUCHIPUDI** (Siddhendra Yogi's *Bhamakalapam* tradition; **Tana Shah's 1678 agraharam grant** ⭐).
- **Yakshagana** (Telugu & Kannada popular opera; Rudrakavi's *Sugriva Vijayam*; Thanjavur court yakshaganas) and **Bhagavata Mela** (Melattur); devadasi/sadir temple dance throughout → later Bharatanatyam; Vasantotsava court performance (Reddi).

### 4.3 Painting
- **Vijayanagara murals:** **LEPAKSHI** (grandest), Virupaksha mandapa ceiling, Somapalem/Tirupati-region traditions.
- **DECCANI MINIATURES:** Ahmadnagar (*Tarif-i-Husain Shahi*), **Bijapur (Kitab-i-Nauras culture, Ragamala)**, **GOLCONDA school** (opulent portraits) — a school **independent of Mughal painting** ⭐.
- **KALAMKARI:** Srikalahasti (freehand pen, temple cloths) vs Machilipatnam-Pedana (block-print, Persian-export chintz) — patronised via Golconda's trade.

### 4.4 Architecture & crafts (dynasty → signature)
Kakatiya **toranas & Ramappa engineering** → Reddi **Kondavidu & Srisailam mandapas** → Vijayanagara **rayagopuram-kalyanamandapa-monolith + Lotus-Mahal composite** → Bahmani **Gulbarga covered mosque, Bidar madrasa, BIDRIWARE** → Qutb Shahi **Charminar, tombs necropolis, Golconda acoustics**. Crafts: Pembarthi brass, Warangal durries, Kollur diamonds, Masulipatnam chintz, Nirmal painted woodwork (Deccani-era craft lineage).

## 5. Socio-cultural conditions — cross-dynasty synthesis (ready paragraphs)

- **Caste in motion:** Kakatiya-era inscriptions show **status by service** (Shudra dynasties proudly so); Reddi-Velama kingship = peasant-warrior castes crowned; Vijayanagara's Balija/merchant-nayaka mobility; yet valangai-idangai divides, devadasi institutionalisation and agrarian hierarchy persisted — *mobility at the top, structure at the base*.
- **Women:** sovereign **Rudramadevi**; authors **Gangadevi, Tirumalamba, Timmakka, Madhuravani, Ramabhadramba**; power-regent **Hayat Bakshi Begum**; but also sati-stones and Nuniz's harem numbers — argue both sides with names.
- **Religious texture:** Golaki-matha Shaivism, Veerashaiva-Aradhya popular wave, Sri Vaishnava Tirumala, Madhva Haridasas, Gajapati-Chaitanya bhakti, Deccan Sufism (Gesudaraz), **shared festivals (Peerla Panduga; Ahmad Shah Wali's urs)** — plural by practice, occasionally violent by politics (temple desecrations in war) — balance is the examiner's demand.
- **Economy of culture:** tanks (dasabandha) & temples financed art; ports (**Motupalli → Masulipatnam**) financed courts; diamonds & chintz tied Andhra to Isfahan, Amsterdam and London by 1650.

## 8. Mains questions (§2)

1. **"The Kakatiyas integrated the Telugu-speaking land for the first time." Substantiate with reference to irrigation, administration and language.** *(skeleton below)*
2. "The age between Warangal's fall (1323) and Golconda's rise (1518) was Telugu literature's second golden age." Discuss.
3. "Krishnadevaraya's court made Telugu an imperial language." Examine, with reference to Amuktamalyada and the Ashtadiggajas.
4. Reconstruct the socio-economic life of Vijayanagara from the accounts of Paes, Nuniz, Razzaq and Barbosa.
5. "Mahmud Gawan's rise and fall is the history of the Bahmani kingdom in miniature." Comment.
6. **"Assess the Qutb Shahi contribution to Telugu language, literature and the fine arts."** *(skeleton below)*
7. Trace the growth of Dakhni-Urdu in the Deccan up to 1687. Why did it precede Delhi Urdu?
8. Trace the evolution of South Indian music treatises from the Sangita Ratnakara to the Chaturdandi Prakashika, noting royal patronage at each step.
9. "Talikota (1565) redistributed, rather than destroyed, South Indian high culture." Evaluate with reference to the Aravidu and Nayaka courts.
10. **"From Perini to Kuchipudi: the state was midwife to Andhra's classical dance."** Elaborate. *(skeleton below)*

### Skeletons

**Q1 (Kakatiya integration):** thesis — first unification of Telangana + coastal Andhra + Rayalaseema fringe under one Telugu crown; evidence — tank chains & dasabandha (agrarian frontier), nayankara & 72 niyogas (service elite), Telugu-majority inscriptions + Ketana's grammar/law (administrative vernacular), Motupalli charter (commerce), trikuta temples & Perini (ideology); close — Kapaya's "Andhra Suratrana" shows the idea of "Andhra" survived the dynasty (→ File 35's "making of Andhra" thesis).

**Q6 (Qutb Shahi & Telugu):** open — a Shia-Persianate dynasty became Telugu's unlikely patron; body — Ibrahim "Malkibharama" (dedications by Gangadhara; Telaganarya's accha-Telugu kavya; Rudrakavi's yakshagana), administration in Telugu up to Madanna-Akkanna, devotional flowering (Kshetrayya at court; Ramadasu's Bhadrachalam and kirtanas), dance (Kuchipudi agraharam 1678), crafts & painting (kalamkari, Golconda school); balance — Persian/Dakhni high culture and Shia liturgy alongside, occasional friction (Madanna's murder); close — the Hyderabadi composite culture as the durable result.

**Q10 (dance):** Perini codified by a Kakatiya general's treatise and carved at Ramappa; temple dance endowed by every dynasty (devadasi inscriptions); Reddi kings writing natya treatises (Vasantarajiyam); Vijayanagara's festival stage (Mahanavami; Lepakshi's dance imagery); Kuchipudi's village charter from Tana Shah; Nayaka Thanjavur's Bhagavata Mela & yakshagana; modern revivals (Nataraja Ramakrishna; Kuchipudi's 20th-c. classical status) — state patronage as the continuous thread.
