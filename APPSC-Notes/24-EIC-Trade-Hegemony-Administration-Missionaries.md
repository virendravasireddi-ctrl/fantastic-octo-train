## 0. Frame

Between **1757 (Plassey)** and **1856 (annexation of Awadh)** a trading corporation became the paramount power of India. The sequence to hold: **trade → plunder of Bengal → Diwani (1765) → ring-fence & subsidiary alliances (Wellesley) → outright annexation (Dalhousie)**, with Parliament tightening its grip on the Company at every step (**Regulating Act 1773 → Pitt's Act 1784 → Charter Acts 1813/1833/1853**). Alongside conquest came a new **administrative state** (civil service, army, police, courts) and — through **missionaries, English education and social legislation** — the first deliberate intervention in Indian society and culture. *(This file answers 2025 Q3a — establishment & early operations of the EIC — and 2025 Q3b — impact of Christian missionaries; the Company's advent itself is File 22.)*

## 1. Trade practices of the Company era ⭐

- **The "Investment":** the EIC shipped **bullion (silver)** to India to buy **cotton textiles, silk, saltpetre, indigo, spices** — India was the world's textile workshop and a **bullion sink** (the mercantilist grievance in Britain).
- **Dastak abuse ⭐:** **Farrukhsiyar's farman (1717)** gave the Company **duty-free trade in Bengal**; its servants illegally extended the **dastak (free pass)** to their **private inland trade** in salt, betel-nut and tobacco — ruining Indian merchants, draining the Nawab's customs, and creating the collision that led to **Plassey (1757)**.
- **After Diwani (1765) the trade turned parasitic:** the Company financed its exports **from Bengal's own land revenue** — the *"Investment out of revenue"* — so India effectively **paid for its own exports**: the start of the **Drain of Wealth** (Dadabhai Naoroji's "un-British rule"; File 25 §3).
- **Monopoly → free trade:** Company servants' **private fortunes ("nabobs")**, the **monopoly** over Indian trade until **1813**, and over **tea & China trade until 1833**; after 1813 **British mill cloth flooded India duty-free** while Indian goods faced high tariffs in Britain — **one-way free trade** and **deindustrialisation** (File 25).

## 2. Rise of the Company to hegemony, 1757–1856 ⭐⭐⭐

### 2.1 Bengal — the springboard
| Event | Year | Substance |
|---|---|---|
| **Battle of PLASSEY** | **23 June 1757** | **Clive** vs **Siraj-ud-daula**; treachery of **Mir Jafar**, Jagat Seths, Omichand — a "transaction, not a battle" ⭐; Company becomes king-maker in Bengal |
| **Mir Qasim & the dastak clash** | 1760–63 | Mir Qasim abolished all inland duties to equalise trade → war |
| **Battle of BUXAR** | **22 Oct 1764** | **Munro** defeated the combined **Mir Qasim + Shuja-ud-daula (Awadh) + Shah Alam II** — militarily far more decisive than Plassey ⭐ |
| **Treaty of Allahabad & DIWANI** | **1765** | Shah Alam II granted the Company the **Diwani (revenue rights) of Bengal-Bihar-Orissa** for ₹26 lakh/yr — legal mask of empire |
| **Dual Government** | 1765–72 | Company took revenue (diwani), Nawab kept administration (nizamat) — **power without responsibility**; contributed to the **Great Bengal Famine (1770**, ~1/3 of Bengal's people) ⭐; ended by **Warren Hastings (1772)** |

### 2.2 The instruments of expansion ⭐⭐ (2020 Q4a)
1. **War & outright conquest** — Mysore (four wars; **Tipu Sultan** fell at **Srirangapatna, 1799**), Marathas (three wars; confederacy dissolved, **Peshwaship abolished 1818**), Sindh (**1843**, "Napier — *Peccavi*"), Sikhs (two wars; **Punjab annexed 1849**).
2. **SUBSIDIARY ALLIANCE — Wellesley (1798–1805)** ⭐⭐⭐: the ally accepted a **permanent British force** in his territory and **paid for it** (or ceded territory in lieu), took a **British Resident**, expelled **all other Europeans**, and surrendered **foreign relations** — sovereignty hollowed out without the cost of administration. *Takers:* **Hyderabad first (1798)** ⭐ (the Nizam — hence Andhra's Circars-Ceded Districts geography, File 36), Mysore 1799, **Awadh 1801**, Peshwa (Bassein, 1802), Bhonsle, Scindia. *Effect:* Indian states demilitarised, financed their own subjection, British power reached everywhere without frontiers moving on the map.
3. **DOCTRINE OF LAPSE — Dalhousie (1848–56)** ⭐⭐: heirless states (adoption unrecognised for sovereignty) "lapsed" to the Company — **Satara (1848), Jaitpur & Sambalpur (1849), Udaipur (1852), JHANSI (1853), NAGPUR (1854)**; pensions/titles extinguished (Nana Saheb's pension, the Carnatic Nawabship, the Tanjore Raj).
4. **Annexation on grounds of "misgovernment" — AWADH, 1856** ⭐: the final straw; taluqdars dispossessed, the sepoys' home province humiliated → prime fuel for **1857** (File 25).

> **Mains skeleton (2020 Q4a — Subsidiary Alliance):** define → terms (4 points) → sequence of takers starting with Hyderabad 1798 → why states accepted (fear of Marathas/mutual wars, guarantee of throne) → consequences (demilitarisation, drain of state finances, Residents as super-ministers, ring of British paramountcy before a shot was fired) → link to Lapse & 1856 Awadh → close: by 1856 the map was red without most rulers ever fighting the Company.

### 2.3 Parliament tightens control over the Company ⭐⭐
| Act | Provisions to remember |
|---|---|
| **Regulating Act 1773** | first parliamentary control; **Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings)** + Council of 4; **Supreme Court, Calcutta (1774)**; Bombay & Madras subordinated |
| **Pitt's India Act 1784** | **dual control — Board of Control** (Crown) over the **Court of Directors** (Company); "possessions of the Crown" language ⭐ |
| **Charter Act 1813** | Indian trade monopoly ended (China/tea kept); **₹1 lakh/yr for education** ⭐; **missionaries allowed into British India** ⭐ (the "pious clause") |
| **Charter Act 1833** | all trade monopoly ended — Company became **purely administrative**; **Governor-General of INDIA (Bentinck first)**; **Law Member (Macaulay)** added; Sec 87 — no discrimination in employment (dead letter) |
| **Charter Act 1853** | **open competition for the Civil Service** ⭐; separate **Legislative Council**; Company kept territories "in trust for the Crown" — renewal without time limit (the last Charter) |

## 3. Changes in administration ⭐⭐

- **Civil Service:** **Cornwallis** — the "father of the Indian Civil Service" — purified it (banned private trade, raised salaries, **Cornwallis Code 1793** separating revenue and justice); **Wellesley's Fort William College (1800)**; **Haileybury (1806)**; **open competition from 1853/55** — but exams in London, in English, entry age lowered later: Indians effectively excluded (**Satyendranath Tagore, first Indian ICS, 1863**) — a standing nationalist grievance ⭐.
- **Army:** the sepoy army (Indians ~5/6 of strength under British officers) — conqueror of India with Indian blood and Indian revenue; caste/religious anxieties in the Bengal Army → 1857.
- **Police:** Cornwallis's **thanadari system** (SP–daroga chain) — the first regular police in the modern sense; village watchmen retained.
- **Judiciary — "Rule of Law":** hierarchy of **Diwani & Faujdari Adalats** (Hastings 1772) → Cornwallis's district courts & **Sadr Adalats**; **equality before law** (with the glaring exception — Europeans triable only by their own courts ⭐); codification later via Macaulay's Law Commission (**IPC drafted 1837, enacted 1860**).
- **Character of the state:** annual **drain**, revenue-maximising land settlements (File 25), commercial exploitation — an administration **efficient, expensive and extractive**, run in English at the top, Persian/vernacular below (Persian dropped as court language, **1837**).

## 4. Changes in the social & cultural spheres ⭐⭐

- **Orientalists vs Anglicists:** early Company patronage of Indian learning — **Calcutta Madrasa (Hastings, 1781)**, **Sanskrit College Banaras (Jonathan Duncan, 1791)**, **Asiatic Society of Bengal (Sir William Jones, 1784)** ⭐ — gave way after **Macaulay's Minute (2 Feb 1835)** to English as the medium and the **"filtration theory"** (educate a class "Indian in blood... English in taste") — details in File 26.
- **Social legislation (state as reformer)** ⭐⭐: **Abolition of SATI — Regulation XVII, 4 Dec 1829 (Bentinck, pressed by Raja Ram Mohan Roy)**; suppression of **thagi** (Sleeman, 1830s) and female infanticide; **Slavery abolished 1843**; **Widow Remarriage Act 1856 (Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar's campaign)** ⭐; **Religious Disabilities Act 1850** (converts keep inheritance — seen by many as pro-missionary); **Lex Loci** anxieties fed 1857.
- **New props of a colonial-modern culture:** printing press & vernacular prose, newspapers (File 26), **railways & telegraph (from 1853, Dalhousie)** ⭐, uniform postage (1854), Western medicine and colleges — modernisation **as by-product of imperial need**, not benevolence (write this balance in Mains).
- The combined effect — legal equality in principle, racial hierarchy in practice; an English-educated middle class that would become **the carrier of nationalism** (Files 26–27).

## 5. Role of Christian Missionaries ⭐⭐ (2025 Q3b)

- **Before 1813** missionaries operated from non-Company enclaves — **Serampore (Danish)**: the **"Serampore Trio" — William Carey, Joshua Marshman, William Ward** ⭐ — first Bengali Bible translations, **Bengali prose & printing**, **Serampore College (1818)**, campaign against sati. In the south, the older **Tranquebar mission (Ziegenbalg, 1706 — first Protestant mission in India)** ⭐ and Catholic missions from the Portuguese era.
- **Charter Act 1813** opened British India to missionaries; **1833** removed remaining restrictions.
- **Contributions (the "credit side"):**
  1. **Education** — pioneer English and girls' schools; **Bethune's & missionary girls' schools**, colleges like **Wilson (Bombay), Madras Christian College, St. Stephen's**; in Andhra, the LMS/Baptist chain from **Visakhapatnam (1805)** to **Clough's Ongole** (File 37).
  2. **Language & print** — grammars, dictionaries, translations; first printing presses and journals in many vernaculars (Carey's Bengali; missionary Telugu print — File 37).
  3. **Social critique & humanitarianism** — against sati, infanticide, caste disabilities; medical missions, famine relief, orphanages.
  4. **Uplift of depressed classes** — mass movements among Dalits (coastal Andhra's Malas & Madigas ⭐, File 37) offering education and dignity.
- **The debit side (write both):** proselytisation was the motive — education an instrument; contempt for Hinduism & Islam in polemics; alignment with the colonial state ("the three Cs"); conversion anxieties (Religious Disabilities Act 1850, army proselytising officers) became a **cause of 1857** ⭐.
- **Net impact:** limited conversions overall, but a **catalytic shock** — the missionary challenge provoked the **socio-religious reform and revivalist movements** (Brahmo, Arya Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Aligarh — File 26) that reshaped modern Indian religion.
