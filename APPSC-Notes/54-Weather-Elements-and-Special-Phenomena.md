## 1. Temperature ⭐

- **Insolation** (incoming solar radiation) is the base; the **heat budget** balances incoming shortwave against outgoing longwave.
- **Controls of temperature (answer frame):** **latitude** (angle of sun), **altitude** (~6.5 °C fall/km — the *normal lapse rate*), **distance from sea** (continentality → extremes), **ocean currents**, **prevailing winds**, **cloud cover** (moderates day/night range), **aspect** (slope facing the sun), vegetation & albedo.
- **Inversion of temperature** — warm air over cold near the surface (calm, clear winter nights, valleys); traps pollution & frost/fog ⭐.
- **India pattern:** **January** — Chennai (~24-25 °C) warmer than Delhi (~14 °C), isotherms bend along the coast; **May–June** hottest in the **NW (Rajasthan 45-48 °C)**; **isotherms run east-west** in winter, distorted by relief & coast. **AP:** interior extremes at **Rentachintala/Palnadu (46-48 °C+)** vs humid coast (File 18) ⭐.

## 2. Humidity ⭐

- **Absolute humidity** (mass of vapour per m³) vs **specific humidity** (per kg of air) vs **relative humidity (RH)** — the % of saturation, the everyday measure; **dew point** = temperature at which air saturates.
- Condensation forms: **dew, frost, fog, mist, clouds**; RH is highest at dawn/coast, lowest in hot dry afternoons/interiors.
- **India/AP:** coastal AP has high RH (muggy summers, Visakhapatnam–Machilipatnam); Rayalaseema is dry — the RH gradient tracks the rainfall map (Files 16, 18).

## 3. Cloudiness ⭐

- Measured in **oktas** (eighths of sky covered); clouds classified by height & form (**Luke Howard's Latin scheme**):
  - **High (cirrus, cirrostratus, cirrocumulus)** — icy, wispy.
  - **Middle (altostratus, altocumulus)**.
  - **Low (stratus, stratocumulus, nimbostratus)** — overcast, steady rain.
  - **Vertical (cumulus → CUMULONIMBUS)** — the **thunderstorm/cyclone cloud**, towering, lightning-hail-bearing ⭐.
- Effect: cloud cover reduces the diurnal temperature range (warmer nights, cooler days); the monsoon's cloud bands are the rain-givers.

## 4. Winds ⭐⭐

### 4.1 The three scales

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Planetary (permanent)** | **Trade winds** (NE, drive tropics), **Westerlies**, Polar easterlies; deflected by the **Coriolis force** (Ferrel's law — right in N. hemisphere) |
| **Periodic (seasonal/daily)** | **Monsoon** (the giant sea-breeze reversal — File 16), **land & sea breeze**, **mountain & valley breeze** |
| **Local** | **Loo** (hot dry NW India summer wind ⭐), **Kal Baisakhi/Norwesters** (Bengal-Assam pre-monsoon squalls), **Mango showers** (pre-monsoon S. India), **Foehn/Chinook, Mistral, Sirocco** (world) |

### 4.2 Jet streams (upper-air, hazard-relevant) ⭐⭐

- **Subtropical Westerly Jet (STWJ)** — steers **western disturbances** into NW India (winter rain, cold waves) and its northward shift signals monsoon onset.
- **Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ)** — forms over the Tibetan high in summer, strengthens the SW monsoon.
- **Somali Jet** — low-level cross-equatorial jet feeding the monsoon.

## 5. Special weather phenomena ⭐⭐ (the high-yield section)

### 5.1 Ocean-atmosphere oscillations

| Phenomenon | What it is | India/AP effect |
|---|---|---|
| **El Niño (ENSO warm phase)** | Abnormal warming of the **central-east equatorial Pacific** | **Weakens the SW monsoon → drought risk** (2009, 2015 links) ⭐⭐ |
| **La Niña (ENSO cool phase)** | Pacific cooling | Generally **good monsoon**; more Bay cyclones |
| **Southern Oscillation (SOI)** | Tahiti–Darwin pressure see-saw | The "SO" in ENSO |
| **Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)** | SST gradient across the Indian Ocean; **+ve IOD aids the monsoon** even during El Niño ⭐ | Modulates monsoon & Bay cyclogenesis |
| **Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO)** | Eastward-moving tropical rain pulse (30–60 days) | Triggers monsoon "active/break" spells & cyclone bursts |

### 5.2 Other special phenomena

- **Western disturbances** — extratropical lows from the Mediterranean via the STWJ → NW-India winter rain/snow (rabi crops) and cold waves ⭐.
- **Thunderstorms, hail & lightning** — convective; **lightning is now India's biggest natural-hazard killer (~2,500+ deaths/yr)** ⭐, AP among the higher-incidence states (Damini app, IMD warnings).
- **Tornadoes** — rare in India (localised, Bengal/east).
- **Fog** (winter, Indo-Gangetic plain — transport hazard), **frost** (rabi damage), **heat waves & cold waves** (IMD criteria; heat waves a rising killer — AP's 2015 toll of 1,700+, File 18) ⭐.
- **Cloudbursts** (>100 mm/hr over a small area — an orographic Himalayan hazard) → developed in File 55.

**Mains hook:** these phenomena convert "climate" into "hazard" — an El Niño year foreshadows Rayalaseema drought (File 56), a warm Bay + La Niña foreshadows an AP cyclone season (File 56); linking the special phenomenon to the hazard is what lifts an environmental-geography answer.
