## 0. Frame

Part-B Q6 territory: the ancient Andhra dynastic chain — **Satavahanas → Ikshvakus → (Brihatphalayanas-Anandagotras-Salankayanas) → early Pallavas → Vishnukundins** — carrying Andhra from the megalithic-Buddhist age to the eve of the Eastern Chalukyas (624 CE). The Satavahana core (rulers, economy, Amaravati) is in **File 00F** — this file gives the **Part-B angles**: social-economic conditions, women's prominence (2023 Q6a), the successor dynasties the syllabus names, and the consolidated **Jainism & Buddhism in Andhra** chain (2025 Q7b).

### 0.1 Origins of the Andhra people & language ⭐⭐ (2025 Q7a — asked verbatim)
- **Earliest mention — AITAREYA BRAHMANA** ⭐⭐ (the cursed sons of **Vishvamitra** became Andhras, Pundras, Sabaras…) — an "outside the Aryan pale" tribe; then Mahabharata (Andhras in the Kaurava army), Ramayana, Ashoka's records (borderers alongside Bhojas-Pitinikas ⭐), **Pliny/Megasthenes — "Andarae": 30 walled towns, 1 lakh infantry, 1,000 elephants** ⭐.
- **Tribe → territory:** "Andhra" first a people, later the land (Andhrapatha/Andhradesa in Ikshvaku-Pallava records); **Trilinga-desa tradition** — the land of the three lingas (**Srisailam, Kaleswaram, Draksharama**) ⭐ → "Telinga/Telangana/Telugu" (one derivation); folk etymology **tene** (honey) → Tenugu; the sober linguistic verdict — **Telugu = a Central Dravidian tongue** that separated from Proto-Dravidian, unconnected in origin with the Sanskritic "Andhra" label.
- **Earliest Telugu traces:** Telugu place-and-personal names inside Prakrit inscriptions (the word **"NAGABU" at Amaravati, c. 1st c. BCE** ⭐ is the show-piece); full Telugu inscription only c. 575 (Kalamalla — File 36). *Skeleton for 2025 Q7a: legends (Aitareya-epics) → foreign notices (Megasthenes) → tribe-to-region shift → Trilinga & tene etymologies → Dravidian linguistics → Nagabu-to-Kalamalla evidence chain.*

## 1. Satavahanas — the Part-B angles ⭐⭐⭐ (dynastic narrative: File 00F)

- **Social conditions (2020 Q6a agrarian + 2023 Q6a women):** four-varna society declared in the **Nasik prasasti** (Gautamiputra "the unique Brahmana — ekabamhana", "destroyer of the pride of Kshatriyas", enforcer against varna-mixing) yet a practical, trade-open society; **METRONYMICS** ⭐⭐ — kings named after mothers (Gautamiputra, Vasishthiputra); **women's prominence (2023 Q6a):** **NAGANIKA** issued the Naneghat inscription & coins jointly ⭐, **GAUTAMI BALASHRI's** Nasik prasasti records her son's achievements, royal women made lavish Buddhist donations, queens performed Vedic sacrifices alongside kings — high visibility without formal sovereignty (write that balance).
- **Agrarian & economic system (2020 Q6a):** crown villages & tax-free **land grants to Brahmanas and Buddhist monks — India's FIRST recorded land grants (Naneghat/Nasik)** ⭐⭐, the seed of feudalism (00F); village headman (*gramika*); tank irrigation; **guilds (srenis)** of weavers, potters, oil-pressers acting as banks (Nasik inscription records deposits at interest ⭐); **Roman trade** — Periplus's ports of the **Maisolos** coast: **Kontakossyla (Ghantasala), Koddura (Kodur), Allosygne**; exports muslin-ivory-pearls, Roman gold influx (hoards); **lead, potin & the SHIP-TYPE COINS of Yajna Sri** ⭐; inland marts linked by the Dakshinapatha.
- **Religion & culture:** Vedic sacrifices (Naneghat lists ashvamedha, rajasuya) + massive Buddhist patronage (Amaravati-Nagarjunakonda donations) — the Satavahana double policy ⭐; Prakrit official language (**Hala's *Gathasaptashati***, Gunadhya's *Brihatkatha* tradition); **AMARAVATI SCHOOL** of sculpture (white marble/palnad limestone, narrative crowded panels, the "lotus-and-wheel" aesthetic) — India's third great school with Gandhara-Mathura (00F §1.5).

## 2. Ikshvakus (c. 225–325 CE) ⭐⭐⭐ — Vijayapuri/Nagarjunakonda

- **Vasishthiputra CHAMTAMULA** — founder; performed **ashvamedha, agnihotra, vajapeya** ⭐ (Vedic revival); **VIRAPURUSHADATTA** — the "Buddhist phase": under him the **royal WOMEN — CHAMTISIRI** (his aunt, "the great donor" of the **Mahachaitya**), Bapisiri, and others — financed Nagarjunakonda's monasteries while the kings stayed Vedic ⭐⭐ (the classic gender-religion pattern: men sacrificed, women donated — reuse in 2023 Q6a); **Ehuvala Chamtamula**; ended by the Pallavas.
- **NAGARJUNAKONDA (Vijayapuri)** ⭐⭐: mahachaitya & 30+ monasteries (Aparamahavinaseliya, Bahusrutiya…), **an amphitheatre, ghat & citadel**; sculptures continuing Amaravati style; pilgrims from **Ceylon (Sihala Vihara), Gandhara, China** — a Buddhist world-university on the Krishna (00G's table); memorial pillars (*chhaya-stambhas* ⭐); association with the **Sriparvata** of **ACHARYA NAGARJUNA** (Madhyamika founder, *Suhrllekha* to a Satavahana king — tradition) ⭐.

## 3. The interlude dynasties ⭐⭐ (the syllabus's named gap-fillers)

| Dynasty | Facts to hold |
|---|---|
| **Brihatphalayanas** (c. 3rd c., Krishna delta; **Kodur** capital) | one known king — **Jayavarman** (Kondamudi Prakrit plates ⭐) |
| **Anandagotras/Anandas** (c. 4th–5th c., Guntur; **Kandarapura**) | Kapotesvara temple tradition at **Chejerla** ⭐ (apsidal — a converted chaitya form); kings Damodaravarman, Attivarman |
| **SALANKAYANAS (c. 300–440 CE, VENGI)** ⭐⭐ | founder **Devavarman (ashvamedha)**; **HASTIVARMAN of Vengi — named among Samudragupta's defeated southern kings (Allahabad prasasti)** ⭐⭐ (the pan-India anchor date, c. 350); family deity **CHITRARATHASVAMI (the Sun) of Vengi** ⭐; bull (Nandi) emblem; transition from Prakrit to **Sanskrit charters**; patronised both Vedic religion and Buddhism |
| **Early PALLAVAS in Andhra** (c. 275–350+, from the Krishna-Guntur base) | **Sivaskandavarman's PRAKRIT charters — Mayidavolu & Hirahadagalli** ⭐ (the earliest Pallava records are Andhran!); held Andhrapatha before shifting to Kanchi (their Tamil-country empire = File 01; art at Kanchi-Mahabalipuram = 2020 Q6b) |

## 4. Vishnukundins (c. 420/440–624 CE) ⭐⭐⭐ — the bridge to the Chalukyas

- Capitals **Indrapalanagara (Nalgonda) → Denduluru/Amarapura (Vengi region)**; family deity **SRIPARVATASVAMI** ⭐ (Srisailam link); matrimonial alliance with the **VAKATAKAS** ⭐ (Madhavavarma m. a Vakataka princess — the Deccan power axis).
- **MADHAVAVARMA II — the greatest** ⭐⭐: claimed **11 ashvamedhas & 1,000 agnishtomas**; expanded to the coast; tradition credits him with the **"bell of justice" at Bezawada** and even the execution of his own son for a chariot accident ("write *tradition holds…*") ⭐; other kings — Vikramendravarma I-II, Govindavarma (Buddhist patron ⭐ — his queen's Indrapalanagara grants), Manchana Bhattaraka.
- **Rock-cut architecture — Andhra's cave moment** ⭐⭐: **UNDAVALLI (four-storeyed cave, reclining Anantasayana Vishnu)**, **Mogalrajapuram, Bhairavakonda** — Vishnukundin-era caves that carried the Deccan cave idiom into Andhra; coins ("lion" types), Sanskrit charters with early Telugu names.
- **End:** **Pulakesin II's conquest of Vengi (c. 616–624)** → brother **Kubja Vishnuvardhana** installed → the **EASTERN CHALUKYA** dynasty (File 36) — the political frame of Telugu culture for four centuries.

## 5. Jainism & Buddhism in Andhra ⭐⭐⭐ (the consolidated chain — 2025 Q7b)

### 5.1 Buddhism (the exam's favourite)
- **Arrival & first shrine:** Mauryan-era entry (tradition takes it to the Buddha's lifetime — Bavari's disciples on the Godavari ⭐); **BHATTIPROLU** — pre-Mauryan/Mauryan **relic casket** with the aberrant **"Bhattiprolu script"** ⭐⭐ (File 36 §4 — seed of the Telugu script story).
- **The site chain (map it):** **AMARAVATI (Dhanyakataka) — the Mahachaitya**, Mauryan founding, Satavahana glory ⭐⭐; **NAGARJUNAKONDA** (Ikshvaku peak, §2); **Jaggayyapeta, Ghantasala, Chandavaram, GUNTUPALLI (rock-cut chaitya — the "Andhra Karle")** ⭐; the **Visakhapatnam cluster — THOTLAKONDA, BAVIKONDA, Pavurallakonda**; **SALIHUNDAM (Srikakulam)**; **SANKARAM/Bojjannakonda (Anakapalli)** — the chain runs down the whole coast.
- **Why the coast? — TRADE (2025 Q7b):** monasteries sat on **ports & the Dakshinapatha**; merchants and guilds were the donors (inscriptions name gahapatis, goldsmiths, caravan leaders ⭐); monks travelled the sea lanes — hence Andhra's export of Buddhism to **Ceylon & Southeast Asia** (Sihala Vihara at Nagarjunakonda; Amaravati-style sculpture found in Sri Lanka & Siam ⭐). *Answer skeleton: ports-routes map → guild-donor inscriptions → monastery-as-caravanserai logic → overseas diffusion evidence.*
- **Schools & thinkers:** **Mahasanghika offshoots — the CHAITYAKAS of Amaravati** ⭐; **ACHARYA NAGARJUNA** (Madhyamika/shunyavada) at Sriparvata; later **Dignaga & Bhavaviveka** linked to Andhra by tradition; Amaravati as the seed of **Kalachakra Vajrayana** tradition (Tibetan memory of Dhanyakataka ⭐).
- **Decline:** post-Ikshvaku patronage shift to Brahmanical dynasties; **Hiuen Tsang (c. 639) found Andhra's monasteries "mostly deserted"** ⭐; absorbed into Shaiva-Vaishnava revival by the 7th–8th c.
- **AP's living showcase:** Amaravati's relic-diplomacy, Buddhavanam (Nagarjunasagar), the 00C §5 chain — quote in any culture-tourism answer.

### 5.2 Jainism in Andhra ⭐
- Entered with trade & the southern migration tradition (Bhadrabahu route via Karnataka); strongest in **Rayalaseema-Telangana borderlands**: **DANAVULAPADU (Kadapa) — the Rashtrakuta-era Jain tirtha** ⭐ (Jina images, nishidhi memorials), **Munulagutta (Karimnagar), KOLANUPAKA (Nalgonda — the Manikyasvami/Rishabha shrine, still living)** ⭐, Vaddamanu (Guntur, early), Penukonda later.
- Peak under **Rashtrakuta influence & the VEMULAVADA CHALUKYAS** (File 36 §3 — Jain court culture); decline with the **Veerashaiva surge (12th c.)** — Palkuriki Somanatha's polemics; temples converted (write "Jainism lost Andhra to Shaivism, not to Buddhism").
