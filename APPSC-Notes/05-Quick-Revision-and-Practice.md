## 3. "Firsts & onlys"

- **First (earliest) Telugu inscription:** Kalamalla (c. 575, Renati Cholas). **First Telugu poet (Adikavi):** Nannaya. **First known Telugu poetess:** Tallapaka Timmakka.
- **First Kannada work extant:** Kavirajamarga. **Kannada Adikavi:** Pampa.
- **Only woman Delhi sultan:** Razia. **First Afghan dynasty:** Lodi.
- **First Islamic tomb in India:** Sultan Ghari. **First true arch:** Balban's tomb. **First true dome:** Alai Darwaza. **First double dome:** Sikandar Lodi's tomb.
- **First sultan to tax Brahmins (jizya):** Firoz Shah Tughlaq. **First standing cash-paid army:** Alauddin.
- **First Sufi premakhyan in Hindavi/Awadhi:** Chandayan (Mulla Daud, 1379).
- **Only woman Alvar:** Andal. **Largest monolithic temple:** Kailasa, Ellora. **UNESCO trio of Chola temples:** Thanjavur–Gangaikondacholapuram–Darasuram.

## 7. Mains practice questions (10/15-mark pattern)

1. **"Evaluate the contribution of the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi to the growth of Telugu language and literature."** *(skeleton below)*
2. "The Pallava period marks the transition of South Indian architecture from rock-cut to structural forms." Elaborate with examples.
3. **"Chola village self-government was democracy with a difference." Examine with reference to the Uttaramerur inscriptions.** *(skeleton below)*
4. Compare the contributions of the Badami and Kalyani Chalukyas to Deccan temple architecture and literature.
5. "The Rashtrakutas were a political and cultural bridge between north and south India." Discuss.
6. Analyse the objectives, mechanisms and results of Alauddin Khalji's market and agrarian reforms.
7. "Muhammad bin Tughlaq failed not for want of ideas but of means." Comment, noting the unintended consequences of his reign for the Deccan and Andhra.
8. Trace the evolution of Indo-Islamic architecture under the Delhi Sultanate.
9. "The advent of Islam transformed Indian society more through interaction than through conquest." Discuss with reference to economy, language and culture.
10. **"Bhakti and Sufi movements were parallel streams that often met." Compare their teachings and assess their combined influence on Indian society, with special reference to Andhra.** *(skeleton below)*

### Answer skeletons

**Q1 (Eastern Chalukyas & Telugu):**
- *Opening:* Vengi's dynasty (624–1075) turned Telugu from speech into script and from script into poetry.
- *Body:* (i) inscriptional Telugu rising through their records — pre-history in Kalamalla (Renati Chola, c. 575); (ii) verse in stone — Pandaranga's Addanki record, Yuddhamalla's Bezawada verses; (iii) the leap — Nannaya at Rajaraja Narendra's court: champu Mahabharatamu, Adikavi, Vaganusasanudu, Narayana Bhatta's collaboration; (iv) court context — Chola alliance & rivalry with Kalyani (where Kannada was flowering) made language patronage statecraft; (v) durability — Kavitraya completed over three later polities; script matured toward modern Telugu.
- *Close:* every successor state (Kakatiya → Reddi → Vijayanagara) inherited a ready literary language — Vengi's most permanent conquest.

**Q3 (Uttaramerur):**
- *Opening:* Two inscriptions of Parantaka I (919, 921 CE) codify the most sophisticated village constitution of medieval world history.
- *Body:* structure (30 kudumbus, kudavolai lot), franchise-with-filters (age, ¼-veli land, Vedic learning; audit-based disqualification, 3-year cooling), functional committees (eri/totta/pon/samvatsara variyams), accountability (annual rotation, account submission).
- *"Difference":* property-learning franchise not universal suffrage; operated within (and because of) imperial oversight; brahmadeya bias (sabha, not ur, is described).
- *Close:* bounded but real constitutionalism — the state trusted audited local bodies with tanks, temples and taxes; cite Rajaraja's survey as the imperial frame above it.

**Q10 (Bhakti–Sufi with Andhra focus):**
- *Opening:* Two mysticisms, one social outcome — a vernacular, musical, egalitarian devotional culture.
- *Compare:* guru/pir, kirtan/sama, temple-matha/khanqah-dargah, saguna-nirguna/wujudi monism; both anti-formalist, both vernacular.
- *Meetings:* Kabir-Nanak's idiom; Farid in Adi Granth; premakhyans; shared shrines; Nath-Sufi dialogues.
- *Andhra:* Palkuriki's dwipada Veerashaivism; Annamayya's "Brahmam Okate" (sankirtana equality at Tirumala); Potana's patronage-free Bhagavatamu; Deccan Sufis — Gesudaraz at Gulbarga, Dakhni's birth; living syncretism (Rottela Panduga, Nellore).
- *Assess:* real gains (vernacular literatures, music, women's voice, communal bridges) vs limits (agrarian caste order intact; movements institutionalising into new orthodoxies).
- *Close:* the "emotional integration" of medieval India — the cultural capital modern secularism still draws on.
