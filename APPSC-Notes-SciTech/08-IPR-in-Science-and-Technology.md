## 0. Why IPR belongs in an S&T paper
Innovation needs an incentive (the **monopoly reward** of a patent) *and* society needs **access** (affordable medicines, open knowledge). IPR is where **science policy meets law, trade and ethics** — and India, as both a **generics/"pharmacy of the world"** and a rising **patent filer**, sits exactly on that fault line. Learn IPR as the **incentive-vs-access balance**.

---

## 1. The types of IPR ⭐⭐ (match right ↔ law ↔ term ↔ office)

| IPR | Protects | Governing law | Term | Registry / office |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Patent** | New, non-obvious, useful **invention** | **Patents Act 1970** (amended 1999/2002/**2005**) | **20 yrs** from filing | Patent Office (HQ **Kolkata**) under **CGPDTM** |
| **Copyright** | Literary/artistic/musical/software works | **Copyright Act 1957** (amended 2012) | Life + **60 yrs** | Copyright Office, **DPIIT** |
| **Trademark** | Brand — marks, logos | **Trade Marks Act 1999** | **10 yrs**, renewable | Trade Marks Registry (Mumbai) |
| **Industrial Design** | Aesthetic shape/pattern | **Designs Act 2000** | 10 (+5) yrs | under CGPDTM |
| **Geographical Indication (GI)** | Origin-linked goods | **GI of Goods Act 1999** | 10 yrs, renewable | **GI Registry, Chennai** ⭐ |
| **Plant varieties** | New/farmers' varieties | **PPV&FR Act 2001** | 15–18 yrs | **PPV&FR Authority** (Agri Ministry) |
| **Semiconductor layout** | Chip layout-designs | **SICLD Act 2000** | 10 yrs | MeitY |
| **Trade secrets** | Confidential know-how | (common law/contract — **no dedicated statute**) ⭐ | indefinite | — |
| **Traditional knowledge** | Community/heritage knowledge | *sui generis* + **Biological Diversity Act 2002** | — | NBA (Chennai) |

- **Administration:** most industrial IP sits under **CGPDTM → DPIIT → Ministry of Commerce & Industry** (patents, designs, trademarks, GI); copyright also DPIIT; plant varieties under Agriculture; biodiversity under Environment.

## 2. India & the global regime — TRIPS ⭐⭐ (the pivot)
- **TRIPS (1995, WTO)** — minimum global IP standards; India as a WTO member had to make its law TRIPS-compliant.
- India used the **transition period to 2005**, then the **Patents (Amendment) Act 2005** introduced **product patents** in pharma/food/chemicals (earlier India recognised only **process patents** — the 1970 Act's masterstroke that built the generics industry) ⭐⭐.
- India uses **TRIPS flexibilities** (Doha Declaration 2001, "TRIPS and Public Health"): **compulsory licensing, parallel imports, pre-grant opposition, strict patentability (§3(d))**.
- **Not signed:** many **TRIPS-plus** demands (data exclusivity, patent-term extension) resisted in FTA talks — a recurring India-vs-West friction (EU/EFTA/US FTAs).

## 3. The landmark provisions & cases ⭐⭐⭐ (guaranteed material)

| Provision / case | What it did |
|---|---|
| **Section 3(d), Patents Act** ⭐ | Bars patents on **new forms of known substances** without enhanced efficacy — India's **anti-"evergreening"** wall |
| **Novartis v. Union of India (SC, 2013)** ⭐⭐ | SC **denied** a patent on cancer drug **Glivec (imatinib)** under §3(d) — global landmark protecting generics/access |
| **Natco v. Bayer (2012)** ⭐⭐ | India's **first compulsory licence** (Sec. 84) — cancer drug **Nexavar (sorafenib)**; price cut ~97%; grounds = affordability/public need |
| **Section 3(b), 3(j)** | Excludes inventions against morality; **plants/animals & essentially biological processes** not patentable (seeds→PPV&FR instead) |
| **Pre-grant & post-grant opposition** | Lets third parties/patients challenge weak patents — a public-health safeguard |
| **PepsiCo v. Gujarat farmers (2019)** | PPV&FR Authority **revoked** PepsiCo's potato variety registration — asserted **farmers' rights** to save/sow seed ⭐ |

## 4. National IPR Policy 2016 & the current framework ⭐⭐
- **National IPR Policy 2016** — slogan **"Creative India; Innovative India"**; **DPIIT** the nodal department; **7 objectives** (IP **awareness, generation, legal & legislative framework, administration & management, commercialisation, enforcement & adjudication, human-capital development**).
- **CIPAM (Cell for IPR Promotion & Management)** — implementation & awareness arm (IP awareness in schools/MSMEs; expedited exam for startups).
- **Patents (Amendment) Rules 2024** ⭐ (notified 15 Mar 2024) — the current-affairs update:
  - **Request for Examination window cut 48 → 31 months** (faster grants);
  - **Form 27 (working statement)** simplified — once every **3 years**, no sales-value disclosure;
  - **Certificate of Inventorship (Form 8A)**;
  - reduced fees & grace-period ease for **educational institutions/startups**.
- **Results (2024-25):** **patent filings crossed 1 lakh for the first time** (≈20% YoY jump); total IPR filings ≈**7.5 lakh** (+19.75%); **resident filings now the majority**; India rising in the **Global Innovation Index (38th, 2025)** and **6th in patent filings by origin (WIPO)** ⭐.

## 5. The ISSUES — the syllabus keyword ⭐⭐⭐ (six live debates)

| Issue | The tension | India's stance/instrument |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Access to medicines vs monopoly** | Patents raise prices; India supplies the world's cheap generics | §3(d), compulsory licensing, pre-grant opposition, **COVID TRIPS-waiver proposal (India + South Africa, WTO 2020-22)** ⭐ |
| **B. Evergreening & TRIPS-plus** | Minor tweaks extend monopolies; FTAs push data exclusivity | §3(d) firewall; resisting TRIPS-plus in EU/EFTA FTAs |
| **C. Biopiracy & traditional knowledge** | Foreign patents on **turmeric, neem, basmati** | **TKDL (2001)** revoked them; **Biodiversity Act 2002/NBA**; see [File 07 §3.3](07-Indian-Scientists-and-Achievements.md) |
| **D. Farmers' vs breeders' rights** | GM/hybrid seed IP vs farmers' seed-saving | **PPV&FR Act 2001** balances both; PepsiCo case |
| **E. Patent backlog & weak commercialisation** | Examiner shortage; **university research rarely licensed** (no "Bayh-Dole"; the **PUPFIP Bill was shelved**) | 2024 Rules speed exams; ANRF/RDI push translation |
| **F. New-tech frontiers** | **AI-generated inventions/authorship** (DABUS debate), software/CRI patentability, gene/CRISPR patents, data & trade-secret protection | CRI guidelines; DPDP Act 2023 (data); evolving jurisprudence |

- **The balance line for Mains:** *"India's IPR philosophy is neither anti-patent nor pro-monopoly — it is pro-access: reward genuine innovation (product patents since 2005) while guarding the public interest (§3(d), compulsory licensing, TKDL)."*

## 6. GI tags — the friendliest IPR for a state ⭐ (AP is GI-rich)
- GI protects **origin-linked, community-owned** products — no single owner, so it directly rewards **artisans and farmers**.
- **Andhra Pradesh GIs to remember (name 5-6):** **Kondapalli Bommalu (toys), Machilipatnam & Srikalahasti Kalamkari, Etikoppaka lacquer toys, Bobbili Veena, Budithi brassware, Dharmavaram & Venkatagiri & Mangalagiri handloom sarees, Uppada Jamdani, Araku Valley coffee, Banaganapalle mango, Guntur Sannam chilli, Tirupati Laddu, Bandar Laddu, Udayagiri wooden cutlery** ⭐.
- Use in Mains: GI = **IPR that protects heritage + boosts rural incomes + fights cheap imitation** — the developmental face of IPR.

## 7. Mains skeletons
**Q. "India's patent regime balances innovation incentives with public health." Examine.**
Process→product patents (2005) as the incentive turn → §3(d), Novartis 2013, Natco CL 2012 as the access guardrails → TRIPS flexibilities + COVID waiver → close: the "pro-access" balance, with backlog/commercialisation as unfinished agenda.

**Q. "Discuss the issues related to IPR in the field of science and technology in India."**
Use §5's six debates (A–F) as the spine; one instrument + one case each; balance with the 2016 Policy + 2024 Rules reforms; AP GI tie-in.

**Q. "Traditional knowledge needs sui generis protection." Comment.**
TKDL/biopiracy (File 07) → Biodiversity Act/NBA/ABS → GI tags (AP examples) → gap: documentation ≠ commercialisation.
