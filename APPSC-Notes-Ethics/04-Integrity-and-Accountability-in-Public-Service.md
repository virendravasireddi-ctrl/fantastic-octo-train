## 1. Ethics in public service

### 1.1 What makes *public-service* ethics special

Public servants (a) exercise **delegated sovereign power** over citizens who cannot opt out, (b) spend **other people's money**, (c) act as **trustees**, not owners, of authority, and (d) work under a constitutional oath. Hence stricter-than-market standards: the fiduciary logic — *public office is a public trust*.

### 1.2 The foundational values of civil service (syllabus list, with content)

| Value | Meaning in practice |
|---|---|
| **Integrity** | Wholeness of character — §2 below |
| **Impartiality & non-partisanship** | Same treatment to all citizens; no political colour in advice or implementation; survives changes of government |
| **Objectivity** | Decisions on **evidence and merit**, recorded reasons, not impression or pressure |
| **Dedication to public service** | Service-first orientation; the "**civil servant as karma-yogi**" frame |
| **Empathy, tolerance & compassion for weaker sections** | Gandhi's Talisman operationalised — last-mile orientation, patience with the unlettered citizen |
| **Courage of conviction** | Frank, fearless advice; refusing illegal orders in writing |
| **Neutrality + anonymity** | Minister answers publicly; official advises confidentially and executes loyally within law |

### 1.3 Nolan Committee — Seven Principles of Public Life (UK, 1994–95) ⭐⭐

**S-I-O-A-O-H-L:** **Selflessness · Integrity · Objectivity · Accountability · Openness · Honesty · Leadership.** (Committee on Standards in Public Life, chaired by Lord Nolan — the international gold-standard list; quote it in *every* probity answer.)

## 2. Integrity

### 2.1 Meaning

From Latin ***integer*** = whole. Integrity = **wholeness/consistency of character** — alignment of values, words and deeds, *even when unobserved*: "doing the right thing when no one is watching." It is **incorruptibility**: the person cannot be bought, bullied or flattered off their principles.

### 2.2 Integrity vs honesty ⭐ (classic 2/10-marker)

- **Honesty** = truthfulness in statements and dealings — a *component*.
- **Integrity** = the whole architecture — honesty *plus* consistency of principle, courage, and refusal of the wrong even when legal ("an honest officer tells no lies; an officer of integrity also refuses the lawful-but-wrong shortcut").
- A person can be bluntly honest yet lack integrity (selective courage); integrity implies honesty, not vice versa.

### 2.3 Kinds of integrity

- **Moral integrity** — fidelity to ethical principles across contexts.
- **Intellectual integrity** — honesty in reasoning: acknowledging inconvenient evidence, no plagiarism, advising without spin (the value behind "objectivity").
- **Professional/financial integrity** — zero tolerance of bribes, kickbacks, misuse of assets; strict conflict-of-interest hygiene.
- **Institutional integrity** — systems designed so the honest choice is the easy choice (e-procurement, faceless assessment, audit trails). *2nd ARC line: shift from trying to produce saints to designing systems that make corruption "a high-risk, low-reward activity."*

### 2.4 Threats and cultivation

Threats: discretion + monopoly − accountability (**Klitgaard's formula: C = M + D − A**), political interference, weak witness/whistle-blower protection, social tolerance of "speed money."
Cultivation: value-based recruitment and training (**Mission Karmayogi, 2020**), ethical leadership ("tone at the top"), transparent procedures, prompt punishment, and personal disciplines (the Gita's *nishkama karma*; Gandhi's daily self-audit).

## 3. Accountability

### 3.1 Meaning and anatomy

**Accountability = the obligation of power-holders to (a) *answer* — explain and justify their actions against set standards, and (b) *face enforcement* — consequences for failure.** Two elements: **answerability + enforceability**. It is *external, ex-post and institutional*, and it presupposes transparency (you cannot question what you cannot see).

### 3.2 Accountability vs responsibility ⭐ (and the Friedrich–Finer debate)

| | **Responsibility** | **Accountability** |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Internal** — felt obligation, conscience, professional standards | **External** — imposed obligation to answer to another |
| Direction | Taken *by* the agent | Demanded *of* the agent |
| Timing | Before/during action | Typically after action |
| Sanction | Guilt, self-correction | Censure, penalty, removal |
| Classic champion | **Carl Friedrich** (1940): rely on the administrator's inner professional-moral sense | **Herman Finer** (1941): "the political and administrative history of all ages" teaches that only **external control** restrains power |

Exam synthesis: mature systems need **both** — Finer's external checks set the floor, Friedrich's internalised responsibility supplies everything checks can't reach (2nd ARC's "ethical competence").

### 3.3 Types of accountability

- **Vertical** — to the people: elections, media, civil society, social audit.
- **Horizontal** — state institutions checking each other: legislature, judiciary, CAG, CVC, CIC, Lokpal.
- By content: **political** (minister to legislature), **legal** (courts, judicial review), **administrative/managerial** (hierarchy, performance appraisal, conduct rules), **professional** (peer standards), **social/moral** (public opinion, conscience).

### 3.4 Accountability mechanisms in India — the master table ⭐⭐

| Channel | Instruments | Key facts to drop |
|---|---|---|
| **Legislative** | Questions, motions, no-confidence; **Parliamentary committees** (PAC, Estimates, DRSCs); budgetary control | Collective & individual ministerial responsibility (**Art. 75(3)**, 164) |
| **Audit** | **CAG (Art. 148–151)** — legality + propriety + performance audit | "Guardian of the public purse"; reports → PAC |
| **Judicial** | Judicial review, writs (Art. 32/226), PILs; criminal law | **Prevention of Corruption Act 1988** (amended **2018**: bribe-giving an offence; **§17A** prior approval for investigating officials) |
| **Executive/internal** | **CCS (Conduct) Rules 1964; AIS (Conduct) Rules 1968**; vigilance machinery; **Art. 311** safeguards (and their misuse as shield); performance appraisal, **e-office audit trails** | 2nd ARC asked to ease Art. 311's rigidity for the corrupt while protecting the honest |
| **Statutory anti-corruption** | **CVC** (1964 by resolution on **Santhanam Committee** advice → statutory **2003**); **CBI** (DSPE Act 1946); **Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act 2013** (first Lokpal appointed **2019**); **Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014** (assent 2014, yet to be fully operationalised — a standard critique point) | Santhanam Committee (1962–64) → CVC ⭐ |
| **Transparency/citizen-centric** | **RTI Act 2005** ("master key to good governance" — 2nd ARC 1st Report); **Citizens' Charters** (India since **1997**; origin UK 1991, John Major); **Sevottam** model (DARPG); **social audit** (statutory under **MGNREGA §17**); e-governance/DBT; grievance portals (CPGRAMS) | Undivided **AP pioneered institutionalised social audit of MGNREGA (2006)** ⭐ |
| **Electoral/political** | Elections, ADR disclosures, anti-defection (10th Schedule) | T. N. Seshan as enforcement exemplar |

### 3.5 Andhra Pradesh tie-ins ⭐

- **Spandana / Public Grievance Redressal System** — institutionalised weekly grievance hearing down to mandal level.
- **Village & Ward Secretariats + Volunteers (2019)** — doorstep delivery with named, local accountability for ~every 50–100 households.
- **Real Time Governance Society (RTGS, 2018)** — dashboard monitoring of schemes; technology as an accountability instrument.
- **Social-audit legacy:** undivided AP's Rural Development social audit unit (from 2006) became the national model later mandated under MGNREGA — the go-to example for **vertical/social accountability**.
- **Meeseva** (2011, undivided AP) — early one-stop electronic citizen services; reduces discretion at the counter.

## 4. Codes, the 2nd ARC, and why ethics regimes fail

### 4.1 Code of Conduct vs Code of Ethics ⭐

| | **Code of Conduct** | **Code of Ethics** |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Specific **do's and don'ts** (gifts, speculation, political activity) | Broad **values and principles** (integrity, impartiality) |
| Nature | Rule-based, enforceable, negative | Aspirational, guiding, positive |
| India status | Exists — CCS 1964 / AIS 1968 Conduct Rules | **No statutory code of ethics yet**; draft Public Service Bill/2nd ARC proposals pending — standard critique line |

### 4.2 2nd ARC, 4th Report — *Ethics in Governance* (2007): points worth citing

- Partial **state funding of elections**; tightening anti-defection (decision to Speaker → President/Governor on EC advice); dealing with **office-of-profit** distortions.
- **Code of ethics for ministers and legislators**; values to be statutorily embedded for civil servants.
- **Rashtriya Lokayukta**; Lokayuktas in states; CVC strengthening; protection to **whistle-blowers**.
- Make corruption **"high-risk, low-reward"**: speedier trials, special courts, confiscation of illegally acquired assets (Benami/asset-forfeiture logic).
- **Ethical framework = values + institutions + processes**; simplification of transactions (single windows, e-services) to cut rent-seeking opportunities.

### 4.3 Why codes and mechanisms underperform (evaluation paragraph)

Overlapping agencies with thin coordination; political control over investigators ("caged parrot"); §17A-type prior-approval filters; chronic vacancies (information commissions, Lokpal benches); whistle-blower law dormant; conduct rules policing form (gifts) more than substance (crony policy capture); and a compliance culture that mistakes *paper* accountability for performance. Remedy line: **combine Finer and Friedrich** — sharper external institutions *plus* Mission-Karmayogi-style value internalisation, and technology that makes integrity the default (DBT, e-procurement, open dashboards).
