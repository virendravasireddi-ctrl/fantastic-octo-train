# 03 · Ethics in Private and Public Relationships

## 0. Why this topic matters

This unit supplies the single most common GS-IV dilemma frame: **the collision between private loyalty and public duty** (a relative seeks a favour; a friend's firm bids for your tender). Mains expects: (a) the norms proper to each sphere, (b) a sharp comparison, (c) the **one-morality vs two-moralities debate** with thinkers, and (d) a principled way to resolve collisions — Gandhi's Talisman, recusal, conflict-of-interest rules.

---

## 1. The two spheres

**Private relationships** = bonds we choose or are born into — family, marriage, friendship, neighbourhood, community. **Public relationships** = role-based bonds with strangers mediated by institutions — official↔citizen, legislator↔constituent, doctor↔patient, employer↔employee, citizen↔citizen in the public square.

### 1.1 Ethics in private relationships

- **Governing norms:** love, loyalty, fidelity, care, gratitude, honesty, respect for autonomy, filial and parental duty, keeping confidences.
- **Nature:** **partial by design** — you *may* (indeed should) prefer your child over a stranger; obligations are diffuse, open-ended, enforced by emotion and social sanction, not law.
- **Ethical failures here:** betrayal, deceit, domestic violence, elder neglect, dowry, gender injustice inside the home — private sphere is *not* an ethics-free zone; the Constitution reaches into it (Art. 15, 17, 21; DV Act 2005; Hindu Succession (Amendment) 2005).
- **Care ethics** (Gilligan, Noddings) is the natural theory of this sphere: morality as responsiveness within relationships.

### 1.2 Ethics in public relationships

- **Governing norms:** **impartiality, objectivity, equality before rules, justice, accountability, transparency, probity, tolerance** — the "foundational values" of public service are simply the ethics of public relationships codified.
- **Nature:** **impersonal and universal by design** — the citizen at your counter has the same claim whether kin or stranger; obligations are defined by role, office and law; enforced by external accountability (courts, CAG, media, RTI).
- **Weber's insight:** bureaucracy is legitimate precisely because it is **impersonal** — *sine ira et studio*, "without anger or fondness."

### 1.3 Comparison table ⭐ (standard 10-marker)

| Dimension | Private relationships | Public relationships |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Affection, kinship, choice | Role, office, law, citizenship |
| Guiding norm | **Partiality** — special obligations to *particular* people | **Impartiality** — equal treatment of all |
| Sanction | Conscience, emotion, social opinion | Law, conduct rules, courts, electorate |
| Accountability | Informal, to the persons involved | Formal, to institutions and the public |
| Discretion | Wide, personal | Bounded by rules and reasons (recorded) |
| Transparency | Privacy is the value | Openness is the value (RTI) |
| Failure mode | Betrayal, neglect | Corruption, nepotism, abuse of office |
| Theory fit | Care ethics, virtue | Deontology, justice, utilitarianism |

## 2. One morality or two? — the debate ⭐

**"Two moralities" view (role morality):**
- Public roles license (even require) conduct forbidden privately — the "**dirty hands**" problem (Michael **Walzer**, 1973): a leader may deceive an enemy, order force, keep secrets. **Thomas Nagel** ("Ruthlessness in Public Life," in Hampshire ed., *Public and Private Morality*, 1978): public morality is more **consequence-and-impartiality weighted**; private morality more agent-and-relationship weighted. **Machiavelli** is the extreme: the prince must learn "how not to be good."
- Conversely, virtues mandatory in private (favouring your own) become **vices in public** (nepotism).

**"One morality" view (integrity as indivisibility):**
- **Gandhi:** no watertight compartments — "one man cannot do right in one department of life whilst attempting to do wrong in another; life is **one indivisible whole**." Character leaks: the privately corrupt will be publicly corruptible.
- Public trust arguments: citizens reasonably read private conduct (tax fraud, cruelty) as evidence of public unfitness.

**Balanced exam position:** the **values are continuous, the rules of application differ**. Honesty, non-cruelty and promise-keeping bind everywhere (one morality at the level of values); but the *distribution rule* flips — partiality is the virtue of the private sphere and the vice of the public one (two moralities at the level of roles). A civil servant needs both: warmth at home, impartial rectitude at office, and the integrity that keeps the two consistent.

## 3. When the spheres collide — the classic dilemmas

| Collision | Example | Ethical handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Nepotism/favouritism** | Relative asks for a job, transfer, contract | Refuse to use office; point to due process |
| **Conflict of interest** ⭐ | Friend's/spouse's firm in your tender; you own shares in a regulated entity | **Disclose → recuse → divest** — the three-step rule; CCS/AIS Conduct Rules require disclosure |
| **Gifts and hospitality** | "Harmless" festival gift from a vendor | Conduct Rules cap gifts; when in doubt, decline/report |
| **Confidentiality vs family** | Sharing insider policy info at home | Official secrets/market-sensitive info stays in office |
| **Private life, public office** | Legislator's private tax fraud; official's domestic violence | Not "private" — bears on fitness for public trust |
| **Whistle-blowing on one's own** | Colleague-friend is corrupt | Loyalty to institution and public > personal loyalty; use protected channels |
| **Duty vs family time** | Disaster duty during a family emergency | Situational balancing; institutions should build humane rosters, not martyrdom |

**Resolution toolkit for case studies:** (1) legality first; (2) **Gandhi's Talisman** — weakest affected person; (3) **publicity test** (Kant/Rawls — could I defend this on the front page?); (4) reversibility test (roles swapped?); (5) disclose–recuse–document; (6) seek institutional remedy (report up, vigilance) before heroics.

- Constitutional echo: **Art. 14** (equality) is the legal form of public impartiality; oath of office ("without fear or favour, affection or ill-will") is its ritual form.
- Historical hooks: the Gita's Arjuna is precisely a **private-loyalty vs public-duty** dilemma (kin vs righteous war); Rama abandoning private happiness for public opinion is debated as excess of public over private duty — usable as an "evaluate" example.
