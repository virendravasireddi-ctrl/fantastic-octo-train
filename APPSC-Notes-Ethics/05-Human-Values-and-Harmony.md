## 0. Why this topic matters

This unit fuses two syllabus streams: the GS-IV "human values" item (role of family, society and educational institutions; lessons from lives of great leaders, reformers and administrators) and the **Universal Human Values (UHV) "harmony" framework** ("understanding the harmony in existence… relationships in society and in nature") drawn from A. Nagraj's *Madhyasth Darshan / Jeevan Vidya*, now taught nationwide through the AICTE UHV course (Gaur–Sangal–Bagaria textbook). Answers score when you (a) define and classify values with Rokeach, (b) reproduce the **four levels of harmony**, (c) handle **gender equability** with reformers + law + schemes, and (d) keep a ready **person → value → lesson** table.

## 1. What human values are

- **Definition:** values are **enduring beliefs about what is worthwhile** — standards that guide choice and judgement across situations ("compass of conduct"). They sit deeper than attitudes (File 06): one value (compassion) generates many attitudes.
- **Milton Rokeach** (*The Nature of Human Values*, 1973) ⭐ — the exam-standard classification:

| **Terminal values** (ends — desirable end-states) | **Instrumental values** (means — desirable modes of conduct) |
|---|---|
| A world at peace, equality, freedom, family security, self-respect, social recognition, salvation, wisdom, true friendship, a comfortable life… (**18**) | Honesty, responsibility, courage, forgiveness, helpfulness, self-control, ambition, broad-mindedness, obedience, politeness… (**18**) |

- Other classifications to name-drop: **intrinsic vs extrinsic** (valued for itself vs for what it brings); **personal–social–moral–spiritual–aesthetic**; **constitutional/democratic values** (justice, liberty, equality, fraternity, dignity, secularism, scientific temper — Preamble + Art. 51A); **Schwartz's** circular model of ten basic values (achievement ↔ benevolence etc.) for a theory flourish.
- **Universal human values** — recur across all civilisations: **truth (satya), love/compassion (prema/karuna), non-violence (ahimsa), peace (shanti), righteousness (dharma)**. They are universal because they flow from shared human nature, not culture — the premise of the UHV framework below.
- Values vs skills (UHV language): education must deliver **value (what to do) + skill (how to do)** — skills without values are dangerous (a skilled hacker), values without skills are ineffective.

## 2. Understanding the harmony in existence — the UHV four-level scheme ⭐⭐

The framework proceeds from self outward; each level's harmony is a precondition of the next.

### 2.1 Harmony in the human being (self ↔ body)

- The human being = **co-existence of the sentient "I" (jeevan/self)** and the **material body**.
- Their needs differ: the **self needs happiness (*sukh*)** — continuous, qualitative, met by right understanding and right feeling; the **body needs physical facilities (*suvidha*)** — limited, quantitative, met by physico-chemical things.
- Master-diagnosis of modern discontent: we try to buy *sukh* with *suvidha* — piling up facilities cannot fill a deficit of understanding and relationship (usable line in essays on consumerism).
- Harmony = the self understanding, and the body serving as instrument (*svasthya* — self-health: "being anchored in the self").

### 2.2 Harmony in the family — values in human relationships

- Family is the **first laboratory of relationship**; relationship is **between one self and another self** (not body–body), and it *already exists* — it needs to be **recognised and fulfilled**, not created.
- The **nine universal values (feelings) in relationship** ⭐ (UHV standard list):

| # | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trust (*vishwas*)** | The **foundation value** — assurance that the other intends my happiness |
| 2 | **Respect (*samman*)** | **Right evaluation** of the other (over- or under-evaluation both disrespect) |
| 3 | **Affection (*sneha*)** | Acceptance of the other as one's relative |
| 4 | **Care (*mamata*)** | Nurturing the other's **body** |
| 5 | **Guidance (*vatsalya*)** | Nurturing the other's **self** (right understanding) |
| 6 | **Reverence (*shraddha*)** | Acceptance of excellence in the other |
| 7 | **Glory (*gaurava*)** | Feeling for those who have achieved excellence |
| 8 | **Gratitude (*kritagyata*)** | Feeling for those who have aided one's excellence |
| 9 | **Love (*prema*)** | The **complete value** — affection extended to all beings (from family to *vasudhaiva kutumbakam*) |

- Exam-ready distinction: **respect = right evaluation**. Differentiating people by body (age, sex, race), physical facilities (wealth) or beliefs (sect) is *disrespect* — the UHV root-diagnosis of discrimination, directly usable for the **gender** section (§5).

### 2.3 Harmony in society

- A harmonious society is a chain: person → family → village → nation → **world family** (*akhand samaj, sarvabhaum vyavastha*).
- **Human goals in society (four, in order):** **right understanding (*samadhan*) → prosperity (*samriddhi*) → fearlessness/trust (*abhay*) → co-existence (*sah-astitva*)** ⭐. Contrast with the current inversion: accumulation without understanding → deprivation amid plenty → fear → exploitation of nature.
- **Five dimensions of human order** (the UHV "systems" list): **education-*sanskar*** (right living), **health–self-regulation**, **justice–preservation**, **production–work**, **exchange–storage** — a ready framework for "what should a good society organise?" answers.

### 2.4 Harmony in nature and existence

- **Nature = four interconnected orders:** **material (*padartha*)** → **plant/bio (*pranic*)** → **animal (*jiva*)** → **human (*gyana*/knowledge order)**. The first three are in **mutual fulfilment** (soil feeds plant, plant feeds animal, all enrich soil); only humans currently fail to fulfil their role — hence the ecological crisis. Human destiny is to complete the circle (*abhyudaya* — all-round wellbeing).
- **Existence as co-existence:** units (material and sentient) **submerged in all-pervading space**; harmony is not to be manufactured but *understood* — "existence is coexistence" is the framework's final line and a strong essay-closer.

## 3. Human relationships in society and in nature — the applied reading

- **In society:** interdependence is the fact (division of labour, care chains); values (trust → justice) are what convert interdependence from exploitation into cooperation. **Justice in UHV terms = recognition, fulfilment and evaluation of relationship, leading to mutual happiness** — justice begins in the family and scales to courts only when it fails there.
- **In nature — three ethical stances:**
  - **Anthropocentrism** — nature as resource for humans (instrumental value only); underlies the extractive economy.
  - **Biocentrism** — all *life* has moral standing (Schweitzer's "reverence for life"; Taylor).
  - **Ecocentrism/deep ecology** — ecosystems as wholes matter: **Aldo Leopold's land ethic** (*A Sand County Almanac*, 1949 — "a thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity, stability and beauty of the biotic community"); **Arne Naess** (deep vs shallow ecology, 1973).
- **Indian tradition is natively ecocentric:** *vasudhaiva kutumbakam*; the *pancha-mahabhuta* cosmology; sacred groves; **Bishnoi sacrifice (Khejarli, 1730, Amrita Devi)** and **Chipko (1973, Sunderlal Bahuguna, Gaura Devi)** as value-movements ⭐; Gandhi: "Earth provides enough for every man's **need** but not for every man's **greed**."
- **Constitutional/legal form:** Art. **48A** (DPSP) + **51A(g)** (fundamental duty); Art. 21 jurisprudence (M.C. Mehta line of cases); **sustainable development** (Brundtland 1987 — meeting present needs "without compromising the ability of future generations") and **intergenerational equity** as the policy translation of harmony with nature.

## 4. Gender equability in relationships ⭐

- **Concept:** *equality* = identical rights and treatment; **equity/equability = fairness** — differential support so outcomes are genuinely equal (maternity protection, reservation). The syllabus word "equability" signals **fair, balanced relationships** in family, workplace and public life — not mere formal sameness.
- **UHV diagnosis:** gender discrimination = **disrespect** — evaluating a self on the basis of the *body* (sex). Since relationship is between self and self, gender hierarchy is an evaluation error institutionalised as patriarchy.
- **Where inequity lives:** family (son preference, unpaid care work, decision-making), economy (wage gaps, low female LFPR), public sphere (under-representation), violence (dowry, DV, workplace harassment).
- **Constitutional armature:** Art. **14, 15(1), 15(3)** (special provision for women), **16**, **39(a),(d)** (equal pay), **42** (maternity relief), **51A(e)** — duty to **renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women** ⭐; 73rd/74th Amendments (⅓ panchayat/municipal reservation; many states, including AP, raised it to 50%); **106th Amendment — Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023** (⅓ in Lok Sabha and Assemblies, implementation tied to census/delimitation).
- **Statutes:** Dowry Prohibition 1961; Equal Remuneration 1976 (now Code on Wages 2019); PWDV Act 2005; Hindu Succession (Amendment) 2005 — daughters as coparceners; **POSH Act 2013** (from *Vishaka* guidelines, 1997); Maternity Benefit (Amendment) 2017; PCPNDT 1994.
- **Reformer lineage (fits §6's "lessons" too):** Raja Ram Mohan Roy (abolition of sati, 1829) → **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** (Widow Remarriage Act, **1856**) → **Jyotirao & Savitribai Phule** (first girls' school, Pune, **1848**) → **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** ⭐ (first widow remarriage in the Telugu country, **Rajahmundry, 1881**; *Vivekavardhini*; "Gadya Tikkana") → **Gurajada Apparao's *Kanyasulkam* (1892)** ⭐ — the great Telugu play against bride-price and child marriage — the two indispensable **AP tie-ins**.
- **Schemes/practice:** Beti Bachao Beti Padhao; Sukanya Samriddhi; SHG movement — undivided **AP's DWCRA → SERP/Velugu (2000)** made women's self-help groups the state's development backbone ⭐; Mission Shakti; gender budgeting (since 2005-06).
- **Mains synthesis line:** *equability is achieved not when women are treated like men, but when relationships — familial, economic, political — are re-founded on right evaluation of persons as persons.*

## 5. Role of family, society and educational institutions in imparting values

### 5.1 Family — the first school

- **Primary socialisation**: values transmitted by **imitation and identification** before they are ever taught (Bandura's observational learning); the child learns honesty from what parents *do*, not say.
- Mechanisms: modelling, storytelling (Panchatantra/epics as value pedagogy), discipline style (induction vs punishment — induction builds internalised values), gender-role formation (equitable families produce equitable citizens), grandparents as culture-carriers.
- Failure modes: hypocrisy gap ("do as I say"), materialist signalling, son preference — values *caught*, not taught, can be bad values too.

### 5.2 Society — the ambient curriculum

- Peer groups (adolescence's strongest determinant), religion and festivals, neighbourhood norms, **media and social media** (parasocial role models, virality of outrage), cinema, civil society and NGOs, the law as moral teacher (File 01 §3), and **role models in public life** — a society gets the conduct it celebrates.
- Community institutions that *practise* values teach them best: blood-donation drives, langar/seva traditions, disaster volunteerism.

### 5.3 Educational institutions — the formal channel

- **Committee/policy spine ⭐ (prelims seam):**
  - **Radhakrishnan Commission (University Education, 1948–49)** — spiritual and moral values central to education.
  - **Sri Prakasa Committee (1959)** — on **religious and moral instruction**.
  - **Kothari Commission (1964–66)** — "the destiny of India is being shaped in her classrooms"; education for national values and productivity.
  - **NPE 1986** — value education to combat "erosion of essential values."
  - **NEP 2020** — explicit **constitutional values**, ethics courses, service learning; **AICTE's Universal Human Values course** (the source of §2) institutionalises harmony-education in technical colleges.
- Mechanisms: curriculum (civics, literature), the **hidden curriculum** (how the school itself behaves — fairness of teachers, dignity of the peon), teacher as exemplar, pedagogy (debate, service projects, NSS/NCC/Scouts), assessment integrity (a school that tolerates cheating teaches cheating).
- Higher stakes for civil services: **LBSNAA foundation values, Mission Karmayogi (2020)** — value-and-competency training as lifelong, not one-time.

## 6. Lessons from lives and teachings — leaders, reformers, administrators ⭐

Keep one line per person; deploy one or two per answer, never a parade.

### 6.1 Great leaders

| Person | Core value(s) | Lesson / hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Mahatma Gandhi** | Truth, ahimsa, means–end purity, trusteeship | The Talisman; "My life is my message" |
| **Sardar Patel** | Unity, decisiveness, institutional integrity | Integration of 560+ states; "patron saint" of the civil services |
| **Jawaharlal Nehru** | Democratic temper, scientific temper, institution-building | Universities/IITs/PSUs as "temples of modern India" |
| **B. R. Ambedkar** | Justice, dignity, constitutional morality | "Educate, agitate, organise"; annihilation of caste as ethical project |
| **Abraham Lincoln** | Honesty, humility, magnanimity | "With malice toward none, with charity for all"; the (popularly attributed) letter to his son's teacher |
| **Nelson Mandela** | Forgiveness, reconciliation | Truth & Reconciliation over revenge after 27 years' prison |
| **Martin Luther King Jr.** | Non-violent resistance, hope | "Injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere" |
| **A. P. J. Abdul Kalam** | Simplicity, integrity, meritocracy | Paid for relatives' stay at Rashtrapati Bhavan; "dreams are not what you see in sleep…" |
| **Swami Vivekananda** | Fearlessness, service | *Daridra-narayana* — service of the poor as worship |
| **Mother Teresa** | Compassion in action | "Not all of us can do great things, but we can do small things with great love" |

### 6.2 Social reformers

| Person | Value | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** | Reason applied to tradition | Sati abolition 1829 — reform *through* engagement with scripture and state |
| **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** | Compassion + scholarship | Widow Remarriage Act 1856; charity kept anonymous |
| **Jyotirao & Savitribai Phule** | Education as liberation | First girls' school 1848; Satyashodhak Samaj |
| **Sree Narayana Guru** | Equality, dignity | "One caste, one religion, one God for man" |
| **Periyar E. V. Ramasamy** | Self-respect, rationalism | Self-Respect Movement |
| **Kandukuri Veeresalingam** ⭐ | Courage against orthodoxy | Widow remarriage in coastal Andhra (1881) at personal ruin — reform as lived value |
| **Gurajada Apparao** ⭐ | Art as reform | *Kanyasulkam* (1892); "desamante matti kaadoi, desamante manushuloi" — "a nation is not its soil, a nation is its people" |

### 6.3 Administrators (the GS-IV differentiator)

| Person | Value | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **M. Visvesvaraya** | Professional integrity, punctuality | The famously told two-candles practice — separating official from personal expense |
| **T. N. Seshan** | Fearless enforcement | Model Code of Conduct made real; institutions are as strong as their occupants |
| **E. Sreedharan** | Punctuality, probity, delegation | Delhi Metro's "integrity pact" culture; accountability accepted personally after accidents |
| **S. R. Sankaran** ⭐ | Austerity, compassion for the last person | AP-cadre "people's IAS officer" — bonded-labour abolition, SC/ST sub-plan; never owned property, unmarried, lifelong service |
| **Armstrong Pame** | Initiative, community mobilisation | The 100-km crowd-funded "People's Road" in Manipur |
| **Verghese Kurien** | Institution-building, farmer-centricity | Amul/White Revolution — professional management held accountable to producers |

**How to use in Mains:** name → one verifiable act → the value it instantiates → one line linking to the question's demand (e.g., "Sankaran's refusal of security escorts while negotiating with extremists shows moral courage as an administrative resource, not a private virtue").
