## 0. Why this topic matters

EI is the syllabus's most *applied* psychology item: examiners ask for **concept + model** (Salovey–Mayer vs Goleman — keep both), then pivot to **"utility and application in administration and governance"** — where marks come from concrete administrative scenes (grievance counters, communal flashpoints, disaster response) rather than theory. Keep the Gita's *sthitaprajna* and one AP crisis example (Hudhud, 2014) as your differentiators.

---

## 1. The concept and its lineage

- Pre-history: **E. L. Thorndike's "social intelligence" (1920)** — ability to understand and manage people; **Howard Gardner's multiple intelligences (*Frames of Mind*, 1983)** — **intrapersonal** and **interpersonal** intelligences are EI's direct ancestors.
- **The term and first model:** **Peter Salovey & John Mayer (1990)** ⭐ — EI is "the ability to **monitor one's own and others' feelings and emotions, to discriminate among them, and to use this information to guide one's thinking and actions**." (The exam definition — quote it.)
- **Popularisation:** **Daniel Goleman, *Emotional Intelligence: Why It Can Matter More Than IQ* (1995)** ⭐; extended to work life in *Working with Emotional Intelligence* (1998) and leadership in *Primal Leadership* (2002).
- **"EQ" (emotional quotient):** the measurable-score framing, credited to **Reuven Bar-On** (EQ-i inventory).
- Core insight: emotions are not the enemy of reason but **data for it** — the well-governed feeling, not the absent feeling, is the ideal (Aristotle's line: anyone can become angry, but to be angry "with the right person, to the right degree, at the right time, for the right purpose, and in the right way — that is not easy" — Goleman's own epigraph).

## 2. The three model families ⭐

| Model | Author | EI is… | Components / measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ability model** | **Mayer–Salovey–Caruso** | A genuine *intelligence* — measurable like IQ | **Four branches:** (1) **perceiving** emotions → (2) **using** emotions to facilitate thought → (3) **understanding** emotions → (4) **managing** emotions; MSCEIT test |
| **Mixed model** | **Goleman** | Abilities **+ traits + competencies** predicting workplace success | Five components (below); ESCI competence inventory |
| **Trait model** | **K. V. Petrides** | Self-perceived emotional dispositions (personality-like) | TEIQue self-report |

### 2.1 Goleman's five components ⭐⭐ (the answer skeleton for every EI question)

| Component | Meaning | Administrative face |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Self-awareness** | Knowing one's emotions, strengths, limits in real time | Recognising one's biases/irritation before deciding a file or a hearing |
| **2. Self-regulation** | Controlling impulses; integrity under provocation | Not signing in anger; composure under political pressure or abuse at a dharna |
| **3. Motivation** | Drive from meaning, not just reward; optimism after setbacks | Service-orientation in hard postings; persistence in long reforms |
| **4. Empathy** | Reading and weighing others' emotions | Sensing the unstated fear of an illiterate applicant; weaker-section orientation |
| **5. Social skills** | Managing relationships, networks, conflict, communication | Negotiation with agitating groups; team-building across departments |

(1–3 = **personal competence** — managing self; 4–5 = **social competence** — managing relationships.)

- **EI vs IQ:** IQ gets you recruited (threshold competence); EI determines effectiveness thereafter — leadership derailment tracks emotional failures (arrogance, insensitivity), not cognitive ones. Treat Goleman's stronger quantitative claims cautiously (§6), but the threshold–differentiator framing is safe.
- **Neuroscience hook:** **Antonio Damasio (*Descartes' Error*, 1994) — the somatic marker hypothesis** ⭐: patients with damaged emotion circuitry (ventromedial prefrontal cortex) retain IQ yet **cannot decide** — proof that emotion is a component of rational choice, not its opposite. Also Goleman's "**amygdala hijack**" — the neural story behind losing one's temper.

## 3. Utility and application in administration ⭐⭐ (the scoring section)

### 3.1 Managing the self

- **Stress and burnout control** in overload postings; composure prevents the "amygdala hijack" decisions (a lathi-charge ordered in anger).
- **Integrity support:** self-regulation is the psychological muscle behind resisting temptation, flattery and intimidation — EI operationalises the "foundational values" (File 04).
- **Sound judgement:** self-aware officers recognise mood and bias as inputs (angry-file syndrome) and defer decisions; Damasio shows emotion-informed judgement is *better* judgement.

### 3.2 Leading teams and organisations

- Motivation of subordinates without carrots-and-sticks alone: recognition, meaning, fairness; **morale is an emotional variable**.
- **Conflict management** within and between departments; negotiation with unions and contractors.
- Building **psychologically safe** offices where bad news travels up — the anti-groupthink, anti-cover-up culture; ethical leadership = emotional labour done honestly ("tone at the top," File 04).

### 3.3 The citizen interface

- **Empathy at the counter:** the citizen often arrives anxious, unlettered, wronged; EI turns "disposal of grievance" into *redressal* — the difference Spandana-type systems (AP) try to institutionalise.
- **Weaker sections:** compassion for SC/ST, women, aged, disabled applicants is the syllabus value "empathy, tolerance and compassion" — EI is its skill-form.
- **Communal and law-and-order flashpoints:** reading crowd emotion, de-escalating rumour panics, choosing dialogue before force — district officers' accounts consistently credit emotional reading of situations over legal instruments.
- **Disaster management:** calm authority under chaos; AP example ⭐ — the handling of **Cyclone Hudhud (Visakhapatnam, 2014)** and the mass-evacuation tradition of the AP/Odisha coast (from the 1977 Diviseema lesson to near-zero-casualty evacuations): communicating urgency **without panic** is applied EI.

### 3.4 Governance-level applications

- **Training and capacity building:** behavioural competencies in **Mission Karmayogi (2020)**; LBSNAA/ATI modules on self-awareness and citizen-centricity; police soft-skills training.
- **Recruitment and appraisal debates:** the UPSC/state interview ("personality test") as a crude EI probe; proposals to weight empathy/integrity in selection; 360° appraisal capturing how officers *treat* people, not just outputs.
- **Policy design:** emotionally intelligent governance reads public sentiment (fear during pandemics, dignity in sanitation) and designs communication accordingly — the persuasion/nudge toolkit of File 06 is EI applied at population scale.
- **Emotional labour of the frontline:** ASHA workers, police constables, disaster responders absorb citizens' distress daily; humane rosters, counselling and recognition are *institutional* EI.

### 3.5 The caution — EI's dark side

EI is a **capability, not a virtue**: manipulators, demagogues and con-men are often emotionally brilliant. EI without integrity = sophisticated manipulation (File 06's manipulation–persuasion line). Hence pair EI with values: **EI supplies the *how*, ethics the *what for*.**

## 4. Cultivating EI — and the Indian anchors

- **Practices:** mindfulness/meditation (attention to one's own states), reflective journaling, active listening drills, seeking candid feedback (360°), empathy exposure (field immersion — night halts in villages, the Bharat Darshan logic of training), after-action reviews of one's own flashpoints.
- **Indian anchors ⭐:** the Gita's ***sthitaprajna*** (2.54–72) — the person of steady wisdom, unshaken by fortune, senses under command — is a complete self-regulation ideal; ***samatva*** (equanimity) as "yoga is skill in action"; **Buddhist mindfulness (*sati*)** and *karuna* (trained compassion); Gandhi's fasts as public self-regulation. These give an EI answer civilisational depth in two lines.

## 5. Criticisms (for the "critically examine" tail)

Conceptual stretch (is it an "intelligence" or repackaged personality traits?); **measurement problems** (self-report models are fakeable; MSCEIT scoring debates); **predictive-validity inflation** in popular claims (Locke's and others' critiques of Goleman's "80%" rhetoric); cultural variation in emotional display rules; risk of hiring discrimination via fuzzy EI scores. Balanced close: imperfectly measured but administratively indispensable — the remedy is triangulated assessment and pairing EI with ethical standards, not abandoning it.
