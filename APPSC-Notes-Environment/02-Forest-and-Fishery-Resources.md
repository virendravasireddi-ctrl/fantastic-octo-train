## PART A — FOREST RESOURCES

## 1. Why forests matter — the value stack

- **Ecological (indirect) value:** carbon sink (India's Net-Zero-2070 plan needs a **2.5–3.0 billion-tonne CO₂-equivalent additional sink by 2030** ⭐), watershed & soil protection, biodiversity habitat, rainfall/microclimate regulation, nutrient cycling.
- **Economic (direct) value:** timber, fuelwood, fodder, bamboo, **NTFPs** (non-timber forest products — tendu leaf, honey, gum, lac, medicinal plants), paper pulp.
- **Social/cultural value:** livelihoods of ~**275 million forest-dependent people** in India; sacred groves; tribal culture.
- **Concept — Forests as "life-support":** the classic distinction is **direct use value vs. indirect (ecosystem-service) value vs. option value vs. existence value.**

## 2. India's forest cover — the ISFR data ⭐⭐

**India State of Forest Report (ISFR) 2023** (18th edition, released Dec 2024; by **Forest Survey of India, Dehradun**, biennial since 1987):

| Metric | Figure (ISFR 2023) |
|---|---|
| **Forest cover** | **7,15,343 km² = 21.76%** of geographical area ⭐ |
| **Tree cover** | 1,12,014 km² = 3.41% |
| **Forest + Tree cover** | **8,27,357 km² = 25.17%** ⭐ |
| **Mangrove cover** | ~4,992 km² |
| **National Forest Policy 1988 target** | **33% forest/tree cover (67% in hills)** ⭐ — still unmet |

- **Definitions (exam-critical):** FSI maps **"forest cover" = all land ≥1 ha with tree canopy density ≥10%**, *regardless of ownership or legal status* (so plantations, orchards count). Split by density: **Very Dense Forest (≥70%), Moderately Dense (40–70%), Open Forest (10–40%)**. Distinguish from **"Recorded Forest Area" (RFA)** = legally notified **Reserved / Protected / Unclassed** forests.
- **Largest forest area (state):** **Madhya Pradesh** ⭐; largest **% of area:** north-eastern & island states (Mizoram, Lakshadweep, A&N). North-east shows worrying decline (shifting cultivation, development).
- **Champion & Seth classification** (the standard forest-type scheme): Tropical (wet evergreen, moist/dry deciduous, thorn), Montane (temperate, alpine), Littoral & swamp (mangroves). **Tropical moist & dry deciduous = the dominant Indian type.**

### 2.1 Andhra Pradesh forests ⭐

- **AP forest cover ≈ 18.2% of state area (ISFR 2023)** — *below* the national average; ISFR 2023 recorded a **loss of ~139 km²** in AP over 2021, though **mangrove cover rose ~13 km²**.
- **Forest types:** Southern tropical **dry deciduous & thorn** (Rayalaseema, interior), **moist deciduous** (Eastern Ghats — teak, terminalia), **mangroves at Coringa (Godavari estuary) & Krishna estuary** ⭐, **Red Sanders (Pterocarpus santalinus) — endemic to Seshachalam–Nallamala, a CITES-listed, smuggling-prone species** ⭐⭐ (AP-signature fact).
- **Key areas:** **Nallamala** (Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam Tiger Reserve — Project Tiger), **Seshachalam Biosphere Reserve (Tirumala)**, **Papikonda NP, Coringa Wildlife Sanctuary (mangroves), Kolleru** (lake — Ramsar), **Eastern Ghats agency forests** (bauxite-tribal tension — File 03).

## 3. Deforestation — causes, effects, remedies

**Causes (mnemonic — "SLIMED F"):** **S**hifting cultivation (jhum/podu), **L**ogging/timber, **I**nfrastructure & mining (forest-land diversion), **M**onoculture plantation & agriculture expansion, **E**ncroachment & fuelwood/fodder over-collection, **D**ams & submergence, **F**orest fires (ISFR: large area fire-prone; AP among affected).

**Effects:** biodiversity loss, soil erosion & siltation of reservoirs, altered hydrology (floods + droughts), carbon release (climate feedback), desertification, loss of tribal livelihood, **human–wildlife conflict**.

**Remedies:** afforestation/reforestation, **Forest (Conservation) Act 1980** (diversion needs Central nod + **compensatory afforestation**), **CAMPA** funds (Compensatory Afforestation Fund — Act 2016), **Joint Forest Management**, agroforestry, alternative fuels (LPG–**Ujjwala**), eco-restoration.

## 4. Afforestation vs. Reforestation vs. Social Forestry

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Afforestation** | Planting forest on land that was **not forest** before (new forest) |
| **Reforestation** | Replanting on land that was **recently forested** |
| **Compensatory afforestation** | Mandatory replanting to offset forest land diverted for a project (funded via **CAMPA**) |

### 4.1 Social Forestry ⭐ (asked directly in the syllabus)

- **Definition:** raising trees on **community & private/waste land outside conventional forests** to meet fuelwood, fodder, small-timber and soil-protection needs of *local people* — **taking the pressure off natural forests**. Term popularised by the **National Commission on Agriculture (NCA), 1976** ⭐.
- **Four components (learn these):**
  1. **Farm forestry** — trees on private farmland (bunds, fields) by farmers.
  2. **Community forestry** — on community/village common land for shared benefit.
  3. **Extension / strip forestry** — along roads, canals, railways, wastelands.
  4. **Agroforestry** — trees + crops (and/or livestock) on the same land (e.g., *taungya*, alley cropping).
- **National Agroforestry Policy 2014** — India was the **first country in the world to adopt a dedicated agroforestry policy** ⭐.
- **Critique:** early social forestry drifted into **commercial monoculture (eucalyptus)** on good farmland — displaced food crops, lowered water tables — the cautionary tale to cite.

### 4.2 Joint Forest Management (JFM) ⭐

- **JFM (national resolution 1990)** — the state Forest Department and a local **Van Suraksha Samiti / Vana Samrakshana Samithi (VSS)** *co-manage* degraded forest and **share the produce** ⭐. **Andhra Pradesh was a JFM pioneer** — its **Vana Samrakshana Samithis (VSS)** are a standard success exhibit. Contrast with the rights-based **Forest Rights Act 2006** (transfers *rights*, not just management).

### 4.3 Programmes & targets to quote

- **Green India Mission (2014)** — one of the 8 missions under NAPCC; increase forest/tree cover & improve quality.
- **Nagar Van Yojana** (urban forests), **CAMPA (2016)**, **"trees outside forests"** push, **National Mission for a Green India** carbon-sink goal (2.5–3.0 bn t CO₂e by 2030).
- **AP:** VSS-based JFM; social-forestry & avenue plantation drives; mangrove restoration at **Coringa**.

## PART B — FISHERY RESOURCES

## 5. Fisheries — the resource & its value

- **Two systems:** **capture fisheries** (wild — marine + inland/riverine) and **culture fisheries = aquaculture** (farmed — freshwater, brackish-water, mariculture). **Aquaculture is the fast-growing engine.**
- **India's standing ⭐⭐:** **2nd-largest fish producer in the world** (after China; ~8% of global output), **2nd in aquaculture**, and the **world's largest shrimp exporter**. Total fish production **≈197.75 lakh tonnes (FY 2024-25)**, roughly **75% inland/aquaculture, 25% marine** ⭐ (the inland share has overtaken marine — a key trend).
- **Economic weight:** fish is a top **agri-export** (marine-products export led by **frozen shrimp**); livelihoods for ~28 million; a cheap animal-protein source (food & nutrition security).

## 6. The Blue Revolution & policy stack

| Milestone | Note |
|---|---|
| **Blue Revolution** | The programme to boost fisheries/aquaculture (echoing the Green Revolution) — restructured as **"Neel Kranti Mission"** |
| **PMMSY — Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (2020)** ⭐ | Flagship: **₹20,050 crore**, targets, cluster model; the current headline scheme |
| **FIDF** | Fisheries & Aquaculture Infrastructure Development Fund |
| **Kisan Credit Card** | Extended to fishers/aqua-farmers |
| **Institutions** | **CMFRI (Kochi — marine), CIFRI (Barrackpore — inland), CIBA (brackish-water), MPEDA (exports), NFDB (Hyderabad!)** ⭐ |
| **Sagar Parikrama / e-Gopala** | Outreach & digital support |

- **NFDB — National Fisheries Development Board is headquartered in Hyderabad** ⭐ (handy AP/Telangana regional link).
- **Governance:** a separate **Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying (2019)** and a **Department of Fisheries** signal the sector's new priority; India's **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) ≈ 2.02 million km²** is the marine resource base.

## 7. Andhra Pradesh — India's aquaculture powerhouse ⭐⭐ (AP-core)

- **AP is the LARGEST fish-producing state in India (2024)** and contributes the dominant share of the country's **inland/aquaculture** output ⭐⭐.
- **AP alone accounts for ~70% of India's shrimp exports** ⭐⭐ — the **"aqua hub"** of India; **Vannamei (L. vannamei) shrimp** and freshwater fish (rohu, catla) farming across **West Godavari, Krishna, East Godavari, Nellore (Kolleru belt)**.
- **Kolleru Lake** (largest freshwater lake in the region, **Ramsar site**) — the classic **aquaculture-vs-wetland conflict**: illegal fish tanks encroached the lake → *Operation Kolleru* demolitions → ecological-restoration case study ⭐.
- **AP fisheries policy** targets huge export value; **long coastline (~974 km traditional / 1,053 km revised 2024) + Godavari–Krishna deltas** = the natural endowment; fishing harbours (Visakhapatnam, Nizampatnam, Machilipatnam).

## 8. Threats to fishery resources — and sustainable management

- **Over-fishing & the "tragedy of the commons"** at sea (File 01), **by-catch**, **bottom trawling** damage, **juvenile fishing**.
- **Habitat loss:** mangrove destruction (nurseries), wetland conversion (**Kolleru**), dam-blocked fish migration, coastal pollution & eutrophication.
- **Aquaculture's own footprint:** mangrove clearance for ponds, **salinisation of groundwater/soil**, antibiotic & effluent load, disease outbreaks (white-spot).
- **Climate:** warming/acidifying oceans, shifting stocks, coral bleaching, cyclones.
- **Sustainable tools:** **closed seasons / fishing bans (monsoon ban on east & west coasts)**, mesh-size & Minimum Legal Size rules, **Marine Protected Areas**, **Maximum Sustainable Yield (MSY)** management, cage culture, **Integrated Multi-Trophic Aquaculture**, seaweed & mariculture, effluent-treatment norms, **CRZ regulation** of coastal ponds.
