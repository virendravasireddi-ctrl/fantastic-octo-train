## 0. The framing question of the whole paper

Every topic here is one variation on a single tension: **the economy grows by drawing down nature's stock, but the same nature is the *source and sink* of the economy.** "Development vs. environment" is not a slogan — it is the design problem that **sustainable development** (File 06), **EIA** (File 07) and **conservation policy** (this file) all try to solve. Learn this framing and you can answer almost any Mains question in the unit from first principles.

## 1. Development vs. Nature / Environment — the debate

**The two classical positions:**

| View | Core claim | Associated ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **Anthropocentric / "growth-first"** | Nature is a resource for human use; environmental limits can be pushed back by technology and substitution. | "Grow now, clean up later"; Environmental **Kuznets Curve** (pollution rises then falls as income grows) ⭐; Simon–Ehrlich wager |
| **Ecocentric / "limits-first"** | Nature has intrinsic value and hard limits; growth beyond carrying capacity is self-defeating. | **Gaia hypothesis (Lovelock)**; Deep Ecology (Arne Naess); *Limits to Growth* (Club of Rome, **1972**) ⭐ |

**The Indian synthesis (exam-preferred answer):** neither pure growth nor pure preservation but **sustainable development** — Gandhi's *"Earth provides enough for every man's need but not for every man's greed"* ⭐ is the standard opening quote; the **Chipko movement (1973, Uttarakhand — Sunderlal Bahuguna, Chandi Prasad Bhatt)** ⭐ and **Appiko (1983, Karnataka)** are the grassroots exhibits; **Silent Spring (Rachel Carson, 1962)** ⭐ is the book that launched the global environmental movement.

**Why the tension is unavoidable for a developing country:** India must lift millions out of poverty (needs energy, steel, cement, roads) yet is among the most **climate-vulnerable** nations — so it argues for **"development space"** and **climate justice** (File 06) internationally while greening domestically.

## 2. Depletion of natural resources — the problem

- **Depletion** = extraction faster than natural (or human) replacement. For **non-renewables** (metals, minerals, fossil fuels) *any* use is depletion; for **renewables** (forests, fish, groundwater) depletion happens when the **harvest rate exceeds the regeneration rate**.
- **"Tragedy of the Commons" (Garrett Hardin, 1968)** ⭐ — the master concept: individually rational over-use of an unowned/shared resource (a pasture, a fishery, the atmosphere) ruins it for all. The policy antidotes: **regulation, privatisation/property rights, or community governance (Elinor Ostrom's Nobel work on the commons)** ⭐.
- **Reserves vs. Resources** (mineral vocabulary): a **resource** is the total concentration; a **reserve** is the part economically & technically extractable *now*. Prices, technology and policy convert resources into reserves — which is why "we are running out" is more complex than it sounds.
- **Peak theory:** "peak oil"/"peak minerals" — the point of maximum production after which output declines; empirically pushed back repeatedly by new finds (shale, deep-water) and substitution.

### 2.1 Metals & minerals — the depletion + conservation policy angle ⭐

- India is mineral-rich but **import-dependent for "critical minerals"** (lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earths) that the energy transition needs.
- **Conservation of minerals — the toolkit:**
  1. **Efficient mining & beneficiation** (reduce ore losses, use low-grade ore).
  2. **Recycling & "urban mining"** (scrap steel, e-waste metal recovery) — a tonne recycled = a tonne not mined.
  3. **Substitution** (aluminium/composites for copper; fibre-optic for copper wire).
  4. **Regulation & auctions** — **MMDR Act 1957 → 2015 amendment (compulsory auctions, District Mineral Foundation, NMET) → 2023 amendment (critical minerals, private exploration)** (full policy arc in File 03).
  5. **Strategic stockpiling & overseas assets** — **KABIL (2019)** acquiring lithium/cobalt abroad; **National Critical Mineral Mission (Jan 2025)**.
  6. **Circular economy** — design for reuse; **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**.
- **The conservation principle:** minerals are a **one-time endowment** — a state that mines out its ore has converted a permanent asset into a temporary income unless it **reinvests the proceeds** (the DMF logic: mining revenue back to affected districts).

## 3. Types of natural resources

**Master classification (learn this tree):**

```
Natural Resources
├── By origin:      Biotic (forests, fisheries, wildlife) | Abiotic (minerals, water, land, air)
├── By development:  Actual (surveyed & usable now) | Potential (known but not yet usable)
├── By distribution: Ubiquitous (air, sunlight) | Localised (coal, oil, metals)
└── By renewability:
      ├── RENEWABLE (flow) — regenerate within a human timescale
      └── NON-RENEWABLE (stock) — form over geological time; effectively fixed
```

### 3.1 Renewable vs. Non-renewable — the core distinction ⭐⭐

| | **Renewable (flow) resources** | **Non-renewable (stock) resources** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Replenished by natural processes within a human timescale | Formed over geological ages; fixed stock |
| **Examples** | Solar, wind, hydro, tidal, geothermal, biomass; forests, fisheries, soil fertility, freshwater | Coal, petroleum, natural gas; metallic & non-metallic minerals; nuclear fuels (uranium) |
| **Key caveat** | Renewable **only if harvested ≤ regeneration rate** — over-fished stocks, felled forests and mined groundwater behave like non-renewables ⭐ (the "flow vs. fund" trap) | Some are **recyclable** (metals) and some are not (fossil fuels — burnt once) |
| **Conservation logic** | Manage the **rate of use** (sustainable yield) | **Stretch, substitute, recycle** and reinvest proceeds |

- **TRAP / nuance ⭐:** "renewable" ≠ "inexhaustible." **Groundwater, soil and fisheries are renewable but exhaustible** — the exam loves this distinction. Conversely, solar/wind are renewable *and* practically inexhaustible.
- **Recyclable vs. non-recyclable non-renewables:** metals (iron, aluminium, copper, gold) can be recovered indefinitely; **fossil fuels are non-recyclable** — the strongest argument for the energy transition.

## 4. Conservation — principles & the Indian institutional frame

**Three R-ladders you can quote:**
- **Resource use:** *Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover → (Refuse/Rethink)*.
- **Conservation strategies:** *In-situ* (protect in place — national parks, sanctuaries) vs *ex-situ* (zoos, gene banks, seed banks).
- **Approaches:** *Preservation* (leave untouched) vs *Conservation* (wise, sustainable use) vs *Restoration* (repair degraded land).

**Landmark Indian conservation law (one-line each — details in later files):**

| Law / body | Year | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Wildlife (Protection) Act** | 1972 | Species & protected-area protection |
| **Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act** | 1974 | Set up CPCB/SPCBs |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act** — now **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam** (renamed by **2023 amendment**) | 1980 | Restricts diversion of forest land |
| **Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act** | 1981 | Air-quality regulation |
| **Environment (Protection) Act — the "umbrella" Act** | **1986** ⭐ (post-Bhopal 1984) | Enables EIA, CRZ, hazardous-waste rules |
| **Biological Diversity Act** | 2002 | CBD compliance; access-benefit sharing |
| **Scheduled Tribes & Other Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act — "FRA"** | 2006 | Community & individual forest rights |
| **National Green Tribunal Act** | 2010 | Fast-track environmental adjudication |

**Constitutional hooks (must-cite in Mains):** **Article 48A (DPSP — State to protect environment)** and **Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty — every citizen)**, both inserted by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** ⭐; the judiciary has read the **Right to a clean/healthy environment into Article 21** (right to life) — *Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar (1991)*, *M.C. Mehta* cases.

## 6. Mains angles

1. **"There is no development without environment, and no environment without development." Discuss** — set up the anthropocentric/ecocentric poles → the false binary → sustainable development as synthesis → India's twin duty (poverty *and* climate) → EIA/SDG instruments.
