## PART A — ENVIRONMENT IMPACT ASSESSMENT (EIA)

## 1. Concept & purpose ⭐⭐

- **EIA = a planning tool that predicts, evaluates and mitigates the environmental (and social) consequences of a proposed project *before* it is cleared** — so decisions are informed and the "development vs. environment" trade-off (File 01) is made transparently. Its motto: **"look before you leap."**
- **Objectives:** identify impacts early, weigh alternatives (including the "no-project" option), build in **mitigation**, ensure **public participation**, and enable **informed, accountable clearance**. It operationalises the **precautionary & polluter-pays** principles and **sustainable development**.
- **Origin:** USA's **National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), 1969** ⭐ — the world's first EIA law. India adopted EIA administratively in **1978–79 (river-valley projects)**, then made it statutory.

## 2. The Indian EIA framework ⭐⭐

- **Legal basis:** **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** → **EIA Notification 1994** → **revamped EIA Notification 2006** (the current regime) → controversial **Draft EIA 2020** (criticised for diluting public consultation & allowing post-facto clearance — not finalised) ⭐.
- **Project categories:**
  - **Category A** — large/sensitive projects: appraised **centrally** by **MoEFCC / Expert Appraisal Committee (EAC)** — **EIA compulsory**.
  - **Category B** — smaller projects: appraised by **State (SEIAA / SEAC)**; split into **B1 (needs EIA)** and **B2 (exempt from full EIA)** ⭐.

## 3. The EIA process — the stages ⭐⭐ (learn the sequence)

1. **Screening** — does the project need EIA, and at what level (A/B/B1/B2)?
2. **Scoping** — the EAC frames the **Terms of Reference (ToR)** — which impacts to study.
3. **Baseline data collection** — existing air/water/soil/biodiversity/socio-economic status.
4. **Impact prediction & assessment** — magnitude of likely impacts + alternatives.
5. **EMP — Environment Management Plan** — mitigation, monitoring, safeguards.
6. **Public Consultation / Public Hearing** — affected people & the public comment ⭐ (the democratic heart; weakened by Draft-2020).
7. **Appraisal** — EAC reviews EIA + EMP + hearing → recommends.
8. **Decision (Environmental Clearance, EC)** — granted with conditions or rejected; followed by **post-clearance monitoring & compliance**.

## 4. Critique & the way forward ⭐

- **Strengths:** institutionalises foresight, public voice, mitigation & accountability.
- **Weaknesses:** poor-quality/plagiarised EIA reports, **conflict of interest** (proponent pays the consultant), weak/tokenistic public hearings, **post-facto/ex-post clearances**, feeble **post-EC monitoring & enforcement**, delays, exemptions creeping up.
- **Reforms suggested:** independent, accredited EIA authority; cumulative & regional (carrying-capacity) assessment; **Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA)** at policy level; genuine, early public participation; **Social Impact Assessment** integration; digital transparency (**PARIVESH portal**); strong penalties (**NGT**).
- **AP link:** APPCB/SEIAA appraise Category-B projects; EIA is central to clearing **KG-basin drilling, ports (Machilipatnam, Ramayapatnam), thermal plants, aqua-clusters and Eastern-Ghats mining** — the "development-vs-environment" battleground in the state.

## PART B — NATURAL DISASTERS: PREDICTION & MANAGEMENT

## 5. Framing — hazard, vulnerability, disaster ⭐

- **Disaster = Hazard × Vulnerability × Exposure ÷ Capacity** ⭐ — a hazard becomes a disaster only when it meets vulnerable, exposed people with weak coping capacity. So management targets **all four terms**, not just the hazard.
- **Disaster Management Cycle:** **Mitigation → Preparedness → Response → Recovery/Rehabilitation** (pre- and post-event). Modern doctrine shifts from **relief-centric → prevention & preparedness-centric** ⭐.
- **India's institutional frame ⭐⭐:** **Disaster Management Act, 2005** → **NDMA (National Disaster Management Authority — chaired by the PM)**, **SDMA (chaired by CM), DDMA (District Collector)**; **NDRF** (response force), **NIDM** (training); **Sendai Framework for DRR (2015–2030)** ⭐ — the global blueprint (4 priorities, 7 targets), succeeding the Hyogo Framework. India also leads the **CDRI (2019)**.

## 6. CYCLONES ⭐⭐ (AP-core hazard)

- **What:** intense low-pressure tropical storms (called **cyclones** in the Indian Ocean, hurricanes in the Atlantic, typhoons in the Pacific) with high winds, storm surge & torrential rain; India's **east coast (Bay of Bengal) is far more cyclone-prone than the west** (Bay is warmer, shallower) ⭐.
- **AP vulnerability ⭐⭐:** **Andhra Pradesh's long coast (~974 km traditional / 1,053 km revised 2024) is among India's most cyclone- & storm-surge-exposed** — recurrent landfalls (e.g., **Hudhud, 2014, devastated Visakhapatnam**); the deltas (Godavari–Krishna) face surge + flooding. A **repeat APPSC theme** ("vulnerability of AP to cyclones and floods").
- **Prediction & warning ⭐:** **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** is the official cyclone-warning agency — satellite (**INSAT**), Doppler weather radar, numerical models; **IMD is a WMO Regional Specialised Meteorological Centre (RSMC), New Delhi** that **names cyclones for the north Indian Ocean** ⭐; colour-coded alerts; the **Cyclone Warning Centres (Visakhapatnam CWC serves AP)** ⭐.
- **Management:** cyclone shelters, embankments, evacuation (Odisha's near-zero-casualty **"Phailin/Fani" model** is the benchmark), mangrove **bio-shields (Coringa)**, **National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project (NCRMP)**, early-warning dissemination, coastal-zone regulation (**CRZ**).

## 7. EARTHQUAKES ⭐

- **What:** sudden release of energy along faults (plate tectonics); measured by **magnitude (Richter / Moment-magnitude scale — energy)** vs **intensity (Mercalli — felt damage)** ⭐; focus/hypocentre & epicentre.
- **India's seismic zoning ⭐:** **BIS Zones II–V (Zone V = highest risk)** ⭐ — **Zone V = Himalayas, NE India, Kutch, A&N**; the **Himalaya = young fold mountains on the India–Eurasia collision → the most active belt**. Most of **peninsular India (incl. much of AP) = Zone II/III (low–moderate)**, but not immune (**Latur 1993, Koyna reservoir-induced 1967**).
- **Prediction:** earthquakes are **NOT reliably predictable** (only probabilistic hazard mapping) ⭐ — so management = **preparedness & resilient construction**, not forecasting. Monitoring by the **National Center for Seismology (NCS)**.
- **Management:** **earthquake-resistant/ductile construction & building codes (BIS), retrofitting, land-use zoning, micro-zonation of cities, mock drills, NDRF search-&-rescue**. The lesson: *"earthquakes don't kill people, badly-built buildings do."*

## 8. LANDSLIDES ⭐

- **What:** downslope mass movement of rock/soil/debris; triggers = **heavy rain, earthquakes, slope-cutting for roads/dams, deforestation, mining**. Hotspots: **Himalayas & NE, Western Ghats, Nilgiris** (e.g., Wayanad-Kerala 2024; Himalayan highways).
- **AP link:** the **Eastern Ghats** (Araku–Visakhapatnam agency) see localised rain-triggered slides — lower-frequency than the Himalayas but relevant to hilly AP.
- **Prediction & mapping:** **GSI (Geological Survey of India)** is the nodal agency — **National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping**; rainfall-threshold **early-warning systems** (a growing area).
- **Management:** slope stabilisation (retaining walls, terracing, drainage/dewatering), afforestation & bioengineering, hazard-zonation & land-use control, avoiding slope-toe cutting, relocation of settlements.

## 9. TSUNAMIS ⭐⭐

- **What:** long-wavelength ocean waves from **undersea earthquakes (usually >7 magnitude), landslides or volcanic eruptions** — not tides & not wind waves; imperceptible in deep ocean, they **rear up near shore**. The **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami (Dec 26, Sumatra-Andaman quake)** killed ~230,000 across the region and devastated **Tamil Nadu, AP, Kerala, A&N** — the event that built India's warning system ⭐.
- **AP impact:** the 2004 tsunami hit AP's coast (Nellore–Krishna–Godavari districts) — a live vulnerability given the long, low delta coastline.
- **Prediction & warning ⭐⭐:** the **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC) at INCOIS, Hyderabad** ⭐⭐ (set up 2007, under MoES) — a network of **seismic sensors + Bottom Pressure Recorders (tsunami buoys) + tide gauges** issues warnings within minutes; India is a **Regional Tsunami Service Provider** for the Indian Ocean. **(INCOIS-Hyderabad = the standout regional fact.)**
- **Management:** early-warning dissemination & evacuation, coastal **bio-shields (mangroves, casuarina)**, CRZ & set-back lines, vertical-evacuation structures, community drills.

## 10. Cross-cutting: floods, droughts & the DM system

- **Floods** (most frequent Indian disaster) & **droughts** (Rayalaseema, the AP set-piece) are closely linked to the hazards above; managed via CWC flood forecasting, reservoir regulation, drought-relief codes, MGNREGA/watershed drought-proofing.
- **Andhra Pradesh** is a **multi-hazard state** — cyclones + storm surge + floods (coast/deltas) and **droughts** (Rayalaseema) — making disaster management a permanent governance priority; **AP State Disaster Management Authority (APSDMA)** coordinates.

## 11. Prelims nuggets

1. **EIA origin = NEPA 1969 (USA)**; India: **EP Act 1986 → EIA Notification 1994 → 2006 (current) → Draft 2020 (controversial)**.
2. **Category A = MoEFCC/EAC (central); Category B = SEIAA/SEAC (state), B1 needs EIA / B2 exempt**.
3. EIA stages: **Screening → Scoping (ToR) → Baseline → Prediction → EMP → Public Hearing → Appraisal → Clearance → Monitoring**; portal = **PARIVESH**.
4. **Disaster = Hazard × Vulnerability × Exposure ÷ Capacity**; **DM Act 2005 → NDMA (PM)/SDMA (CM)/DDMA (Collector); NDRF; Sendai 2015–30; CDRI 2019**.
5. **Cyclones: IMD warns & names (RSMC New Delhi); Bay of Bengal > Arabian Sea; Visakhapatnam CWC; Hudhud 2014; NCRMP**.
6. **Earthquakes: BIS Zones II–V (V highest = Himalaya/NE/Kutch); Richter/Mw vs Mercalli; NCS; not predictable → build safe**.
7. **Landslides: GSI nodal; Himalaya/Western Ghats/NE; rain & slope-cutting triggers**.
8. **Tsunami: 2004 Indian Ocean (Sumatra quake, Dec 26); warning by ITEWC/INCOIS, Hyderabad (2007)** ⭐.
9. **CRZ, mangrove bio-shields (Coringa), Odisha zero-casualty model** = coastal-disaster best practice.
10. **AP = multi-hazard: cyclones/surge/floods (coast) + drought (Rayalaseema); APSDMA**.

## 12. Mains angles

1. **"What is Environment Impact Assessment? Evaluate it and suggest how to make it effective" (2023 PYQ-style)** — definition/objectives → India's 1994/2006 framework & stages → strengths → critique (conflict of interest, weak hearings, post-facto EC, poor monitoring) → reforms (independent authority, SEA, PARIVESH, strong penalties).
2. **"Discuss the vulnerability of Andhra Pradesh to cyclones and floods — causes, regional patterns and impacts" (2025 PYQ-style)** — Bay-of-Bengal geography + long delta coast → surge/flood mechanics → district patterns (north-coastal/Vizag vs deltas) → impacts → IMD warning + NCRMP + shelters + mangroves + evacuation.
3. **"India's disaster-management paradigm has shifted from relief to resilience." Examine** — DM Act 2005/NDMA → Sendai/CDRI → hazard-wise prediction (IMD, NCS, GSI, INCOIS) → gaps (last-mile warning, enforcement of building codes).
4. **"For a tsunami and an earthquake, prediction differs but preparedness converges." Discuss** — quakes unpredictable (build safe) vs tsunami minutes-warning (INCOIS) → common tools: zoning, codes, early-warning, drills, bio-shields.
5. **Andhra Pradesh as a multi-hazard state — a disaster-governance challenge.** cyclone/flood/drought/tsunami exposure → APSDMA + IMD/INCOIS + watershed drought-proofing → climate-change amplification (File 06).
