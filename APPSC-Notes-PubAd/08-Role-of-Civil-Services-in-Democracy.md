# 08 — Role of Civil Services in a Democracy

**Syllabus item (second half):** *"…Role of Civil Services in Democracy."*

---

## 1. What are the civil services? — structure first

- **Civil service** = the permanent, professional, politically neutral **executive personnel** of the State — appointed on merit, serving successive elected governments. In Max **Weber's** terms, the archetypal **rational-legal bureaucracy** (hierarchy, rules, written records, merit, career).
- **Three tiers in India:**
  1. **All-India Services (AIS)** — common to Union & States: **IAS, IPS, IFoS** (Indian Forest Service, brought under Art. 312 in 1966). Created under **Art. 312** — Rajya Sabha resolution by **special majority** needed to create a new AIS; governed by the **All India Services Act, 1951**. Recruited by UPSC, allotted to State cadres, controlled jointly (ultimate disciplinary control with the Centre).
  2. **Central services** — IRS, IFS (Foreign), IA&AS, IRTS/IRMS, etc.
  3. **State services** — recruited by **State PSCs (Art. 315)** — in AP by the **APPSC (constituted 1956 with the State; post-bifurcation reconstituted 2014)**: Deputy Collectors, DSPs, CTOs, MROs, etc., plus subordinate services.
- **Constitutional armour:** **Part XIV** — Art. **308–314**: Art. 309 (recruitment & conditions), Art. 310 (**doctrine of pleasure**), **Art. 311** (safeguards: no dismissal by authority subordinate to appointing authority; inquiry with opportunity of being heard — with exceptions: conviction, impracticability, security of State), Art. 312 (AIS), Arts. 315–323 (PSCs).
- **Patel's defence (Constituent Assembly, October 1949):** called the services the **"steel frame"** of India (phrase originally **Lloyd George's, 1922**) — *"you will not have a united India if you do not have a good All-India Service… an efficient, disciplined and contented service, assured of its prospects."* Use this quote in intros.

## 2. Why a permanent civil service in a democracy? (the classic Mains core)

A democracy is run by **amateur, temporary politicians** answerable to the people; it needs a **professional, permanent machine** to convert mandates into services. The civil service supplies:

1. **Continuity & stability** — governments change; administration must not. The "**permanent executive**" bridges electoral transitions (e.g., conduct of business continues during elections under the caretaker convention + Model Code of Conduct).
2. **Policy advice & formulation** — providing ministers with **institutional memory, data and feasibility analysis**; drafting cabinet notes, bills, rules. (Politicians decide *what*; civil servants advise *whether/how*.)
3. **Policy implementation** — the actual delivery of schemes: the District Collector runs everything from land records to disaster relief; last-mile delivery via tehsildars/MROs, BDOs/MPDOs, village secretariats.
4. **Neutral instrument of the elected government** — serving any party with equal fidelity: **political neutrality** and **anonymity** (the minister takes public responsibility; the adviser stays unnamed).
5. **Rule of law & constitutionalism** — acting within statutes; a check against illegal or arbitrary political demands (a civil servant may seek **written orders** — now backed by *T.S.R. Subramanian*, below).
6. **Welfare & development agent** — from regulator (colonial DNA) to **development administrator**: land reforms, Green Revolution logistics, literacy and health missions, MGNREGA, DBT.
7. **Conducting democracy itself** — the electoral machinery (DEOs, ROs, POs) is the civil service on deputation to the ECI; census; delimitation support.
8. **Crisis & disaster management** — pandemics, cyclones (AP's cyclone-preparedness from 1977 Diviseema to Hudhud 2014 and Titli 2018 is a model), law & order.
9. **National integration** — AIS as a unifying thread across states (Patel's argument); uniform standards in administration.
10. **Interface with citizens** — grievance redressal (CPGRAMS; AP **Spandana**), certificates & entitlements (AP **MeeSeva**, village/ward secretariats).

## 3. Values expected of civil servants

| Value | Meaning | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Neutrality** | Party-agnostic loyalty to the government of the day | Weber; UK Northcote–Trevelyan legacy |
| **Impartiality** | Equal treatment of citizens irrespective of identity | Art. 14 |
| **Anonymity** | Adviser behind the minister's responsibility | Parliamentary convention |
| **Integrity** | Incorruptibility, financial probity | Conduct Rules 1964/1968 |
| **Commitment & responsiveness** | To the Constitution and the citizen — not to a party ("committed bureaucracy" in the partisan sense is a **negative**: Indira-era controversy) | 2nd ARC 10th Report |
| **Efficiency, economy, effectiveness** | Value for public money | — |
| **Courage of conviction / independence of advice** | Frank advice, written dissent | *T.S.R. Subramanian* (2013) |

- **Central Civil Services (Conduct) Rules 1964 / AIS (Conduct) Rules 1968** codify integrity & devotion to duty; the **2nd ARC (10th Report, "Refurbishing of Personnel Administration")** and the draft **Civil Services Bill** proposed a statutory **civil-service code of ethics & values**.

## 4. The debit side — criticisms of the Indian civil service

1. **Politicisation** — arbitrary transfers/postings as reward-punishment; "committed" officers; weakened neutrality (average IAS tenure in a post has often been under 16 months — cited by 2nd ARC).
2. **Corruption & rent-seeking** — collusion in the politician–bureaucrat–business nexus (**Santhanam Committee 1962-64** first mapped it; Vohra Committee 1993 on the nexus).
3. **Red-tapism & procedure-obsession** — rule-orientation over result-orientation (Weberian pathology; Merton's "goal displacement").
4. **Elitism & status-quoism** — distance from citizens; colonial hangover of the district "mai-baap" state.
5. **Generalist vs specialist imbalance** — IAS monopoly of top posts vs domain experts (an ARC theme since the **first ARC, 1966–70, Morarji Desai then K. Hanumanthaiah**).
6. **Weak accountability** — Art. 311's protective armour + rarity of penalties; performance appraisal opaque; "safe" mediocrity rewarded by seniority.
7. **Resistance to reform** — from citizen's charters to lateral entry.
8. **Moral/ethical erosion** — highlighted by high-profile prosecutions; conflict-of-interest and post-retirement sinecures.

## 5. Civil services reform — committees, cases, current initiatives

### 5.1 Committee/commission trail (chronological hooks)

| Year | Body | Core recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1962–64 | **Santhanam Committee** | Anti-corruption architecture → **CVC (1964)** |
| 1966–70 | **First ARC** | Specialisation, personnel department, Lokpal-Lokayukta idea |
| 1976 | **Kothari Committee** | Civil-services exam pattern (Prelims-Mains-Interview) |
| 1989 | **Satish Chandra Committee** | Essay, interview weight |
| 2000 | **Surinder Nath Committee** | Performance appraisal, domain assignment |
| 2003 | **P.C. Hota Committee** | Mid-career exams, fixed tenure, Code of Ethics |
| 2004 | **Yugandhar Committee** | Mid-career training (→ Phase III/IV/V programmes) |
| 2005–09 | **2nd ARC (esp. 10th Report)** | Fixed tenure, Civil Services Authority, ethics code, safe-guarded dismissal for non-performance (review at 14 & 20 years), lateral entry |
| 2012 | **Nilekani-era / Alagh ideas** (exam reform) | Aptitude focus (CSAT 2011) |
| 2017– | **NITI Aayog "Strategy for New India @75"** | Lateral entry, outcome-based evaluation, reducing services' number |

### 5.2 Landmark Supreme Court interventions

- ***T.S.R. Subramanian v. Union of India (2013):*** directed (i) **fixed minimum tenure** for civil servants, (ii) **Civil Services Boards** for transfers/postings, (iii) officers should act on **written (not oral) orders** — the judicial charter of bureaucratic independence.
- ***Prakash Singh v. Union of India (2006):*** police reforms — State Security Commission, 2-year tenure for DGP/SP, separation of investigation from law & order, Police Establishment Boards, Police Complaints Authority (implementation still patchy — good critical point).
- ***Vineet Narain (1997):*** CVC statutory status, CBI director's 2-year tenure — insulation of investigative machinery.

### 5.3 Current reform instruments (use as "way forward")

1. **Mission Karmayogi (2020)** — National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building: **iGOT-Karmayogi platform**, shift from **rule-based to role-based** HR, FRACs (framework of roles, activities, competencies), Capacity Building Commission.
2. **Lateral entry (2018 onwards)** — Joint Secretary/Director-level induction of domain experts via UPSC.
3. **e-Governance & DBT** — reduces discretion: PFMS, GeM, faceless assessment (IT Dept), **AP examples: MeeSeva, Spandana, village/ward secretariats (2019) with ~1.3 lakh functionaries + Grama/Ward Volunteers, Rythu Bharosa Kendras** — doorstep, disintermediated delivery.
4. **360-degree appraisal** for empanelment; **probity screening** (compulsory retirement under FR 56(j) reviews).
5. **Citizen-centric instruments** — Citizen's Charters, **Sevottam**, Right to Public Services Acts in many states (AP's **Public Services Delivery Guarantee** framework), social audit.
6. **Good-governance indices** — DARPG's Good Governance Index; district-level ranking (Aspirational Districts Programme, NITI) — competitive federalism reaching administration.

## 6. Minister–civil servant relations (a favourite analytical question)

- **Ideal:** minister sets **policy & takes responsibility**; secretary gives **frank advice, then loyal execution** — "civil servants advise, ministers decide."
- **Pathologies:** oral orders, transfer raj, mutual capture (politicisation of bureaucracy ↔ bureaucratisation of politics), blame-shifting.
- **Balancing doctrines:** *T.S.R. Subramanian* (written orders, tenure); **Art. 311** protection; conduct rules restricting political activity; 2nd ARC's proposal to make the **relationship rule-bound** through Civil Services Boards.
- Quote option: **Appleby (1953 report)** praised India's administrative structure among the best but flagged the need for a professional personnel system; **Nehru** called the ICS "neither Indian, nor civil, nor a service" (pre-1947 criticism — contrast with Patel for a balanced intro).
