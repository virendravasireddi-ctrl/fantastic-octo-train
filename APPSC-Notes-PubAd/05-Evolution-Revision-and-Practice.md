## 1. The evolution spine (one page)

```
c. 4th c. BCE  Arthashastra: saptanga state, adhyakshas, samaharta, spies, dharmasthiya courts
c. 10th c.     Chola village assemblies (Uttaramerur) — classical local self-government
1206–1526      Delhi Sultanate: iqta, diwan-i-wizarat (history series File 02)
1556–1707      Akbar to Aurangzeb: mansab + jagir, diwan/mir bakhshi, suba-sarkar-pargana,
               dahsala (1580), patwari-qanungo records
1765           DIWANI to the Company — Mughal office, British occupant
1772/1793      Collector created (Hastings) · Cornwallis Code & Permanent Settlement
1800–20        Munro: Ceded Districts, ryotwari, the personal-rule district model
1853–61        Open ICS exam (1855) · Crown rule 1858 · Police Act 1861 · codes (IPC 1860)
1870/1882      Mayo → RIPON: local self-government resolutions
1919/1926/1929 Dyarchy · Public Service Commission · Madras Service Commission (first provincial)
1935–50        Provincial autonomy → Constitution: AIS (Art. 312), UPSC/State PSCs, CAG
1959–          Panchayati Raj (Rajasthan 1st, AP 2nd) → 73rd/74th (1992–93) → RTI (2005),
               e-governance, village/ward secretariats (AP, 2019)
```

## 2. Master comparison — Kautilyan · Mughal · British ⭐⭐ (the exam's favourite synthesis)

| Dimension | Kautilyan/Mauryan | Mughal | British |
|---|---|---|---|
| State ideal | **Yogakshema** — king's happiness in subjects' happiness | **Sulh-i-kul** paternalism; farr-i-izadi kingship | **Rule of law**, but colonial-extractive purpose |
| Head | Rajarshi king, bound by dharma-danda | Padshah — personal, no succession law | Governor-General/Viceroy under Parliament |
| Central machinery | Mantriparishad + 18 tirthas | Diwan, mir bakhshi, sadr, khan-i-saman — no collective cabinet | Executive Council → **portfolio system (1861)**, secretariat |
| Personnel | Amatyas: **upadha tests**, pay-scale 48,000→60 panas, transfers | **Mansabdari** (zat-sawar, dagh-chehra, escheat) | **ICS**: open exam (1855), covenanted "steel frame", PSC 1926 |
| Revenue core | Samaharta; **1/6 bhaga**; seven ayasharira; audit (akshapatala) | **Dahsala/zabti** cash rates, ~1/3 demand; jagir assignments | Zamindari/**ryotwari**/mahalwari cash settlements; budget 1860 |
| District/local | Sthanika-gopa tiers under samaharta | **Faujdar + amalguzar** (sarkar); shiqqdar (pargana) | **Collector-Magistrate** (1772) + SP under Police Act 1861 |
| Village records | **Gopa** census & field register | **Patwari + qanungo** | Patwari/**karanam** under settlements; survey & census state |
| City | **Nagaraka** (sanitation, fire, census) | **Kotwal** (watch, markets, curfew) | Municipal corporations (Madras 1687), Ripon's boards |
| Intelligence | Gudhapurushas (samstha/sanchara); 3-source rule | Waqia-navis, harkaras, dak chowkis | Thagi & Dakaiti Dept → **CID (1902)**, IB |
| Justice | **Dharmasthiya/kantakasodhana**; four feet of law | Qazi courts; emperor as fountain; Fatawa-i-Alamgiri | Codified law (IPC 1860), graded courts, High Courts 1862 |
| Welfare face | Famine relief, consumer protection, irrigation | Taccavi, remissions, sarais | Famine Codes, canals (Cotton's anicuts) — but drain & famines |
| Chief weakness | Surveillance state; king-centric | Succession wars; jagirdari crisis | Alien, extractive, participation denied |
| Chief legacy | Departments, audit, vigilance doctrine | Revenue vocabulary & village records; district tier | Almost the entire modern skeleton — services, courts, police, budget, local bodies |

## 3. Continuity threads (quote one per Mains answer)

1. **Village accountant:** gopa → patwari/qanungo → **karanam** (abolished in AP 1984) → VRO → **village secretariat (2019)**.
2. **District officer:** samaharta's field line → faujdar + amalguzar → **Collector (1772)** → today's Collector-DM coordinating welfare & disasters.
3. **Intelligence & vigilance:** gudhapurusha (3-source corroboration) → waqia-navis → CID/IB → CVC-style vigilance.
4. **Treasury primacy:** kosha ("foremost attention") → diwan-i-kul → finance member & **budget (1860)** → Finance Ministry + **CAG** (akshapatala's heir).
5. **Transfers as integrity device:** Kautilya's rotation → jagir transfers every 3–4 years → modern transfer policy (and its politicisation — a critique point).
6. **Grievance redressal:** "no petitioner waits at the door" → jharokha darshan / **Jahangir's chain of justice** → collector's darbar → **RTI, Spandana**.
7. **Municipal office:** nagaraka → kotwal → municipal commissioner (74th Amendment).
8. **Welfare rhetoric:** prajasukhe sukham rajnah → mai-baap paternalism → **DPSPs** — the test is whether structure serves it.

## 7. Ten Mains questions with answer skeletons

1. **Define public administration and examine its changing nature and scope in the era of governance.** — Etymology + Wilson/White → integral vs managerial → POSDCORB + Meriam → welfare-state expansion → NPM/governance turn → India: ARC-II, digital delivery.
2. **"The politics–administration dichotomy was buried long ago, yet refuses to die." Comment.** — Wilson/Goodnow → Simon's fact-value refinement → Appleby's fusion → Indian neutrality vs committed bureaucracy → conclusion: distinction useful as accountability line, false as description.
3. **Public and private administration are "similar in techniques, different in soul." Discuss.** — Fayol/Urwick vs Appleby/Stamp → table points → NPM borrowing and its limits (equity, accountability).
4. **"Kautilya treats administration as a science two millennia before Wilson."** — Adhyaksha specialisation, upadha merit tests, pay schedule, records-audit, transfer policy, espionage-vigilance → map to modern equivalents → caveat: king-centric, not democratic.
5. **"The Kautilyan state was a welfare state run by surveillance." Evaluate.** — Happiness dictum, calamity relief, consumer protection vs spies, danda → verdict on ends vs means.
6. **Analyse mansabdari–jagirdari as the integrative frame of the Mughal empire and the seed of its crisis.** — Composite nobility, zat-sawar discipline, escheat → bejagiri, Deccan overload, Satish Chandra/Habib → lesson: personnel systems fail when rewards outrun resources.
7. **"Todar Mal's dahsala outlived the empire that created it." Discuss.** — Method → predictability & monetisation → patwari-qanungo records feeding British settlements → survival in today's revenue vocabulary (and AP's land resurvey).
8. **Draw up the administrative balance sheet of British rule.** — Positives (services, collector, law, police, budget-CAG, local bodies, unification) vs negatives (drain, famines, repression, distance, divide-and-rule) → "continuity of structure, change of purpose".
9. **"The District Collector embodies the whole evolution of Indian administration." Examine (AP focus).** — Samaharta's line → faujdar/amalguzar → 1772 → Munro's Ceded Districts model → constitutional-era development collector → overload & PRI tension → village secretariats.
10. **"Indian administration is Kautilyan in memory, Mughal in vocabulary, British in structure and constitutional in purpose." Do you agree?** — One paragraph per layer with two evidences each → close with decolonising agenda (police reform, transparency, citizen-centricity per 2nd ARC).
