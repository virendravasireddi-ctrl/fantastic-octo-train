## 1. Concept of Public Service

### 1.1 What makes service "public"?

- **Public service** = activity performed in the exercise of a **public duty** — a duty in whose discharge **the State, the public or the community at large has an interest** (this is the statutory language: **Sec. 2(b), Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988**).
- Three senses in usage: (i) the **services delivered** (water, health, certificates); (ii) the **body of personnel** (the civil/public services — File 08); (iii) the **ethos/vocation** of serving the public — this section's concern.
- **Public office is a public trust** — the fiduciary idea: the official holds power *on behalf of* citizens, never for personal benefit. (SC has repeatedly used the **public trust doctrine**, e.g., *CPIO v. Subhash Agarwal*; 2nd ARC opens its ethics report with it.)

### 1.2 Who is a "public servant"? (definition MCQs)

- **PCA 1988, Sec. 2(c):** very wide — anyone in the service/pay of the government or **remunerated for performing a public duty**, employees of government companies/aided bodies, judges, arbitrators, office-bearers of cooperative societies receiving government aid, university VCs/examiners, etc.
- ***P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State* (1998):** **MPs are public servants** under the PCA. (In ***Sita Soren v. Union of India*, 2024**, a 7-judge bench overruled the bribery-immunity part of *Narasimha Rao* — legislators taking bribes for votes/speech enjoy **no immunity** under Arts. 105/194.)
- General criminal law: **Sec. 21 IPC → now Sec. 2(28), Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023**.

### 1.3 How public service differs from private employment (ethic, not just contract)

| Dimension | Private employment | Public service |
|---|---|---|
| Master | Employer/shareholders | **Constitution & citizens** (the "government of the day" is the instrument, not the master) |
| Test of success | Profit/efficiency | **Public interest, equity, legality** + efficiency |
| Clientele | Chosen customers | **Everyone, especially those with no exit option** (the poor cannot "switch providers") |
| Power used | Economic | **Coercive/sovereign** power — hence higher duty of fairness (Art. 14) |
| Accountability | Market, board | Legislature, courts, CAG, Lokpal, citizens (RTI, social audit) |

- **NPM caution for Mains:** treating the citizen as a "customer" improves responsiveness but cannot replace the **citizen-as-rights-holder** frame — customers can exit; citizens are entitled.
- **Values distilled** (use as your standard list): integrity, impartiality & non-partisanship, objectivity, dedication to public service, **empathy and compassion for the weaker sections** — the officially adopted civil-services value set (mirrored in Mission Karmayogi and the 2014 conduct-rule amendments, Sec. 4 below).
- **Anchor quotes:** Patel's "steel frame" address (File 08); Gandhi's **talisman** — *"Recall the face of the poorest and weakest man… ask if the step you contemplate is going to be of any use to him"*; Kautilya — *"in the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness."*

## 2. Philosophical basis of governance

### 2.1 Western foundations (thinker–idea pairs)

| Thinker | Contribution to the philosophy of governance |
|---|---|
| **Plato** | Rule of the wise — the **philosopher-king**; governance as knowledge of the Good |
| **Aristotle** | Man as a political animal; the **golden mean**; virtue ethics — good governance grows from good character of rulers |
| **Hobbes → Locke → Rousseau** | **Social contract**: the State's authority is *derived* from the governed — the deepest basis of accountability (Locke: government's end is protection of life, liberty, property; right to resist breach of trust; Rousseau: **general will**) |
| **Machiavelli** | The ends–means separation ("the Prince") — the position ethics-in-governance answers are written **against** |
| **Bentham & J.S. Mill** | **Utilitarianism** — "greatest happiness of the greatest number"; the working logic of public policy (cost–benefit) — with the known danger: majorities can trample minorities |
| **Immanuel Kant** | **Deontology** — the categorical imperative: act only on maxims you can universalise; treat persons **always as ends, never merely as means** — the moral floor beneath utilitarian calculation (due process, rights) |
| **John Rawls** | **Justice as fairness** — behind the **veil of ignorance**, choose principles that help the **least advantaged** (difference principle) — the philosophical case for affirmative action and welfare |
| **Nolan Committee (UK, 1995)** | The **Seven Principles of Public Life**: **Selflessness, Integrity, Objectivity, Accountability, Openness, Honesty, Leadership** — the modern operational creed (memorise: "SIO-AOHL") |

### 2.2 Indian foundations

- **Raj dharma:** the ruler is the **servant of dharma**, not its source — Shanti Parva (Mahabharata), Ramayana's Ram Rajya ideal; the king's *danda* (coercion) is legitimate only when yoked to *dharma* (righteous duty).
- **Kautilya:** *yogakshema* (welfare + security) as the State's purpose; a detailed anti-corruption code (forty ways embezzlers work — "as fish drink water while swimming, so officials take money while handling it") — links to File 02.
- **Ashoka's Dhamma:** governance as moral persuasion — welfare bureaucracy (*dhamma-mahamattas*), tolerance, non-violence (links to APPSC-Notes File 00D).
- **Buddhist & Jain ethics:** compassion (*karuna*), non-violence, right conduct (the Eightfold Path's *samyak* — "right" — anticipates "right understanding" in Sec. 3).
- **Bhagavad Gita:** ***nishkama karma*** — duty without attachment to fruits — the classical answer to burnout and to corruption's temptation; *"yogah karmasu kaushalam"* (excellence in action is yoga).
- **Thiruvalluvar (Thirukkural):** ethical statecraft — the just sceptre (*sengol*) vs the cruel sceptre.
- **Gandhi:** **means–ends purity** ("means are after all everything"), **trusteeship**, *sarvodaya* (welfare of all) and **antyodaya** (rise of the last person — later Deendayal Upadhyaya's Integral Humanism made antyodaya its touchstone); Su-raj.
- **Constitutional philosophy** — the operative synthesis: Preamble (justice–liberty–equality–fraternity), FRs (limits on power), DPSPs (positive welfare duties), Art. 14 (non-arbitrariness as the constitutional ethic), Art. 51A (citizen's duties).

### 2.3 The one-line synthesis for Mains

> Governance draws its **authority** from consent (social contract/elections), its **purpose** from welfare (yogakshema/DPSPs), its **method** from dharma/rule of law, and its **discipline** from accountability (Nolan values, audit, ombudsman). Ethics is what keeps the four aligned when no one is watching.

## 3. Professional ethics in the light of Right Understanding and the Vision for Holistic Technologies

*(This phrase comes from the **Universal Human Values (UHV)** framework — propounded by **A. Nagraj** (Madhyasth Darshan / co-existentialism, Amarkantak) and mainstreamed through the **AICTE UHV courses** and Jeevan Vidya shivirs. APPSC has lifted the phrasing directly, so answer in this vocabulary.)*

### 3.1 Right understanding (*samajhdari*)

- Human choices can be driven by (i) **preconditioning** (beliefs absorbed unexamined), (ii) **sensation** (immediate likes/dislikes), or (iii) **right understanding** — knowledge of reality **verified by one's own natural acceptance and experiential validation**, not borrowed or imposed.
- **The human being = Self (consciousness, *jeevan*) + Body.** The Self's need is **happiness (harmony)** — continuous, qualitative; the Body's need is **physical facilities** — limited, quantitative. Confusing the two (seeking continuous happiness from limitless consumption) is the root error behind both personal unhappiness and unsustainable "development".
- **Prosperity vs wealth:** *prosperity* is the **feeling of having more than required** physical facilities — it needs (a) correct assessment of need and (b) capacity to produce; mere accumulation without right assessment leaves even the rich feeling deprived — a direct critique of GDP-only development (ties to File 06 Sec. 1).

### 3.2 Harmony at four levels — and the comprehensive human goal

| Level | Harmony looks like | Component of the human goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual (self)** | Right understanding within | **Samadhan** (resolution) |
| **Family** | Mutual fulfilment of relationships (values: trust, respect, affection, care, guidance, gratitude, love) | **Samriddhi** (prosperity) |
| **Society** | Fearlessness based on trust | **Abhay** (trust/fearlessness) |
| **Nature/Existence** | Mutual enrichment with all four orders (material, plant/bio, animal, human) | **Sah-astitva** (co-existence) |

- The **universal human order** = social systems (education, health, production, exchange, justice) designed so all four are secured for all.

### 3.3 What this does to professional ethics

- **Definition (UHV):** professional ethics = **developing the competence to use one's profession in consonance with the comprehensive human goal** — the profession becomes participation in the universal human order, not merely a livelihood.
- **The three competencies:** (1) **clarity** of the comprehensive human goal; (2) **confidence in, and practice of, ethical human conduct** — values, policies, character consistent with right understanding; (3) ability to design/operate **mutually fulfilling systems** — people-friendly institutions and **eco-friendly (holistic) technologies**.
- **The crucial claim (quote it):** codes, incentives and policing produce only **compliance**; *durable* ethical conduct comes from **right understanding** — an internally verified sense of what is right. Fear-based and greed-based motivation fluctuates; understanding-based conduct is **definitive**. This is the philosophical answer to "why do scams recur despite laws?"

### 3.4 Vision for Holistic Technologies, production systems and management models

Criteria for a technology/production system to be "holistic":

1. Caters to **real human needs** (not manufactured wants);
2. **People-friendly** — safe, skill-compatible, employment-generating, decentralising rather than displacing;
3. **Eco-friendly** — uses **renewable resources and cyclic processes** (output of one process feeds another; nature's own pattern), low waste, recyclable;
4. **Local self-reliance** — producible and maintainable with local resources and skills (*swadeshi*; Schumacher's **"Small is Beautiful" / appropriate technology**; Gandhi's khadi & village industries);
5. **Management models of mutual fulfilment** — cooperation/complementarity (cooperatives, SHG federations) rather than exploitation and domination.

**Ready examples (AP-flavoured):** **APCNF/RySS natural farming (2016)** — the world-scale demonstration of an eco-friendly, knowledge-intensive, women-led production system (File 06); watershed development and **Neeru-Meeru**; biogas/solar decentralised energy; rainwater harvesting (Rajendra Singh's johads); vernacular low-cost architecture (**Laurie Baker**); **Amul/SHG-federation models** as mutually fulfilling management; millet revival (Deccan Development Society).

## 4. Ethical and moral values in governance; Codes of Ethics and Codes of Conduct

### 4.1 Getting the terms straight (definition questions)

- **Values** = enduring beliefs about what is desirable (honesty, courage); **morals** = society's notions of right/wrong; **ethics** = the *reasoned, applied* discipline of right conduct in a role/profession; **law** = the enforceable minimum. Ethics **begins where law ends** — law punishes bribery; ethics also forbids the favouritism no statute can reach.
- **Values in governance:** the Nolan seven (Sec. 2.1) + Indian additions: **empathy/compassion for the weaker sections, tolerance, dedication, political neutrality, probity**.

### 4.2 Code of Ethics vs Code of Conduct (classic 10-marker)

| | **Code of Ethics** | **Code of Conduct** |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Broad **principles/values** ("integrity", "impartiality") | Specific **do's and don'ts** (rules) |
| Form | Aspirational, general | Prescriptive, enforceable |
| Breach | Moral censure; guides discretion | **Disciplinary action** |
| India's position | **No formally adopted Code of Ethics yet** — a draft has existed since 1997; **2nd ARC (4th & 10th Reports) recommended** enacting public-service values + a Code of Ethics in law (draft Public Services Bill) | **CCS (Conduct) Rules, 1964** and **AIS (Conduct) Rules, 1968** — in force |

### 4.3 The Conduct Rules — what they actually say (high-yield)

- **Rule 3 (the ethics core):** every member shall at all times maintain **absolute integrity** and **devotion to duty**, and do nothing **unbecoming**.
- **2014 amendments** (post-Santhanam's long shadow) wrote the values *into* the rules: commitment to the **Constitution**, professionalism, **political neutrality**, merit, fairness, **accountability & transparency**, responsiveness (esp. to weaker sections), courtesy, proper use of public resources, high ethical standards, no conflict of interest, no misuse of position.
- Specific bars: no participation in **politics** or standing for election; restrictions on **criticising government policy** publicly; **gifts** beyond limits barred; no **speculation** in stocks; property transactions to be reported; annual **immovable-property returns**; no dowry giving/taking; bar on employing children below 14 as domestic help; post-retirement commercial employment needs sanction (cooling-off).
- **Also know:** **Code of Conduct for Ministers** (Ministry of Home Affairs) — asset disclosure, no business connection with government dealings; **Ethics Committees** of Rajya Sabha (1997) & Lok Sabha (2000); the **Model Code of Conduct (ECI)** is an *electoral* code — don't confuse it with service conduct rules.
- **2nd ARC (10th Report) big-ticket ideas:** a **Civil Services Law** codifying values + ethics code; **Civil Services Authority**; and — its boldest — **repeal Arts. 310 & 311** and replace with statutory protection, so that honest officers are protected but the corrupt cannot hide behind procedure.

### 4.4 RTI and Public Services Acts — the ethical reading (cross-reference File 09)

- **RTI 2005** operationalises the value of **transparency**: it reverses the information asymmetry on which both corruption and arrogance of office rest — "sunlight as disinfectant." The ethical duty it creates: **Sec. 4 proactive disclosure** — openness as default, secrecy as the exception needing justification (inverting the Official Secrets Act culture).
- **Public Services Acts** operationalise **responsiveness as an enforceable ethical obligation**: the citizen's time is treated as valuable; delay becomes *personally* costly to the delaying officer (penalty), converting courtesy from grace to duty.
- Together with **social audit** (File 09 Sec. 7), they build the **external ethical infrastructure**; Sections 3–4 here supply the **internal** one. Mains formula: *institutions make unethical conduct difficult; right understanding makes it unnecessary.*

## 5. Leadership ethics

### 5.1 What ethical leadership is

- **Treviño's two pillars:** the **moral person** (integrity, fairness, humility in private conduct) + the **moral manager** (visibly models values, communicates ethics, rewards/punishes accordingly) — leaders set the **"tone at the top"**; followers calibrate to what leaders *do*, not what they say.
- **Theories (name-drop set):** **transformational leadership** (Burns/Bass — elevates followers' motives and morality; contrast *transactional* reward-exchange); **servant leadership** (Greenleaf, 1970 — the leader as servant first; institutional echo: "public *servant*"); **Level-5 leadership** (Jim Collins — professional will + personal humility); authentic leadership.
- **Traits to cite:** moral courage (dissent in file, refusing illegal oral orders — link *T.S.R. Subramanian*, File 08), consistency, empathy, accountability ("the buck stops here"), justice in rewards, mentoring.

### 5.2 Exemplars (one line each — use 2–3 per answer)

- **S.R. Sankaran, IAS (AP cadre)** — "the people's IAS officer": drove abolition of **bonded labour**, SC/ST **sub-plan** budgeting, lived austerely, unmarried, gave away much of his salary; negotiated as one of the hostages' representatives during the 1987 Gurtedu (naxal) episode. **The AP answer to any "ethical leader" question.**
- **T.N. Seshan** — institutional assertion: enforced the MCC and voter ID, showed one determined officer can activate a dormant institution (ECI).
- **E. Sreedharan** — "Metro Man": punctuality-and-integrity work culture on Konkan Railway/Delhi Metro (reverse clocks counting down to deadlines).
- **Armstrong Pame, IAS** — the crowd-funded "People's Road" in Manipur; **U. Sagayam, Ashok Khemka** — probity at personal cost (repeated transfers).
- **Kalam** — humility + vision as leadership ethics; **Gandhi** — the talisman as a leadership decision-filter.

### 5.3 Leadership failures to contrast

Moral muteness of seniors during scams; "loyalty over legality" cultures; competitive populism ordering illegal acts — each shows that **followership ethics** (the courage to refuse) is leadership ethics' twin.

## 6. Work culture

- **Definition:** the shared **values, norms, habits and unwritten rules** that shape how work actually gets done in an organisation — "the way we do things here" (Schein's levels: artefacts → espoused values → deep assumptions).
- **Healthy work culture markers:** punctuality & time-respect, meritocracy, teamwork/*esprit de corps* (Fayol's 14th principle), openness to dissent, citizen-first orientation, learning orientation, integrity as default, work–life balance.
- **Pathologies of sarkari work culture (be balanced, not cynical):** hierarchy-worship and file-pushing (**red tape**), precedent over problem-solving, risk-aversion ("no one was ever punished for delay"), input- not outcome-focus, insider-first (citizen queues, staff shortcuts), moral muteness.
- **Theories for garnish:** McGregor's **Theory X/Y** (assumptions about workers shape culture), Ouchi's **Theory Z** (trust + long-term orientation — Japanese lesson), **kaizen/5S** continuous improvement.
- **Indian ethical resources:** *"work is worship"*; Gita's *yogah karmasu kaushalam*; the **karma-yogi** ideal — which is exactly why the 2020 capacity-building mission is named **Mission Karmayogi** (rule-based → **role-based**, FRAC competency mapping, iGOT — File 08).
- **Levers that actually change culture:** leadership modelling (Sec. 5), e-Office/paperless flows (delay visible), citizen charters with time-limits (File 09), performance-linked appraisal, recognition of honest officers, training (UHV/ethics modules), and **AP examples** — secretariat-level service timelines, **Spandana** grievance-day discipline every Monday, RTGS dashboards making delay measurable.

## 7. Ethical principles within an organisational context

### 7.1 The standard dilemmas (know one example each)

1. **Conflict of interest** — private interest vs public duty (relative's firm in a tender) → **disclose + recuse**; post-retirement jobs (cooling-off rules).
2. **Obedience vs conscience** — illegal/improper orders → seek **written orders**, record dissent, escalate (*T.S.R. Subramanian*); the Nuremberg lesson: "superior orders" is no defence.
3. **Whistle-blowing** — loyalty to organisation vs public interest → internal channels first, then statutory (**Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014** — enacted but **rules never notified/not operationalised**; a standing critique — cite Satyendra Dubey (NHAI) and Shanmughan Manjunath (IOC) cases).
4. **Gifts & hospitality** — the grey zone where capture begins → conduct-rule limits, refuse or deposit.
5. **Nepotism/favouritism in postings & contracts**; **misuse of official resources**; **confidentiality vs transparency** (RTI Sec. 8 balancing).
6. **Speed vs process** in emergencies — document deviations, ratify later (disaster procurement).

### 7.2 Ethical decision-making — a usable framework

Test a proposed action through **four lenses**: (i) **Utilitarian** — best net consequences for all affected? (ii) **Rights/duties (Kantian)** — does it treat every person as an end; would I universalise it? (iii) **Justice (Rawlsian)** — how does it affect the least advantaged; is the procedure fair? (iv) **Virtue/publicity** — could I defend it to my mentor, on the front page, to Gandhi's talisman? If the lenses conflict, rights are the floor, consequences pick among rights-respecting options.

### 7.3 The organisation's ethical infrastructure

- **Compliance-based vs integrity-based** strategies (**Lynn Sharp Paine**): rules-and-audit *plus* values-and-culture — either alone fails.
- Components: code of conduct + ethics training (UHV), **vigilance machinery** (CVO in every department, reporting to **CVC**), **Integrity Pacts** in large procurement (Transparency International instrument, mandated by CVC — with Independent External Monitors), e-procurement/GeM (discretion out), transparent transfer-posting boards (File 08), whistle-blower channels, ethics committees, exemplary and quick disciplinary action (certainty over severity), and protection of honest decisions (PCA Sec. 17A balance, Sec. 9.3 below).
- **Group pathologies to name:** **groupthink** (Janis), diffusion of responsibility ("everyone signed"), moral disengagement/euphemism ("speed money", "adjustment").

## 8. Ethical issues in international relations

### 8.1 The framing tension

**Realism** (Morgenthau/Kautilya's mandala: states pursue power; survival first) vs **idealism/liberal internationalism** (states are bound by universal moral norms — human rights, institutions). Practical ethics in IR = managing this tension: *national interest pursued within moral side-constraints.* India's self-image: **Vishwa-bandhu / Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam** ("the world is one family" — the G20-2023 theme, "One Earth · One Family · One Future").

### 8.2 The issue map (each with the ethical question + Indian position/example)

| Issue | Ethical question | Handy material |
|---|---|---|
| **War & use of force** | **Just War** tests — *jus ad bellum* (just cause, last resort, proportionality), *jus in bello* (discrimination between combatants/civilians; Geneva Conventions) | India's restraint doctrine; UN Charter Art. 2(4) vs Art. 51 self-defence |
| **Humanitarian intervention / R2P** | Sovereignty vs saving lives — who authorises, who benefits? | Libya 2011 (R2P stretched to regime change) — why India abstains from interventionism; contrast India's 1971 Bangladesh action debate |
| **Nuclear ethics** | Can deterrence built on threatening millions be moral? Discriminatory NPT order | India's **No First Use** & credible-minimum deterrence; disarmament advocacy (Rajiv Gandhi Action Plan, 1988) |
| **Terrorism & counter-terror** | Targeted killings, drones, collective punishment vs due process | India's push for **CCIT** (Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism, pending since 1996) |
| **Refugees & migration** | **Non-refoulement** vs capacity/security | India: not a party to the 1951 Convention yet hosts Tibetans, Sri Lankan Tamils, Chakmas; Rohingya cases — humanity vs security balancing |
| **Climate justice** | Historical responsibility vs present emissions — **CBDR-RC**; who pays for transition? | India: per-capita framing, **Panchamrit** pledges, ISA/CDRI/LiFE as global public goods |
| **Trade & health equity** | Patents vs lives (TRIPS) | Doha Declaration 2001; **India–South Africa TRIPS-waiver proposal (2020)** for COVID vaccines; India as "pharmacy of the global South" |
| **Foreign aid & conditionality** | Aid as leverage vs solidarity | India's "**development partnership**" idiom — demand-driven, no policy conditionality (ITEC, Lines of Credit); **Vaccine Maitri (2021)**; HADR — Op **Rahat** (Yemen 2015), **Ganga** (Ukraine 2022), **Dost** (Türkiye-Syria 2023), **Ajay** (Israel 2023) |
| **Sanctions** | Do broad sanctions punish peoples for rulers' sins? | Oil-for-food lessons; India's preference for UN-mandated over unilateral measures |
| **Emerging-tech ethics** | Cyber attacks on civilian infrastructure, **LAWS** ("killer robots"), surveillance exports, AI bias, data colonialism, space debris | UN GGE processes; India's DPDP/AI mission positions |
| **Ethics of abstention** | Is neutrality complicity? | India's Russia–Ukraine votes — "dialogue and diplomacy", *"this is not an era of war"* (2022) as the ethical wrapper of strategic autonomy — present both readings in Mains |

### 8.3 India's normative toolkit (close any IR-ethics answer with 3 of these)

**Panchsheel (1954)** five principles of peaceful coexistence; **NAM** — strategic autonomy with moral voice; **Gujral Doctrine (1996–97)** — non-reciprocal generosity to smaller neighbours (ethics as foreign policy); Gandhi–Buddha heritage in disarmament & anti-racism (India's first UN cause: apartheid); **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam**; global-public-goods diplomacy (ISA, CDRI, DPI-sharing, Global South voice — Voice of Global South Summits).

## 9. Corruption

### 9.1 Concept, types, causes

- **Definition:** the **abuse of entrusted public power for private gain** (Transparency International). Wider ethical sense: any subordination of public duty to private advantage — including nepotism, patronage, "legal corruption" (conflict-marred but lawful decisions).
- **Types:** petty (retail — citizen-facing bribes) vs **grand** (policy/procurement capture); **collusive** (both sides gain — hardest to detect: evasion, cartels, sweetheart PPPs) vs **coercive** (extortion from the citizen); systemic vs individual.
- **Klitgaard's formula (quote it):** **C = M + D − A** — *Corruption = Monopoly + Discretion − Accountability.* Every reform in this file attacks one term: competition/e-markets ↓M, rules/technology ↓D, RTI-audit-Lokpal ↑A.
- **Causes in India:** discretion-rich regulation (licence-permit legacy), **election finance** (the mother problem — 2nd ARC), low certainty of punishment (long trials), social tolerance ("speed money"), asymmetric information, weak protection of whistle-blowers, colonial secrecy culture.
- **Consequences:** a regressive **tax on the poor**; diverts development spending (leakages — Rajiv Gandhi's "15 paise" remark); deters investment; corrodes **trust**, the state's core capital; feeds organised crime–politics nexus (**Vohra Committee, 1993**).
- **Measurement:** TI's **Corruption Perceptions Index** (India ~93rd/180, score 39 in CPI-2023; slipped to ~96th in CPI-2024 — *check the latest edition before the exam*); Global Corruption Barometer; NCRB PCA-case data.

### 9.2 The legal-institutional arsenal (learn as a table)

| Instrument | Year | Core content |
|---|---|---|
| **IPC/BNS provisions** | 1860/2023 | Cheating, criminal breach of trust by public servant |
| **Prevention of Corruption Act** | **1988** | Consolidated PCA; public servant defined widely; presumption on proof of gratification |
| **PCA (Amendment) Act** | **2018** | **Bribe-giving = specific offence** (7 yrs; defence: report within 7 days if coerced); criminal misconduct **narrowed** (Sec. 13 — now needs misappropriation/disproportionate assets); **Sec. 17A** — prior approval of competent authority before investigating decisions of public servants (shield for honest decisions; criticised as investigation-blocker); prior-sanction (Sec. 19) protection extended to **retired** officials; trial to conclude in 2 yrs (extendable to 4); **attachment/forfeiture** of bribe-tainted property |
| **Benami Transactions (Prohibition) Act** | 1988, **teeth via 2016 amendment** | Confiscation of benami property |
| **Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA)** | **2002** | ED attachment/prosecution; *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* (2022) upheld powers |
| **Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act** | **2013** | Sec. 10 below |
| **Whistle Blowers Protection Act** | **2014** | Enacted, **not operationalised** (rules pending; 2015 dilution bill pending) |
| **Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income) Act** | 2015 | Foreign-asset disclosure |
| **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act** | **2018** | Confiscation upon absconding (₹100 cr+ offences) |
| **Companies Act CSR/fraud (Sec. 447), e-procurement/GeM, DBT** | — | Preventive architecture |
| **UNCAC** | signed 2005, **ratified 2011** | The global convention (prevention, criminalisation, asset recovery) |
| Institutions | — | **CVC (2003 Act), CBI (DSPE Act 1946), ED, SFIO, CAG, Lokpal/Lokayukta**, departmental vigilance (CVOs) |

- ***Vineet Narain* (1997)** — CVC statutory, CBI insulation; ***Subramanian Swamy* (2012)** — sanction decisions time-bound (3 months); ***Lalita Kumari* (2013)** — mandatory FIR for cognisable offences; **electoral bonds struck down (SC, Feb 2024)** — opacity in political funding held unconstitutional (link corruption ↔ election finance).

### 9.3 2nd ARC, 4th Report — "Ethics in Governance" (2007): the recommendation bank

Partial **state funding of elections**; tighten anti-defection; **Sec. 8 RPA** — bar on charge-framed (heinous/corruption) candidates; **collegium for CBI Director**; **Serious Economic Offences Office**; **False Claims Act** (citizens can sue for fraud on government and share recovery); **confiscation of illegally acquired assets** of public servants; ethics codes for ministers/legislators/servants; **Lokpal (with PM excluded, it then said) + Lokayuktas**; protect honest civil servants; reduce discretion; **repeal Art. 310/311** (with statutory safeguards).

### 9.4 Andhra Pradesh machinery (home-turf)

- **Anti-Corruption Bureau (ACB)** — the State's PCA investigating agency (traps, disproportionate-assets cases; est. **1961**, among India's earliest); toll-free **14400** complaint line (2019) made trap-complaints citizen-easy.
- **AP Vigilance Commission** (post-**Santhanam**, 1964 — among the first states) — advisory vigilance apex; **Vigilance & Enforcement Department** (1985) for government-wide checks.
- **AP Lokayukta (1983 Act)** — Sec. 11 below; **APSHRC, SIC, SSAAT social audit, Spandana** — the accountability ring (Files 07 & 09).
- Preventive AP e-governance: e-procurement (an early-2000s AP first), **MeeSeva/secretariat delivery** (discretion ↓), **RTGS dashboards** (delay visible), **DBT saturation** schemes.

## 10. Lokpal — the national ombudsman

### 10.1 The ombudsman idea & the long road (chronology = MCQ set)

- **Sweden, 1809** — the first *Justitieombudsman* ("citizen's defender"); **Finland 1919; Denmark 1955; New Zealand 1962** — first in the Commonwealth; the institution = an **independent high officer who receives citizens' complaints of maladministration/corruption against public functionaries and investigates them**.
- India: **Dr. L.M. Singhvi coined "Lokpal" and "Lokayukta" (1963)**; **ARC-I interim report (1966, Morarji Desai)** recommended a two-tier system — **Lokpal** (Centre: ministers & secretaries) + **Lokayuktas** (states).
- **Eight/nine failed Lokpal Bills — 1968, '71, '77, '85, '89, '96, '98, 2001, 2011** (the 1968 bill passed the Lok Sabha in 1969 but lapsed with dissolution — a favourite trap).
- **2011:** the **India Against Corruption / Anna Hazare** movement (Jan Lokpal demand, Ramlila fasts) — civil society forcing the agenda (link to File 06's advocacy function); joint drafting committee; parliamentary passage December 2013.
- **The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013** — assent **1 Jan 2014**, in force **16 Jan 2014**. But the institution stayed vacant until the SC (in *Common Cause*) pushed — **first Lokpal chairperson: Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose, sworn in 23 March 2019** (a five-year gap that is itself a Mains point). Later chair: **Justice A.M. Khanwilkar (March 2024)**.

### 10.2 Composition & appointment

- **Chairperson + up to 8 members** (max 9 total). Chair: **former CJI / former SC judge / eminent person** of impeccable integrity with 25 years' relevant expertise.
- **≥ 50% judicial members** (SC judges/HC Chief Justices); **≥ 50% of members from SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women**.
- **Selection Committee:** **PM (chair) + Lok Sabha Speaker + Leader of Opposition (LS) + CJI or his SC-judge nominee + one eminent jurist** (nominated by President on the others' recommendation); assisted by a **Search Committee** (same diversity rule). *When there is no recognised LoP, the leader of the single largest opposition party sits (2016 practice/amendment context).*
- **Term:** 5 years or age 70; removal by President on SC inquiry; post-tenure bars (no government employment; no election for 5 years).

### 10.3 Jurisdiction (Sec. 14 — the heart)

1. **Prime Minister — included, with a triple lock:** only corruption allegations; **excluded subjects** — international relations, external & internal **security, public order, atomic energy, space**; inquiry needs the **full bench** and approval of **⅔ members**; held **in camera**, records sealed if complaint dismissed.
2. **Union Ministers & MPs** — but **not** for anything said or a vote **in Parliament** (Arts. 105 protection).
3. **All Union public servants — Groups A, B, C, D**: Lokpal orders preliminary inquiry/investigation for **A & B**; complaints against **C & D go via the CVC** (which reports back).
4. **Bodies financed/controlled by the Union**, and **societies/trusts/associations receiving foreign contribution above ₹10 lakh** (FCRA) or government grants above notified thresholds — i.e., large NGOs' officials are "public servants" for the Act (link to File 06 debates).

### 10.4 Powers & process

- **Inquiry Wing** (preliminary inquiry — within **90 days**, extendable 90) and **Prosecution Wing** (Director of Prosecution); investigation by any agency incl. **CBI — over which Lokpal has superintendence for referred cases**; the investigating officer in a Lokpal-referred CBI case **cannot be transferred without Lokpal's approval**; investigation normally **6 months** (extendable by 6 at a time).
- **Civil-court powers**; **search & seizure**; **provisional attachment and confiscation** of corruption-tainted assets; may recommend **suspension/transfer** of the accused public servant during inquiry; **Special Courts** for trial (aim: within 1–2 years).
- **Sec. 44:** public servants' **asset declarations** (2016 amendment simplified form/timelines).
- **False & frivolous complaints:** punishable (up to 1 year + fine) — a deterrence-vs-chilling debate point.

### 10.5 Assessment (the critical paragraph examiners want)

**Design strengths:** PM within ambit (unlike many drafts), judicial-diversity composition, CBI superintendence, asset-confiscation powers, NGO coverage. **Working weaknesses:** the **5-year appointment delay** and later member vacancies; complaint numbers modest and **conviction record thin**; **prosecution wing under-built**; dependence on government for budget/staff; **no suo motu** initiation (acts on complaints); Group C/D routed to CVC (dilution vs ARC vision); **judiciary excluded** (judicial-accountability gap); the **Sec. 17A (PCA)** prior-approval layer can stall cases; states' Lokayuktas uneven (Sec. 11). **Way forward:** time-bound appointments by statute, functional autonomy (charged budget), strengthen prosecution wing, integrate with CVC/CBI case-flow, protect whistle-blowers (operationalise the 2014 Act), publish disposal dashboards. **One-liner close:** *an ombudsman succeeds not by its powers on paper but by its independence in practice — Lokpal has the first, and is still proving the second.*

## 11. Lokayukta — the state ombudsman (with AP detail)

### 11.1 The institution across states

- Statutory anti-corruption/grievance ombudsman in the states — investigates allegations against **ministers, legislators and officials**; created by **state Acts**, so design varies widely (jurisdiction over CM, suo motu power, staffing all differ — a standing criticism: "one country, many ombudsmen").
- **Chronology traps:** **Odisha passed the first Lokayukta Act (1970)**, but **Maharashtra established the institution first (1971)**; Odisha's own body came later (and was even abolished 1993, revived by a 2014 Act). **Karnataka Lokayukta** (1984 Act) became the strongest brand — **Justice Santosh Hegde's illegal-mining report (2011)** brought down a CM — proof the model can bite when given investigation machinery (its own police wing).
- **Lokpal Act 2013, Sec. 63:** every state **shall establish a Lokayukta within 365 days** of the Act's commencement — but the Act leaves design to states (criticised as toothless federal courtesy). SC has repeatedly nudged laggard states.
- **Common design:** retired HC Chief Justice/judge as Lokayukta (+ Upa-Lokayuktas); appointed by **Governor** on CM's advice after consulting the **Chief Justice of the HC and the Leader of Opposition**; fixed term (usually 5 years); reports to the legislature; most Acts cover ministers & officers, **many exclude the CM** (Karnataka includes; MP/AP exclude); recommendations generally **not self-executing** — forwarded to the competent authority.
- **Kerala controversy (2022):** ordinance letting the government reject Lokayukta findings — the live "who guards the guardian's guardian" debate.

### 11.2 Andhra Pradesh Lokayukta (home-turf detail)

- **The A.P. Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayukta Act, 1983** — in force **1 November 1983** (among the earlier states; enacted under the N.T. Rama Rao government).
- **Composition & appointment:** Lokayukta = **sitting/retired High Court judge** (in practice retired), Upa-Lokayukta(s) = district-judge-grade/senior officers as per Act; appointed by the **Governor** on the advice of the CM **in consultation with the Chief Justice of the AP High Court and the Leader of the Opposition**; term **5 years**; independent secretariat; annual report to the Legislature.
- **Jurisdiction:** **Ministers (the CM is outside the Act's purview)**, secretaries and government officers of notified categories, heads of local bodies/public undertakings, universities' officers etc.; both **"allegations"** (corruption/abuse) and **"grievances"** (maladministration) — the classic twin-track ombudsman design.
- **Limitations (same family as elsewhere):** recommendatory findings; excludes CM & (for House conduct) MLAs; dependent on government staffing; long **vacancy gaps** — post-bifurcation the office remained unfilled for years until **Justice P. Lakshmana Reddy was appointed the successor-State's Lokayukta in 2019** (verify incumbent before the exam); overlap/dependence vis-à-vis **ACB** (which answers to government, not the Lokayukta) blunts investigations.
- **AP answer kit:** pair the Lokayukta with the **ACB + Vigilance Commission + SSAAT + SIC + Spandana** to describe the State's full accountability ecosystem — and note the recurring AP lesson: *institutions exist; independence and timely appointments decide whether they work.*
