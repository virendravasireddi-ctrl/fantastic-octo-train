## 0. Quick facts — nature of the Mughal state

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Character | **Centralised, military-bureaucratic monarchy** — a Perso-Islamic (Iranian wazarat) + Central Asian (Turko-Mongol) + **Indian (revenue practice)** synthesis; J. N. Sarkar's shorthand: a Perso-Arabic system in an Indian setting |
| Pivot | The **emperor** — no fixed law of succession → recurring wars of succession (structural weakness) |
| Official language | **Persian** — made the language of administration at all levels under Akbar (revenue records switched to Persian, a change associated with **Todar Mal**) |
| Ruling class | Composite nobility — Turani, Irani, Afghan, **Rajput**, later Deccani — knit together by **mansab + jagir + sulh-i-kul** |
| Key sources | **Abul Fazl's Ain-i-Akbari** (the empire's administrative gazetteer) & Akbarnama; dastur-ul-amals (office manuals); European travellers (**Bernier's** letters) |
| Scholarship | J. N. Sarkar (*Mughal Administration*), **Ibn Hasan** (*Central Structure of the Mughal Empire*), **Irfan Habib** (*Agrarian System of Mughal India*), Athar Ali (*Mughal Nobility under Aurangzeb*) |

**Precursor box — Sher Shah Suri (1540–45):** silver **rupiya** + copper dam; **patta–qabuliyat** (title deed & acceptance) to cultivators; land measurement; restored the **Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-i-Azam)** with **sarais** (also served as dak stages); pargana officers (shiqqdar, amin, fotadar, karkuns). **Akbar built the Mughal system on Sher Shah's foundations** ⭐.

## 1. Emperor and the theory of kingship

- **Abul Fazl's theory:** kingship is **farr-i-izadi — divine light** received directly by the emperor; the padshah stands above sects as father of all subjects → underwrites **sulh-i-kul (peace with all)** as state policy.
- Public accessibility rituals: **jharokha darshan** (daily balcony appearance — begun under Akbar, **abolished by Aurangzeb** as un-Islamic); **Jahangir's zanjir-i-adl** — the golden **chain of justice** at Agra fort any petitioner could pull ⭐ (grievance-redressal image for Mains).
- Justice: emperor = fountain of justice; **Fatawa-i-Alamgiri** — the great digest of Hanafi law compiled under **Aurangzeb**.
- Policy markers of state character: Akbar abolished the pilgrim tax (1563) and **jizya (1564)**; **Aurangzeb reimposed jizya (1679)** — the inclusive-to-narrow arc examiners like.

## 2. Central government — the four pillars + two ⭐

No collective cabinet: each minister individually responsible to the emperor (contrast with modern council of ministers).

| Office | Charge | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vakil (vakil-i-mutlaq)** | Regent/prime minister | All-powerful under **Bairam Khan** (1556–60); Akbar then made it **honorific** — a deliberate check |
| **Diwan / Wazir (diwan-i-kul)** ⭐ | **Revenue & finance** — income, expenditure, provincial diwans report to him | The working head of administration; Todar Mal held it as **Diwan-i-Ashraf** |
| **Mir Bakhshi** ⭐ | **Military pay & muster** — paymaster-general; processes **mansab** recommendations; posts **waqia-navis** (news-writers) → intelligence flows through him | NOT the commander-in-chief (trap) |
| **Mir Saman / Khan-i-Saman** | Imperial household & **karkhanas** (state workshops/stores) | Court logistics |
| **Sadr-us-Sudur** | Religious grants (**madad-i-maash / suyurghal**), charities, scholars | Patronage head |
| **Qazi-ul-Quzat** (+ **Muhtasib**) | Chief justice of qazi courts; censor of morals (muhtasibs prominent under Aurangzeb) | Sharia for Muslims; Hindu personal law applied through pandits |

## 3. Mansabdari and jagirdari — the steel frame of the empire ⭐⭐

### 3.1 Mansabdari (instituted by Akbar, c. 1571–77)
- Every officer holds a **mansab (rank)** — a single gradation covering civil & military service; ranks for nobles ran **10 to 5,000** (7,000 for premier nobles like Man Singh; higher only for princes).
- **Dual rank from the 1590s:** **zat** (personal standing & pay) + **sawar** (cavalry to be maintained). Three classes: 1st — sawar = zat; 2nd — sawar ≥ ½ zat; 3rd — sawar < ½ zat.
- Verification machinery revived from Alauddin Khalji/Sher Shah: **dagh** (branding of horses) and **chehra** (descriptive roll of troopers).
- Refinements: **du-aspa sih-aspa** (double/triple sawar obligation for favoured nobles) — **Jahangir**; **month-scale (mahana) pay ratios** — Shah Jahan (a jagir "worth 6 months" pays half the schedule).
- Pay in cash (**naqdi**) or by **jagir**; on death, property subject to **escheat (zabt)** pending settlement of state dues — kept the nobility service-dependent, not hereditary-propertied.

### 3.2 Jagirdari
- **Jagir = assignment of land REVENUE, not land ownership** ⭐ (the jama assessed in **dams**); collection through the jagirdar's agents alongside imperial officials.
- Types: **tankha** (transferable pay-jagir, shifted every 3–4 years), **watan** (hereditary homeland of rajas/zamindars — the Rajput integration device), **mashrut** (conditional), **altamgha** (permanent grant, from Jahangir).
- **Jagirdari crisis (late 17th c.):** Deccan wars swelled the nobility faster than paying jagirs (**bejagiri**) → over-extraction, factionalism → **Satish Chandra's** classic explanation of Mughal decline; Bernier's letter (to Colbert) blamed absence of private property — Irfan Habib reads it instead as an **agrarian crisis of over-taxation**.
- **Trap to master:** **zabt/escheat** (confiscation on death) ≠ **zabti** (the cash-assessment revenue system, §5).

## 4. Provincial and local ladder ⭐

Akbar organised the empire into **12 subas (1580)** → 15 by his death → **~20 under Aurangzeb** (with the Deccan subas).

| Tier | Executive | Revenue | Others |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Suba** | **Subadar (nazim/sipahsalar)** — order, army, criminal justice | **Provincial diwan — appointed independently, reports to the imperial diwan** ⭐ (the famous **dual check** on governors) | Bakhshi, sadr, qazi, **waqia-navis** (news-writer), kotwal in cities |
| **Sarkar** (district) | **Faujdar** — military-police chief (jurisdictions didn't always coincide with sarkar borders) | **Amalguzar (amil)** — collection | Khazandar (treasurer), bitikchi (accountant) |
| **Pargana** | **Shiqqdar** | **Amil/amin** (assessment), **fotadar** (treasurer) | **Qanungo — hereditary keeper of revenue records** ⭐; karkuns (clerks) |
| **Village** | **Muqaddam** (headman) | **Patwari** (village accountant) ⭐ | Village largely self-governing (panchayat) |
| **City** | **Kotwal** — police, watch & ward, curfew, markets/weights, registration of arrivals — the municipal magistrate | | Mir bahri (ports/ferries) |

**Intelligence & communication:** waqia-navis & sawanih-nigar (open/secret news-writers), **harkaras** (couriers-spies), **dak chowkis** relay posts — the emperor read the provinces daily.

## 5. Land revenue — the empire's bloodstream ⭐⭐

- Assessment methods in use: **batai/ghalla-bakhshi** (physical crop division), **kankut** (estimate on standing crop), and **zabti** — measurement + **fixed cash rates per crop per locality**.
- **Ain-i-Dahsala (1580), Raja Todar Mal** ⭐: ten-year average of produce & prices → cash revenue schedules (**dasturs**) by crop and locality; state demand about **one-third** of average produce.
- Land classes by continuity of cultivation: **polaj** (cultivated annually) — **parauti** (briefly fallow) — **chachar** (fallow 3–4 years) — **banjar** (fallow 5+ years; concessional rates to bring it back under plough).
- Measurement: **ilahi gaz** (yard) and the **jarib** — bamboo chain joined with iron rings (resists stretching fraud).
- Peasant-facing features: **taccavi** (improvement/distress loans), remissions in famine, direct pattas; the **patwari–qanungo written record** is the system's real legacy.
- Revenue-free grants: **madad-i-maash/suyurghal** (to scholars, pious men) — administered by the sadr.

## 6. Army, justice, household

- **Army:** mansabdar contingents (tabinan) + **ahadis** ("gentlemen troopers" directly recruited, better paid) + **dakhili** (state-paid supplements); strong artillery (topkhana); **navy negligible** — the blind spot Europeans exploited.
- **Justice:** qazi courts (sharia, personal law via pandits for Hindus); secular/revenue disputes before diwans; faujdar & kotwal handle criminal order; emperor as final appeal.
- **Karkhanas:** state workshops (arms, textiles, mint inputs) under the khan-i-saman — proto-departmental "public sector" of court needs.

## 7. Deccan & AP tie-in (bridge to the history series)

- Aurangzeb annexed **Bijapur (1686)** and **Golconda (1687)** → the Telugu country entered the Mughal frame as part of the **Deccan subas**; see history series [File 09 — Bahmani & Qutb Shahi](../APPSC-Notes/09-Bahmani-and-Qutb-Shahi.md).
- 1724: **Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah**, Mughal viceroy of the Deccan, became effectively independent → the **Hyderabad state** within which Telangana lived until 1948 — Mughal provincial administration (suba diwan, faujdars, Persian records) persisting there into the 20th century.

## 8. Assessment and legacy

**Strengths:** uniform currency (rupaya–dam) and weights; graded unified service (mansab); cash-nexus revenue with written village records; provincial dual check; information system. **Weaknesses:** everything personal to the emperor; succession anarchy; nobility-jagir arithmetic fragile; navy & technology neglected; **no popular participation** — extraction, however sophisticated, remained the point (Habib).

**Continuities into British/modern India ⭐ (Mains gold):**
1. **Diwani** — the very office the East India Company acquired in **1765** to become a state (File 04).
2. **Patwari, qanungo, dastur, jama, mahal** — the entire revenue vocabulary and village-record system passed into British settlements and survives in tehsil/mandal offices today.
3. **District-tier logic:** faujdar + amalguzar prefigure the **Collector-Magistrate** pairing of functions in one district head.
4. **Kotwal** → city police chief image (the word survived into modern police usage).
5. Subadar-vs-diwan **separation of executive and finance** → the exchequer-control instinct of later Indian administration.
6. Persian **secretariat culture** — written orders (farmans), office manuals (dastur-ul-amal), news-diaries → files, gazettes and the "note file" habit.

## 9. Prelims nuggets

1. **Ain-i-Akbari — Abul Fazl** — administrative statistics/gazetteer of Akbar's empire.
2. Vakil made ornamental after **Bairam Khan**; working finance head = **diwan-i-kul**.
3. **Mir bakhshi** — paymaster + mansab recommendations + posts **waqia-navis** (not army commander — trap).
4. **Sadr-us-sudur** — madad-i-maash grants; **muhtasib** — censor (Aurangzeb).
5. Mansab: **zat = personal rank, sawar = cavalry**; classes by sawar:zat ratio; **du-aspa sih-aspa — Jahangir**; month-scales — Shah Jahan.
6. **Dagh & chehra** revived from **Alauddin Khalji** practice.
7. **Jagir = revenue assignment, not ownership**; watan jagir = Rajput homelands; **zabt (escheat) ≠ zabti (cash assessment)**.
8. Subas: **12 in 1580** → ~20 under Aurangzeb; provincial **diwan independent of subadar**.
9. Ladder matches: **faujdar–sarkar; shiqqdar–pargana; muqaddam+patwari–village; kotwal–city; qanungo–records**.
10. **Dahsala 1580 — Todar Mal**; land classes **polaj–parauti–chachar–banjar**; demand ≈ 1/3; **jarib** chain; **ilahi gaz**.
11. **Taccavi = loans to peasants**; dasturs = cash-rate schedules.
12. **Ahadi** = directly recruited trooper; **Fatawa-i-Alamgiri** under Aurangzeb.
13. **Jharokha darshan abolished by Aurangzeb**; chain of justice — **Jahangir**.
14. Golconda annexed **1687**; **Asaf Jah's Hyderabad, 1724** — Mughal administration's Deccan afterlife.
15. Sher Shah: **rupiya, patta–qabuliyat, GT Road, sarais** — Akbar's template.

## 10. Mains angles

1. **"Mansabdari–jagirdari was at once the strength and the solvent of the Mughal empire."** Integration of a composite nobility + graded service + escheat vs bejagiri crisis, over-extraction, factionalism (Satish Chandra, Habib) → decline argument.
2. **"Todar Mal's dahsala was the most consequential administrative reform of medieval India."** Method (10-yr averages, dasturs, cash) → effects (predictability, monetisation, records) → afterlife (British settlements leaned on patwari-qanungo data; the vocabulary survives).
3. **"The Mughal provincial system institutionalised checks and balances."** Subadar vs diwan; bakhshi & waqia-navis as parallel eyes; transfers & escheat — compare with modern separation of executive & accounts.
4. **Assess the Mughal contribution to the evolution of Indian administration.** Use §8 continuities; balance with its extractive, non-participatory character; close: the British inherited a working revenue state, not a vacuum.
