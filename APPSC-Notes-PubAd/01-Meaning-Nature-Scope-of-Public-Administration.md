# 01 · Meaning, Nature and Scope of Public Administration

## 0. Why this topic matters

Every governance question in the paper ultimately rests on this unit: what administration *is*, what it *covers*, and how the Indian administrative system was assembled layer by layer (Mauryan → Sultanate → Mughal → British → constitutional). Prelims mines the definitions and thinker–book pairs; Mains expects you to move from definition → evolution → Indian application in one arc.

---

## 1. Meaning

- **Etymology:** Latin *ad* + *ministrare* = **"to serve", "to care for"** — administration is fundamentally *service* and *management of affairs*.
- Two everyday senses: (a) **as an activity** — the actual work of running government; (b) **as a discipline** — the academic study of that activity (born 1887).
- "Public" administration = administration **in the governmental setting** — tax-funded, law-bound, publicly accountable.

### 1.1 Definitions to quote (thinker → line → catch-phrase)

| Thinker | Definition / line | Remember as |
|---|---|---|
| **Woodrow Wilson** (1887, *"The Study of Administration"*, Political Science Quarterly) | "Public administration is detailed and systematic execution of public law"; administration is "**government in action**"; "it is getting harder to run a constitution than to frame one" | **Founder of the discipline** ⭐ |
| **L. D. White** (*Introduction to the Study of Public Administration*, **1926 — first textbook**) | "All those operations having for their purpose the **fulfilment or enforcement of public policy**" | Integral view |
| **Luther Gulick** | "Administration has to do with **getting things done**; with the accomplishment of defined objectives"; PA concerns "primarily the **executive branch**" | POSDCORB man |
| **Herbert Simon** (*Administrative Behavior*, 1947) | "The activities of the **executive branches** of national, state and local governments" | Narrow/managerial view |
| **Dwight Waldo** | "The **art and science of management** as applied to the affairs of state" | Art *and* science |
| **John Pfiffner** | "Getting the work of government done by **coordinating the efforts of people** so that they can work together" | Coordination |
| **F. A. & L. G. Nigro** | Cooperative group effort in a public setting; covers **all three branches**; has an important role in **policy formulation**; differs from private administration | Widest modern definition |
| **F. J. Goodnow** (*Politics and Administration*, 1900) | "Politics has to do with the **expression** of the state will; administration with the **execution** of that will" | Dichotomy classic |

### 1.2 Integral vs managerial view (standard 2-marker / MCQ)

| | **Integral view** | **Managerial view** |
|---|---|---|
| Administration is | **Sum-total of ALL activities** — manual, clerical, technical, managerial — undertaken to achieve the objective | Only the work of **managers at the top** — planning, organising, commanding, coordinating, controlling |
| Who counts as "administration" | From attender to Chief Secretary | Only those who direct: the managerial elite |
| Champions | **L. D. White** (and Henri Fayol's broad usage) | **Simon, Gulick, Ordway Tead** |
| Implication | Administration differs from agency to agency (subject-matter matters) | Administration is a **universal technique**, same everywhere ("management is management") |

## 2. Nature

- **Art or science?** Science-claim: observable regularities, principles, comparative method (the 1927–37 "principles" school; Simon demanded a genuine behavioural science). Art-claim: it handles **human beings and values**, needs judgement, leadership, tact. Settled exam line: **PA is both — a "social science" in progress and an art in practice** (Waldo's "art and science of management").
- **Politics–administration dichotomy:** Wilson ("administration lies outside the proper sphere of politics") and Goodnow separated *deciding* from *executing*. Critique — policy and administration interpenetrate: administrators **advise, draft, interpret and effectively make policy** (delegated legislation, discretion). Post-1945 consensus (Appleby): **"administration is politics"** — PA is a political process. Indian echo: the "committed bureaucracy" debate (1970s) vs the constitutional ideal of a **politically neutral, professionally committed** civil service.
- **Anonymous, permanent, professional:** in parliamentary systems the minister answers publicly; the official acts anonymously under **ministerial responsibility** — a nature-point that doubles as a public-vs-private difference.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 As an activity
Everything the modern state does through its agencies: maintaining order, defence, revenue, regulation, **welfare and development services** (education, health, PDS), public enterprise, disaster response. In the welfare state the citizen meets the state "**from cradle to grave**" through administrators.

### 3.2 As a discipline — three classic formulations

1. **POSDCORB view — Luther Gulick** (1937, *Papers on the Science of Administration*, ed. Gulick & **Urwick**) ⭐
   - **P**lanning, **O**rganising, **S**taffing, **D**irecting, **CO**-ordinating, **R**eporting, **B**udgeting.
   - Strength: captures common managerial techniques. Weakness: **technique-only**; ignores subject-matter, values, the public.
2. **Subject-matter view — Lewis Meriam's scissors:** "Public administration is an instrument with **two blades like a pair of scissors** — one blade is knowledge of the field covered by POSDCORB, the other is **knowledge of the subject-matter** in which these techniques are applied." Both blades must cut together (a health secretary needs management *and* public health).
   - **Marshall Dimock:** administration is concerned with "**the WHAT and the HOW of government**" — the *what* (subject-matter/technical knowledge), the *how* (management technique).
3. **Harvey Walker's two-fold scope:** **administrative theory** (organisation, personnel, finance, procedures) + **applied administration** (functional areas — police, education, agriculture...).

### 3.3 Public vs private administration ⭐ (perennial Mains 10-marker)

**Those who see similarity:** **Henri Fayol** — "we are no longer confronted with several administrative sciences but with **one** which can be applied equally well to public and to private affairs"; Urwick, Follett — common techniques (planning, coordination, leadership).

**Those who insist on difference:**
- **Paul Appleby** (*Big Democracy*, 1945; *Policy and Administration*, 1949): "**Government is different**" — because of (i) breadth, scope and impact, (ii) **public accountability**, (iii) its **political character**.
- **Sir Josiah Stamp** — four differentiating principles: **uniformity** (equal treatment), **external financial control** (legislature controls the purse), **ministerial responsibility**, **marginal return** (services not judged by profit).
- **Herbert Simon** — the popular images: public admin seen as "bureaucratic, red-tape-bound"; business admin as "businesslike, efficient" (he noted these are caricatures).

| Dimension | Public administration | Private administration |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | **Service & public interest** | Profit |
| Framework | Law, rules, constitutionality (*intra vires*) | Contract & market; wide discretion |
| Accountability | Legislature, courts, CAG, media, RTI — the **public gaze** | Shareholders/board |
| Treatment | **Uniformity & equality** before rules | Can discriminate between customers |
| Scope/impact | Society-wide, often **monopoly** (police, currency) | Segmental, competitive |
| Efficiency test | Multiple: legality, equity, responsiveness, economy | Single bottom line |

Balanced close: the NPM era **borrowed private techniques** (targets, outsourcing, citizen-as-customer) but the **publicness** — accountability, equity, constitutional values — remains irreducible.

## 4. Evolution of the discipline (from Wilson to governance)

**Birth:** **Woodrow Wilson, 1887** — PA as self-conscious study; he called it "the latest fruit of that study of the science of politics which began some **2,200 years ago**" (i.e., with Plato/Aristotle/Kautilya's world).

### 4.1 Nicholas Henry's five paradigms ⭐ (standard MCQ table)

| Paradigm | Period | Theme | Landmark works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Politics–administration dichotomy** | 1900–1926 | PA distinct from politics; locus = government bureaucracy | **Goodnow, *Politics and Administration* (1900)**; **White (1926)** |
| 2. **Principles of administration** | 1927–1937 | Universal, discoverable principles; "high noon of orthodoxy" (1937) | **Willoughby, *Principles of Public Administration* (1927)**; Fayol; Mooney & Reiley; **Gulick & Urwick (1937)** |
| 3. Era of **challenge** (then PA as **political science**) | 1938/1947–1970 | Principles attacked as "**proverbs**"; dichotomy rejected; PA loses identity, returns under political science | **Barnard, *Functions of the Executive* (1938)**; **Simon, "The Proverbs of Administration" (1946) & *Administrative Behavior* (1947)**; **Waldo, *The Administrative State* (1948)**; Hawthorne studies → **Elton Mayo's human relations** |
| 4. PA as **administrative science/management** | 1956–1970 | Generic management: organisation theory, behavioural science | Journals of management science; comparative admin (Riggs) |
| 5. **PA as Public Administration** | 1970– | Separate identity; policy science orientation; NASPAA schools | Public policy turn (Lasswell) |

### 4.2 After 1970 — the currents you must name-drop

| Current | Core idea | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| **Development Administration** | Administration as engine of nation-building in new states; goal- and change-oriented | **Edward Weidner**; **Fred Riggs** — ecological approach, **prismatic-sala model** |
| **New Public Administration (NPA)** | Anti-technocratic turn: **relevance, values, social equity, change** | **Minnowbrook I (1968)**, convened under **Dwight Waldo**; **Frank Marini** (ed., *Toward a New Public Administration*, 1971); **George Frederickson** (social equity). Minnowbrook II (1988), III (2008) |
| **New Public Management (NPM)** | Managerialism: mission not rules, **outputs not inputs**, competition, agencification, citizen-as-customer, "**steering not rowing**" | **Christopher Hood, "A Public Management for All Seasons?" (1991 — coined NPM)**; **Osborne & Gaebler, *Reinventing Government* (1992)**; UK Next Steps (1988) |
| **Good Governance** | Quality of the governing process itself | **World Bank** (1989 Africa report — "crisis of governance"; ***Governance and Development*, 1992**); UN's 8 characteristics (participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus, equity, effectiveness, accountability) |
| **New Public Service (NPS)** | "**Serve citizens, not customers**; seek the public interest" — democratic citizenship over managerialism | **Denhardt & Denhardt (2000)** |
| **Digital-Era Governance / e-governance** | Reintegration + needs-based holism + digitisation; SMART government (Simple, Moral, Accountable, Responsive, Transparent) | **Patrick Dunleavy (2006)**; India: NeGP 2006, Digital India 2015 |

### 4.3 The discipline and its reform machinery in India

- Ancient pedigree: **Kautilya's Arthashastra** — the world's most complete pre-modern manual of administration (File 02).
- **Indian Institute of Public Administration (IIPA), 1954** — set up on **Paul H. Appleby's** recommendation; **Jawaharlal Nehru its first President**.
- **Appleby reports:** *Public Administration in India — Report of a Survey* (**1953**; famously rated India "among the dozen or so best administered" states while damning its procedures) and *Re-examination of India's Administrative System* (**1956**, on delegation and speed).
- **First ARC (1966)** — chaired by **Morarji Desai**, then **K. Hanumanthaiah**; ~20 reports; recommended **Lokpal & Lokayukta (1966)** ⭐ and led to the **Department of Personnel (1970 → DoPT)**.
- **Second ARC (2005)** — **M. Veerappa Moily**; 15 reports incl. *Right to Information — Master Key to Good Governance* (1st), *Unlocking Human Capital* (2nd), *Refurbishing of Personnel Administration* (10th), *Citizen Centric Administration* (12th).
- Related: Sarkaria Commission (1983, Centre–State), Punchhi Commission (2007), **Mission Karmayogi (2020)** — national civil-service capacity building.
- **AP tie-in:** the **Centre for Good Governance, Hyderabad (2001)** — an early Indian good-governance institution born in undivided AP; AP's **Spandana** grievance-redressal and **village/ward secretariats (2019)** are current-affairs hooks for this unit.

## 5. Significance in the modern state (quotes bank)

- Welfare + development + regulatory state ⇒ administration is the **delivery arm of constitutional promises** (DPSPs travel to the citizen through an office).
- **Charles A. Beard:** "There is no subject more important than administration. The future of civilised government, and even, I think, of civilisation itself, rests upon our ability to develop a science and a philosophy and a practice of administration…"
- **W. B. Donham** (quoted by L. D. White): "If our civilisation fails, it will be mainly because of a breakdown of administration."
- **Wilson:** "It is getting harder to run a constitution than to frame one."
- Live illustration for Mains: pandemic management (2020–21) — administration as society's **crisis nervous-system**.

## 6. "Evolution in India" — the 30-second bridge (to Files 02–04)

| Layer | Signature institutions | What survived |
|---|---|---|
| **Mauryan/Kautilyan** (File 02) | Saptanga state, adhyakshas (departments), samaharta (revenue), espionage, dharmasthiya courts | Departmentalisation, revenue primacy, vigilance idea, welfare rhetoric of the state |
| Classical interlude | **Chola village assemblies (Uttaramerur inscriptions)** — India's classic local self-government exhibit (see history series, [File 01](../APPSC-Notes/01-South-Indian-Dynasties.md)) | Panchayat imagination |
| **Delhi Sultanate** (history series, [File 02](../APPSC-Notes/02-Delhi-Sultanate.md)) | Iqta assignments, diwan-i-wizarat, market control | Revenue-assignment logic the Mughals refined |
| **Mughal** (File 03) | Mansabdari–jagirdari, diwan/mir bakhshi, suba–sarkar–pargana, **patwari–qanungo records**, Todar Mal's dahsala | Revenue vocabulary & village records, district-tier offices, secretariat habits |
| **British** (File 04) | ICS "steel frame", **District Collector**, Police Act 1861, codified law & courts, budget & audit, Ripon's local self-government | Almost the whole skeleton of today's administration |
| **Constitutional** | All-India Services (Art. 312), UPSC/State PSCs, CAG, Panchayati Raj (73rd/74th) | The inherited frame, repurposed for welfare & democracy |

## 8. Mains angles

1. **"Public administration is government in action." Explain its meaning, nature and scope.** Skeleton: etymology → Wilson/White definitions → integral vs managerial → POSDCORB + Meriam's scissors → welfare-state significance → close with Indian delivery examples (PDS, MGNREGS, Spandana).
2. **"The politics–administration dichotomy is neither possible nor desirable." Discuss.** Wilson/Goodnow origins → Appleby's demolition → Indian reality (neutral civil service vs committed-bureaucracy episode; policy role of secretaries) → constitutional balance.
3. **Public and private administration: "similar in techniques, different in soul."** Fayol vs Appleby/Stamp table → NPM borrowings → irreducible publicness (accountability, equity).
4. **Trace the evolution of public administration as a discipline. Where does it stand in the governance era?** Henry's five paradigms → NPA → NPM → good governance/NPS/digital era → India's ARC-II "citizen-centric" turn.
