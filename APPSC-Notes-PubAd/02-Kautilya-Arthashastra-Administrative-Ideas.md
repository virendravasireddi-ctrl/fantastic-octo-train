## 0. Quick facts — the text

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Author (tradition) | **Kautilya = Chanakya = Vishnugupta**, minister of **Chandragupta Maurya** (c. 4th century BCE); modern scholarship (Trautmann, Olivelle) sees a **composite text finalised in the early centuries CE** — flag this in Mains |
| Title | *Artha* = material well-being/wealth; Arthashastra = "science of polity and political economy" |
| Structure | **15 books (adhikaranas), 150 chapters, ~6,000 verses**; Book I — king & training; **Book II (Adhyakshapracara) — the administrative core**; Books III–IV — law & justice; Book V — service rules & salaries; Books VI–VII — mandala & foreign policy |
| Rediscovery | Manuscript surfaced with **R. Shamasastry** (Mysore Oriental Library) — announced 1905, **text published 1909, English translation 1915** ⭐; critical edition by **R. P. Kangle (1960–65)** |
| Fame abroad | **Max Weber**: compared with the Arthashastra, "Machiavelli's *Prince* is **harmless**" — i.e., Kautilya is the more radical realist |

**Corroboration box:** **Megasthenes' Indica** (fragments in Strabo/Arrian/Diodorus) describes Pataliputra run by **six boards of five members** (crafts, foreigners, births-deaths, trade, manufactures, tithes) — Mauryan practice matching the Arthashastra's urban machinery. **Ashoka added** the dhamma-mahamattas, rajukas (judicial officers) and pradesikas.

**AP tie-in:** Ashoka's **Rock Edict XIII counts the Andhras within the imperial fold**; **Pliny (citing Megasthenes)** credits the "Andarae" with **30 walled towns** and a large army — the Arthashastra's administrative world touched the Krishna delta (Bhattiprolu, Dhanyakataka/Amaravati).

## 1. The state and the king

### 1.1 Saptanga theory ⭐ (the seven limbs / prakritis)

| Limb | Meaning | Modern echo |
|---|---|---|
| **Swami** | The king — head, but bound by dharma & danda | Head of state/executive |
| **Amatya** | Ministers & the entire officialdom | Council of ministers + civil service |
| **Janapada** | Territory **with population** | Territory + people |
| **Durga** | Fortified capital | Security infrastructure |
| **Kosha** | Treasury | Finance ("all undertakings depend upon finance — **foremost attention shall be paid to the treasury**") |
| **Danda / Bala** | Army & coercive power | Defence, police, sanctions |
| **Mitra** | Ally | Foreign relations |

Limbs are **interdependent** — an organic, almost systems-theory view of the state.

### 1.2 Kingship = duty, not enjoyment

- The ideal is the **rajarshi** — the sage-king: disciplined senses, trained in the four vidyas (anvikshiki-philosophy, trayi-Vedas, varta-economics, **dandaniti-statecraft**).
- ⭐ THE quote (Book I.19): "**In the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare his welfare.** The king shall not consider as good what pleases himself, but whatever pleases his subjects." (*Prajasukhe sukham rajnah…*) — the classical Indian welfare-state text.
- **Yogakshema** (acquisition + secure enjoyment of well-being) is the state's purpose.
- The king's **timetable**: day and night each divided into **eight 90-minute slots** — audiences, accounts, correspondence, inspection; "when in the court, he shall **never make petitioners wait at the door**" → the ancestral text for grievance redressal (AP's **Spandana** makes a neat modern contrast in Mains).

## 2. Council and top offices

- **Mantrins (inner councillors)** + a larger **Mantriparishad**; consultation is mandatory before major undertakings, secrecy paramount ("a secret known to more than four ears breaks"). On size Kautilya is flexible — "**as the need dictates**" (against Manu's fixed 12, Brihaspati's 16, Ushanas' 20) — a very managerial touch.
- Recruitment of amatyas by graded **qualifications (amatya-sampat)** and integrity tests (§5.1 below).
- **The eighteen tirthas (high offices)** — the classical "senior secretariat". Key ones:

| Tirtha | Function |
|---|---|
| **Mantrin** / **Purohita** / **Senapati** / **Yuvaraja** | Councillor / high priest / commander / heir |
| **Samaharta** ⭐ | **Collector-general of revenue** — also the kingdom's chief record-and-survey officer |
| **Sannidhata** ⭐ | **Chief treasurer** — custodian of treasury, stores, granaries |
| **Dauvarika / Antarvamshika** | Palace gate & palace guards |
| **Prashastri / Pradeshtri** | Camp/records; **magistrates** (criminal justice) |
| **Nayaka / Dandapala** | Field commander / head of army logistics-police |
| **Durgapala / Antapala / Atavipala** | Forts / frontiers / forests |
| **Paura-vyavaharika** | **City judge** |
| **Karmantika** | Director of works/manufactories |
| **Mantriparishad-adhyaksha** | Secretary to the council |

## 3. Departmental machinery — the adhyakshas (Book II) ⭐

Around **30 superintendents (adhyakshas)** run specialised departments — the most detailed departmentalisation anywhere in the ancient world. Representative list (office → charge):

| Adhyaksha | Department |
|---|---|
| **Koshadhyaksha** | Treasury & precious objects |
| **Akaradhyaksha** | **Mines** ("the treasury is born of the mines") |
| **Lavanadhyaksha / Suvarnadhyaksha** | Salt / gold & mint workshop |
| **Lakshanadhyaksha** | **Mint** (coinage) |
| **Akshapataladhyaksha** ⭐ | **Records-cum-audit office** — accounts of every department, annual audit |
| **Sitadhyaksha** | Crown agriculture |
| **Kosthagaradhyaksha** | Granaries/stores |
| **Panyadhyaksha / Samsthadhyaksha** | State trading / markets |
| **Sulkadhyaksha** | **Customs & tolls** |
| **Pautavadhyaksha** ⭐ | **Weights & measures** (consumer protection; profit caps — about 5% on local, 10% on foreign goods; fines for adulteration) |
| **Navadhyaksha** | Shipping, ports, ferries |
| **Vivitadhyaksha** | Pastures |
| **Ayudhagaradhyaksha** | Armoury |
| **Ashva- / Hasti- / Ratha- / Patty-adhyaksha** | Horse / elephant / chariot / infantry |
| **Suradhyaksha / Dyutadhyaksha / Ganikadhyaksha** | Liquor / gambling / courtesans (regulate & tax the vices, don't pretend they vanish) |
| **Mudradhyaksha** | **Passports/sealed passes** for movement |

Design principles visible: **specialisation, hierarchy, written records, audit, fixed pay** — Weberian bureaucracy two millennia early (Mains line).

## 4. Revenue and treasury

- Twin pillars: **Samaharta** (assessment & collection) and **Sannidhata** (custody & disbursement) — assessment separated from custody: an internal check.
- **Seven sources of income (ayasharira):** **durga** (city), **rashtra** (countryside), **khani** (mines), **setu** (irrigation & orchards), **vana** (forests), **vraja** (herds), **vanikpatha** (trade routes).
- Land revenue: traditionally **shadbhaga — one-sixth** (bhaga); plus bali, kara (levies), **shulka** (customs), water rates for state-irrigated land; monopolies (mines, salt); **pranaya** — emergency levy in distress (only from the prosperous, meant to be exceptional).
- Budget-mindedness: departments submit **daily accounts**; the akshapatala runs an **annual audit**; deficits traced to officer negligence are recovered from the officer.

## 5. Personnel administration & anti-corruption ⭐⭐ (the most quotable section)

1. **Merit + integrity testing:** candidates for amatya posts screened by the **four upadhas (secret tests)** — **dharmopadha** (piety), **arthopadha** (money), **kamopadha** (lust), **bhayopadha** (fear). Only those who pass all four handle the most sensitive charges.
2. **Graded, cash salaries** (Book V.3): from **48,000 panas/year** (mantrin, purohita, senapati) down to **60 panas** — a full pay-scale schedule; pensions/maintenance for families of officers who die in service.
3. **The 40 ways of embezzlement (haranopayas)** catalogued (Book II.8) — advance collection shown late, income shown as expenditure, price manipulation… an ancient audit manual.
4. THE two similes (Book II.9–10):
   - "Just as it is impossible **not to taste honey or poison placed at the tip of the tongue**, so it is impossible for a government servant not to eat up at least a bit of the king's revenue."
   - "**As fish moving under water** cannot be known when drinking water, so government servants cannot be found out while taking money for themselves."
5. **Remedies:** surprise inspections; **informants (suchakas) rewarded** with a share of the sum recovered; **frequent transfer/rotation of officers** ("heads of departments shall be transferred, so they cannot digest what they have eaten"); confiscation + graded fines + public disgrace for the corrupt; equally, **rewards and promotion** for honest performance.
6. Modern mapping for Mains: upadhas → integrity vetting; haranopayas → Prevention of Corruption Act offences; suchakas → whistle-blower protection; rotation → transfer policies; akshapatala → **CAG**.

## 6. Espionage — the state's eyes

- **Gudhapurushas** (secret agents) of two kinds:
  - **Samstha (stationary):** kapatika (disguised student), udasthita (apostate monk), grihapatika (householder), vaidehaka (merchant), **tapasa** (fake ascetic).
  - **Sanchara (roving):** satri, **tikshna** (desperado), **rasada** (poisoner), **bhikshuki/parivrajika** (wandering nun — access to households).
- Targets: **officials' integrity**, public opinion, foreign courts (spies under cover of envoys), criminals.
- Method discipline: a report is acted on only when **three independent sources concur** — an ancient corroboration rule (→ modern IB/vigilance parallel).

## 7. Law and justice

- Two parallel court systems ⭐:
  - **Dharmasthiya** — **civil** courts (judges = *dharmasthas*): contracts, marriage, inheritance, property, debts.
  - **Kantakasodhana** — literally "**removal of thorns**": **criminal/regulatory** courts (magistrates = *pradeshtris*): official oppression, adulteration, short-weights, theft, violence — i.e., protecting the public *including from the administration itself*.
- **Four feet of law** (order of reliance): **dharma** (sacred norm), **vyavahara** (evidence/contract), **charitra** (custom), **rajasasana** (royal edict) — with the king's edict prevailing in conflict: statute law asserting itself over custom.
- **Danda** (sanction) is graded and proportionate — "a king severe with the rod is oppressive; one mild with the rod is despised; **justly used, danda secures dharma, artha and kama**."

## 8. Local and municipal administration

### 8.1 Countryside (janapada)
- Settlement hierarchy for administration: **sangrahana** (centre for 10 villages) → **karvatika** (200) → **dronamukha** (400) → **sthaniya** (800 villages).
- Field officials: **gopa** — records for **5–10 villages** (boundaries, fields, houses, census of people, cattle, taxes — the **ancestor of the village accountant/patwari/karanam** ⭐); **sthanika** — a quarter of the janapada; both under the **samaharta**; village headman (**gramika**) remains a community figure.

### 8.2 The city (Book II.36)
- **Nagaraka** (city superintendent) — the ancient **municipal commissioner**: assisted by gopas (each covering 10–40 households) and sthanikas (quarters).
- Functions: census & movement register (travellers must be reported by innkeepers), **fire prevention** (cooking hours, water pots at doors), **sanitation with graded fines for littering**, curfew, prisons (with periodic releases).

---

## 9. Defence and foreign policy (know the labels)

- **Shadgunya** — six measures: sandhi (peace), vigraha (war), asana (neutrality), yana (march), samshraya (shelter), dvaidhibhava (dual policy).
- **Mandala theory** — the circle of states around the **vijigishu** (would-be conqueror): neighbour = natural enemy (ari), neighbour's neighbour = natural friend (mitra)…
- **Four upayas:** sama (conciliation), dana (gifts), bheda (division), danda (force).
- Three conquerors: **dharma-vijayin** (righteous — submission suffices), **lobha-vijayin** (greedy — wants wealth), **asura-vijayin** (demonic — annexes everything).

## 10. Assessment — police state or welfare state?

- **Realist/coercive face:** pervasive espionage, harsh punishments, state monopolies, manipulation — Weber's "radical Machiavellianism".
- **Welfare face:** the happiness dictum; state duty in **famine and calamity relief** (seed, food, works); support for the helpless (orphans, aged, disabled, pregnant women — even for **female slaves**, protections); consumer protection; irrigation (setu) as state investment.
- Balanced Mains verdict: a **centralised, law-bound, welfare-oriented but surveillance-heavy** state — administration treated as an autonomous science, with the **treasury and personnel integrity as its twin obsessions**. Continuity claim: departments, records, audit, revenue primacy and vigilance run from the Arthashastra through Mughal and British practice into the present (File 05 master table).

## 11. Prelims nuggets

1. Arthashastra: **15 books / 150 chapters / ~6,000 verses**; Book II = administration.
2. **Shamasastry** — published **1909**, translated **1915**; critical edition **Kangle**.
3. Saptanga order: **swami, amatya, janapada, durga, kosha, danda, mitra**.
4. "In the happiness of his subjects…" — Book **I.19**.
5. **Samaharta = collector-general; Sannidhata = treasurer** (classic match pair).
6. **Upadhas = 4** (dharma, artha, kama, bhaya); **embezzlement ways = 40**; salaries **48,000 → 60 panas**.
7. Spies: **samstha (5 types, stationary) vs sanchara (roving)**; the fish-in-water simile = corrupt officials.
8. **Dharmasthiya = civil; Kantakasodhana = criminal** ("removal of thorns").
9. Four feet of law: **dharma, vyavahara, charitra, rajasasana**.
10. **Gopa** = record-keeper of 5–10 villages; **Nagaraka** = city chief.
11. Seven revenue heads include **setu = irrigation** (not bridges — trap).
12. Shadgunya (6 foreign-policy measures) ≠ upayas (4 methods) — don't mix.
13. Megasthenes: Pataliputra's **six boards of five**; Weber's "Machiavelli is harmless" line.
14. Ashoka's Rock Edict XIII mentions the **Andhras**; Pliny — "Andarae" with **30 walled towns**.

## 12. Mains angles

1. **"The Arthashastra anticipates modern public administration."** Evidence: departmentalisation (adhyakshas), merit & integrity testing (upadhas), pay-scales, records & audit (akshapatala), anti-corruption doctrine (40 ways, transfers, informers), municipal administration (nagaraka) → map each to a modern institution (departments, UPSC vetting, Pay Commission, CAG, PC Act/CVC, municipal commissioner).
2. **"Kautilya's state: welfare state guarded by a police state."** Argue both faces (happiness dictum, calamity relief, consumer protection vs espionage, harsh danda) → verdict: welfare ends, surveillance means.
3. **"Kosha is the root of the state." Discuss Kautilya's revenue thought.** Seven ayasharira → assessment/custody separation → audit → pranaya's restraint → link to modern fiscal prudence.
4. **Compare Kautilya and Machiavelli.** Same realism; Kautilya broader (a full administrative code, welfare telos) vs Machiavelli's slim manual of power; quote Weber.
