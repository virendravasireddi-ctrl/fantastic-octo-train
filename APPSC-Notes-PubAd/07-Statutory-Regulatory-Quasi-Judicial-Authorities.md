# 07 — Statutory, Regulatory and Various Quasi-Judicial Authorities

**Syllabus item (first half):** *"Statutory, regulatory and various quasi-judicial authorities."*

---

## 1. The classification — get the vocabulary right first

| Type of body | Source of existence | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Constitutional body** | Created **by the Constitution itself** (an Article names it) | Election Commission (Art. 324), UPSC/**APPSC** (Art. 315), CAG (Art. 148), Finance Commission (Art. 280), Attorney-General (Art. 76), NCSC (338), NCST (338-A), **NCBC (338-B — 102nd Amendment, 2018)**, GST Council (279-A), State Election Commission (243-K) |
| **Statutory body** | Created by an **Act of Parliament/State legislature** | NHRC, CIC, CVC, Lokpal, NGT, NCW, NCPCR, NCM, RBI, SEBI, TRAI |
| **Regulatory body** | A statutory body whose *function* is to **regulate a sector** (licensing, tariff, standards, conduct) | RBI, SEBI, TRAI, IRDAI, PFRDA, CERC/**APERC**, CCI, FSSAI |
| **Quasi-judicial body/authority** | Any body (constitutional, statutory or executive) that **decides disputes / determines rights** like a court but is **not a court** | Tribunals (NGT, CAT, ITAT), consumer commissions, EC deciding defection-adjacent/symbol disputes, CIC, Lokayukta |
| **Executive (non-statutory) body** | Created by **executive resolution/order** only | **NITI Aayog (2015)**, erstwhile Planning Commission, CBI (Delhi Special Police Establishment Act gives partial statutory basis, but CBI as such is by resolution, 1963), earlier **CVC (1964 resolution → statutory 2003)** |

**Three MCQ traps:**
1. **CVC**: created by executive resolution in **1964 (Santhanam Committee)** → became **statutory by the CVC Act, 2003**.
2. **NCBC**: was statutory (1993 Act) → became **constitutional** by the **102nd Amendment (2018)**.
3. **NITI Aayog and CBI are neither constitutional nor statutory**.

**Overlap logic:** the categories are not exclusive — SEBI is statutory **and** regulatory **and** exercises quasi-judicial powers. Classify by *origin* (constitutional/statutory/executive) and separately by *function* (advisory/regulatory/quasi-judicial).

## 2. Statutory authorities — the ones APPSC actually asks

Learn each as **Act–year–composition–one function**.

### 2.1 Rights & accountability bodies

| Body | Act & year | Composition / headship | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NHRC** | Protection of Human Rights Act, **1993** (amended **2019**) | Chairperson: a **former CJI *or* SC judge** (post-2019); members incl. 3 with human-rights knowledge (one woman); deemed members: chairs of NCSC/NCST/NCW/NCM/NCBC/NCPCR & Chief Commissioner for PwDs | Term **3 years or 70** (2019 amendment); recommendations are **not binding**; cannot investigate matters older than 1 year; APSHRC is the AP counterpart (State HRC — chair: former HC Chief Justice/judge) |
| **CIC / SIC** | **RTI Act, 2005** | CIC + up to 10 ICs; appointed by President on committee of **PM + LoP + a Union minister** | RTI (Amendment) Act **2019**: term & salary now as **prescribed by Centre** (earlier 5 yrs & ECI-equivalent status); decisions binding; AP has its **State Information Commission** |
| **CVC** | CVC Act, **2003** | CVC + ≤2 VCs; committee: PM + Home Minister + LoP | Supervises CBI in corruption cases (under CVC Act & Lokpal Act); "designated agency" under Whistle Blowers framework; advisory jurisdiction — advice not binding |
| **Lokpal** | Lokpal & Lokayuktas Act, **2013** | Chair + ≤8 members (**50% judicial; 50% from SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women**); selection: PM, Speaker, LoP, CJI/nominee, eminent jurist | Jurisdiction: PM (with safeguards), ministers, MPs, Groups A–D, trusts/societies receiving big government/foreign funds; first Lokpal appointed **2019 (Justice P.C. | **NCW** | NCW Act, **1990** (body set up 1992) | Chairperson + 5 members | Reviews women-related laws; NCW recommendations advisory |
| **NCM** | NCM Act, **1992** | 7 members incl. chair | For notified minorities (6 communities incl. Jains, added 2014) |
| **NCPCR / SCPCR** | CPCR Act, **2005** | — | Monitors RTE, POCSO, JJ Act |
| **National Commission for Safai Karamcharis** | 1993 Act (lapsed 2004; continues as **non-statutory** now) | — | Classic trap: currently non-statutory |

### 2.2 Green & land

- **NGT — National Green Tribunal Act, 2010**: Chair (former SC judge/HC CJ) + judicial + **expert members**; principal bench Delhi, zonal benches (Chennai bench covers AP); applies **polluter pays, precautionary principle, sustainable development (Sec. 20)**; appeal to **Supreme Court within 90 days**; not bound by CPC/Evidence Act — natural justice.
- **CPCB/SPCB** — Water Act **1974** (CPCB), Air Act 1981 add-on; **AP Pollution Control Board** at state level.

### 2.3 Others frequently listed

**National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (2005)**, **NCBC (now constitutional)**, **Press Council of India (1978 Act)**, **National Commission for Homoeopathy/Indian System of Medicine (2020)**, **Bar Council of India (Advocates Act 1961)**, **UGC (UGC Act 1956)**, **AICTE (1987)**, **National Medical Commission (NMC Act 2019 — replaced MCI)**.

## 3. Regulatory authorities — the post-1991 "regulatory State"

### 3.1 Why regulators? (Mains framing)

After **LPG (1991)** the State withdrew from *producing* and moved to *regulating*: to correct **market failures** (monopoly, information asymmetry, externalities), protect consumers, ensure competition, and de-politicise tariff/licensing decisions. Result: the **"regulatory State"** — independent, expert, arm's-length bodies combining **legislative (regulation-making), executive (licensing/enforcement) and judicial (adjudication/penalty)** functions — a deliberate, controlled exception to strict separation of powers.

### 3.2 Sector-wise master table

| Sector | Regulator | Act & year | One-line function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money & banking | **RBI** | RBI Act **1934**; Banking Regulation Act 1949 | Monetary policy (MPC — FEMA/RBI Act amendment 2016, **4% ± 2% CPI target**), bank licensing & supervision |
| Securities | **SEBI** | SEBI Act **1992** (statutory after Harshad Mehta scam) | Protect investors, regulate markets; quasi-judicial orders → appeal to **SAT** |
| Telecom | **TRAI** | TRAI Act **1997** | Tariffs, QoS; disputes go to **TDSAT (2000)** |
| Insurance | **IRDAI** | IRDA Act **1999** (Malhotra Committee) | Licensing, policyholder protection |
| Pensions | **PFRDA** | PFRDA Act **2013** | NPS regulation |
| Electricity | **CERC / SERCs (APERC)** | Electricity Act **2003** (APERC originally under AP Electricity Reform Act, **1998** — AP was a reform pioneer) | Tariff setting, licensing; appeal to **APTEL** |
| Competition | **CCI** | Competition Act **2002** (replaced MRTP 1969; Raghavan Committee) | Anti-competitive agreements, abuse of dominance, combinations; appeals now to **NCLAT** |
| Food | **FSSAI** | FSS Act **2006** | Food standards & safety |
| Drugs | **CDSCO/DCGI** | Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 | Drug approval |
| Atomic safety | **AERB** | 1983 (by executive order under Atomic Energy Act 1962) | Nuclear safety (trap: not statutory) |
| Real estate | **RERA authorities** | RERA Act **2016** | Project registration, buyer protection — **AP RERA** |
| Airports/aviation | **AERA (2008)**, DGCA | — | Tariff at major airports; safety |
| Ports, warehousing, etc. | TAMP (now under MPT Act 2021), WDRA 2007 | — | — |
| Audit/accounting | **NFRA** | Companies Act 2013 (Sec. 132; set up 2018 post-IL&FS) | Audit-quality oversight |
| Data (upcoming) | **Data Protection Board** | DPDP Act **2023** | Adjudicates data breaches |

### 3.3 Issues with regulators (Mains paragraph bank)

1. **Independence vs accountability:** appointed and funded by government; retired-bureaucrat capture of chairs; "revolving door".
2. **Regulatory capture** (Stigler) — industry influence over its own regulator.
3. **Overlap & turf wars:** SEBI–IRDAI (ULIPs, 2010), CCI–sector regulators, RBI–government (Sec. 7 RBI Act debate, 2018).
4. **Accountability gap:** regulators legislate (regulations) without parliamentary scrutiny — 2nd ARC and the **Financial Sector Legislative Reforms Commission (FSLRC, 2013, Justice B.N. Srikrishna)** recommended clearer objectives, regulatory impact assessment, and appellate oversight (FSLRC even proposed a unified financial agency and a Financial Sector Appellate Tribunal).
5. **Skill deficits, vacancies, weak enforcement** (penalties challenged, delays).
6. **Way forward:** transparent selection, fixed tenures, regulatory impact assessment, parliamentary committee review of regulators, sunset/periodic review of regulations, strengthening appellate tribunals.

## 4. Quasi-judicial authorities

### 4.1 Meaning & characteristics

A **quasi-judicial authority** determines the **rights and liabilities of parties** and its orders bind them — but it is **not a full court**:

- Powers derive from a **specific statute**, jurisdiction limited to that field (a court has general jurisdiction).
- **Not bound** by the CPC/Evidence Act; must follow **principles of natural justice** — *audi alteram partem* (hear the other side), *nemo judex in causa sua* (no one a judge in own cause), reasoned (speaking) orders.
- May include **technical/expert members**; procedures simpler, cheaper, faster.
- Subject to **judicial review** — writs under **Art. 226 (HC)**; and per ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)**, tribunal decisions go first to the **Division Bench of the High Court**, since judicial review under Arts. 32/226 is part of the **basic structure**.

### 4.2 Tribunals — the constitutional hook

- Added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** (Swaran Singh Committee): **Part XIV-A** —
  - **Art. 323-A:** **administrative tribunals** for public-service matters (only Parliament can create; e.g., **CAT, 1985** under the Administrative Tribunals Act).
  - **Art. 323-B:** tribunals for **other matters** (tax, land reform, elections, foreign exchange…) — both Parliament and State legislatures.
- ***S.P. Sampath Kumar* (1987):** tribunals valid as substitutes if as effective as HCs. ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997):** struck down exclusion of judicial review — tribunals **supplement, not supplant**, High Courts.
- **CAT:** Chair + judicial & administrative members; benches across India (bench at **Hyderabad; Vijayawada circuit/bench for AP** after bifurcation-era reorganisation). **Note the AP twist:** the **AP Administrative Tribunal (APAT)** — a Sampath-Kumar-era State tribunal — was **abolished (2020)**; its cases moved to the **AP High Court**, and CAT serves central employees.
- **Tribunal rationalisation:** Finance Act **2017** merged tribunals (8 wound up) and let government set service conditions by rules — struck down in part in ***Rojer Mathew* (2019)**; **Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021** abolished 9 appellate tribunals (incl. FTO/IPAB) transferring work to HCs; **Madras Bar Association cases (2020–21)** repeatedly policed tenure (5 years, not 4) and search-committee composition — a live **separation-of-powers vs executive-control** story for Mains.

### 4.3 Catalogue of quasi-judicial bodies (with statute)

| Body | Statute | Jurisdiction / appeal path |
|---|---|---|
| **CAT** | Administrative Tribunals Act, **1985** | Central civil-service disputes → HC Division Bench (*L. Chandra Kumar*) |
| **NGT** | NGT Act, 2010 | Environment → SC |
| **ITAT** (oldest, **1941**) | Income-tax Act | Tax appeals → HC (substantial question of law) |
| **CESTAT** | Customs Act 1962 | Indirect-tax appeals |
| **GSTAT** | CGST Act 2017 (benches operationalised recently) | GST appeals |
| **NCLT/NCLAT** | Companies Act 2013 | Company law + **IBC 2016** insolvency; NCLAT also hears **CCI** appeals → SC |
| **SAT** | SEBI Act 1992 | Appeals from SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA orders → SC |
| **TDSAT** | TRAI Act (2000 amendment) | Telecom disputes → SC |
| **APTEL** | Electricity Act 2003 | Appeals from CERC/APERC |
| **AFT** | Armed Forces Tribunal Act, 2007 | Service + court-martial appeals |
| **Consumer Commissions (District/State/National — NCDRC)** | Consumer Protection Act, **2019** (replaced 1986) | Pecuniary: District ≤ ₹50 lakh; State ₹50 lakh–2 crore; National > ₹2 crore (2021 rules); AP State Commission at Vijayawada |
| **DRT/DRAT** | RDB Act 1993; SARFAESI 2002 | Bank-debt recovery |
| **Waqf Tribunals, Family Courts (1984), Industrial Tribunals (ID Act 1947), Railway Claims Tribunal (1987), MACT (MV Act)** | — | Sectoral |
| **Election Commission** | Art. 324 | Quasi-judicial when deciding **symbol disputes (Symbols Order 1968)**, advising President/Governor on **disqualifications (Arts. 103/192)** |
| **Speaker under Tenth Schedule** | 52nd Amendment 1985 | Anti-defection — quasi-judicial (*Kihoto Hollohan*, 1992: judicial review applies) |
| **CIC/SICs, Lokpal/Lokayukta, NHRC (deemed civil-court powers)** | respective Acts | Quasi-judicial powers of summoning, inquiry |
| **Revenue courts & officers** (e.g., under the **AP (Andhra Area) / Board of Revenue lineage — today RDO/Joint Collector appeals, AP Land Grabbing (Prohibition) Act 1982 Special Courts**) | State land laws | Everyday quasi-judicial tier citizens meet — good AP tie-in |

### 4.4 Merits and demerits of tribunalisation

**Merits:** speed & cheapness; technical expertise; reduces court arrears; flexible procedure; accessibility.

**Demerits:** executive control over appointments/service conditions (**threat to judicial independence**); tribunal pendency now mirrors courts; vacancies; multiplicity confuses litigants; many lack benches outside Delhi (access); post-retirement-job incentive problem; **Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 vs SC** standoff shows fragile autonomy.

**Way forward (SC + Law Commission 272nd Report, 2017):** a single **National Tribunals Commission** to oversee appointments/administration; benches in all states; judicial-majority selection committees; assured tenure and funding.

## 5. Practice

### 5.1 Rapid-fire pairs

1. Art. 323-A — administrative tribunals (Parliament only); 323-B — other tribunals (Parliament + States); both — **42nd Amendment 1976**.
2. *L. Chandra Kumar* 1997 — judicial review basic structure; tribunals → HC Division Bench first.
3. CVC — 1964 resolution → **statutory 2003**; NCBC — statutory 1993 → **constitutional 2018 (102nd Am.)**; NITI Aayog/CBI/AERB — non-statutory.
4. NHRC 2019 amendment — chair may be **SC judge** too; term **3 yrs/70**.
5. SEBI appeals — **SAT**; TRAI disputes — **TDSAT**; CCI appeals — **NCLAT**; CERC/APERC appeals — **APTEL**; NGT appeals — **SC (90 days)**.
6. Oldest tribunal — **ITAT 1941**; Consumer Protection Act — **2019**; Lokpal Act — **2013**, first Lokpal **2019**.
7. AP: **APERC (1998 Reform Act pioneer), AP Lokayukta (1983 Act), APSHRC, AP RERA, APAT abolished 2020 → AP High Court**.

### 5.2 Mains questions

1. **"Independent regulators mark a shift from the 'producing State' to the 'regulatory State'." Examine with reference to post-1991 India.** *(LPG logic → RBI/SEBI/TRAI/CERC examples → tri-functional powers & separation-of-powers tension → issues: capture, overlap, accountability → FSLRC/2nd ARC reforms.)*
2. **Tribunalisation of justice in India: boon or bane? Discuss in the light of recent Supreme Court judgments.** *(42nd Am. → Sampath Kumar → L. Chandra Kumar → Rojer Mathew/Madras Bar Assn./Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 → NTC as way forward.)*
3. **Distinguish constitutional, statutory and quasi-judicial bodies with two examples each. Why did the NCBC's status change in 2018?** *(Definition table → 102nd Amendment context — Indra Sawhney backdrop, parity with NCSC/NCST.)*
4. **Quasi-judicial authorities must follow natural justice though not the Evidence Act. Explain with examples.** *(Audi alteram partem, nemo judex, speaking orders → CAT/consumer fora/EC symbol cases → judicial review as check.)*
