# 04 · Legacy of British Rule — How the Modern Indian Administrative State Was Built

## 0. The frame

A trading company (charter of 1600) became a state: **Diwani of Bengal, 1765** — the Company literally stepped into the **Mughal diwan's office** (File 03). Everything after is the conversion of a commercial-extractive apparatus into a **legal-rational colonial state** — designed for **revenue, order and control**, yet bequeathing the skeleton that independent India **repurposed for democracy and development**. Hold both halves of that sentence: it *is* the Mains answer.

---

## 1. Constitutional-administrative milestones (timeline you must know cold) ⭐

| Act/Event | What it built |
|---|---|
| **Regulating Act 1773** | Governor-General of Bengal (**Warren Hastings**) + Council; **Supreme Court, Calcutta (1774)** — first crown court |
| **Pitt's India Act 1784** | **Dual control**: Board of Control (Crown) over the Court of Directors — political vs commercial split |
| Charter Act **1833** | **Governor-General of India** (Bentinck); all-India legislation centralised; **Law Commission (Macaulay)** |
| Charter Act **1853** | Legislative council separated; **civil service thrown open to competition** ⭐ |
| **Government of India Act 1858** | Company → **Crown**; Secretary of State + Viceroy; the "civil service of the Crown in India" |
| Indian Councils Act **1861** | **Portfolio system** (Canning) — the cabinet/departmental germ; legislative devolution restarts |
| **1909 Morley–Minto** | First Indian in Executive Council (**S. P. Sinha**); separate electorates (the divisive legacy) |
| **GoI Act 1919 (Montford)** | **Dyarchy** in provinces (reserved vs transferred subjects); central **Public Service Commission promised (sec. 96C)**; central–provincial finance separated |
| **GoI Act 1935** | **Provincial autonomy (1937)**, Federal Court (1937), federal scheme — the Constitution's structural quarry (~250 provisions echoed) |
| 1947–50 | Independence → the inherited machine placed under a democratic Constitution |

## 2. The civil service — from patronage to "steel frame" ⭐⭐

- **Cornwallis — "father of the Indian civil service":** covenanted service purged of private trade, **decent salaries to buy integrity**, the **Cornwallis Code (1793)** separating revenue from judicial functions; but also **Europeanisation** — Indians shut out of higher posts.
- Training: **Fort William College (Wellesley, 1800)** → **Haileybury College (1806)**.
- **Charter Act 1853 + Macaulay Committee (1854)** → open competitive examination; **first exam 1855** (London). **Satyendranath Tagore — first Indian ICS (1863–64)**; 1869 trio included **Surendranath Banerjee** & R. C. Dutt.
- Indianisation milestones: Lytton's **Statutory Civil Service (1878–79** — failed); **Aitchison Commission (1886)** — Imperial/Provincial/Subordinate tiers; **Islington Commission (1912–15)** — 25% higher posts for Indians; **Montford Report (1918)** — 33% + annual increase; **Lee Commission (1924)** — 50:50 in 15 years and a **Public Service Commission**.
- **Public Service Commission set up 1926** (chairman **Sir Ross Barker**) → Federal PSC (1935 Act) → **UPSC (1950)**.
- ⭐ **AP lineage:** **Madras Service Commission (1929) — the FIRST provincial service commission in India** → Andhra PSC (1953, after the Andhra State was carved out) → **APPSC (1956)**. The exam you are writing descends from this line.
- Exams held **simultaneously in India from 1922 (Allahabad)**.
- "**Steel frame**": Lloyd George's Commons speech (**1922**) on the ICS. After 1947 **Sardar Patel** defended continuity — All-India Services (**Art. 312**; IAS/IPS as ICS/IP successors): "you will not have a united India if you do not have a good all-India service…"

## 3. District administration — the Collector, the institution APPSC loves ⭐⭐

- Office of **District Collector created 1772 (Warren Hastings)** — revenue collection + magistracy fused in one officer; briefly stripped of judicial power by Cornwallis, magistracy restored by **Bentinck (1829–31)**, who also created **Divisional Commissioners (1829)**.
- **Two rival philosophies** (classic PubAd contrast, via Eric Stokes):
  - **Cornwallis system** — rule of **regulation**: impersonal law, separation of powers, the official as umpire (Bengal).
  - **Munro system** — rule of the **man on the spot**: paternal, personal government; collector as revenue officer + magistrate + protector (Madras) — the model that won across India.
- **Thomas Munro in AP** ⭐: Principal **Collector of the Ceded Districts (Rayalaseema) 1800–07** — the Nizam ceded them to the Company in 1800; later **Governor of Madras (1820)** who generalised **ryotwari**. Munro remains a live memory in Rayalaseema.
- Oversight: **Board of Revenue, Madras (1786)** — collectors reported to it; its functions live on in AP's **Chief Commissioner of Land Administration (CCLA)**.
- Village level: the hereditary **karanam (accountant) / munsif-reddy (headman) / talari (watchman)** offices continued under ryotwari — abolished in AP only in **1984** (part-time village officers), succeeded by VROs/VAOs and now the **village & ward secretariat system (2019)** — the perfect continuity-and-reform illustration for Mains.

## 4. Police, justice and the rule of law ⭐

- **Police:** Cornwallis's **daroga**-thana experiment (1791–93); Sind/Punjab constabulary model; Madras a pioneer (**Madras District Police Act, 1859**); after 1857 the **Police Act 1861** — provincial IG, district **SP under DM's control** — a structure still substantially in force (its colonial "order over service" DNA is the standard critique; Fraser Commission 1902–03 → CID).
- **Courts:** Mayor's Courts (1726) → Supreme Court Calcutta (1774) → Hastings' adalat network (1772: diwani & faujdari adalats — again the Mughal names) → Cornwallis's Sadr courts & gradation → **Indian High Courts Act 1861** (Calcutta, Bombay, **Madras HCs 1862**) → **Federal Court 1937** → Supreme Court 1950. (AP line: Madras HC → Andhra HC, Guntur 1954 → AP High Court.)
- **Codification — the Macaulay revolution:** First Law Commission (1834): **IPC (enacted 1860)**, CPC (1859), **CrPC (1861)** — one law replacing plural personal-cum-discretionary regimes; **Indian Evidence Act (1872)**.
- **Rule of law & equality before law** — the doctrine Indians turned against the Raj itself; its limit exposed by the **Ilbert Bill controversy (1883)** (European jurors refusing Indian judges) and coercive statutes (**Rowlatt Act 1919; Official Secrets Act 1923**).

## 5. Land-revenue settlements — administration meets society ⭐

| System | Author/date | Area (approx.) | Design | Social legacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Permanent Settlement (zamindari)** | **Cornwallis, 1793** (with John Shore) — Bengal, Bihar, Orissa | ~19% | Zamindar = owner; revenue **fixed forever**; **sunset law** (pay by sunset or estate sold) | Parasitic landlordism, rack-rented tenants, absentee estates → zamindari abolition = free India's first land reform |
| **Ryotwari** | **Munro & Alexander Read** (Baramahal 1792; Madras-wide 1820); Wingate-Elphinstone in Bombay | ~51% | **State ↔ ryot direct**; periodic (30-yr) settlement; the **collector-centred** system | No zamindar screen, but heavy assessment; indebtedness; the AP peasantry's system ⭐ |
| **Mahalwari** | **Holt Mackenzie (Reg. VII of 1822)**, revised **Bird/Thomason (1833)** — NWP, Punjab | ~30% | Village/estate (**mahal**) jointly liable | Village-community fiction under revenue pressure |

Common effects: **cash revenue + rigid collection** → commercialisation of agriculture, land as saleable commodity, moneylender's rise, and famine vulnerability — **Guntur famine (1832–33)** in Andhra; Great famines of 1876–78 & 1896–1900; the **Famine Commission (Strachey, 1880) → Famine Codes (1883)** — colonial India's disaster-management manuals.

## 6. Local self-government — the Ripon thread ⭐

- Pre-British village autonomy (Metcalfe's "**little republics**", 1830) had decayed under revenue centralisation.
- **Madras Corporation, 1687 — India's oldest municipal body** ⭐.
- **Mayo's Resolution 1870** — financial decentralisation begins.
- **Ripon's Resolution, 18 May 1882 — the "Magna Carta of local self-government"**; Ripon = "**father of local self-government in India**": local boards with **non-official majorities and, where possible, elected non-official chairmen**; local government as **political education**, not just efficiency.
- **Royal Commission on Decentralisation (1907–09)**; **GoI Act 1919** made local self-government a **transferred** subject; 1935 → provincial ambit.
- After 1947: Community Development (1952) → **Balwant Rai Mehta (1957)** → **Rajasthan first (Nagaur, 2 Oct 1959) and ANDHRA PRADESH SECOND (1959)** to launch Panchayati Raj ⭐ → 73rd/74th Amendments (1992–93) constitutionalised the Ripon promise.

## 7. Finance, secretariat and the knowledge state

- **Budget:** first Indian budget **1860 — James Wilson** (finance member); annual budgeting, audit and **the Auditor-General's office (1860)** → today's **CAG**; legislature-controlled purse arrives with 1919/1935 reforms.
- **Secretariat system & tenure system:** department secretariats with noting/filing discipline; officers rotating between secretariat and district ("tenure") — both persist.
- **Infrastructure of unification (Dalhousie, 1848–56):** first **railway 1853 (Bombay–Thane)** on the guarantee system; **Telegraph & the Post Office Act (1854)**; PWD.
- ⭐ **AP's engineering legacy: Sir Arthur Cotton** — **Dowleswaram anicut on the Godavari (1847–52)** and the Krishna anicut at Vijayawada (1850s): the delta's prosperity, and Cotton's near-saintly memory in coastal Andhra, are standard AP tie-ins.
- **Education:** Macaulay's **Minute (1835)** — English medium; **Wood's Despatch (1854)** — "Magna Carta of English education"; **universities of Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1857)** — produced the clerks the state wanted *and* the nationalists it didn't.
- **The knowledge state:** Survey of India (1767), **Census — 1872 (first) / 1881 (first synchronous)**, district gazetteers, **ASI (1861, Cunningham)**, **Forest Department (1864, Brandis)** & Forest Acts (1865/1878) — enumeration as an instrument of control that democratic India reuses for planning.
- **RBI (1935)**; Imperial Bank (1921) → SBI (1955).

## 8. The balance sheet ⭐⭐ (Mains core)

**Positive legacy (institutions):** all-India merit civil service & PSCs; District Collector; uniform codified law, professional judiciary, rule-of-law idea; police organisation; budget-audit (CAG); secretariat method; local-government framework; railways-post-telegraph-canals; English as link language; census & statistical administration; parliamentary experience (1861→1935) that made the Constitution workable.

**Negative legacy:** the machine was **extractive and repressive by design** — **Dadabhai Naoroji's drain theory** (*Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*, 1901) and **R. C. Dutt's** *Economic History*; deindustrialisation & famines; **law-and-order state, not welfare state** — the "**mai-baap sarkar**" distance between officer and citizen; over-centralisation & secrecy (OSA 1923); racial ceiling in services & justice (Ilbert Bill); **divide-and-rule** (separate electorates 1909 → Partition); a **generalist, elitist** bureaucracy suspicious of the people it ruled.

**Synthesis line:** *continuity of structure, change of purpose* — after 1947 the same frame was set to welfare and development; the **2nd ARC's "citizen-centric administration"** agenda is precisely the effort to squeeze the remaining colonial habits (opacity, distance, discretion) out of an inherited machine. RTI (2005), citizens' charters, e-governance and AP's Spandana/village secretariats are the current instalments.

## 10. Mains angles

1. **"The British gave India an administration, not a government of the people." Evaluate.** Balance-sheet structure (§8): institutions vs purpose; close with post-1947 repurposing and what remains colonial (police act, secrecy, distance).
2. **"The District Collector is the most enduring British legacy." Discuss with reference to Andhra Pradesh.** 1772 origins → Cornwallis vs **Munro** systems → Ceded Districts → collector as development coordinator today (elections, disasters, welfare schemes) → critiques (overload, 73rd/74th tension) → AP village secretariats as the newest layer.
3. **"Ripon's Resolution of 1882 sowed what the 73rd/74th Amendments harvested."** Trace Madras 1687 → Mayo → Ripon (political-education rationale) → 1907/1919 → Balwant Rai Mehta → AP 1959 → 1992–93; assess the persisting gap (funds, functions, functionaries).
4. **"Rule of law was the Raj's gift and its indictment."** Codification & courts vs Ilbert Bill, Rowlatt, OSA; nationalists turning legality against empire; the constitutional inheritance.
5. **Land settlements as social engineering:** compare zamindari/ryotwari/mahalwari and their long shadows (land reform agenda, tenancy, records — Bhoodan to digital land records like AP's resurvey).
