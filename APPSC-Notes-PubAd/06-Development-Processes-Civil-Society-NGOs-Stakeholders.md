# 06 — Development Processes: the Role of Civil Society, NGOs and Other Stakeholders

**Syllabus item:** *"Development processes — the role of civil society, NGOs and other stakeholders."*

---

## 1. The idea of "development" — a quick conceptual base

Every Mains answer on this topic should open by showing you know that *development* is a contested, evolving concept — then move to the actors.

| Phase | Dominant idea of development | Chief actor |
|---|---|---|
| 1950s–60s | **Growth-centred** — GNP, capital accumulation, big dams & steel plants ("temples of modern India" — Nehru) | The **State** (planning, public sector) |
| 1970s | **Growth with redistribution / basic needs** — garibi hatao, ILO basic-needs approach | State + targeted programmes |
| 1980s–90s | **Market-led development** — Washington Consensus, LPG reforms (1991) | The **Market** |
| 1990s onwards | **Human development** — **Amartya Sen** (*Development as Freedom*, capability approach); **Mahbub-ul-Haq** — first **Human Development Report, UNDP 1990** | State + market + **civil society** |
| 2000s onwards | **Participatory, rights-based, sustainable & inclusive development** — MDGs (2000) → **SDGs (2015, 17 goals)**; rights-based laws (RTI, MGNREGA, RTE, NFSA) | **Multi-stakeholder governance** |

- **Key distinction (1-mark favourite):** *Growth* = quantitative rise in output; *Development* = growth **plus** distributive justice, capability expansion and institutional change.
- **"Governance" turn:** The World Bank (1992, *Governance and Development*) and UNDP defined **good governance** as participatory, transparent, accountable, effective, equitable and rule-of-law based. This is precisely the doorway through which **civil society and NGOs** entered the development process — the State stopped being the sole developer and became a **facilitator/regulator** working with other stakeholders.
- **2nd ARC anchor:** the Second Administrative Reforms Commission devoted its **9th Report — "Social Capital: A Shared Destiny" (2008)** — to societies, trusts, cooperatives, SHGs and professional bodies. Quote it by name in Mains.

## 2. Civil society — meaning, thinkers, features

### 2.1 What is civil society?

- **Working definition:** the sphere of **voluntary, organised collective action** that lies **between the family, the State and the market** — associations citizens form around shared interests, purposes and values.
- It includes: NGOs, community-based organisations (CBOs), self-help groups (SHGs), trade unions, cooperatives, religious and charitable bodies, professional associations, media, think tanks, social movements, resident welfare associations.

### 2.2 Thinkers (name–idea pairs — harvest as flashcards)

| Thinker | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Alexis de Tocqueville** (*Democracy in America*, 1835) | Voluntary associations are the **"schools of democracy"** — they teach citizens self-government |
| **Hegel / Marx** | Civil society as the sphere of particular (economic) interests between family and State |
| **Antonio Gramsci** | Civil society as the arena where **hegemony** (consent) is built and contested |
| **Robert Putnam** (*Making Democracy Work*; *Bowling Alone*) | **Social capital** — networks, norms and trust that enable cooperation; strong associational life → better government performance |
| **Amartya Sen** | Development = expansion of **freedoms/capabilities**; public discussion and participation are constitutive of development |
| **Mahatma Gandhi** | **Constructive work** programme (khadi, village industries, Harijan welfare, basic education) — the indigenous root of Indian voluntarism; trusteeship |

### 2.3 Features of civil society organisations (CSOs)

1. **Voluntary** membership; 2. **Non-profit** distribution; 3. **Autonomous** of the State (though may be funded/regulated by it); 4. **Self-governing**; 5. Oriented to a **public or mutual purpose**.

### 2.4 Constitutional footing in India

- **Art. 19(1)(c)** — fundamental right to form **associations or unions** (the legal bedrock of civil society).
- **Art. 43** (DPSP) — promotion of **cooperatives**; **Art. 43-B** (added by **97th Amendment, 2011**) — voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control of **cooperative societies**; **Art. 19(1)(c)** was also amended by the 97th Amendment to add "co-operative societies".
- **Part IX & IX-A (73rd/74th Amendments, 1992)** — institutionalised **people's participation** through panchayats and municipalities; **Gram Sabha (Art. 243A)** is direct democracy at the base.
- **Art. 51A** — fundamental duties presuppose an active citizenry.

## 3. Roles of civil society in the development process

Organise Mains answers around these **seven functions** (with one example each):

| Function | What it means | Ready example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Watchdog / accountability** | Monitoring State performance, exposing corruption | **MKSS (Rajasthan, Aruna Roy)** — jan sunwais → **RTI Act 2005**; social audits of MGNREGA |
| 2. **Advocacy & agenda-setting** | Pushing issues onto the policy agenda | Campaigns behind **RTI (2005), MGNREGA (2005), RTE (2009), NFSA (2013)**, Nirbhaya-era criminal-law reform |
| 3. **Service delivery / gap-filling** | Reaching where the State is thin — health, education, disability, old age | **Pratham** (education, publishes **ASER**), **Akshaya Patra** (mid-day meals), **HelpAge India**, missionary hospitals |
| 4. **Empowerment & capacity building** | Organising the poor, women, workers into collectives | **SEWA (Ela Bhatt, 1972)** — self-employed women; **SHG movement** |
| 5. **Innovation & piloting** | Testing models the State later scales up | **Amul/Anand pattern (Verghese Kurien)** → Operation Flood; **Barefoot College** (solar engineers); SEWA's micro-insurance |
| 6. **Mobilisation / social movements** | Voice for the voiceless; environmental & livelihood defence | **Chipko (Sunderlal Bahuguna, Chandi Prasad Bhatt)**, **Narmada Bachao Andolan (Medha Patkar)**, Silent Valley movement |
| 7. **Disaster response & relief** | Speed and local reach in emergencies | Gujarat earthquake 2001, tsunami 2004, Kerala floods 2018, **COVID-19** (oxygen, food, migrant relief) |

**Democracy dividend:** civil society deepens democracy **between elections** — it converts formal (electoral) democracy into **participatory democracy**, builds social capital, and mediates between citizen and State (the "third sector" after State and market).

## 4. NGOs in India — evolution, types, legal framework

### 4.1 Evolution

1. **Pre-independence:** religious charity (dharamshalas, wakfs, temple endowments); 19th-century reform societies (**Brahmo Samaj 1828, Arya Samaj 1875, Ramakrishna Mission 1897**); **Servants of India Society (Gopal Krishna Gokhale, 1905)**; Gandhian constructive-work institutions (**Sabarmati & Sevagram ashrams, Harijan Sevak Sangh 1932**).
2. **1950s–70s:** Gandhian voluntarism (Bhoodan–Gramdan of **Vinoba Bhave**); welfare NGOs complementing the planning State; **CSWB (Central Social Welfare Board, 1953)** created to fund voluntary bodies.
3. **1980s–90s:** professional "development NGOs"; foreign funding grows → **FCRA 1976** (now 2010); NGOs enter watershed, health, literacy missions.
4. **Post-1991:** State retreats, NGOs become **partners in delivery** (DPEP, watershed guidelines, National Rural Health Mission ASHA support NGOs); rights-based campaigns peak (RTI, MGNREGA).
5. **Post-2014:** tighter compliance regime — **FCRA Amendment 2020**, cancellation of registrations; simultaneously **CSR era** (Companies Act 2013) opens corporate money to NGOs; **NITI Aayog NGO-DARPAN portal** for registration/unique ID.

### 4.2 Types (classification MCQs)

- **By function:** *Operational* (implement projects) vs *Advocacy* (campaign for policy change).
- **By level:** community-based (CBO) → city/state → national → international (INGO — Oxfam, MSF).
- **Acronym zoo:** **GONGO** (government-organised NGO), **BINGO** (business/big international NGO), **QUANGO** (quasi-autonomous NGO), **DONGO** (donor-organised NGO).

### 4.3 Legal & regulatory framework (high-yield table)

| Law | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Societies Registration Act, 1860** | Registration of literary/scientific/charitable societies (min. **7** members); many states have their own versions (in AP: **AP Societies Registration Act, 2001**) |
| **Indian Trusts Act, 1882** / state public-trust Acts | Private trusts; public charitable trusts under state Acts |
| **Companies Act, 2013 — Section 8** | Non-profit companies (formerly Sec. 25 of the 1956 Act) |
| **Indian Wakf Act / Religious Endowments** | Religious charities |
| **Income-tax Act, 1961 — Sec. 12A/12AB (exemption), 80G (donor deduction)** | Tax framework for charities |
| **FCRA, 2010** (replacing 1976 Act) + **FCRA (Amendment) Act, 2020** | Regulates **foreign contribution**; 2020 changes: **Aadhaar for office-bearers, FCRA account only in SBI New Delhi main branch, administrative-expense cap cut 50% → 20%, ban on sub-granting (transfer of foreign contribution to other persons)** |
| **Companies Act, 2013 — Sec. 135 (CSR)** | Companies (net worth ≥ ₹500 cr / turnover ≥ ₹1000 cr / net profit ≥ ₹5 cr) must spend **2% of average net profit** of preceding 3 years on CSR — much of it flows through NGOs |
| **National Policy on the Voluntary Sector, 2007** | First national policy recognising the sector as a partner; promised an enabling environment, accreditation, partnership norms |
| **NGO-DARPAN (NITI Aayog)** | Portal giving NGOs a **unique ID**, mandatory for central grants |

- **Vijay Kumar case (SC, 2017 — *ML Sharma / voluntary-sector PILs*)** and repeated Supreme Court observations have pushed for **NGO accountability and audit** of the lakhs of NGOs receiving public funds. (An oft-cited CBI submission to the SC estimated ~**31 lakh registered NGOs** — more than one per 400 Indians — most not filing returns.)

## 5. Other stakeholders in the development process

A "stakeholder" is any actor with an interest in, or affected by, development interventions. Beyond the State and NGOs:

1. **Self-Help Groups (SHGs)** — 10–20 members (mostly women), savings-first micro-collectives.
   - **SHG–Bank Linkage Programme — NABARD, 1992** (world's largest microfinance programme).
   - **DAY-NRLM (National Rural Livelihoods Mission, 2011)** — federates SHGs into VOs (village organisations) and CLFs.
   - **AP is the historic heartland (must-use AP tie-in):** **DWCRA** (Development of Women & Children in Rural Areas) boom of the 1990s; **Velugu / Indira Kranthi Patham** projects run by **SERP (Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty, 2000)**; AP's SHG network is among India's densest; **"Podupu Lakshmi"** (thrift) culture; the model fed into NRLM nationally.
2. **Cooperatives** — user-owned economic enterprises: **Amul (AMUL/GCMMF — Verghese Kurien, Operation Flood)**; PACS in credit; **97th Amendment 2011** (constitutional status: Art. 19(1)(c), 43-B, Part IX-B); **Ministry of Cooperation created 2021**; multi-state cooperatives under **MSCS Act 2002 (amended 2023)**.
   - *Note:* In **Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah (2021)** the SC struck down Part IX-B **for cooperatives confined to one State** (no ratification by half the States) — it survives for multi-state cooperatives.
3. **Corporate sector / CSR** — Sec. 135 funds; PPPs in infrastructure; CSR in AP (e.g., corporate adoption of schools/anganwadis).
4. **Media** — the "fourth estate": agenda-setting (exposés → policy), social-media mobilisation; also risks (paid news, misinformation).
5. **Academia & think tanks** — evidence for policy: ICRIER, CPR, NIPFP; ASER (Pratham) shaping education debate.
6. **International organisations & donors** — UNDP, World Bank, WHO, UNICEF, bilateral donors (JICA, DFID/FCDO), Gates Foundation; SDG frameworks.
7. **Panchayati Raj Institutions & Urban Local Bodies** — constitutional stakeholders (73rd/74th Amendments); Gram Sabha as the participation forum; **PESA 1996** in Scheduled Areas (AP has Scheduled Areas in the agency tracts of ASR, Parvathipuram Manyam, Eluru etc.).
8. **Community-based organisations & user groups** — **Water Users' Associations under the AP Farmers' Management of Irrigation Systems (APFMIS) Act, 1997** (a pioneering participatory-irrigation law, widely copied); **Vana Samrakshana Samitis** (joint forest management); school management committees (RTE); **mothers' committees** in ICDS; village education committees.
9. **Pressure groups & trade unions** — sectional interest articulation (FICCI/CII, farm unions, BMS/INTUC/CITU).
10. **Citizens as individual stakeholders** — RTI applicants, social auditors, whistle-blowers (**Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014**), Citizen's Charters (from the UK 1991 model; India 1997 conference of CMs), **Sevottam** model (2nd ARC 12th Report, "Citizen Centric Administration").

### AP-specific participation architecture (gold for AP tie-ins)

- **Janmabhoomi (1997)** and later **Janmabhoomi–Maa Vooru** — state-led participatory micro-planning campaigns.
- **SERP & SHG federations** (Zilla/ Mandal Samakhyas), **MEPMA** for urban poor.
- **Spandana** — public grievance redressal platform.
- **Village/Ward Secretariats & Volunteers system (2019)** — doorstep delivery interface between citizen and administration.
- **Rythu Bharosa Kendras (RBKs, 2020)** — village-level farm service hubs (input sales, advisory, procurement).
- **Water Users' Associations (APFMIS Act 1997)**, **Vana Samrakshana Samitis**, **Mothers' committees / school committees** — sectoral user bodies.

## 6. Government–civil society interface: instruments of partnership

1. **Policy:** National Policy on the Voluntary Sector, **2007**; NITI Aayog's **NGO-DARPAN**; Vivekananda-committee-style accreditation proposals.
2. **Funding routes:** grants-in-aid (CSWB, ministries), scheme partnerships (NRLM resource organisations, ASHA support), CSR, philanthropic foundations.
3. **Participatory instruments in schemes:** social audit (statutory under **MGNREGA Sec. 17**; Meghalaya passed the first state Social Audit Act, 2017; AP's **Society for Social Audit, Accountability & Transparency — SSAAT** — was a national pioneer), gram sabha approval of plans (GPDP), community monitoring in NHM.
4. **Legal empowerment of citizens:** **RTI 2005**, **Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014**, Citizen's Charters, **Sevottam**, e-governance/grievance portals (CPGRAMS; AP's Spandana).

## 7. Criticisms, challenges and the way forward

### 7.1 Concerns about NGOs/CSOs

- **Accountability deficit:** they demand transparency from the State but many do not file returns/audits themselves ("who watches the watchdog?").
- **Foreign-funding & sovereignty concerns:** IB report (2014) alleged foreign-funded campaigns stalled development projects; large-scale **FCRA licence cancellations** (tens of thousands since 2011, incl. high-profile ones like Greenpeace India, and later CSOs such as Oxfam India, CPR).
- **Elite capture / urban bias; briefcase NGOs;** donor-driven agendas displacing community priorities.
- **Fragmentation & duplication;** weak second-line leadership; "projectisation" of social work.
- **Political entanglement** — GONGOs, patronage networks.

### 7.2 Concerns about the State's approach

- Over-regulation chilling legitimate advocacy (FCRA 2020's **20% admin cap and sub-granting ban** hit grassroots partnerships); delays in 12A/80G/FCRA renewals; shrinking "civic space" (a standard Mains counter-point — cite it neutrally).

### 7.3 Way forward (use as Mains conclusion kit)

1. Implement the **2nd ARC (9th Report)** vision: an enabling legal framework, accreditation of voluntary organisations, transparent funding norms.
2. **National accreditation body** for NGOs + mandatory public disclosure (income, audited accounts) — trust through transparency, not just policing.
3. **Rationalise FCRA/tax compliance** — time-bound renewals, distinguish service-delivery from political funding.
4. **Institutionalise partnership**, not patronage: outcome-based grants, social-stock-exchange listing (SEBI's **Social Stock Exchange, 2022** — NPOs can raise funds via **Zero Coupon Zero Principal instruments**).
5. Deepen **social audit** and Gram Sabha oversight; scale AP-style SHG federations and SSAAT nationally.
6. **Sen's insight as the closer:** development is done *by* people, not merely *for* them — civil society is how people enter the development process.
