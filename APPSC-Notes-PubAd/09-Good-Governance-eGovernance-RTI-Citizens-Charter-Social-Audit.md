## 1. Good governance — the concept

### 1.1 From government to governance

- **Government** = the formal institutions of the State. **Governance** = the *process* of decision-making and implementation, involving State + market + civil society. "Good governance" adds a **normative standard** to that process.
- The term entered development vocabulary through the **World Bank** (first in its 1989 Africa report; systematically in **"Governance and Development", 1992**) — governance as "the manner in which power is exercised in the management of a country's economic and social resources for development."

### 1.2 The UNDP/UNESCAP eight characteristics (the standard list — memorise)

1. **Participation** · 2. **Rule of law** · 3. **Transparency** · 4. **Responsiveness** · 5. **Consensus-orientation** · 6. **Equity & inclusiveness** · 7. **Effectiveness & efficiency** · 8. **Accountability**.

### 1.3 Indian roots & anchors (intro material)

- **Kautilya (Arthashastra):** *"In the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare his welfare"* — the classical Indian statement of good governance (links to File 02).
- **Gandhian *Su-raj*** (good rule) as the substance of *Swaraj*; Ram Rajya as a governance metaphor.
- **Constitutional anchors:** Preamble (justice, liberty, equality), Art. 14 (non-arbitrariness), Art. 21 (due process after *Maneka Gandhi*), DPSPs (Art. 38, 39), Art. 51A.
- **2nd ARC** — the entire commission (2005–09, **Veerappa Moily**) is a good-governance manual; its 12th Report **"Citizen Centric Administration — the Heart of Governance"** is the one to cite for this whole file.
- **Good Governance Day — 25 December** (Vajpayee's birthday, since 2014); **Good Governance Index (DARPG, 2019)** ranking states on 10 sectors; **District Good Governance Index** (first in J&K, 2022).
- **SMART governance** (the e-governance vision statement): **S**imple, **M**oral, **A**ccountable, **R**esponsive, **T**ransparent government.

## 2. Transparency, accountability, responsiveness — the three working values

| Value | Meaning | Chief instruments in India |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparency** | Decisions taken and enforced in a manner open to scrutiny; information accessible to those affected | **RTI 2005**, Sec. 4 proactive disclosure, e-procurement/GeM, budget documents, open data (data.gov.in), **social audit** |
| **Accountability** | Answerability (explain) + enforcement (consequences) — of officials to institutions and citizens | Parliament/legislature (questions, committees, CAG→PAC), **Lokpal/Lokayukta, CVC**, judiciary/judicial review, **Public Services Acts** (penalty clauses), performance appraisal, social audit |
| **Responsiveness** | Serving citizens within reasonable time, with courtesy and redress | **Citizen's Charters, Sevottam**, grievance systems (**CPGRAMS**; AP **Spandana / PGRS**), time-limits under **Public Services Acts**, single-window & doorstep delivery (AP village/ward secretariats) |

- **Two directions of accountability:** *vertical* (citizens → elections, RTI, social audit, media) and *horizontal* (state institutions checking each other — CAG, courts, commissions). Good governance needs both.
- **Mains framing:** RTI supplies *transparency*, Public Services Acts supply *responsiveness with enforceable accountability*, Citizen's Charters supply *declared standards*, social audit supplies *community-verified accountability* — the four syllabus instruments map exactly onto the three values.

## 3. E-governance

### 3.1 Concept and interactions

- **E-governance** = use of ICT to deliver public services, exchange information and integrate systems between government and citizens — the technological route to SMART governance.
- **Four interaction types (MCQ staple):** **G2C** (services to citizens — MeeSeva), **G2B** (business — e-procurement, MCA21), **G2G** (within government — e-Office, PFMS), **G2E** (employees — iGOT, HRMS).
- **e-Government vs e-Governance:** the first is computerising government; the second is transforming the *relationship* between state and citizen (2nd ARC 11th Report — **"Promoting e-Governance: The SMART Way Forward"**).

### 3.2 Evolution in India (timeline hooks)

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1976 | **NIC** established; 1987 — NICNET |
| 1990s | Railway/land-record computerisation pilots |
| 2006 | **National e-Governance Plan (NeGP)** — 27 (later 31) **Mission Mode Projects** + core infrastructure (**SWANs, State Data Centres, Common Services Centres, e-District**) |
| 2009 | **Aadhaar (UIDAI)**; statutory base — Aadhaar Act **2016** (money bill) |
| 2015 | **Digital India** — 9 pillars; **e-Kranti** (NeGP 2.0) |
| 2014– | **JAM trinity** (Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile) → **DBT**; India Stack: **UPI (2016), DigiLocker, e-Sign**; **GeM (2016)**; **PRAGATI (2015)** — PM's ICT-based multi-modal review platform; **UMANG**; **CPGRAMS** |
| 2020s | **DPDP Act 2023** (data protection); ONDC; Bhashini; AI-in-governance pilots |

### 3.3 Andhra Pradesh — the e-governance pioneer (home-turf material)

AP (undivided) was **India's first-mover state** in e-governance — usable as the case study for any e-governance answer:

- **First State IT policy era (late 1990s)** under N. Chandrababu Naidu — "laptop CM"; **HITEC City (1998)**; the World Bank-aided **AP: Vision 2020** framing technology-led governance.
- **CARD (1998)** — Computer-aided Administration of Registration Department: one of India's first big service-computerisation successes (deeds registration from days to hours).
- **TWINS pilot (1999, Banjara Hills) → e-Seva (2001)** — India's **first integrated citizen one-stop e-service centres** (bill payments, certificates, licences across departments/utilities) — the model that inspired national CSCs.
- **MeeSeva (2011)** — "at your service": web-based delivery of (eventually) 300+ services of 35+ departments through kiosks with **digitally signed certificates** and **service-wise time-limits notified by government order** — a national award-winner replicated in other states.
- **e-Pragati (2016)** — whole-of-government enterprise architecture attempt (single citizen record across departments).
- **Real Time Governance Society (RTGS, 2017)** — CM dashboard ("CORE dashboard"), Parishkara Vedika video grievance hearings, disaster early-warning integration — an early "control-room governance" experiment.
- **Post-2019 stack:** **village/ward secretariats (2019)** with digital service delivery at the doorstep (~540+ services), **Spandana/PGRS** grievance system, **Rythu Bharosa Kendras** with e-crop booking, **Jagananna-era DBT saturation**; **AP Fibernet** for last-mile connectivity.
- **Caution for Mains balance:** AP also illustrates the risks — dashboard-driven target chasing, data-heavy governance vs privacy, volunteer-based data collection controversies — cite *Puttaswamy* (below) for the counterweight.

### 3.4 Benefits and challenges

**Benefits:** speed, convenience, reduced discretion/corruption (disintermediation), audit trails, saturation delivery (DBT plugged leakages — "I dream of a day when every rupee reaches..." vs Rajiv Gandhi's 15-paise remark), inclusion at scale.

**Challenges:** **digital divide** (rural/gender/age gaps), connectivity & power, **privacy** — ***K.S. Puttaswamy* (2017): privacy a fundamental right under Art. 21**; *Aadhaar* judgment (2018) upheld the Act but struck Sec. 57 (private use); **DPDP Act 2023** as the new framework; cybersecurity (CERT-In); capacity & change-resistance; exclusion errors in Aadhaar-seeded welfare; "digital by default" risks for the poorest.

**Way forward:** 2nd ARC 11th Report (process re-engineering *before* computerisation), universal connectivity (BharatNet), assisted digital access (CSCs/secretariats), data-protection compliance, vernacular interfaces (Bhashini).

## 4. Citizen's Charter

### 4.1 Concept & origin

- A **Citizen's Charter** is a public, unilateral declaration by a service organisation of the **services it provides, the standards/time-limits for each, and the remedy when standards fail** — shifting the citizen from supplicant to entitled customer.
- **Origin: United Kingdom, 1991 — John Major's** Citizen's Charter programme (Charter Mark awards); the "six principles": standards, information & openness, choice & consultation, courtesy & helpfulness, putting things right, value for money.
- **India:** adopted at the **Chief Ministers' Conference on "Effective and Responsive Administration" (May 1997)**; **DARPG** is the nodal agency; hundreds of central/state organisations have charters.

### 4.2 Components of a good charter

Vision & mission → list of services with **quantified standards** (time, cost, quality) → grievance mechanism with named officers → expectations *from* citizens → review/consultation commitment.

### 4.3 The Indian record — why charters underperformed

- Evaluations (IIPA study for DARPG; 2nd ARC 12th Report) found: charters drafted **top-down without consultation**, standards vague, staff untrained/unaware, **no legal enforceability or penalty**, rarely updated, "one-size-fits-all" copies.
- **2nd ARC prescription:** make charters **specific, consultative, periodically evaluated (external audit), with in-built remedies** — and institutionalise via **Sevottam**.

### 4.4 Sevottam (the exam-favourite model)

- **Sevottam** (*Seva* + *Uttam* — service excellence), developed by DARPG; certified as **BIS standard IS 15700:2005**.
- **Three modules:** (1) **Citizen's Charter** (declared standards), (2) **Public Grievance Redress** (receipt-redress-prevention), (3) **Service Delivery Capability** (people, infrastructure, processes).
- Think of Sevottam as the **quality-management wrapper** that gives charters teeth; pilots in income tax (ASK centres), passports (PSKs), posts.
- **Legislative attempt:** the **Right of Citizens for Time-Bound Delivery of Goods and Services and Redressal of their Grievances Bill, 2011** ("Citizens' Charter Bill") — would have made charters mandatory & enforceable; **lapsed** with the 15th Lok Sabha. Its substance survives in **state Public Services Acts** (Section 6 below).

## 5. Right to Information Act, 2005

### 5.1 Background (the pedigree paragraph)

- **Global:** Sweden's Freedom of the Press Act **1766** (world's first); Art. 19, UDHR/ICCPR.
- **Judicial:** *State of UP v. Raj Narain* (**1975**) — right to know flows from **Art. 19(1)(a)**; *S.P. Gupta* (1981) — open government is the rule.
- **Movement:** **MKSS jan sunwais** (Rajasthan, 1990s) — "hamara paisa, hamara hisab"; **Tamil Nadu (1997)** first state RTI law; **Freedom of Information Act 2002** (never notified) → **RTI Act 2005** (effective **12 October 2005**).

### 5.2 Key provisions (clause-level MCQs)

| Provision | Content |
|---|---|
| Sec. 2(f)/2(j) | Wide definition of "information"/"right to information" (records, emails, samples; inspection, certified copies) |
| Sec. 2(h) | **"Public authority"** — bodies established by Constitution/law/notification, incl. bodies **owned, controlled or substantially financed** by government, and NGOs substantially financed |
| **Sec. 4** | **Proactive (suo motu) disclosure** — the Act's preventive half; chronic weak spot |
| Sec. 5 | PIOs/APIOs in every public authority |
| Sec. 6(2) | **No reason need be given** for the request |
| Sec. 7 | **30 days** (35 via APIO); **48 hours** where **life or liberty** is involved |
| **Sec. 8(1)** | Exemptions — security, foreign relations, cabinet papers (till decision + conclusion), trade secrets, fiduciary, **8(1)(j) personal information** |
| Sec. 8(2) | **Public-interest override** — even exempt information disclosable if public interest outweighs harm |
| Sec. 10 | Severability; Sec. 11 — third-party procedure |
| Secs. 12–17 | **CIC/SICs** (composition & appointment — see File 07) |
| Sec. 19 | Two-tier appeal: first appellate authority → Information Commission |
| Sec. 20 | Penalty on PIO: **₹250/day, max ₹25,000** + disciplinary recommendation |
| Sec. 24 | Intelligence/security organisations (Second Schedule) exempt — **except** information on **corruption and human-rights violations** |
| Sec. 22 | **Overriding effect** over the Official Secrets Act, 1923 |

### 5.3 Landmark cases & developments

- ***CBSE v. Aditya Bandopadhyay* (2011)** — evaluated answer-scripts are information.
- ***RBI v. Jayantilal Mistry* (2015)** — RBI cannot withhold bank-inspection reports pleading fiduciary duty.
- ***CPIO, Supreme Court v. Subhash Chandra Agarwal* (2019, Constitution Bench)** — the **office of the CJI is a public authority** under RTI (subject to Sec. 8 balancing).
- **CIC order (2013):** six national political parties are public authorities — **never complied with** (standing critique).
- **RTI (Amendment) Act, 2019** — Centre prescribes tenure/salary of CIC/ICs (criticised as diluting parity & independence).
- **DPDP Act, 2023 (Sec. 44(3))** — amended **Sec. 8(1)(j)**: personal information now exempt **without** the earlier public-interest/"relationship to public activity" balancing — flagged by activists as narrowing RTI.

### 5.4 Implications & assessment

**Achievements:** ~60 lakh+ applications a year — the world's most-used FOI law; exposed **Adarsh, Commonwealth Games, 2G-era decisions, MGNREGA muster fraud**; normalised the citizen's *right* to question files; fed social audit and investigative journalism; deepened Art. 19(1)(a).

**Concerns:** massive **pendency & vacancies** in Commissions (*Anjali Bhardwaj v. UoI, 2019* — SC directed time-bound filling); weak **Sec. 4** compliance; **attacks on RTI users** (scores killed — link to Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014, still not operationalised); frivolous/vexatious use debate; 2019 amendment + DPDP change as institutional dilution.

**AP tie-in:** **AP State Information Commission** (post-bifurcation reconstitution suffered long vacancy gaps — a ready critical example); RTI works alongside Spandana/PGRS as the citizen's information-plus-grievance toolkit.

## 6. Public Services Acts (Right to Public Services legislation)

### 6.1 Concept

A **Right to Public Services Act** converts charter promises into a **statutory right**: notified services must be delivered within a **stipulated time-limit** by a **designated officer**, failing which the citizen appeals, and the **defaulting official pays a penalty** (sometimes routed as **compensation** to the citizen). It is the Citizen's Charter **with legal teeth** — accountability shifted from moral to enforceable.

### 6.2 Spread (chronology hooks)

- **Madhya Pradesh — first, 2010** (Lok Sevaon ke Pradan ki Guarantee Adhiniyam).
- **Bihar (2011)** — Right to Public Services Act (with the celebrated e-tracking rollout); **Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand, Karnataka ("Sakala", 2011)**; Kerala (2012), West Bengal (2013), Maharashtra (2015 — with a dedicated **Right to Services Commission**), most states since — **20+ states**; **no central law** (the 2011 Citizens' Charter Bill lapsed).
- **Andhra Pradesh's route:** time-limits were first made operational through **MeeSeva (2011) government orders** notifying service-wise deadlines (categories A/B), enforced departmentally; a statutory **Public Services Delivery Guarantee** framework followed in the late 2010s and doorstep-delivery timelines are now embedded in the **village/ward secretariat service standards** — *verify the Act's current name/year from the latest AP yearbook before the exam; the examinable core is MeeSeva-notified timelines + secretariat-era guarantees.*

### 6.3 Standard architecture of these Acts

Notified services list → designated officer + stipulated period → **first & second appellate authorities** → **penalty (₹250–₹5,000 typical) on the erring official** → compensation to applicant (some states) → disciplinary action for repeated default → many states add an independent **Commission** (Maharashtra) and online tracking.

### 6.4 Implications and limits

**Implications:** citizen becomes **rights-holder, not applicant**; internalises accountability (penalty on the *individual* officer, not the anonymous department); measurable responsiveness (dashboards); complements RTI (information) with delivery (service).

**Limits (critical paragraph):** low **citizen awareness**; penalties **rarely actually imposed**; covers only *notified* services and **time, not quality**; appellate layers are departmental (self-judging); works poorly where the bottleneck is upstream (records, staffing); needs the e-governance backbone (Sec. 3) to be self-executing.

## 7. Social audit

### 7.1 Concept

- **Social audit** = a **continuous public process** in which the **users of a programme themselves verify** its records against ground reality — works, muster rolls, expenditure, beneficiary lists — culminating in a **public hearing (jan sunwai/gram sabha)** where officials answer discrepancies.
- **Contrast with financial/CAG audit (favourite comparison):**

| | Financial/CAG audit | Social audit |
|---|---|---|
| Auditor | Professional, external | **Stakeholders/community** (facilitated) |
| Focus | Legality & accuracy of accounts | **Outcomes on the ground**; leakage, quality, inclusion |
| Timing | Post-facto, periodic | Concurrent/continuous |
| Forum | Reports to legislature | **Public hearing** in the Gram Sabha |
| Effect | Institutional accountability | **Direct, local, deterrent** accountability |

### 7.2 Evolution — with AP as the pioneer (home-turf again)

1. **TISCO (Tata Steel), 1979** — first corporate social audit in India (MCQ trivia).
2. **MKSS jan sunwais (1990s)** — the method's true parent: reading muster rolls aloud in villages → led to both RTI and social audit.
3. **73rd Amendment (1992)** — **Gram Sabha (Art. 243A)** as the natural social-audit forum; state Panchayat Acts empower it to audit accounts.
4. **MGNREGA, 2005 — Sec. 17:** statutory mandate — Gram Sabha "shall conduct regular social audits of all projects"; **MGNREGA Audit of Schemes Rules, 2011** (framed in consultation with **CAG**) require an **independent Social Audit Unit (SAU)** in every state, audits of every GP **at least once in six months**.
5. **Andhra Pradesh — the first and best institutionalisation:** statewide NREGS social audits from **2006** through **SSAAT (Society for Social Audit, Accountability and Transparency)** — an **autonomous society** (deliberately outside the implementing department), using **village social auditors** (educated youth of wage-seeker families) trained to verify records door-to-door, followed by **mandal-level public hearings** with a decisions-taken record; findings led to **recoveries of crores and thousands of disciplinary actions/dismissals of field staff**. AP's SSAAT became the **national template** for the 2011 Rules and trained other states' SAUs. (Post-2014 both AP and Telangana continue SSAAT-model societies.)
6. **Meghalaya Community Participation and Public Services Social Audit Act, 2017** — **first state law extending social audit to all departments/schemes** (not just MGNREGA).
7. **Extension:** NFSA (Sec. 28 provides for social audits), **NSAP pensions**, PMAY-G pilots; SC in *Swaraj Abhiyan* (2015–17 orders) pushed states on MGNREGA social-audit compliance; **Mission Antyodaya/People's Plan** use Gram Sabha verification.

### 7.3 Importance (the "and its importance" half of the syllabus line)

1. **Accountability made local and immediate** — the official answers the very people affected.
2. **Deterrence** — continuous possibility of audit reduces muster fraud and ghost works (AP recoveries as evidence).
3. **Deepens democracy** — operationalises the Gram Sabha; participation between elections (links to File 06's civil-society argument).
4. **Empowerment & awareness** — villagers learn entitlements while auditing (each audit doubles as an information campaign).
5. **Better data for the state** — ground-truthing that inspections and financial audit miss.
6. **Complements the accountability chain** — feeds CAG performance audit, vigilance and Lokayukta processes.

### 7.4 Challenges & way forward

- **Independence & funding:** SAUs under implementing departments elsewhere lack AP-style autonomy; funds (0.5% of MGNREGA expenditure) flow late.
- **Follow-up gap:** findings ≠ action — recoveries and prosecutions lag hearings; need statutory duty to act on findings (Meghalaya model / a national social-audit law).
- **Capture & fatigue:** local elites, staged Gram Sabhas, auditor safety.
- **Way forward:** every-scheme social audit (2nd ARC & CAG advocacy), protect auditors (whistle-blower cover), integrate findings with e-FMS recoveries and disciplinary databases, scale the **AP SSAAT model** nationally — a ready-made conclusion: *AP showed social audit works when the auditor is independent, trained, and the hearing is public.*
