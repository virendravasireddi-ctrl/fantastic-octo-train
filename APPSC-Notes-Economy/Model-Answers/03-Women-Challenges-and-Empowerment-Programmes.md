# Model Answer 03 — Challenges Faced by Women & Recent Empowerment Programmes

[← Model-Answers Index](README.md) · [Economy Index](../README.md) · Related: [File 10 §3C](../10-Inclusive-Growth-and-Financial-Inclusion.md) · [File 09 §9](../09-Major-Challenges-of-Indian-Economy.md)

**Q. What are the challenges faced by women in India? Throw some light on the recent programmes initiated by the government for women empowerment.**

*APPSC Group-I Mains style — point-wise, scheme-and-data anchored, with Andhra Pradesh integration. (~10–15 marks.)*

---

## Introduction

Women are nearly **half of India's population** but remain far from equal partners in development. **Empowerment** means expanding their **access** (to education, jobs, credit, health), **agency** (decision-making, mobility, political voice) and **safety**. India's low standing on the **WEF Global Gender Gap Index (~129/146, 2024)** — weakest on *economic participation* and *health* — shows why women's empowerment is central to **inclusive growth** (File 09 §9). Andhra Pradesh, a pioneer of the women's SHG movement, offers both models and cautionary lessons.

## Challenges faced by women in India

**1. Economic**
- **Low female labour-force participation** — risen to **~37–42% (PLFS 2023-24)** from ~23% (2017-18), but still low and largely **unpaid/self-employed/farm** work.
- **Gender pay gap** and occupational segregation; disproportionate **unpaid care work** (Time Use Survey).
- **Asset gap** — few women own land/property despite the **Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act 2005**.

**2. Social**
- **Son preference & adverse sex ratio** — sex ratio at birth **~929 (NFHS-5)**; female foeticide.
- **Early marriage** — ~23% of women 20–24 married before 18 (NFHS-5); dowry; restricted mobility & autonomy under patriarchy.

**3. Safety & violence**
- **Gender-based violence** — ~**29% of women** report spousal violence (NFHS-5); sexual assault, harassment, **trafficking**, dowry deaths, and rising **cyber-crime** against women (NCRB).

**4. Health**
- **Anaemia in 57% of women 15–49 (NFHS-5, worsened)**; maternal mortality **(MMR ~97)** improving but uneven; low autonomy in health decisions; nutrition burden.

**5. Education & digital**
- **Literacy gap** — female ~65.5% vs male ~82% (2011 Census), narrowing; higher-ed enrolment now near-parity, but **secondary dropout** and a **digital gender divide** (fewer women own phones/internet) persist.

**6. Political**
- Under-representation — women are only **~14–15% of the Lok Sabha**; strong only at the **panchayat** level (73rd CAA reservation).

## Recent government programmes for women empowerment

**Union — the umbrella and the landmarks**

| Programme (year) | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mission Shakti (2021-22)** ⭐ | Umbrella scheme, two wings: **Sambal** (safety — **One Stop Centres/Sakhi**, Women Helpline **181**, **Nari Adalat**, Beti Bachao Beti Padhao) + **Samarthya** (empowerment — **PMMVY** maternity benefit, **Shakti Sadan** shelter, **Sakhi Niwas** working-women hostels, **Palna** creches). |
| **Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (2023)** ⭐⭐ | **106th Amendment** — **33% reservation for women** in Lok Sabha & State Assemblies (operational after delimitation) — historic. |
| **Lakhpati Didi (2023)** | Target **3 crore** women SHG members earning ₹1 lakh+/yr via DAY-NRLM. |
| **Namo Drone Didi (2024)** | Drones to women's SHGs for agri-services — new income stream. |
| **Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (2015)** | Sex ratio + girl-child education. |
| **PM Ujjwala Yojana (2016/2.0)** | Free LPG connections to poor women. |
| **Sukanya Samriddhi (2015)** | High-interest girl-child savings. |
| **Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017** | Paid maternity leave raised to **26 weeks**. |
| **Financial & entrepreneurial** | **PMJDY** (majority women account-holders), **MUDRA** (~70% women borrowers), **Stand-Up India** (SC/ST-women entrepreneurs). |
| **Safety infrastructure** | **Nirbhaya Fund**, emergency number **112**, Safe City projects, fast-track POCSO/rape courts, cyber-crime portal. |

*Theme:* India's **G20 presidency (2023)** reframed the goal from "women's development" to **"women-led development"** in the Amrit Kaal.

**Andhra Pradesh** *(current TDP–NDA schemes; names/amounts rebrand with governments — verify from the latest AP Budget)*

- **Aadabidda Nidhi** — ₹1,500/month to women (19–59); **Deepam-2** — 3 free LPG cylinders; **free APSRTC bus travel for women**; **Thalliki Vandanam** — ₹15,000/yr routed to mothers (Super Six).
- **NTR Bharosa** widow/single-women pensions.
- **The SHG engine ⭐:** AP pioneered **DWCRA/"Podupu Lakshmi"**; today **SERP (Velugu/IKP)** women's federations + **Sthree Nidhi Credit Cooperative (2011)** deliver low-cost credit — the backbone of women's financial inclusion (File 10 §10C).

## Critical note (evaluation)

- Schemes are **many, but implementation and mindset lag** — patriarchy, safety and the **care-work burden** keep FLFPR low; the **Women's Reservation Act awaits delimitation**; anaemia and violence indicators remain stubborn.
- The **binding shift** is from *welfare transfers* to *agency* — jobs, assets, safety and representation — backed by convergent delivery (JAM/DBT).

## Conclusion

Women's empowerment is the **highest-return investment** in inclusive and sustainable development — educated, healthy, earning women lift household nutrition, child education and the demographic dividend. India's pivot to **"women-led development"**, and Andhra Pradesh's **SHG-plus-DBT** model, point the right way — provided schemes are matched by **safety, jobs and genuine agency**, not transfers alone. Realising **Viksit Bharat @2047** and **Swarna Andhra @2047** is impossible without half the population as equal partners.
