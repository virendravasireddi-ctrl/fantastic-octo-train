## 0. Frame

- GST is three things at once: a **tax reform** (one nation-one tax), a **constitutional surgery** (101st Amendment), and a **federal bargain** (Council + compensation) ⭐. "Related issues" questions almost always sit in the third box.

## 1. Why GST — and the road to it

- Pre-2017 pathology: **cascading** (tax on tax), fragmented state VATs, CST on inter-state sales, entry taxes/octroi — an internal market broken at every border.
- Arc: **Kelkar task force (2004)** → announcement (2006-07, target 2010) → **101st Constitution Amendment Act, 2016** → **launch midnight 30 June/1 July 2017** ⭐; India chose **dual GST** (Centre + state levies on a common base) unlike single-GST federations.

## 2. Constitutional architecture ⭐⭐

| Provision | Content |
|---|---|
| **Art 246A** | concurrent power of Union & states to make GST laws |
| **Art 269A** | **IGST** on inter-state supplies & imports, apportioned Centre-states |
| **Art 279A** | **GST Council** — Union FM (chair) + Union MoS (Finance) + a minister per state; decisions by **≥3/4 weighted majority: Centre 1/3, states together 2/3** ⭐⭐; quorum half |
| **Art 366(12A)** | GST defined — tax on supply of goods/services **except alcoholic liquor for human consumption** |
| Kept outside | **alcohol (constitutional exclusion)**; **five petroleum products (crude, petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas) — inside GST but zero-dated until the Council decides** ⭐ (classic trap); electricity & stamp duty also outside |

## 3. Design & compliance machinery

- **CGST + SGST/UTGST** on intra-state, **IGST** on inter-state; **destination-based consumption tax** ⭐ (producer states' grievance).
- Registration thresholds: **₹40 lakh goods / ₹20 lakh services** (lower in special-category states); **composition scheme** up to ₹1.5 crore (flat rate, no ITC).
- Machinery: **GSTN** (the IT backbone), returns (**GSTR-1/3B**, QRMP), **e-way bill (2018)**, **e-invoicing (2020 onwards; now ≥₹5 crore turnover)** — the audit trail that made evasion harder ⭐.

## 4. GST 2.0 — the September 2025 overhaul ⭐⭐

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Decision | **56th GST Council (3 Sept 2025)**; announced as "next-generation GST reforms" from the Red Fort (15 Aug 2025); **effective 22 Sept 2025** |
| New structure | **Two principal slabs: 5% and 18%** — the **12% and 28% slabs abolished** ⭐⭐; nearly all 12% items → 5%; ~90% of 28% items → 18% |
| Demerit rate | **40%** on select sin/luxury goods (pan masala, tobacco products, etc.) |
| Exemptions | **individual life & health insurance premiums fully exempted** ⭐ |
| Niche rates | 3% / 0.25% (gold, precious stones) continue |
| Cess | **compensation cess discontinued for all goods except tobacco from 22-9-2025**; levy fully ends **31 March 2026** after loan repayment ⭐ |
| Fiscal cost | estimated net revenue forgone ≈ **₹48,000 crore/year** — the consumption-side stimulus visible in H2 FY26 demand (File 13) |

## 5. The compensation story ⭐ (the federal bargain, completed)

1. **2017-22 guarantee:** states assured **14% annual revenue growth** (base 2015-16), funded by the **compensation cess** on demerit goods.
2. **COVID shortfall:** cess pool collapsed → Centre raised **back-to-back loans ₹1.10 LC (FY21) + ₹1.59 LC (FY22) ≈ ₹2.7 lakh crore** passed to states ⭐.
3. **Extension:** cess continued **beyond June 2022 up to 31 March 2026 solely to repay those loans** — not fresh compensation.
4. **Closure:** loans repaid; cess ends March 2026 — leaving states fully exposed to GST's actual buoyancy (the anxiety behind rate-cut politics, §7).

## 6. Institutions & landmark friction points

- **GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT):** principal bench + state benches, operationalised **2024-25** — closing the disputes gap (label: verify bench status).
- **Mohit Minerals (SC, 2022) ⭐:** GST Council recommendations are **not binding** — Parliament and legislatures retain power; "Indian federalism is a dialogue". The Council works by consensus-persuasion, not command.
- Inverted-duty corrections, ITC-fraud crackdowns (fake-invoice networks; Aadhaar-authenticated registration, risk scoring).

## 7. Related issues — the debate table ⭐⭐

| Issue | Substance | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Rate multiplicity | 0/5/12/18/28+cess ≠ "one tax" | largely resolved by **GST 2.0's two-slab** design ⭐ |
| **Petroleum & alcohol outside** | breaks the input-credit chain; but they are the states' (and AP's) last flexible taxes | unresolved — needs Council consensus |
| **Revenue adequacy post-cess** | effective GST rate (≈11.6% pre-2025, lower after GST 2.0) sits well below the ~15.3% "revenue-neutral rate" once estimated | states' central anxiety for 2026-31 ⭐ |
| **Fiscal autonomy** | states surrendered VAT autonomy for one collective vote each in the Council | Mohit Minerals softens it doctrinally; the veto arithmetic (Centre's 1/3 = blocking minority ⭐) remains |
| Producer-state grievance | destination principle shifts revenue to consuming states | permanent design feature |
| Compliance burden | returns/e-invoicing weigh on MSMEs | thresholds + QRMP mitigate |
| Evasion & fake ITC | multi-crore invoice-mill frauds | analytics, biometric registration |
| IGST settlement | timing/apportionment disputes with states | recurring CAG/Council item |
| Delayed adjudication | tribunal vacuum 2017-24 | GSTAT now standing up |

## 8. Performance scoreboard (label the year)

- **FY25 gross collections ≈ ₹22.08 lakh crore** (monthly average ≈₹1.84 LC); **April 2025 record ₹2.37 LC** ⭐; registrant base >1.4 crore.
- Post-GST-2.0 (H2 FY26): collections dipped on rate cuts, cushioned by volume/consumption growth — update the latest month's PIB figure before the exam.

## 9. AP angle ⭐

- **SGST is AP's largest own-tax head (≈37% of own-tax revenue)** — GST buoyancy *is* AP's revenue story (File 19).
- Post-bifurcation AP is more **consumption-state** than producer-state — the destination principle is mildly AP-friendly; but the **end of the 14% guarantee (2022) and of the cess (2026)** removed the floor under a Hyderabad-less base.
- **Petrol/diesel VAT and liquor excise remain AP's flexible handles** precisely because they are outside GST ⭐ (File 19's constraint analysis).

## 10. Prelims nuggets

1. **101st CAA 2016**; launch **1 July 2017**; **dual GST**.
2. **Art 279A Council votes: Centre 1/3, states 2/3, decisions by 3/4** ⭐.
3. Alcohol = constitutional exclusion; **five petro products = in GST but zero-dated**.
4. **GST 2.0 (56th Council, effective 22-9-2025): slabs 5% & 18% + 40% demerit; 12% & 28% abolished; individual health/life insurance exempt** ⭐⭐.
5. **Compensation cess: ended for all but tobacco 22-9-2025; fully ends 31-3-2026** after repaying ≈₹2.7 LC COVID loans.
6. **Mohit Minerals 2022 — Council recommendations not binding** ⭐.
7. FY25 GST gross ≈ **₹22.08 lakh crore**; record month **₹2.37 LC (Apr 2025)**.
8. GST = **destination-based**; GSTN = the IT backbone; e-invoicing ≥₹5 crore.
9. AP: **SGST ≈37% of own-tax revenue** — largest own-tax head.

## 11. Mains angles

1. **"GST 2.0 completes the reform GST began in 2017." Evaluate.** 2017 unification (§1-3) → residual flaws (multiplicity, cess) → 2025 two-slab + insurance exemption + cess closure (§4-5) → what remains (petroleum, RNR gap, autonomy) → balanced verdict.
2. **GST as a test of cooperative federalism.** Council design (§2) → compensation bargain honoured through COVID (§5) → Mohit Minerals dialogue doctrine (§6) → post-2026 revenue anxiety & states' demands (§7) — use AP as the worked example (§9).
3. **"One nation, one tax, many exclusions."** Petroleum/alcohol economics — input-chain break vs states' fiscal lifelines; the AP illustration; conditions for eventual inclusion.
