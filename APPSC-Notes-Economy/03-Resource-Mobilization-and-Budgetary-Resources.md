## 0. The concept

**Resource mobilization** = the process by which an economy raises and channels **savings and other resources** (financial, physical, natural, human) into **productive investment**. Development economics framing: growth needs investment; investment needs mobilized savings (domestic) plus foreign capital to bridge the **savings–investment gap**; the state mobilizes via taxes, borrowing and institutions, the private sector via markets and intermediaries.

### 0.1 Sources map ⭐ (reproduce this in every Mains intro)

| Public sector | Private sector |
|---|---|
| **Tax revenue** (direct + indirect) | Internal accruals / **retained earnings** |
| **Non-tax revenue** (dividends, royalties, fees, spectrum) | **Bank & NBFC credit** |
| **Public debt** — market borrowings, small savings, external loans | **Capital markets** — equity (IPO/FPO), corporate bonds |
| **Grants** (external, inter-governmental) | **FDI, private equity/venture capital** |
| **Disinvestment & asset monetisation** (non-debt capital receipts) | **External commercial borrowings (ECB)**, ADR/GDR |
| PSU surpluses (**IEBR** — internal & extra-budgetary resources) | Household savings routed via MFs, insurance, pensions |
| User charges; seigniorage (deficit financing — last resort) | Trade credit, FPO/rights, InvITs/REITs |

- India's **gross savings rate ≈30% of GDP**; households contribute the bulk — but **household net financial savings dipped to ≈5% of GDP (FY23)**, a mobilization worry ⭐.

## 1. The constitutional-budgetary frame

- **Art. 265** — "no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law"; **Art. 112** — Annual Financial Statement; **Art. 266** — Consolidated Fund & Public Account; **Art. 267** — Contingency Fund; **Arts. 270/271** — sharing of taxes/surcharges; **Art. 280** — Finance Commission.
- **Receipts classification tree ⭐ (the No.1 MCQ zone):**
  - **Revenue receipts** (no liability/asset change): **tax** + **non-tax**.
  - **Capital receipts**: **debt-creating** (borrowings, small savings) + **non-debt** (loan recoveries, **disinvestment proceeds** — TRAP: disinvestment is a *capital*, non-debt receipt, never "non-tax revenue").
- Scale (BE 2025-26): receipts other than borrowings **≈₹34.96 lakh crore**; expenditure **≈₹50.65 lakh crore**; the gap = fiscal deficit **4.4% of GDP** (File 06).

## 2. Tax revenue ⭐⭐

### 2.1 Structure after GST

| Level | Major taxes today |
|---|---|
| Centre | **Corporation tax, personal income tax** (direct); **CGST, IGST, customs, central excise** (now mainly petro-fuels), cesses/surcharges |
| State | **SGST, state excise (liquor), stamps & registration, motor vehicle tax, land revenue, taxes on petro-fuels (VAT)** |
| Local | Property tax, user charges (74th Amendment fiscal space — weak) |

### 2.2 GST — the biggest indirect-tax reform
- **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**; launched **1 July 2017**; destination-based, dual (CGST+SGST / IGST on inter-state).
- **GST Council — Art. 279A** — Centre ⅓ vote, states ⅔, decisions by ¾ majority — the flagship of **cooperative (fiscal) federalism**.
- Kept out: **petroleum (5 products), alcohol for human consumption, electricity** (rate-notification pending for petro).
- Compensation cess (5 yrs to June 2022; levy continued to repay the COVID-era back-to-back loans).
- **GST 2.0 (56th Council, Sept 2025)** ⭐: rate structure rationalised to **two main slabs — 5% & 18% — plus a 40% demerit rate** (tobacco, pan masala, luxury); insurance premia exempted; effective 22 Sept 2025 — quote as the current-affairs face of indirect-tax reform.
- Performance: **FY25 gross GST ≈₹22.1 lakh crore**; record month **₹2.37 lakh crore (Apr 2025)**; e-invoicing/e-way bills = formalisation dividend.

### 2.3 Direct taxes
- Personal income tax: the **new regime** is default; **Budget 2025 made income up to ₹12 lakh effectively tax-free** (rebate; ₹12.75 lakh for salaried with standard deduction) ⭐.
- Corporation tax (2019 cuts): **22%** existing companies (no exemptions), **15%** new manufacturing.
- **Income-tax Act, 2025** ⭐ — passed Aug 2025, **replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026**; simplified drafting, single "**tax year**" replacing previous-year/assessment-year.
- Direct taxes now **≈56–57% of gross central taxes** — the desirable (progressive) direction.

### 2.4 Concepts examiners test
- **Progressive/proportional/regressive**; direct vs indirect **incidence & impact**.
- **Tax buoyancy** (actual response of revenue to GDP growth, policy changes included) vs **tax elasticity** (response at unchanged rates/base) — buoyancy >1 recently.
- **Laffer curve**; **tax expenditure** (revenue forgone); **tax-GDP ratio: centre gross ≈12%, combined ≈17–18%** — low vs OECD (~34%) → the classic "raise resources without hurting growth" Mains theme.
- **Pigouvian taxes** (carbon/health cesses); **tobin tax** idea; **GAAR**, equalisation levy (rolled back 2024), global minimum tax (Pillar-2) — one-liners.

## 3. Non-tax revenue ⭐

| Head | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Dividends & profits** | **RBI's record surplus transfer ≈₹2.69 lakh crore (May 2025)** under the revised Economic Capital Framework ⭐; PSU/PSB dividends |
| **Interest receipts** | On loans to states, PSUs |
| **Spectrum & licence fees** | Telecom auctions (lumpy) |
| **Royalties** | Offshore petroleum, mining |
| Fees, fines, RTI/passport/visa fees, escheat | — |
| States' non-tax | Mining royalties, forestry, irrigation charges, lotteries, guarantees fees |

- BE 2025-26 central non-tax revenue **≈₹5.8 lakh crore** — cushioned by the RBI dividend; **volatile by nature** (auction/market dependent) → cannot substitute for a broad tax base (evaluation point).
- **User charges** — efficiency case (price signals, cost recovery) vs equity case (lifeline access) — 2nd ARC favoured rational user charges with targeted subsidies.

## 4. Transfers: devolution, grants, and the cess problem

- **15th Finance Commission (N. K. Singh, award 2021–26): 41% of the divisible pool** to states (was 42%; 1% adjusted for J&K/Ladakh UTs); horizontal formula includes income-distance, population (2011), demographic performance, forest & ecology, **tax effort**.
- **Cesses & surcharges are OUTSIDE the divisible pool (Art. 271)** — now roughly **an eighth to a seventh of gross central taxes** — the states' chief fiscal grievance ⭐.
- Grants: **Art. 275** (statutory, FC-recommended) vs **Art. 282** (discretionary — the channel for central schemes); post-devolution revenue-deficit grants; local-body & disaster grants.
- **16th Finance Commission — chair Arvind Panagariya**; award period **2026–31**, report submitted late 2025 — its devolution share is the number to update before your exam ⭐.

## 5. AP fiscal snapshot (Mains localiser)

- **AP Budget 2025-26 size ≈₹3.22 lakh crore.**
- Structural facts to cite: post-2014 bifurcation cost AP its revenue engine (Hyderabad) → **persistent revenue deficit**, heavy reliance on central transfers and borrowings; **outstanding liabilities ≈30%+ of GSDP** (higher counting off-budget corporation borrowings — a recurring CAG observation); own-tax effort concentrated in state excise, stamps, fuel VAT, vehicle tax.
- Mobilization responses: monetisation/land-based financing for **Amaravati**, multilateral loans (File 04), aggressive pursuit of industrial/energy investment (Files 08–09).

## 6. Prelims nuggets

1. **Disinvestment = non-debt CAPITAL receipt** (not non-tax revenue); loan recovery also non-debt capital.
2. **Art. 265** no tax without law; **279A** GST Council; **271** surcharges/cesses not shared.
3. GST: **101st CAA 2016, launch 1.7.2017**; alcohol & 5 petro products outside; **GST 2.0 (Sept 2025): 5/18 + 40% demerit**.
4. **FY25 GST ≈₹22.1 lakh crore; record ₹2.37 lakh crore (Apr 2025)**.
5. Corporate tax **22%/15% (2019)**; income up to **₹12 lakh tax-free (Budget 2025, new regime)**.
6. **Income-tax Act 2025** effective **1 April 2026** — "tax year" concept.
7. **Buoyancy** includes policy changes; **elasticity** excludes them.
8. Tax-GDP: centre **≈12%**, combined **≈17–18%**; direct share **≈56%+**.
9. **RBI dividend ≈₹2.69 lakh crore (2025)** — largest ever.
10. **15th FC: 41%**, N. K. Singh; **16th FC: Panagariya, 2026–31**.
11. Spectrum/RBI dividend = **non-tax**; small savings = **debt receipt** (Public Account).
12. AP Budget 2025-26 **≈₹3.22 lakh crore**; liabilities **≈30%+ of GSDP**.

## 7. Mains angles

1. **"India's problem is not the absence of resources but their mobilization." Discuss the sources available to the public and private sectors.** Sources map → savings-rate & household-financial-savings dip → tax-GDP gap → deepen markets + widen tax base + asset monetisation → AP close.
2. **"GST 2.0 completes what 2017 began." Assess GST as a resource-mobilization and federalism reform.** Design → Council federalism → revenue record → rate rationalisation 2025 → pending: petro inclusion, compensation-era trust.
3. **Why is India's tax-GDP ratio low, and how can it be raised without hurting growth?** Informality, exemptions (tax expenditure), agriculture outside, thresholds → remedies: base-widening, technology (e-invoice, AIS), direct-tax code 2025, rational GST, property-tax reform at local level.
4. **Evaluate non-tax revenue's growing weight (RBI dividends, spectrum).** Windfall vs structural; volatility risk; user-charge rationality (2nd ARC); verdict: supplement, not substitute.
