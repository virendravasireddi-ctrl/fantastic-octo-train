# 21A · External Assistance & Externally Aided Projects (EAPs) in Andhra Pradesh — Mechanism, Past & Present

> **AP-focused deep-dive appendix to the Economy series.** Builds directly on File 04 §3 (external debt) and §6 (AP debt picture). Read it for the syllabus phrase *"public debt … external debt from multilateral agencies"* and for the AP-specific answer material examiners reward — the **mechanism** of how a World Bank / ADB / NDB rupee reaches an Andhra project, and the **project roll-call**, past and present.

---

## 0. What "external assistance" means (and what it is not)

- **External / externally aided project (EAP)** = a state development project financed wholly or partly by a **loan or grant from a multilateral or bilateral development agency** (World Bank, ADB, JICA, NDB, AIIB, KfW, AFD, DFID/FCDO, IFAD …), routed through the Union government.
- It is a form of **external public debt** (File 04) — concessional or semi-concessional **project-tied** borrowing, distinct from:
  - **FDI/FPI** (File 05) — private equity/portfolio flows, not government debt;
  - **Centrally Sponsored / Central Sector schemes** — Union budget transfers, not external agency money;
  - **Polavaram** — a **centrally funded national project** (grant-type transfer under the **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014**), *not* an EAP. ⭐ **Classic contrast trap.**
- Why AP relies on EAPs: a **revenue-deficit, capital-hungry** state (File 04 §6) uses concessional, long-tenor multilateral money to finance irrigation, roads, urban and capital-city infrastructure that its own borrowing space (Art. 293 + FRBM ceilings) cannot fully fund.

## 1. THE MECHANISM — how external money reaches an Andhra project ⭐⭐ (the exam-core)

### 1.1 Constitutional frame — the state cannot borrow abroad
- **Art. 293(1)** — a state may borrow only **within India**; **Art. 293(3)** — it needs the **Centre's consent** while indebted to it. ⭐
- Therefore **the Union (GoI) is the sovereign borrower of record**; the state is the **end-user / implementing** government. No Indian state signs a loan directly with the World Bank.

### 1.2 The nodal machinery
| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| **Department of Economic Affairs (DEA), Ministry of Finance** ⭐ | **Nodal department** for all external assistance — screens, negotiates and signs agreements with agencies |
| **Controller of Aid Accounts & Audit (CAA&A)** — under DEA ⭐ | **Disbursing & accounting authority**: receives funds into the **Consolidated Fund of India (CFI)**, files claims with the lender, tracks **Additional Central Assistance (ACA)** |
| **Line Ministry** (e.g., Jal Shakti, Road Transport, Health) | Technical clearance & appraisal support |
| **State EAP / PMU cell** (AP: Finance Dept + project **PMUs/SPMUs**) | Prepares the DPR, implements, pre-finances and raises reimbursement claims |

### 1.3 The project cycle (memorise the flow) ⭐
```
State line dept prepares project (DPR)
      → State Finance/Planning → DEA screening & inclusion in the pipeline
      → External agency identification → appraisal → negotiation
      → LOAN AGREEMENT signed (typically tripartite: GoI + State + Agency)
      → Implementation by state PMU (spends first from its budget)
      → CAA&A claims disbursement from agency → into CFI
      → funds released to the STATE treasury
```

### 1.4 Disbursement modes (how the cash actually moves)
- **Reimbursement** — the state **pre-finances** eligible expenditure from its own budget, then CAA&A **claims it back** from the agency (the dominant mode). Implication: the state must have **upfront liquidity**; slow claims → the project "parks" on state finances.
- **Direct payment** — agency pays a supplier/contractor directly.
- **Special account / advance** — a revolving advance to speed liquidity.

### 1.5 Back-to-back transfer — the 2005-06 turning point ⭐⭐ (high-value)
| | **Before 2005-06** | **After 2005-06 (12th Finance Commission)** |
|---|---|---|
| Channel | External aid came as **Additional Central Assistance (ACA)** for State Plans | External aid **passed on "back-to-back"** on the **same terms** GoI received |
| Terms to a **general** state (AP) | Blended, cushioned — historically a fixed **loan : grant** mix (grant element built in) | **Full liability** — same interest, maturity and (in principle) currency terms; **no cushion** |
| Special-category states | **90% grant : 10% loan** softening | Back-to-back removed most of that softening (a contested change) |
| Intermediation | GoI acted as a **financial intermediary**, absorbing part of the terms | GoI steps back to being a **conduit**; the state bears the terms |

- **Bottom line for AP (a general / non-special-category state):** since 2005-06, an external **loan is a real repayable liability of the state** on the lender's terms; only **grants** pass through as grants. This is why EAP borrowing shows up in AP's own debt stock and FRBM space. ⭐
- **12th FC logic (2005-10):** the Centre should confine itself to **plan grants** and let states own their borrowings — end the Centre-as-lender model.

### 1.6 Where it lands in the accounts
- Received as a **receipt in the CFI** (CAA&A), then transferred to the **state**; on the state side it is an **external-aided capital receipt** financing plan/capex, serviced by the state thereafter.
- **Exam nuance:** because the servicing is ultimately the state's, EAP loans (and the **guarantees / off-budget borrowings** of state corporations that sometimes accompany them) feed the **CAG's transparency concerns** about AP's true debt (File 04 §6). ⭐

## 3. PAST externally aided projects (united-AP era, pre-2014 bifurcation) ⭐
> On bifurcation (2 June 2014, **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014**), assets, liabilities and ongoing EAPs of united AP were **apportioned between residual AP and Telangana** — so several of these live on as successor projects in both states.

| Project | Agency · ~size · year | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| **APERP — AP Economic Restructuring Project** ⭐ | World Bank · **≈$543 mn** · **1998** | Landmark **reform / structural-adjustment** loan under CM N. Chandrababu Naidu — power, irrigation, roads **+ human development** (primary health, nutrition, primary education) |
| **AP Power Sector Restructuring (APL)** | World Bank · 1999-2000 | Financed the **unbundling of APSEB → APGENCO / APTRANSCO / DISCOMs** and the **APERC** regulator — the model power reform of its day |
| **APDPIP — "Velugu"** ⭐ | World Bank · credit **≈$111 mn** (cost $134.8 mn) · **2000** | **District Poverty Initiatives** in 6 districts; SHG-based; later renamed **Indira Kranthi Patham (IKP)** |
| **APRPRP** | World Bank · **2003** | **Rural Poverty Reduction Project** — scaled the SHG model statewide; implemented by **SERP (Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty)** ⭐ — a globally cited women's SHG success |
| **AP Hazard Mitigation & Emergency Cyclone Recovery** | World Bank · late-1990s | Reconstruction after the **1996 Godavari-delta cyclone** |
| **AP State Highways Project** | World Bank · mid-2000s | Upgrading the core state-highway network |
| **AP Water Sector Improvement Project (APWSIP)** | World Bank · **≈$450 mn** · **2010** | Modernisation of the **Nagarjuna Sagar** irrigation system; participatory water management |
| **AP Community Forest Management / Forestry** | World Bank / JBIC | **Joint/participatory forest management** via **Vana Samrakshana Samithis** |
| **AP Urban Services for the Poor (APUSP)** | **DFID (UK)** · ~1999-2008 | Municipal poverty-reduction, slum infrastructure & governance reform |

- **SERP / Velugu is the standout legacy** — the world's largest women's self-help-group federation model, funded and scaled on World Bank money; frequently cited in Mains answers on **social capital, financial inclusion and participatory development**. ⭐

## 4. PRESENT externally aided projects (residual AP, post-2014) ⭐⭐
> Residual AP's active/ recent EAP portfolio. **Numbers are lender-approval figures — re-verify from the latest state EAP list / Budget before the exam.**

| Project | Agency · size · year | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **AP Disaster Recovery Project (APDRP)** | **World Bank (IDA)** · **$250 mn** · approved 2015 | Recovery after **Cyclone Hudhud (Oct 2014)** — 4 districts; **underground power cabling**, roads, shelters, livelihoods (25-yr IDA credit, 5-yr grace) |
| **APIIATP — AP Integrated Irrigation & Agriculture Transformation Project** ⭐ | **World Bank** · **$172.2 mn** · signed **Nov 2018** | **Climate-resilient agriculture** — ~**1,000 minor-irrigation tanks**, ~90,000 ha, 12 vulnerable districts; 200,000+ marginal farmers |

| **VCIC — Visakhapatnam–Chennai Industrial Corridor** ⭐⭐ | **ADB** · **≈$631 mn** package · from **2016** | First phase of the **East Coast Economic Corridor (ECEC)** — India's first coastal corridor; industrial nodes & trunk infrastructure over ~800 km of AP coast |
| **AP road & bridge network** | **NDB (BRICS)** · **2 × $323 mn = $646 mn** · 2019-20 | (i) **AP Roads & Bridges Reconstruction** — widen ~1,600 km of state highways; (ii) **AP Mandal & Rural Connectivity Improvement** — ~1,400 km district roads |
| **Amaravati capital city** ⭐⭐ | **World Bank $800 mn + ADB ≈$789 mn ≈ $1.6 bn** · **Dec 2024** | Flagship — see §5 |

### 4.1 VCIC — the corridor detail (worth a paragraph in Mains)
- ADB's **≈$631 mn** support = a **$500 mn multi-tranche facility (MFF)** for infrastructure + a **$125 mn policy-based loan** for reforms + a **$5 mn climate-resilience grant** + **$1 mn TA**; **Tranche-1 loan of $245 mn (Sept 2016)**.
- Part of the **East Coast Economic Corridor (Kolkata→Tuticorin)**; dovetails with the **National Industrial Corridor** programme (NICDC). Nodes: **Visakhapatnam, Machilipatnam, Donakonda, Srikalahasti-Chittoor**. AP tie-in for *industrial investment & infrastructure* (File 08). ⭐

## 5. Amaravati — the case study of pull-out and re-engagement ⭐⭐
A perfect Mains vignette on **conditionality, land acquisition and MDB governance**:

| Phase | What happened |
|---|---|
| **2019 — collapse** | GoI **withdrew** the World Bank request (**$300 mn**) and **AIIB pulled out** (**$200 mn**) from the *Amaravati Sustainable Infrastructure & Institutional Development Project*, citing **non-compliance with social/environmental safeguards** — controversy over the **land-pooling scheme** and displacement complaints. Coincided with the state government shelving the single-capital plan. ⭐ |
| **2024 — re-engagement** | With Amaravati revived, the World Bank Board approved **$800 mn** (Amaravati Integrated Urban Development Program, **20 Dec 2024**; 29-yr maturity, 6-yr grace) and **ADB ≈$788.8 mn** (results-based, in Japanese yen), with further MDB **co-financing mobilised — combined ≈$1.6 bn**. ⭐ |

- **Why it matters:** shows (a) MDB **safeguard conditionality has teeth** — a project can be stopped over resettlement/environment; (b) external finance is **hostage to political continuity**; (c) the **back-to-back** rule means this $1.6 bn is **AP's own liability**, not a Central gift — it enlarges the state's debt and FRBM footprint (File 04 §6). ⭐

## 6. Desirability & consequences for AP ⭐ (the syllabus phrase, localised)
| Test | For an Andhra EAP |
|---|---|
| **Real resources** | Genuine **forex/real-resource inflow** now — funds capex the state's own borrowing space can't (Art. 293 + FRBM) |
| **Concessionality & tenor** | Long maturities, grace periods, sub-market rates (esp. **IDA/JICA**) — cheaper than SDL market borrowing |
| **Policy/technical value** | Brings safeguards, procurement discipline, results-based design, TA — capacity-building beyond cash |
| **Exchange & terms risk** | Post-2005 **back-to-back**: the **state carries the terms** (currency, interest, maturity) — rupee depreciation raises the real burden |
| **Conditionality/sovereignty** | Safeguard & reform conditions can **stall or stop** a project (**Amaravati 2019**) |
| **Fiscal transparency** | Adds to the state's debt stock; the accompanying **guarantees / off-budget corporation borrowings** are repeatedly **flagged by CAG** (File 04 §6) |
| **Golden-rule test** | Defensible **only if it finances durable assets** (irrigation, roads, capital city) that service the debt — not consumption |
| **Verdict** | **Useful at the margin, concessional and project-tied** — a rational strategy for a capex-starved state, provided the debt is transparent and the projects are asset-creating |

## 7. Prelims nuggets
1. **DEA (Ministry of Finance)** = nodal dept for external assistance; **CAA&A** = disbursing/accounting authority (funds enter the **CFI**). ⭐
2. **Art. 293** — states borrow only within India, need Centre's consent; **GoI is the sovereign borrower** for all EAPs.
3. **Back-to-back transfer since 2005-06** (**12th Finance Commission**) — external aid passed to states on the **same terms**; ended the Centre-as-intermediary / built-in-grant model. ⭐
4. **Dominant disbursement mode = reimbursement** (state pre-finances, then claims).
5. **Polavaram = centrally funded national project** (AP Reorganisation Act, 2014), **not** an EAP. ⭐
6. **SERP / Velugu (→ Indira Kranthi Patham)** — **World Bank**-funded SHG model; APDPIP **2000**, credit **≈$111 mn**.
7. **APERP (1998, ≈$543 mn)** — World Bank reform loan; financed the **APSEB unbundling** (APGENCO/APTRANSCO/DISCOMs + APERC).
8. **APIIATP** — World Bank **$172.2 mn (2018)**, climate-resilient agriculture, ~1,000 tanks.
9. **SALT** — World Bank **$250 mn (2021)**, school-education / foundational learning.
10. **AP Health Systems Strengthening** — World Bank **$328 mn (2019)**; **AP Disaster Recovery (Hudhud)** — World Bank IDA **$250 mn (2015)**.
11. **VCIC** — **ADB ≈$631 mn**, first phase of the **East Coast Economic Corridor**; Tranche-1 **$245 mn (2016)**.
12. **NDB (BRICS)** — **2 × $323 mn** for AP **roads & bridges** (2019-20).
13. **Amaravati** — WB & AIIB **withdrew in 2019** (safeguards/land pooling) → **WB $800 mn + ADB ≈$789 mn ≈ $1.6 bn re-engagement, Dec 2024**. ⭐
14. **AIIB** committed **$200 mn** to Amaravati (2019) then pulled out; nationally, **India is AIIB's largest borrower**.

## 8. Mains angles
1. **"How does external assistance reach an Andhra Pradesh project?" Explain the mechanism.** Art. 293 → DEA/CAA&A → tripartite agreement → PMU implementation → reimbursement → **back-to-back (2005-06, 12th FC)** → the liability lands on the state. (A near-guaranteed process question.)
2. **Assess the role of multilateral & bilateral agencies in Andhra Pradesh's development.** Agency-wise evidence — WB (SERP, health, SALT, agri, Amaravati), ADB (VCIC), NDB (roads) → gains (concessional capex, TA) vs risks (conditionality, exchange, off-budget/CAG) → verdict.
3. **"External loans are an opportunity and a liability for a state." Examine with AP examples.** Back-to-back terms → real-resource gain vs debt-stock and currency risk → golden-rule/asset-creation test → transparency prescription.
4. **The Amaravati financing saga (2019 → 2024): what does it reveal about MDB conditionality and cooperative development finance?** Safeguards & land pooling → political-continuity risk → re-engagement → state-liability point.
5. **Trace the evolution of Andhra Pradesh's externally aided projects from APERP (1998) to Amaravati (2024).** Reform-loan era → poverty/SHG era (SERP) → sectoral (health/education/agri/disaster) → corridor & capital-city era; read alongside the **shift to back-to-back terms**.
