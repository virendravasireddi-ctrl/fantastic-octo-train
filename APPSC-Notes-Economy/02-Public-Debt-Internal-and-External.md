## 0. Definitions & constitutional frame

- **Public debt** (narrow) = liabilities contracted against the **Consolidated Fund** (Art. 292 Centre; **Art. 293 states** — states need Centre's consent to borrow while indebted to it, **Art. 293(3)** ⭐; states cannot borrow abroad directly).
- **Public liabilities** (broad) = public debt + **Public Account** liabilities (small savings/NSSF, provident funds).
- Composition fact: central government debt is overwhelmingly **internal (~95%); external <5%**, almost all concessional multilateral/bilateral — a deliberate post-1991 design choice ("no original sin": the 2019 idea of sovereign FX bonds was shelved).

## 1. Instruments of internal debt ⭐

| Instrument | Features |
|---|---|
| **Dated G-secs** | 5–50 year coupon bonds — the core of **market borrowings**; auctioned by RBI (govt's **debt manager**) |
| **Treasury Bills — 91/182/364-day** | Zero-coupon, discount instruments (short-term) |
| **Cash Management Bills (2010)** | <91-day, for temporary mismatches |
| **State Development Loans (SDLs)** | States' market borrowings (auction via RBI) |
| **Sovereign Green Bonds (from Jan 2023)** ⭐ | Proceeds earmarked for green expenditure |
| **Small savings (NSSF)** | PPF, NSC, Sukanya Samriddhi, Senior Citizens' — Public Account; administered rates |
| **Ways & Means Advances (WMA, 1997)** | RBI's temporary accommodation to Centre/states — replaced **ad hoc T-bills (abolished 1997)** ⭐ ending automatic monetisation |
| Gold bonds/loans | SGB scheme (2015; discontinued for fresh issues 2024-25) |

- Holders of G-secs: banks (SLR demand), insurance/pension funds, RBI, and now **FPIs (small but rising after index inclusion — File 03)**; **RBI Retail Direct (2021)** opened G-secs to individuals.

## 2. The numbers & rules (label the year; update before exam)

- **Central government debt ≈57% of GDP (FY25)**; general government (Centre+states) **≈82%**.
- Gross market borrowing **≈₹14.8 lakh crore (BE 2025-26)**; net ≈₹11.5 lakh crore.
- **Interest payments ≈₹12.8 lakh crore (BE FY26) — ≈35–40% of revenue receipts, the largest single expenditure head** ⭐ (the "interest treadmill" argument).
- **FRBM Act 2003** → targets; **N. K. Singh FRBM Review (2017)**: debt anchor **60% general / 40% Centre**; **escape clause** (Sec 4(2)) invoked in COVID (FY21 FD 9.2%).
- Post-COVID glide path: FD **4.4% of GDP (BE FY26)** — "below 4.5% by FY26" met; **from FY27 the anchor shifts to a falling debt-GDP ratio — Centre ≈50±1% by March 2031** ⭐ (Budget 2025 announcement).
- States: net borrowing ceiling ≈3% of GSDP + **0.5% power-reform-linked**; 50-year **interest-free capex loans** from Centre (₹1.5 lakh crore window) — borrowing-financed capex push.

## 3. External debt — profile ⭐

- **India's total external debt ≈$736 billion (end-March 2025) ≈19% of GDP** — moderate by EME standards.
- Composition: **ECBs/commercial borrowings the largest chunk; NRI deposits; short-term trade credit ≈18–20%**; US dollar-denominated ≈54%; **debt-service ratio ≈6–7%**.
- Comfort indicators: forex reserves (record **≈$705 bn, Sept 2024**) cover ~90%+ of external debt; sovereign external debt small and concessional.
- **Sovereign external borrowing = mostly multilateral & bilateral project loans** (below); no FX sovereign bonds.

### 3.1 Multilateral (and key bilateral) agencies table ⭐⭐

| Agency | Facts for exam |
|---|---|
| **IBRD & IDA (World Bank group)** | India historically the **largest IDA beneficiary; "graduated" from IDA in 2014** (now IBRD terms); funds DPL/project loans — e.g., **$800 mn for Amaravati (approved Dec 2024)** after the 2019 pull-out — the re-engagement story ⭐ |
| **ADB (Manila, 1966)** | India a founding member & among the **largest sovereign borrowers**; **Visakhapatnam–Chennai Industrial Corridor (VCIC)** loans since 2016; **co-financing Amaravati (~$790 mn)** ⭐ |
| **AIIB (Beijing, 2016)** | **India = 2nd-largest shareholder & the largest borrower** ⭐ (classic MCQ) |
| **NDB (BRICS bank, Shanghai, 2015)** | Founding members with **equal shareholding**; first president K. V. Kamath; funds Indian state infrastructure |
| **IMF** | Not a project lender — BoP support: **1981 EFF; 1991 crisis (gold pledged)**; India now a net contributor; quota ≈2.75% (8th largest); **SDR allocation 2021** |
| **JICA (bilateral, Japan)** | India's **largest bilateral creditor** — Delhi Metro, **Mumbai–Ahmedabad bullet train** (soft yen loans) |

## 4. Loans & grants distinguished

- **Grants** = no repayment (external grants are now a **tiny sliver** of receipts — India is effectively past aid-dependence; contrast the 1950s–60s **Aid-India Consortium (1958)** era, PL-480 food aid).
- **Loans** = repayable with interest — concessional (IDA/JICA) vs market-linked (IBRD/ADB floating), tied vs untied.
- Internal grant flows: FC grants (Art. 275), central-scheme transfers (Art. 282) — for states these are "budgetary resources", for the Union they are expenditure.

## 5. Desirability & consequences — internal vs external borrowing ⭐ (syllabus phrase)

| Test | Internal debt | External debt |
|---|---|---|
| Resource transfer | Redistribution within economy | **Real resource inflow** now, outflow later |
| Currency/default risk | None (own currency) — but inflation risk | **Exchange-rate risk**; "original sin" danger |
| Crowding out | Yes — pre-empts domestic savings, raises yields | No direct crowding out |
| Conditionality/sovereignty | None | Policy conditions (1991 IMF), procurement ties |
| Intergenerational equity | Golden rule: acceptable **if it finances capex** (assets service the debt) | Same, plus transfer burden abroad |
| Sustainability test | **Domar condition: r < g** keeps debt ratio stable ⭐ | Debt-service & reserves cover ratios |
| Verdict | Mainstay — deep G-sec market is a strength | Useful at the margin, concessional & project-tied; cap on short-term/commercial exposure |

- Debt-financing pathologies to cite: **interest treadmill → revenue deficit → borrowing for consumption** (violates golden rule); ratings pressure; financial repression (SLR captive demand).
- Counter-view (functional finance/Keynes): in slumps, borrowing-financed **capex has multiplier ≈2.5×** (File 04) — composition of spending matters more than the deficit number alone.

## 6. AP debt picture (Mains localiser)

- **Outstanding liabilities ≈30%+ of GSDP**, among the higher major states — plus **off-budget borrowings via state corporations/guarantees repeatedly flagged by CAG** ⭐ (transparency point).
- **Art. 293(3) consent + FRBM ceilings** bind AP's space; power-sector add-on and central 50-year capex loans are the extra windows.
- **Multilateral financing in AP:** World Bank + ADB **≈$1.6 bn for Amaravati (2024-25)**; ADB's **VCIC**; NDB-type state road projects — external project debt as a state development strategy (serviced via the Centre).
- **Polavaram** is centrally funded (national project under the **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014**) — grant-type transfer, not debt (good contrast in an answer).

## 7. Prelims nuggets

1. **Art. 292/293** — Union/state borrowing; **states need central consent (293(3))** and cannot borrow abroad.
2. Small savings sit in the **Public Account** (NSSF); market borrowings against the **Consolidated Fund**.
3. **Ad hoc T-bills abolished 1997 → WMA** (end of automatic deficit monetisation).
4. **Sovereign Green Bonds — first issued Jan 2023**; T-bills = 91/182/364 days; CMB <91 days.
5. Central debt **≈57% of GDP (FY25)**; new anchor **≈50±1% by 2031**; general govt ≈82%.
6. **Interest payments = largest expenditure head (≈35–40% of revenue receipts)**.
7. FRBM **2003**; N. K. Singh review **2017 (60/40 debt anchor, escape clause)**; FY21 FD **9.2%**.
8. External debt **≈$736 bn ≈19% of GDP (Mar 2025)**; commercial borrowings largest; short-term ≈1/5.
9. **India graduated from IDA in 2014**; **AIIB — India largest borrower**; NDB — equal BRICS shares, Shanghai.
10. IMF 1991: **gold pledged**; SDR allocation 2021; India = net creditor now.
11. **JICA — largest bilateral lender** (bullet train, metros).
12. **Domar condition: r < g** for stable debt ratio.
13. Amaravati: **World Bank $800 mn + ADB ≈$790 mn (2024-25)**; Polavaram = **centrally funded national project**.

## 8. Mains angles

1. **"Public debt is neither sin nor free lunch." Examine India's debt position and the new fiscal anchor.** Numbers → golden rule vs interest treadmill → Domar → FY27 debt-path anchor → capex-quality defence.
2. **"India borrows abroad cautiously and wisely." Evaluate the external-debt strategy.** Low sovereign/external share, concessional-multilateral bias, no FX bonds → risk indicators comfortable → but rising ECB/short-term shares and rupee depreciation risk → verdict.
3. **Multilateral development banks in India's development — from IDA schoolrooms to AIIB metros.** Agency-wise evidence incl. Amaravati/VCIC → conditionality debate → India as shaper (AIIB/NDB member-founder).
4. **Assess the states' debt problem with reference to Andhra Pradesh.** Ceilings & 293(3) → AP's revenue-deficit + off-budget issue → capex loans & multilateral projects → fiscal-transparency prescription (CAG, guarantee caps).
