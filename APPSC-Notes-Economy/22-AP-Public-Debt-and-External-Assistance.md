## 0. How a state borrows — the AP toolkit ⭐

| Channel | Mechanics |
|---|---|
| **SDLs (State Development Loans)** | Market borrowings auctioned by **RBI (debt manager)**; AP's workhorse; AP's SDL spreads reflect its fiscal news |
| **Art. 293(3) consent + NBC** | Centre's consent while indebted to it; **net borrowing ceiling ≈3% of GSDP + 0.5% power-reform**; Centre now nets off-budget borrowings against the ceiling ⭐ |
| **NSSF / small savings** | Legacy stock (states exited fresh NSSF lending, 2016 — except a few) |
| **Central loans** | 50-year interest-free capex loans; back-to-back COVID GST-compensation loans |
| **WMA / overdraft (RBI)** | Cash management — AP is a frequent user, a *stress symptom* (File 19) |
| **Off-budget / SPV borrowings** | Corporation loans against future revenues (Beverages Corp, APSDC) — the CAG-flagged shadow channel (File 19 §2.4) |
| **External assistance** | §2 below — **states cannot borrow abroad directly**; EAP loans flow **back-to-back via the Centre (since 2005-06)**, state bears the terms ⭐ |

**Debt stock recap (File 19):** official liabilities ≈₹4.9 lakh cr, ~34-36% of GSDP (FY25); + guarantees ≈10% of GSDP; White-Paper contested total ₹9.74 lakh cr (June 2024).

## 1. External assistance — the constitutional plumbing ⭐

- **Art. 293(1):** states may borrow **only within India** — every "World Bank loan to AP" is legally a **loan to India, passed back-to-back to AP**.
- Since **2005-06**, back-to-back transfer means **the state carries the interest and exchange-rate risk** on the same terms the Centre contracted.
- The 2016 **Special Assistance Measure** (File 21) inverted this for AP: the **Centre services AP's EAP repayments in lieu of SCS** — making EAPs unusually attractive for AP ⭐ (join the dots in Mains).

## 2. The AP EAP portfolio ⭐⭐

### 2.1 The historical arc
- **AP Economic Restructuring Project (World Bank, 1998, ≈$540 mn)** — AP under Naidu-1.0 was **the first Indian state to receive state-specific World Bank adjustment lending** ⭐ — the original "reforming state" story.
- **Velugu / Indira Kranthi Patham (World Bank–SERP rural livelihoods)** — the SHG platform that became a national template (NRLM's ancestor).
- Irrigation/agriculture: World Bank community tank management & **APIIATP** (integrated irrigation & agriculture transformation).

### 2.2 The current big-ticket portfolio (label the year; re-verify)

| Project | Financier & size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Amaravati Integrated Urban Development Program** | **World Bank (IBRD) $800 mn, approved Dec 2024** | The re-engagement after the Bank **dropped the earlier ~$300 mn proposal (with ~$200 mn AIIB co-finance) in July 2019** when the project stalled ⭐ — a case study in *political-risk pricing* |
| **Amaravati Inclusive & Sustainable Capital City Development** | **ADB $788.8 mn (results-based), Dec 2024** | Co-financing twin; together **≈₹13,500 cr**, expected to catalyse ≈$600 mn private investment |
| *(Amaravati domestic complement)* | **HUDCO ≈₹11,000 cr** | Domestic PSU finance rounding out the ≈₹23,000 cr city plan (File 20) |
| **Visakhapatnam–Chennai Industrial Corridor (VCIC)** | **ADB ≈$630 mn package (2016: MFF + policy-based loan)** | India's first coastal corridor; node infrastructure + ease-of-doing-business reforms |
| **Road reconstruction & connectivity** | **NDB ≈$646 mn AP Roads & Bridges (2019)** + mandal-connectivity follow-on (≈$300 mn) | BRICS-bank money in state roads |
| Health/urban/water ongoing lines | World Bank health-systems & urban water projects (various) | Smaller, sectoral |

### 2.3 Grant-type external/central flows (contrast, don't confuse)
- **Polavaram is NOT an EAP** — it is a **centrally funded national project** (S.90, NABARD-LTIF route): grant-like, not debt ⭐ (favourite trap).
- FC grants, CSS transfers = internal grants (File 01).

## 3. Desirability & consequences — EAPs for a stressed state ⭐ (syllabus verb)

| Test | Assessment for AP |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | Multilateral terms (IBRD/ADB floating, long tenor) beat SDL yields; with the 2016 in-lieu-of-SCS measure, parts are effectively serviced by the Centre — cheapest money AP can get |
| **Ceiling interaction** | EAP drawdowns **count within the borrowing ceiling** — they *substitute*, not add headroom |
| **Quality of spend** | EAPs are ring-fenced, milestone-audited **capex** — exactly the golden-rule borrowing AP's own budget fails to do (File 19) — plus embedded reform conditionality (VCIC's business-climate covenants) |
| **Risks** | Exchange-rate pass-through; conditionality/sovereignty debate; **pipeline hostage to politics** — the 2019 Amaravati pull-out shows external lenders exit when policy reverses ⭐ |
| **Verdict** | For a state at AP's debt level, **EAP-financed capex is the best borrowing available** — the constraint is credibility, not appetite |

## 4. Prelims nuggets

1. **Art. 293(1)** — states cannot borrow abroad; EAPs flow **back-to-back via the Centre (since 2005-06)**.
2. **AP Economic Restructuring Project (WB, 1998)** — first state-specific World Bank adjustment loan in India ⭐.
3. **Amaravati 2024: World Bank $800 mn + ADB $788.8 mn (≈₹13,500 cr) + HUDCO ₹11,000 cr**; earlier WB/AIIB proposal **dropped July 2019**.
4. **VCIC — ADB ≈$630 mn (2016)**; India's first coastal industrial corridor.
5. **NDB — AP roads ≈$646 mn (2019)**; BRICS bank lends to Indian state projects via Centre.
6. **Polavaram ≠ external assistance** — S.90 national project, NABARD-LTIF, grant-type ⭐.
7. **Special Assistance Measure 2016** — Centre services AP's EAP repayments (SCS substitute) — links Files 21 & 22.
8. **Velugu/SERP (WB)** — AP's SHG model that seeded NRLM.
9. Off-budget SPV borrowings now **netted against AP's borrowing ceiling** by the Centre.

## 5. Mains angles

1. **"External assistance is the best debt a fiscally stressed state can contract." Evaluate for AP.** Toolkit → EAP terms + in-lieu measure → golden-rule quality → risks (FX, conditionality, 2019 exit) → verdict with ceiling caveat.
2. **"From reform poster-child (1998) to lender pull-out (2019) to re-engagement (2024)."** Trace AP's relationship with multilateral lenders as a political-economy narrative — policy stability as creditworthiness.
3. **Debt for the capital: appraise Amaravati's financing model.** LPS land equity (File 20) + multilateral debt + HUDCO + monetisation → self-financing-city thesis → risks if land values disappoint.
