## 0. Why India built a public sector

- IPR 1956's "**commanding heights**"; Nehru's PSUs as "**temples of modern India**".
- Logic: private capital too small/shy for heavy industry; long-gestation infrastructure; **balanced regional development** (steel towns in backward regions); employment; import substitution; sovereign/strategic functions.

## 1. Contribution to industrial development ⭐ (the "defend the PSU" paragraph)

- **Heavy-industry base:** SAIL's plants built with Cold-War-era collaboration — **Bhilai (USSR), Rourkela (West Germany), Durgapur (UK)**, Bokaro (USSR) ⭐; BHEL (machines), HMT, HAL, ONGC (**1956**), IOC, NTPC (**1975**), GAIL.
- **Nationalisation wave:** coal (1971–73 → **Coal India 1975**), oil (1974–76), banks (1969/1980) — put finance and fuel behind planned industry.
- **Regional spread & townships** (Bhilai, Bokaro, and in AP — Vizag Steel); large formal employment; managerial/technical cadre for the whole economy.
- Scale today: ≈ **390 CPSEs (~270 operational)**; FY24 aggregate net profit ≈ **₹3.2 lakh crore**; large dividend payer to the Budget (non-tax revenue — File 03 link) ⭐.
- Autonomy ladder: **Maharatna (14, HAL latest 2024) — Navratna (~25, additions 2023–25) — Miniratna (~150)** (counts move; re-verify).

## 2. What went wrong — the critique

- Low return on the enormous capital employed; **administered pricing** losses; overstaffing; board decisions second-guessed by ministries; monopoly slack; sick units kept alive (BIFR-era — File 29).
- Fiscal drain exemplars: pre-sale **Air India** (₹20 cr/day losses), **BSNL/MTNL** (revival packages cumulating ≈ **₹3.2 lakh crore**, 2019/2022/2023) ⭐.
- Economic-Survey framing: government should exit where **markets work**, stay where **market failure** is real.

## 3. Disinvestment vs privatization — definitions first ⭐ (examiners love this)

- **Disinvestment:** sale of a **minority** stake; government keeps control. Proceeds = **non-debt capital receipts, NOT revenue** ⭐ (classic trap — File 03).
- **Strategic disinvestment/sale:** sale of a substantial block **with transfer of management control**.
- **Privatization:** ownership/control passes to private hands (strategic sale is the vehicle).

## 4. The disinvestment story — timeline ⭐⭐

| Phase | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1991–92 | First minority sales to financial institutions/MFs |
| **Rangarajan Committee 1993** | Up to 74–100% sale in non-strategic sectors recommended |
| **Disinvestment Commission 1996 (G.V. Ramakrishna)** | Case-by-case advice; "strategic vs non-strategic" vocabulary |
| 1999–2004 (Shourie era) | **First strategic sale: Modern Foods (2000)** ⭐; BALCO, Hindustan Zinc, VSNL, IPCL, CMC, Maruti exit |
| 2005 | **National Investment Fund** (proceeds ring-fenced; restructured 2013) |
| 2014–19 | Minority-stake machine: **CPSE ETF, Bharat-22**, buybacks, OFS; targets chronically missed |
| **2021–22** ⭐⭐ | **New PSE Policy 2021**; **Air India → Tata (Jan 2022, ~₹18,000 cr EV)** — first big privatization in ~2 decades; **LIC IPO (May 2022, ₹21,000 cr — then India's largest; overtaken by Hyundai 2024)**; NINL → Tata Steel (2022) |
| Now | **IDBI Bank strategic sale in process**; SCI, BEML, Concor queued but slow; Budgets since FY24 show **no separate disinvestment target** — folded into "miscellaneous capital receipts" (≈₹47,000 cr BE FY26); emphasis shifted to **dividends & value creation** ⭐ |

**PSE Policy 2021** ⭐⭐ — the doctrine: in **four strategic sectors** — (i) atomic energy, space, defence; (ii) transport & telecom; (iii) power, petroleum, coal, minerals; (iv) banking, insurance, financial services — keep a **bare minimum** CPSE presence, privatise/merge/close the rest; **non-strategic sectors: exit**. Nodal agency: **DIPAM**. Companion instrument: **asset monetisation (NMP)** — File 08.

## 5. The debate — arguments to deploy

**For privatization:** efficiency & professionalisation (Maruti, Hindustan Zinc, VSNL post-sale records); stops fiscal bleeding (Air India); releases capital for public goods; deepens markets (LIC listing).
**Against / cautions:** family-silver & undervaluation charges; private monopoly can replace public monopoly (needs regulators — CCI, sector regulators); job & reservation losses; strategic capacity (defence, energy security); timing risk in weak markets.
**Middle path actually chosen:** list-and-professionalise + selective strategic sales + monetise assets + bigger dividends — *pragmatism over ideology* (good closing line).

## 6. AP box ⭐⭐ — the RINL flashpoint & other AP PSU stories

- **RINL / Visakhapatnam Steel Plant:** CCEA approved **100% strategic disinvestment (Jan 2021)** → sustained "Visakha ukku" protests; structural weakness: **RINL is the only major steel PSU without captive iron-ore mines** ⭐. Instead of sale, **Jan 2025: ₹11,440 crore revival package** (₹10,300 cr equity + working-capital support) — privatization shelved for now. Perfect Mains case study: *politics, employment and strategic-sector logic colliding*.
- **Dredging Corporation of India (HQ Visakhapatnam):** sold 2019 to a **consortium of four major ports** — "PSU buying PSU" disinvestment variant ⭐.
- Defence/industrial PSU cluster in Vizag: **Hindustan Shipyard** (Navy's builder), **NSTL/BDL units**, Naval Dockyard — argue strategic-sector retention here.
- Use pattern: RINL for "against hasty privatization", Air India for "for privatization" — balance in one answer.

## 8. Mains angles

1. **"The public sector built Indian industry; it need not run all of it forever."** Contribution (Section 1) → pathologies (Section 2) → PSE Policy 2021 as the synthesis → RINL/Air India paired case studies.
2. **Evaluate India's disinvestment policy since 1991.** Timeline → chronic target-missing → shift from budget-plugging to strategic doctrine (2021) → assess: privatization pipeline slow; dividends now do the fiscal work.
3. **Is privatization of profit-making PSUs justified?** Efficiency-vs-equity framework; Hindustan Zinc/Maruti evidence; safeguards: competition regulation, transparent valuation, worker protection.
