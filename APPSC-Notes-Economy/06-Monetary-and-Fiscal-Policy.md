## 0. Frame

**Musgrave's three functions** of public finance ⭐ — **allocation, distribution, stabilization**. Fiscal policy (tax-spend-borrow) works all three; monetary policy (money & credit) is the stabilization specialist. Resource-mobilization link: monetary policy sets the **price of loanable funds & inflation environment**; fiscal policy decides **how much society routes through the state**.

## 1. Monetary policy (RBI)

- **RBI: est. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934; Hilton Young Commission 1926); nationalised 1949** ⭐. Functions: currency issue, **banker to government & banks, debt manager**, regulator, forex manager (FEMA), lender of last resort, payment systems (UPI era).

### 1.1 The framework — Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) ⭐⭐
- **Urjit Patel Committee (2014)** → **RBI Act amended (2016, sec 45ZA…)**: target **CPI-combined 4% ± 2%**; framework notified for 5-year blocks (2021–26; **renewal due 1 April 2026** — check the notification).
- **Monetary Policy Committee:** **6 members (3 RBI incl. Governor + 3 external), Governor's casting vote**; repo decided by majority; meets ≥4 (in practice 6) times/year.
- **Failure clause:** inflation outside 2–6% for **3 consecutive quarters** → report to government (**triggered once — Nov 2022**) ⭐.

### 1.2 The toolkit (quantitative)

| Tool | Meaning | Marker (mid-2025 — verify) |
|---|---|---|
| **Repo rate** | Policy rate — RBI lends against collateral (LAF) | **5.50%** after the 2025 easing cycle (6.50%→5.50%, Feb–Jun 2025) |
| **SDF (2022)** | Standing Deposit Facility — **collateral-free absorption floor** (replaced fixed reverse repo) ⭐ | repo −25 bps |
| **MSF** | Marginal Standing Facility — penal ceiling | repo +25 bps |
| **CRR** | Cash reserve (no interest) | **cut 4% → 3% (2025, phased)** ⭐ — releases ~₹2.5 lakh crore lendable funds |
| **SLR** | Statutory liquidity (G-secs etc.) | 18% — the "captive demand" for public debt (File 04 link) |
| **OMO / Operation Twist** | Outright G-sec purchases/sales; maturity swaps | Liquidity manager |
| **Bank rate** | Long-term refinance signal (aligned to MSF) | — |

- **Qualitative/selective tools:** margin requirements, credit rationing, moral suasion, sectoral risk-weights (macro-prudential).
- **Transmission:** repo → money market → lending rates; strengthened by **external benchmark lending rates (EBLR, 2019)** ⭐ replacing sluggish MCLR for retail/MSME.
- Stance vocabulary: accommodative / neutral / withdrawal of accommodation.
- Limitations: supply-shock (food/oil) inflation responds weakly; transmission asymmetry; the **"impossible trinity"** (fixed exchange rate, open capital account, independent monetary policy — pick two).

## 2. Fiscal policy

### 2.1 The deficits ladder ⭐⭐ (definitions + BE 2025-26 markers)

| Deficit | Definition | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue deficit** | Revenue expenditure − revenue receipts | ≈1.5% of GDP |
| **Effective revenue deficit (2011-12)** | RD − grants for creation of capital assets | smaller |
| **Fiscal deficit** | Total expenditure − (revenue receipts + **non-debt** capital receipts) = **borrowing requirement** | **4.4% of GDP** |
| **Primary deficit** | FD − interest payments (this year's fresh fault) | ≈0.8% |
| Trap | Budget deficit (old concept) abolished 1997 with ad hoc T-bills | — |

- Financing of FD: market borrowings (largest), small savings, provident funds, external (tiny) — the bridge to File 04.
- **Capex quality argument ⭐: capital-expenditure multiplier ≈2.5× vs ≈1× for revenue spending** (NIPFP/RBI estimates); Centre's capex **≈₹11.2 lakh crore (BE FY26, ~3.1% of GDP)** — the post-COVID "crowding-IN" strategy.

### 2.2 Rules & institutions
- **FRBM 2003 → N. K. Singh review (2017) → escape clause (COVID: FY21 FD 9.2%) → glide to 4.4% (FY26) → debt-anchor regime from FY27 (Centre ≈50±1% of GDP by 2031)** — the one-line history every answer needs.
- Off-budget cleanup (FY22): FCI-NSSF loans etc. brought on-budget — transparency milestone ⭐.
- States: FRBM acts, **3% + 0.5% (power reforms)** net-borrowing ceilings, Art. 293(3) consent (File 04); **AP note:** repeated deviations/supplementaries and off-budget guarantees make AP a live case study of state-FRBM stress.

### 2.3 Consequences of deficit financing (the classic evaluation)
- **Inflation tax** (monetisation), **crowding out** private credit vs Keynesian **crowding in** during slack; twin-deficit link (fiscal → current account); intergenerational equity (golden rule); ratings & risk premia.
- History markers: automatic monetisation via ad hoc T-bills ended **1997 (WMA)**; FRBM barred RBI's **primary subscription** to G-secs (from 2006); COVID revived the "should RBI monetise?" debate — India chose heavy borrowing + G-SAP purchases (secondary market) instead.

## 3. Coordination (and friction)

- Shared terrain: **debt management** (RBI as banker vs inflation-fighter — "fiscal dominance" risk), liquidity vs borrowing calendar, small-savings rates vs transmission.
- **2020–25 case study ⭐:** pandemic — fiscal expansion (FD 9.2%) + monetary easing (repo 4%, G-SAP); 2022–23 — inflation above 6% → MPC letter + 250 bps hikes to 6.5%; 2025 — **CPI at multi-year lows (~2% mid-2025)** after food disinflation & GST 2.0 → easing cycle (repo 5.5%, CRR 3%) synchronised with fiscal consolidation (4.4%) — textbook counter-cyclical **handshake**; quote it as the model.
- **FSDC** (2010) — apex financial-stability coordination; Ministry of Finance ↔ RBI ↔ SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA.

## 4. Prelims nuggets

1. RBI: **1935, Hilton Young (1926), nationalised 1949**; debt manager & banker to government.
2. FIT: **Urjit Patel 2014 → 2016 RBI Act amendment; CPI 4±2%**; renewal cycle 5 years (next: 2026).
3. **MPC = 6 members, Governor's casting vote**; failure = **3 consecutive quarters** outside band (Nov 2022).
4. **SDF (2022) = collateral-free floor**; MSF = ceiling; corridor ±25 bps.
5. **CRR cut 4%→3% (2025)**; SLR 18%; **EBLR 2019** for transmission.
6. 2025 easing: repo **6.5% → 5.5% (Feb–Jun 2025)** — verify current rate.
7. **FD = borrowing requirement**; PD = FD − interest; **ERD** concept (2011-12); RD ≈1.5%, FD **4.4%**, PD ≈0.8% (BE FY26).
8. **Capex multiplier ≈2.5×** vs revenue ≈1×; capex ₹11.2 lakh crore (BE FY26).
9. Ad hocs abolished **1997**; RBI primary subscription barred (FRBM, 2006); G-SAP = **secondary-market** purchases (2021).
10. **Escape clause** — FRBM sec 4(2); FY21 FD **9.2%**.
11. Impossible trinity; Musgrave's three functions.
12. States: **3% + 0.5% power-linked** ceiling; 50-yr interest-free capex loans (₹1.5 lakh crore window).

## 5. Mains angles

1. **"Monetary policy sets the weather; fiscal policy decides the harvest." Examine their respective roles in resource mobilization.** Rates/inflation environment & SLR-captive debt demand vs tax-borrow-spend; coordination case study 2020–25.
2. **Has inflation targeting served India well?** Design → record (disinflation, one failure episode 2022, ~2% lows 2025) → critiques (food-shock dominance, growth trade-off, core-vs-headline debate at the 2026 review) → verdict: keep flexible.
3. **"Judge deficits by what they buy." Discuss India's post-COVID fiscal strategy.** Escape clause → capex-led consolidation (multiplier logic) → new debt anchor → risks (interest burden, states' off-budget) → AP illustration.
4. **Deficit financing: instrument of development or engine of inflation?** History (1950s–80s monetisation → 1991) → institutional locks (1997, FRBM) → when rules should bend (2020) → discipline with flexibility.
